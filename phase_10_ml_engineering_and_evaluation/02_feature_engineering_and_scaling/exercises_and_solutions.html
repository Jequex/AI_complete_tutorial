Problem 1: The Dummy Variable Trap Proof

Matrix Algebra Proof

Consider a linear model with an intercept column $\mathbf{1}$ and 3 mutually exclusive categorical indicator columns $\mathbf{x}_1, \mathbf{x}_2, \mathbf{x}_3$ from one-hot encoding.

  1. Prove mathematically that the Gram matrix $X^T X$ is singular (non-invertible).
  2. Describe two standard engineering techniques to fix this singularity.
  3. Explain why Ridge regression ($L_2$) avoids this issue.
View Complete Solution

1. Proof of Singularity:

$$\mathbf{x}_1 + \mathbf{x}_2 + \mathbf{x}_3 = \mathbf{1} \implies 1\cdot\mathbf{1} - \mathbf{x}_1 - \mathbf{x}_2 - \mathbf{x}_3 = \mathbf{0}$$

For $v = [1, -1, -1, -1]^T \ne \mathbf{0}$, we have $X v = \mathbf{0}$. Therefore $(X^T X) v = X^T (X v) = \mathbf{0}$, meaning $\lambda = 0$ is an eigenvalue and $\det(X^T X) = 0$. The matrix is singular.

2. Two Resolutions: (a) Drop one dummy column ($K-1$ encoding), letting the intercept absorb the reference class; or (b) Fit the model without an explicit intercept term $w_0$.

3. Ridge Impact: Ridge optimizes $(X^T X + \lambda I)$. Since all eigenvalues shift by $\lambda > 0$, the matrix becomes strictly positive definite and invertible.

Problem 2: Outlier Breakdown Point: StandardScaler vs. RobustScaler

Robust Statistics

A dataset consists of 20 normal values between 40 and 60, plus one extreme billionaire outlier $M = 10,000$.

  1. Compute the median, $Q_1, Q_3$, and $\text{IQR}$.
  2. What happens to $x_{\text{robust}}$ of the median sample as $M \to \infty$?
  3. What happens to the Z-score of typical samples under StandardScaler as $M \to \infty$?
View Complete Solution

1. Order Statistics: $Q_2 = 50$, $Q_1 = 44.5$, $Q_3 = 56.5$, $\text{IQR} = 12.0$.

2. Robust Invariance: $x_{\text{robust}} = \frac{50 - 50}{12.0} = 0.0$. Because $M$ only impacts the highest rank, the order statistics are unchanged as $M \to \infty$.

3. Standard Breakdown: Sample standard deviation $s$ scales as $\mathcal{O}(M)$. As $M \to \infty$, the denominator explodes, squashing all 20 normal samples into an indistinguishable microscopic point near $-0.23$.

Problem 3: Empirical Bayes Target Encoding

Bayesian Smoothing

In ad click prediction with global CTR $\mu_{\text{global}} = 0.05$ and smoothing prior $m = 10$: $$S(c) = \frac{n_c \bar{y}_c + m \mu_{\text{global}}}{n_c + m}$$

  1. Domain A has $n_A = 2$ impressions and $2$ clicks ($\bar{y}_A = 1.0$). Compute $S(A)$.
  2. Domain B has $n_B = 10,000$ impressions and $600$ clicks ($\bar{y}_B = 0.06$). Compute $S(B)$.
  3. Explain how $m$ serves as a pseudo-count prior.
View Complete Solution

1. Small Domain A:

$$S(A) = \frac{2 \times 1.0 + 10 \times 0.05}{2 + 10} = \frac{2.5}{12} \approx 0.2083 \quad (20.8\%)$$

Prevents small sample noise from assigning an unrealistic 100% CTR.

2. Large Domain B:

$$S(B) = \frac{10,000 \times 0.06 + 0.5}{10,010} \approx 0.05999 \quad (6.0\%)$$

Empirical evidence dominates the prior when $n_B \gg m$.

3. Prior Interpretation: $m$ represents the strength of our belief in the global average, acting as $m$ hypothetical prior observations before observing real category data.
