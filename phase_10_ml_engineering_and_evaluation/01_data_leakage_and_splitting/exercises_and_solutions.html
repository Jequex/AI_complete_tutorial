Problem 1: The Churn Post-Decision Confounder

Case Study & Audit

An engineer trains an XGBoost model predicting customer churn ($y \in \{0, 1\}$) using: tenure_months, avg_monthly_bill, support_tickets_count, retention_discount_applied, and data_usage_gb.

Offline 5-fold CV shows 99.4% ROC-AUC, but production accuracy is 53.1%.

  1. Identify the variable causing target leakage and explain why.
  2. Define the exact temporal inequality that valid features must satisfy.
  3. How can the pipeline be corrected?
View Complete Solution

1. Leaked Feature: retention_discount_applied. In customer support operations, retention discounts are only assigned when a customer calls to cancel. The discount is an effect of impending churn, not a predictor. At test time before churn is initiated, this feature is unpopulated.

2. Temporal Constraint: For prediction time $t_{\text{pred}}$ and event realization time $t_{\text{event}}$:

$$\text{timestamp}(x_j) \le t_{\text{pred}} < t_{\text{event}}$$

3. Fix: Drop concurrent flags or replace with historic lag features such as prior_year_discounts computed strictly prior to $t_{\text{pred}}$.

Problem 2: Mathematical Proof of Preprocessing Leakage

Mathematical Proof

Suppose global mean $\hat{\mu} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N x_i$ is computed across the entire dataset of size $N$ prior to partitioning into training (size $N-M$) and test (size $M$). Features are scaled as $z_i = (x_i - \hat{\mu})/\sigma$.

  1. Show that training sample $z_i$ mathematically depends on test sample $x_k$ ($k \in \text{test}$).
  2. Compute the exact covariance $\text{Cov}(z_i, x_k)$ assuming i.i.d. observations with variance $\sigma^2$.
  3. State the correct engineering implementation.
View Complete Solution

1. Proof of Dependency:

$$z_i = \frac{x_i - \left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j \in \text{train}} x_j + \frac{1}{N}\sum_{m \in \text{test}} x_m\right)}{\sigma}$$ $$\frac{\partial z_i}{\partial x_k} = -\frac{1}{N\sigma} \ne 0$$

Since the derivative is non-zero, every training feature is algebraically contaminated by test set values.

2. Exact Covariance:

$$\text{Cov}(z_i, x_k) = \frac{1}{\sigma}\left(\text{Cov}(x_i, x_k) - \frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^N \text{Cov}(x_j, x_k)\right)$$

Because samples are i.i.d., $\text{Cov}(x_i, x_k) = 0$ for $i \ne k$, and $\text{Cov}(x_k, x_k) = \sigma^2$:

$$\text{Cov}(z_i, x_k) = \frac{1}{\sigma}\left(0 - \frac{1}{N}\sigma^2\right) = -\frac{\sigma}{N}$$

3. Protocol: Compute $\hat{\mu}_{\text{train}}$ solely on training samples; apply it as a fixed constant to test instances.

Problem 3: Random vs Stratified K-Fold in Rare Events

Probability Analysis

A medical dataset of $N=100$ patients has only $5$ positive cases ($y=1$). We perform standard 5-fold CV (each fold has size 20).

  1. What is the probability that a single validation fold contains zero positive cases?
  2. What is the probability that at least one of the 5 folds contains zero positive cases?
  3. How does Stratified $K$-Fold eliminate this?
View Complete Solution

1. Single Fold Zero Probability (Hypergeometric):

$$P(X = 0) = \frac{\binom{5}{0}\binom{95}{20}}{\binom{100}{20}} = \frac{80 \times 79 \times 78 \times 77 \times 76}{100 \times 99 \times 98 \times 97 \times 96} \approx 0.3188 \quad (31.9\%)$$

2. At Least One Fold Zero: The only way no fold has zero is if every one of the 5 folds gets exactly 1 positive:

$$P(\text{all get 1}) = \frac{20^5}{\binom{100}{5}} = \frac{3,200,000}{75,287,520} \approx 0.0425$$ $$P(\ge 1 \text{ fold has zero}) = 1 - 0.0425 = 0.9575 \quad (95.75\%)$$

Over 95% of random splits will produce at least one fold with zero positive samples!

3. Stratified Fix: Partitions positives and negatives independently, allocating exactly 1 positive and 19 negatives to every fold.
