Problem 1: Wilcoxon-Mann-Whitney Equivalence to AUC-ROC

Ranking Theory Proof

Let positive set $\mathcal{D}_+$ have $N_+$ instances and negative set $\mathcal{D}_-$ have $N_-$ instances. The Wilcoxon-Mann-Whitney rank statistic is:

$$\hat{A} = \frac{1}{N_+ N_-} \sum_{i=1}^{N_+} \sum_{j=1}^{N_-} \mathbb{I}(s(x_i^+) > s(x_j^-))$$
  1. Prove that the area under the empirical ROC curve (AUC-ROC) equals $\hat{A}$.
  2. What is the expected value of $\hat{A}$ for a random guesser?
  3. How does sorting reduce computation from $\mathcal{O}(N_+ N_-)$ to $\mathcal{O}(N \log N)$?
View Complete Solution

1. Proof: Sorting by descending score, every positive sample creates a vertical step of $\frac{1}{N_+}$ and every negative creates a horizontal step of $\frac{1}{N_-}$. The height of the curve at each negative instance $x_j^-$ is the fraction of positives ranked above it: $\frac{1}{N_+}\sum_{i=1}^{N_+} \mathbb{I}(s_i^+ > s_j^-)$. Summing these vertical bars times width $\frac{1}{N_-}$ across all negative instances yields exactly $\hat{A}$.

2. Random Baseline: For random rankings, by symmetry $P(s^+ > s^-) = 0.5$, so $\mathbb{E}[\hat{A}] = 0.5$.

3. Complexity: Sorting takes $\mathcal{O}(N \log N)$. Once sorted, the sum of ranks formula computes $\hat{A}$ in a single linear $\mathcal{O}(N)$ pass.

Problem 2: Tuning $F_\beta$ for Medical Screening

Harmonic Mean Trade-offs

The generalized $F_\beta$ score is defined as:

$$F_\beta = (1 + \beta^2) \frac{P \cdot R}{\beta^2 P + R}$$
  1. Show that $F_1$ is the harmonic mean of Precision and Recall.
  2. In cancer screening where a False Negative is fatal, should $\beta > 1$ or $\beta < 1$?
  3. Compute $F_2$ and $F_{0.5}$ for $P = 0.50$ and $R = 0.90$.
View Complete Solution

1. Harmonic Mean: For $\beta=1$: $F_1 = \frac{2 P R}{P + R} = \frac{2}{P^{-1} + R^{-1}} = (\frac{P^{-1} + R^{-1}}{2})^{-1}$.

2. Cancer Screening: Choose $\beta > 1$ (e.g. $\beta=2$) to weight Recall more heavily than Precision.

3. Calculations:

$$F_2 = 5 \times \frac{0.45}{4(0.50) + 0.90} = \frac{2.25}{2.90} \approx 0.7759 \quad (77.6\%)$$ $$F_{0.5} = 1.25 \times \frac{0.45}{0.25(0.50) + 0.90} = \frac{0.5625}{1.025} \approx 0.5488 \quad (54.9\%)$$

Problem 3: The Murphy Decomposition of Brier Score

Calibration Analysis

The Brier score decomposes into three orthogonal components:

$$\text{BS} = \text{Reliability} - \text{Resolution} + \text{Uncertainty}$$
  1. What does each component measure intuitively?
  2. Compute the three terms for a naive baseline predicting constant base rate $f = \bar{y}$.
  3. Why does Platt scaling improve Brier score without hurting resolution?
View Complete Solution

1. Definitions:

  • Reliability: Calibration gap $(f_k - \bar{y}_k)^2$. Lower is better.
  • Resolution: Sorting power $(\bar{y}_k - \bar{y})^2$. Higher is better.
  • Uncertainty: Inherent noise $\bar{y}(1 - \bar{y})$ of the problem.

2. Constant Predictor: Reliability = 0 (perfectly calibrated), Resolution = 0 (zero sorting), Brier score = $\bar{y}(1 - \bar{y})$.

3. Platt Scaling Effect: Since the logistic transform is monotonic, rankings and resolution are completely preserved while driving the reliability error toward zero.
