Topic 10.3: Evaluation Metrics, Trade-offs & Probability Calibration

Rigorous quantification of model efficacy: precision vs recall, harmonic mean proofs, ROC vs PR curves in extreme imbalance, and Platt probability calibration.

1. Executive Summary & The 12-Year-Old Analogy

Accuracy is deeply misleading when classes are imbalanced. In fraud detection with 99.9% legitimate traffic, predicting that every transaction is honest produces 99.9% accuracy while catching zero criminals. Production ML requires multidimensional evaluation.

🧭 The 12-Year-Old Analogy: The Rare Disease Medical Test

Imagine a rare condition that affects only 1 out of 1,000 students in a school:

  • The Lazy Doctor (Accuracy = 99.9%): He says "You're fine!" to everyone without looking. He scores 99.9%, but the sick child gets no treatment.
  • Precision (Confidence in Alarms): When the alarm sounds, how likely is it real? High precision means low false alarms.
  • Recall (Catching Sick Children): Out of all genuinely sick children, how many were caught? High recall means no sick child is missed.
  • Probability Calibration: If a weather reporter predicts a 70% chance of rain on 100 days, it should rain on roughly 70 of those days.

2. Mathematical Symbol Decoder

Symbol Formula Meaning
Precision $\frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FP}}$ Positive Predictive Value: fraction of positive predictions that are true.
Recall $\frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FN}}$ Sensitivity / TPR: fraction of actual positives captured.
Specificity $\frac{\text{TN}}{\text{TN} + \text{FP}}$ True Negative Rate: fraction of actual negatives correctly identified.
F1-Score $\frac{2 P R}{P + R}$ Harmonic mean of Precision and Recall.
MCC $\frac{\text{TP}\cdot\text{TN} - \text{FP}\cdot\text{FN}}{\sqrt{(\text{TP}+\text{FP})(\text{TP}+\text{FN})(\text{TN}+\text{FP})(\text{TN}+\text{FN})}}$ Matthews Correlation Coefficient (ranges $[-1, +1]$).
Brier Score $\frac{1}{N}\sum (\hat{p}_i - y_i)^2$ Mean squared error of probabilistic predictions against binary truth.

3. Mathematical Analysis: Why the Harmonic Mean for $F_1$?

The arithmetic mean $A(P, R) = \frac{P + R}{2}$ allows a model with $100\%$ Recall and $1\%$ Precision to score an apparently respectable $50.5\%$.

In contrast, the harmonic mean:

$$F_1 = \left(\frac{P^{-1} + R^{-1}}{2}\right)^{-1} = \frac{2 P R}{P + R}$$

heavily penalizes extreme disparities. For $P=0.01$ and $R=1.0$, $F_1 = \frac{0.02}{1.01} \approx 0.0198$ ($1.9\%$). It approaches zero if either precision or recall collapses.

4. ROC Curves vs. Precision-Recall Curves

The ROC curve plots TPR against FPR across all decision thresholds $\tau$. The area under the ROC curve represents:

$$\text{AUC-ROC} = P(\hat{s}(X^+) > \hat{s}(X^-))$$

However, when $N_{\text{neg}} \gg N_{\text{pos}}$ (e.g. 1,000,000 negatives and 100 positives), False Positive Rate $\frac{\text{FP}}{\text{TN} + \text{FP}}$ stays near 0 even with 1,000 false alarms. The ROC curve deceptively appears near-perfect ($\text{AUC} > 0.98$).

The Precision-Recall (PR) curve exposes this flaw because Precision $\frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FP}}$ directly reflects that 1,000 false alarms reduce precision to under 9%.

5. Probability Calibration & Platt Scaling

Many high-performance classifiers (such as gradient boosted trees, SVMs, and Naive Bayes) provide uncalibrated confidence scores.

Platt Scaling fits a scalar logistic sigmoid on validation scores:

$$P(Y=1 \mid s) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp(A \cdot s + B)}$$

This aligns prediction probabilities with empirical true frequency, minimizing the Brier Score.
