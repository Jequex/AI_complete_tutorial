Topic 4.3: Core Probability Distributions in AI

Neural networks output probability distribution parameters. Understanding the shapes and properties of Bernoulli, Categorical, and Gaussian distributions is essential for choosing loss functions and designing generative architectures.

🧒 1. The 12-Year-Old Analogy: Maya & Barnaby at the Golden Retriever Kennel

Maya visits a kennel with 100 puppies:

  • The Bandana Check (Bernoulli): Does a puppy wear a red bandana? Yes ($1$) or No ($0$). That is Binary Classification (Sigmoid + BCE).
  • The Food Bowl Choice (Categorical): Choosing one of 5 colored bowls is Multi-Class Prediction (Softmax + Cross-Entropy in LLMs).
  • Puppy Heights (Gaussian Bell Curve): Measuring heights of 1,000 puppies clusters around the average height of 20 inches with a symmetric spread $\mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$.
  • Height AND Weight Together (Multivariate Gaussian $\mathcal{N}(\mu, \Sigma)$): Taller puppies weigh more, creating a correlated 2D point cloud. Used in VAE and Diffusion latent spaces!

📋 2. Mathematical Symbol Decoder Table

Symbol Pronunciation Plain English Meaning Concrete Real-World Analogy / Flashcard Example
$\text{Bernoulli}(p)$ "Bernoulli of p" Coin flip with success probability $p$ Asking if Barnaby is wearing a red bandana (Yes/No)
$\text{Cat}(p_1, \dots, p_K)$ "Categorical" Choice among $K$ distinct categories Puppy choosing one of 5 colored food bowls
$\mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$ "Normal / Gaussian" 1D symmetric bell curve with mean $\mu$ and spread $\sigma^2$ The height distribution of 1,000 golden retriever puppies
$\mathcal{N}(\mu, \Sigma)$ "Multivariate normal" $D$-dimensional bell curve with covariance matrix $\Sigma$ Measuring puppy height, weight, and ear length together
$d_M^2$ "Mahalanobis distance" $(\mathbf{x} - \mu)^T \Sigma^{-1} (\mathbf{x} - \mu)$ Measuring how unusual a puppy is, adjusted for correlation

3. The Gaussian PDF Formula

$$f(x) = \frac{1}{\underbrace{\sigma \sqrt{2\pi}}_{\text{Normalizer}}} \exp\left( -\frac{\overbrace{(x - \mu)^2}^{\text{Squared distance}}}{\underbrace{2\sigma^2}_{\text{Spread}}} \right)$$

🧒 What the math is actually doing: When $x = \mu$, distance is zero and $e^0 = 1$ (the highest peak). As $x$ moves away from the mean, $e^{-\text{distance}}$ smoothly tapers off symmetrically to zero.

4. The VAE Reparameterization Trick

$$\mathbf{\mathbf{z} = \mu + \sigma \odot \epsilon \quad \text{where } \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)}$$
Why Deep Learning Needs This: You cannot backpropagate gradients through a random sampling node! By drawing external noise $\epsilon$, the latent code $\mathbf{z}$ becomes a fully differentiable function of $\mu$ and $\sigma$.
