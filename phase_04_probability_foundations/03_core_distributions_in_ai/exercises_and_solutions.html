Problem 1: Bernoulli MLE & Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) Loss

Medium

Derive the Maximum Likelihood Estimator $\hat{p}$ for $N$ independent Bernoulli samples by maximizing $\ell(p) = \sum y_i \ln p + (1-y_i) \ln(1-p)$, and explain its equivalence to BCE loss.

Click to Reveal Step-by-Step Solution
1. Differentiating Log-Likelihood: $$\frac{d\ell}{dp} = \sum_{i=1}^N \left(\frac{y_i}{p} - \frac{1-y_i}{1-p}\right) = 0$$ $$\frac{\sum y_i}{p} = \frac{N - \sum y_i}{1 - p} \implies \hat{p}_{\text{MLE}} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N y_i$$
2. BCE Equivalence: $$\mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}} = -\frac{1}{N} \ell(p) = -\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N [y_i \ln(p) + (1-y_i) \ln(1-p)]$$ Minimizing BCE loss directly maximizes the Bernoulli target likelihood.

Problem 2: 2D Multivariate Gaussian Density by Hand

Medium

Given $\mu = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$ and $\Sigma = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$:

  1. Compute $|\Sigma|$ and $\Sigma^{-1}$.
  2. Compute the Mahalanobis distance squared $D_M^2$ for $\mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$.
  3. Compute density $f(\mathbf{x})$.
Click to Reveal Step-by-Step Solution
1. Determinant & Inverse: $$|\Sigma| = 4 - 1 = 3, \quad \Sigma^{-1} = \frac{1}{3} \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$$
2. Mahalanobis Distance: $$D_M^2 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1/3 \\ 1/3 \end{bmatrix} = \frac{2}{3} \approx 0.6667$$
3. Probability Density: $$f(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{2\pi \sqrt{3}} e^{-1/3} \approx \mathbf{0.0658}$$

Problem 3: The VAE Reparameterization Trick

Challenging

In a VAE, a latent code is parameterized as $z = \mu + \sigma \epsilon$ where $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$. Compute $\frac{\partial z}{\partial \mu}$, $\frac{\partial z}{\partial \sigma}$, and show how backpropagation flows through the loss $\mathcal{L}(z)$.

Click to Reveal Step-by-Step Solution
1. Partial Derivatives: $$\frac{\partial z}{\partial \mu} = 1, \quad \frac{\partial z}{\partial \sigma} = \epsilon$$
2. Chain Rule Gradient Flow: $$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mu} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial z} \times 1 = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial z}$$ $$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \sigma} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial z} \times \epsilon$$ This isolates stochasticity to $\epsilon$, allowing backpropagation to compute deterministic gradients for $\mu$ and $\sigma$.
