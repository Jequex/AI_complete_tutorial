Topic 4.5: Limit Theorems, Monte Carlo & LLM Sampling

Limit theorems explain why training on empirical data succeeds (Law of Large Numbers), why Gaussian curves appear everywhere (Central Limit Theorem), how AI calculates high-dimensional integrals (Monte Carlo), and how LLMs generate human-quality text (Temperature, Top-K, and Top-P).

🧒 1. The 12-Year-Old Analogy: The Popcorn Dartboard & The Storytelling Robot

Maya and Timmy explore three magical probability systems:

  • The Galton Board (Central Limit Theorem): Dropping 10,000 chaotic steel balls through pegs always stacks into a perfect, smooth Gaussian bell curve at the bottom!
  • The Popcorn Dartboard (Monte Carlo Integration): Measuring the area of an irregular pond by throwing 1,000 popcorn kernels at the board and counting what fraction splash inside.
  • The Storytelling Robot (LLM Decoding):
    • Freezing Cold ($T = 0.2$): Robotic, stiff, repeats the safest word every time.
    • Warm & Balanced ($T = 0.7$): Engaging, fluent, creative stories.
    • Boiling Hot ($T = 2.0$): Unhinged, random, chaotic hallucinations!

📋 2. Mathematical Symbol Decoder Table

Symbol Pronunciation Plain English Meaning Concrete Real-World Analogy / Flashcard Example
$\bar{X}_n$ "X bar sub n" Sample Mean: average of $n$ observations Running average score after rolling a die $n$ times
$\text{LLN}$ "Law of Large Numbers" Sample average converges to true expected value $\mu$ Flipping 10,000 coins converges to within 0.1% of 50% Heads
$\text{CLT}$ "Central Limit Theorem" Sum of independent noise sources becomes Gaussian Dropping chaotic marbles on pegs always creates a bell curve
$\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{N})$ "big-O of one over root N" Monte Carlo error convergence rate To cut error in half, you must throw $4\times$ as many darts
$T$ "Temperature" Softmax scaling parameter controlling entropy Creativity dial: cold ($0.2$) is rigid, warm ($0.7$) is natural
$\text{Top-}p$ "Top p" or "Nucleus" Dynamic probability cutoff (smallest set with sum $\ge p$) Keeping only the candidate words sharing 90% of probability

3. The Central Limit Theorem Master Formula

$$\mathbf{Z_n = \frac{\bar{X}_n - \mu}{\sigma / \sqrt{n}} \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0, 1) \quad \text{as } n \to \infty}$$

🧒 What the math is actually doing: Whatever arbitrary distribution you start with, summing up $30+$ random draws guarantees that the sum approaches a symmetric Gaussian bell curve!

4. Monte Carlo Integration

$$\int g(x) f(x) \, dx = \mathbb{E}_{X \sim f}[g(X)] \approx \mathbf{\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N g(x_i)}$$
Beating the Curse of Dimensionality: Standard numerical grid integration explodes exponentially in high dimensions ($10^{100}$). Monte Carlo converges at rate $\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{N})$ independent of dimension $D$!

5. LLM Token Decoding: Temperature, Top-K & Top-P

$$p_i = \frac{\exp(z_i / T)}{\sum_j \exp(z_j / T)}$$
  • Low Temperature ($T < 1.0$): Sharpen probabilities, favoring top choices (factual).
  • Top-$k$: Retain strictly the top $k$ highest-ranked tokens.
  • Top-$p$ (Nucleus): Retain the dynamic smallest set of tokens whose cumulative probability reaches $p$ (e.g. $90\%$).
