Problem 1: Zero Covariance Does NOT Imply Independence

Medium

Let $X \sim \text{Uniform}(-1, 1)$ and $Y = X^2$.

  1. Compute $\mathbb{E}[X]$, $\mathbb{E}[Y]$, and $\mathbb{E}[XY]$.
  2. Compute $\text{Cov}(X, Y)$.
  3. Explain why $X$ and $Y$ are completely dependent despite having zero linear covariance.
Click to Reveal Step-by-Step Solution
1. Expected Values: $$\mathbb{E}[X] = \int_{-1}^1 \frac{x}{2} dx = 0$$ $$\mathbb{E}[Y] = \mathbb{E}[X^2] = \int_{-1}^1 \frac{x^2}{2} dx = \frac{1}{3}$$ $$\mathbb{E}[XY] = \mathbb{E}[X^3] = \int_{-1}^1 \frac{x^3}{2} dx = 0$$
2. Covariance: $$\text{Cov}(X, Y) = \mathbb{E}[XY] - \mathbb{E}[X]\mathbb{E}[Y] = 0 - (0 \times \frac{1}{3}) = \mathbf{0}$$
3. Dependency: $Y$ is completely deterministic given $X$ ($Y = X^2$). Covariance only captures linear relationships, making it blind to quadratic dependencies!

Problem 2: Derivation of He / Kaiming Weight Initialization

Medium

For a linear layer $z = \sum_{i=1}^{d_{\text{in}}} w_i x_i$ followed by ReLU $a = \max(0, z)$ where $\text{Var}(a) = \frac{1}{2} \text{Var}(z)$, derive the required weight variance $\sigma_w^2$ to preserve variance $\text{Var}(a) = \text{Var}(x_i) = \sigma_x^2$.

Click to Reveal Step-by-Step Solution
1. Variance of Pre-activation $z$: $$\text{Var}(z) = \sum_{i=1}^{d_{\text{in}}} \text{Var}(w_i x_i) = d_{\text{in}} \sigma_w^2 \sigma_x^2$$
2. Post-ReLU Activation Variance: $$\text{Var}(a) = \frac{1}{2} d_{\text{in}} \sigma_w^2 \sigma_x^2 = \sigma_x^2 \implies \frac{1}{2} d_{\text{in}} \sigma_w^2 = 1 \implies \mathbf{\sigma_w^2 = \frac{2}{d_{\text{in}}}}$$ This yields the famous He / Kaiming normal initialization.

Problem 3: Covariance Matrix & Correlation by Hand

Challenging

Given 3 points in $\mathbb{R}^2$: $x_1 = [1, 2]^T, x_2 = [2, 5]^T, x_3 = [3, 5]^T$. Compute sample variances, sample covariance, the $2 \times 2$ covariance matrix $\Sigma$, and Pearson correlation $\rho$.

Click to Reveal Step-by-Step Solution
1. Mean Vector $\mu$: $$\mu_1 = \frac{1+2+3}{3} = 2.0, \quad \mu_2 = \frac{2+5+5}{3} = 4.0 \implies \mu = \begin{bmatrix} 2.0 \\ 4.0 \end{bmatrix}$$
2. Covariance Matrix $\Sigma$: $$s_1^2 = \frac{(-1)^2 + 0^2 + 1^2}{2} = 1.0, \quad s_2^2 = \frac{(-2)^2 + 1^2 + 1^2}{2} = 3.0$$ $$s_{12} = \frac{(-1)(-2) + (0)(1) + (1)(1)}{2} = \frac{3}{2} = 1.5$$ $$\Sigma = \begin{bmatrix} 1.0 & 1.5 \\ 1.5 & 3.0 \end{bmatrix}$$
3. Pearson Correlation $\rho$: $$\rho = \frac{1.5}{\sqrt{1.0 \times 3.0}} = \frac{1.5}{\sqrt{3}} \approx \mathbf{0.8660}$$
