Problem 1: Zero Covariance Does NOT Imply Independence

Medium

🧒 The Context: For $X \sim \text{Uniform}(-1, 1)$ and $Y = X^2$, prove they have zero covariance while being $100\%$ dependent.

  1. Compute $\mathbb{E}[X]$, $\mathbb{E}[Y]$, and $\mathbb{E}[XY]$.
  2. Compute covariance $\text{Cov}(X, Y)$.
  3. Explain the geometric meaning.
Click to view solution for Problem 1
1. Expectations: $\mathbb{E}[X] = 0.0$ (by symmetry). $\mathbb{E}[Y] = \mathbb{E}[X^2] = \int_{-1}^1 \frac{1}{2} x^2 dx = \mathbf{1/3}$. $\mathbb{E}[XY] = \mathbb{E}[X^3] = 0.0$ (odd function integral).
2. Covariance: $\text{Cov}(X, Y) = \mathbb{E}[XY] - \mathbb{E}[X]\mathbb{E}[Y] = 0 - (0 \times 1/3) = \mathbf{0.0}$.
3. Geometric Insight: Knowing $X = 0.5$ gives $Y = 0.25$ with certainty ($100\%$ dependent!), but linear covariance cannot detect quadratic parabolas.

Problem 2: Derivation of He (Kaiming) Weight Initialization

Medium

🧒 The Context: Single neuron $y = \sum_{i=1}^{D_{\text{in}}} w_i x_i$ with ReLU activation.

  1. Show $\text{Var}(y) = D_{\text{in}} \text{Var}(w) \text{Var}(x)$.
  2. Account for ReLU variance halving ($\frac{1}{2}\text{Var}(y)$).
  3. Derive the optimal weight variance $\text{Var}(w) = \frac{2}{D_{\text{in}}}$.
Click to view solution for Problem 2
1. Variance of Linear Sum: For zero-mean independent weights and inputs: $\text{Var}(w_i x_i) = \text{Var}(w)\text{Var}(x) \implies \text{Var}(y) = D_{\text{in}}\text{Var}(w)\text{Var}(x)$.
2. ReLU Variance Halving: $\text{Var}(\text{ReLU}(y)) = \frac{1}{2} D_{\text{in}} \text{Var}(w) \text{Var}(x)$.
3. Preserving Variance: Setting $\text{Var}(\text{ReLU}(y)) = \text{Var}(x) \implies \frac{1}{2} D_{\text{in}} \text{Var}(w) = 1 \implies \mathbf{\text{Var}(w) = \frac{2}{D_{\text{in}}}} \implies \sigma_w = \sqrt{\frac{2}{D_{\text{in}}}}$. This is the world-famous Kaiming He initialization formula!

Problem 3: Covariance Matrix & Correlation by Hand

Challenging

🧒 The Context: Centered data vectors $X = [2, 0, -2]^T, Y = [4, 0, -4]^T, Z = [-2, 0, 2]^T$.

  1. Compute sample covariance matrix $\Sigma = \frac{1}{2} A^T A$.
  2. Compute Pearson correlation matrix $P$.
  3. Interpret correlations $\rho_{12}$ and $\rho_{13}$.
Click to view solution for Problem 3
1. Covariance Matrix: $$\Sigma = \begin{bmatrix} 4.0 & 8.0 & -4.0 \\ 8.0 & 16.0 & -8.0 \\ -4.0 & -8.0 & 4.0 \end{bmatrix}$$
2. Pearson Correlation Matrix: $$P = \begin{bmatrix} +1.00 & +1.00 & -1.00 \\ +1.00 & +1.00 & -1.00 \\ -1.00 & -1.00 & +1.00 \end{bmatrix}$$
3. Interpretation: $\rho_{12} = +1.00$ indicates perfect positive linear synchronization, while $\rho_{13} = -1.00$ indicates perfect inverse mirror-image lockstep.
