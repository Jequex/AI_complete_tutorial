Topic 4.4: Expectation, Variance & Covariance

Linearity of Expectation, Spread Metrics, Pearson Correlation & Covariance Matrices

1. Symbol Decoder Table

Symbol Name Formula Plain-English Meaning
$\mathbb{E}[X]$ Expected Value $\sum x p(x)$ or $\int x f(x) dx$ The balance point / center of mass of the distribution
$\text{Var}(X)$ Variance $\mathbb{E}[X^2] - (\mathbb{E}[X])^2$ The average squared distance of outcomes from the center
$\text{Cov}(X, Y)$ Covariance $\mathbb{E}[XY] - \mathbb{E}[X]\mathbb{E}[Y]$ Do two variables move together (+) or opposite (-)?
$\rho(X, Y)$ Pearson Correlation $\frac{\text{Cov}(X, Y)}{\sigma_X \sigma_Y} \in [-1, 1]$ Normalized linear dependency independent of units

2. Linearity of Expectation

The Superpower of Machine Learning:
$$\mathbb{E}[aX + bY + c] = a\mathbb{E}[X] + b\mathbb{E}[Y] + c$$ Crucial Fact: This equality holds true even when $X$ and $Y$ are strongly dependent or non-linearly entangled!

3. Covariance & Variance of Sums

Variance of Summed Random Variables:

$$\text{Var}(X + Y) = \text{Var}(X) + \text{Var}(Y) + 2\text{Cov}(X, Y)$$

If $X$ and $Y$ are uncorrelated ($\text{Cov}(X, Y) = 0$), then:

$$\text{Var}(X + Y) = \text{Var}(X) + \text{Var}(Y)$$

4. The Covariance Matrix in High-Dimensional AI

For feature vector $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^D$:

$$\Sigma = \mathbb{E}[(\mathbf{x} - \mu)(\mathbf{x} - \mu)^T]$$

Diagonal entries are individual feature variances $\text{Var}(X_i)$, while off-diagonal entries represent interactions $\text{Cov}(X_i, X_j)$. This matrix forms the foundation of PCA dimensionality reduction and Batch Normalization.
