Topic 4.1: Probability Fundamentals & Bayes' Theorem

Kolmogorov's Axioms, Conditional Probability, Independence, and the False Positive Paradox

1. Symbol Decoder Table

Symbol Name Formal Meaning 12-Year-Old Analogy
$\Omega$ Sample Space The set of all possible outcomes A big jar holding every marble that could be picked
$P(A)$ Probability Number in $[0, 1]$ measuring event likelihood The fraction of the jar filled with blue marbles
$P(A \mid B)$ Conditional Probability Probability of $A$ given that $B$ happened Looking only at striped marbles, what fraction are blue?
$P(\theta \mid D)$ Posterior Probability Updated belief in hypothesis $\theta$ after seeing data $D$ The detective updating who the culprit is after seeing the footprint

2. Kolmogorov's Three Axioms

The Bedrock Rules of Probability

  1. Non-negativity: $P(A) \ge 0$ for every event $A$. (No negative chances!)
  2. Unitarity: $P(\Omega) = 1.0$. (Something in the universe is guaranteed to happen).
  3. Countable Additivity: If $A_1, A_2, \dots$ are disjoint ($A_i \cap A_j = \emptyset$): $$P\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^\infty A_i\right) = \sum_{i=1}^\infty P(A_i)$$

3. Bayes' Theorem: Updating Beliefs with Evidence

Bayes' Theorem defines how an AI agent refines its parameter estimates or class predictions after observing sensory data:

$$\underbrace{P(\theta \mid D)}_{\text{Posterior}} = \frac{\overbrace{P(D \mid \theta)}^{\text{Likelihood}} \times \overbrace{P(\theta)}^{\text{Prior}}}{\underbrace{P(D)}_{\text{Evidence / Marginal}}}$$

Expanding the denominator using the Law of Total Probability:

$$P(\theta_j \mid D) = \frac{P(D \mid \theta_j) P(\theta_j)}{\sum_{i=1}^k P(D \mid \theta_i) P(\theta_i)}$$

4. The False Positive Paradox

⚠️ The Medical Test Paradox:
A rare disease affects $1$ in $10{,}000$ people ($P(\text{Disease}) = 0.0001$). A rapid test has a 99% True Positive Rate and a 1% False Positive Rate.
If a patient tests positive, what is the probability they are actually sick?

Step-by-Step Bayesian Solution:

$$P(\text{Disease} \mid \text{Pos}) = \frac{P(\text{Pos} \mid \text{Disease}) P(\text{Disease})}{P(\text{Pos} \mid \text{Disease}) P(\text{Disease}) + P(\text{Pos} \mid \text{Healthy}) P(\text{Healthy})}$$

$$P(\text{Disease} \mid \text{Pos}) = \frac{0.99 \times 0.0001}{(0.99 \times 0.0001) + (0.01 \times 0.9999)} = \frac{0.000099}{0.000099 + 0.009999} \approx \mathbf{0.98\%}$$

Even with a 99% accurate test, there is less than a 1% chance the patient has the disease! Because healthy people are overwhelmingly more common, 1% false alarms dwarf true positives!
