Topic 6.2: Joint & Conditional Entropy

How Context Collapses Uncertainty & Why Modern LLMs Rely on the Chain Rule of Entropy.

🧒 1. Intuitive Analogy: The Umbrella Detective & Context Clues

Imagine you are standing inside an office building with no windows. You want to guess what someone walking through the front lobby is holding. Is it an umbrella or a coffee cup? Right now, your uncertainty is high.

Now imagine a security guard shouts: "It is pouring rain outside!"

Suddenly, your uncertainty collapses! You can predict with 99% certainty: "They are holding an umbrella!"

In information theory:

  • Joint Entropy $H(X, Y)$: The total uncertainty about both things together (the weather AND what they are holding).
  • Conditional Entropy $H(Y \mid X)$: The uncertainty that remains about $Y$ (the object) after you already know $X$ (the rain).

The Golden Rule: "Information Never Hurts." Conditioning on context can never increase average uncertainty: $H(Y \mid X) \le H(Y)$.

🔣 2. Mathematical Symbol Decoder Table

Symbol Mathematical Name Plain English Meaning
$X, Y$ Random Variables Two different uncertain events (e.g. Previous Word $X$, Next Word $Y$).
$P(x, y)$ Joint Probability Probability that $X = x$ and $Y = y$ occur at the same time.
$P(y \mid x)$ Conditional Probability Probability of $Y = y$ given that we already observed $X = x$.
$H(X, Y)$ Joint Entropy Total uncertainty in bits when measuring both variables together.
$H(Y \mid X)$ Conditional Entropy Remaining uncertainty about $Y$ once $X$ is known.
$H(X)$ Marginal Entropy Standalone uncertainty of $X$ ignoring everything else.

📐 3. Formal Definitions

Joint Shannon Entropy

The joint entropy of two discrete random variables $(X, Y)$ is:

$$H(X, Y) = -\sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}} \sum_{y \in \mathcal{Y}} P(x, y) \log_2 P(x, y)$$

It represents the average number of optimal binary questions needed to identify both $X$ and $Y$ simultaneously.

Conditional Shannon Entropy

Conditional entropy is the expected value of the entropy of $Y$ given $X$:

$$H(Y \mid X) = \sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}} P(x) H(Y \mid X = x) = -\sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}} \sum_{y \in \mathcal{Y}} P(x, y) \log_2 P(y \mid x)$$

🔬 4. Complete Derivation: The Chain Rule of Entropy

Theorem: The Chain Rule

$$H(X, Y) = H(X) + H(Y \mid X)$$

Proof:

By definition of conditional probability: $P(x, y) = P(x) P(y \mid x)$, which implies:

$$\log_2 P(x, y) = \log_2 P(x) + \log_2 P(y \mid x)$$

Substitute this into the formula for joint entropy:

$$H(X, Y) = -\sum_{x} \sum_{y} P(x, y) \Big[ \log_2 P(x) + \log_2 P(y \mid x) \Big]$$ $$H(X, Y) = -\sum_{x} \sum_{y} P(x, y) \log_2 P(x) - \sum_{x} \sum_{y} P(x, y) \log_2 P(y \mid x)$$

In the first term, $\log_2 P(x)$ is independent of $y$, so factor it out:

$$-\sum_{x} \log_2 P(x) \left( \sum_{y} P(x, y) \right)$$

Since the marginal distribution satisfies $\sum_{y} P(x, y) = P(x)$:

$$-\sum_{x} P(x) \log_2 P(x) = H(X)$$

The second term is precisely the definition of $H(Y \mid X)$:

$$-\sum_{x} \sum_{y} P(x, y) \log_2 P(y \mid x) = H(Y \mid X)$$

Combining both terms proves the Chain Rule:

$$H(X, Y) = H(X) + H(Y \mid X) \quad \blacksquare$$

📉 5. Fundamental Law: Conditioning Never Increases Entropy

Theorem: Information Never Hurts

$$H(Y \mid X) \le H(Y)$$

Equality holds if and only if $X$ and $Y$ are independent ($P(x, y) = P(x)P(y)$).

Knowing extra information $X$ can only ever reduce (or at worst leave unchanged) our uncertainty about $Y$. In modern AI, this explains why expanding the context window of Large Language Models (LLMs) from 512 tokens to 128,000+ tokens makes generation sharper, more factual, and dramatically less prone to hallucinations.
