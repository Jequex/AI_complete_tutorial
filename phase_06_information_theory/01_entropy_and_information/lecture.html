Topic 6.1: Entropy & The Fundamental Currency of Information

Discovering the mathematical nature of information: surprisal, Shannon entropy, the binary entropy function, and the ultimate speed limit of data compression.

1. Executive Summary & The 20 Questions Game

In 1948, Claude Shannon founded the digital age with a singular insight: Information is mathematically identical to the reduction of uncertainty.

The 12-Year-Old Analogy: The "20 Questions" Game

Imagine playing "Guess Who?" with 8 secret characters on the board:

  • Question 1: "Are they female?" $\to$ Cuts candidates from 8 to 4.
  • Question 2: "Do they wear glasses?" $\to$ Cuts candidates from 4 to 2.
  • Question 3: "Do they have red hair?" $\to$ Pins down the exact 1 winner!

You needed exactly 3 questions to identify 1 out of 8 possibilities ($2^3 = 8 \iff \log_2(8) = 3$). In information theory, one perfect yes/no question that halves uncertainty is defined as ONE BIT of information!

2. Surprisal (Self-Information)

Before calculating entropy for a system, we measure the information delivered by a single event $x$. Shannon defined Surprisal as:

$$\mathbf{I(x) = -\log_2 P(x) = \log_2 \left( \frac{1}{P(x)} \right)}$$
  • Certain event ($P = 1.0$): "The sun rose today." Surprisal is $-\log_2(1.0) = \mathbf{0 \text{ bits}}$. Zero surprise, zero news.
  • Fair coin flip ($P = 0.5$): "Heads." Surprisal is $-\log_2(0.5) = \mathbf{1 \text{ bit}}$.
  • Rare event ($P = 0.001$): "A major earthquake struck." Surprisal is $-\log_2(10^{-3}) \approx \mathbf{9.97 \text{ bits}}$.

3. Shannon Entropy Formula

The Shannon Entropy $H(X)$ is the expected surprisal across all possible outcomes:

$$\mathbf{H(X) = \mathbb{E}[I(X)] = -\sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}} P(x) \log_2 P(x)}$$

The Binary Entropy Function $H_b(p)$

For a coin that lands Heads with probability $p$ and Tails with probability $1 - p$:

$$H_b(p) = -p \log_2 p - (1 - p) \log_2(1 - p)$$
Key Properties:
  • Maximum at $p = 0.5$: When a coin is fair, uncertainty is maximal ($H = 1.0 \text{ bit}$).
  • Zero at $p = 0$ or $p = 1$: When the outcome is guaranteed in advance, uncertainty vanishes completely ($H = 0.0 \text{ bits}$).

4. Shannon's Source Coding Theorem

Shannon proved that entropy is not just an abstract idea—it is the physical speed limit of data compression:

$$\mathbf{L \ge H(X)}$$

No lossless compression algorithm (such as ZIP, gzip, or 7-Zip) can ever represent an information source with fewer than $H(X)$ bits per symbol on average without permanently corrupting data!

5. Deep Learning Connection: Language Models as Compressors

A large language model (like GPT-4) predicts the probability of the next token. If the model is exceptionally intelligent, its predicted probability for the actual token is high ($P \to 1.0$), meaning its surprisal $-\log_2 P$ is tiny. Training an LLM to minimize cross-entropy is mathematically identical to building the ultimate data compression engine!
