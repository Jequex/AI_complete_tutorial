Topic 6.5: Mutual Information & Information Bottleneck

Measuring Shared Knowledge & Understanding How Deep Neural Networks Generalize.

🧒 1. Intuitive Analogy: The Walkie-Talkie & The Exam Cheat Sheet

The Walkie-Talkie: You shout a secret code $X$ into a noisy radio. Your friend receives sound $Y$. How much of your original secret made it through the static? That shared secret knowledge is Mutual Information $I(X; Y)$. If the line is crystal clear, $I(X; Y)$ equals total information $H(X)$. If disconnected, $I(X; Y) = 0$.

The Exam Cheat Sheet (Information Bottleneck): You must prepare for a 1,000-page history exam ($X$) to predict the answers ($Y$). You cannot memorize 1,000 pages word-for-word, so you write a 10-page cheat sheet ($T$).

  • Compress: Forget irrelevant details (what color socks the general wore) $\to \min I(X; T)$.
  • Preserve: Keep all key facts that answer the test questions $\to \max I(T; Y)$.

This is exactly how deep neural networks learn: compressing millions of raw pixels into abstract, invariant concepts!

📐 3. Mathematical Definition

Formal Definition of Mutual Information

$$I(X; Y) = \sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}} \sum_{y \in \mathcal{Y}} P(x, y) \log_2 \left( \frac{P(x, y)}{P(x) P(y)} \right)$$

The KL Divergence Equivalence

$$I(X; Y) = D_{\text{KL}}\Big( P(x, y) \parallel P(x) P(y) \Big) \ge 0$$

Mutual information is always non-negative, and equals zero if and only if $X$ and $Y$ are completely independent.

🔄 4. The Fundamental Information Identities

Five Equivalent Representations

  1. $I(X; Y) = H(X) - H(X \mid Y)$ (Uncertainty reduction in $X$ after knowing $Y$)
  2. $I(X; Y) = H(Y) - H(Y \mid X)$ (Uncertainty reduction in $Y$ after knowing $X$)
  3. $I(X; Y) = H(X) + H(Y) - H(X, Y)$ (Sum of individual entropies minus joint entropy)
  4. $I(X; X) = H(X)$ (Self-information is Shannon Entropy)
  5. $I(X; Y) = I(Y; X)$ (Symmetry: $X$ tells you as much about $Y$ as $Y$ tells you about $X$)

🍾 5. Naftali Tishby's Information Bottleneck Principle

The Deep Learning Optimization Problem

In a neural network $X \to T \to Y$, the optimal latent representation $T$ minimizes:

$$\min_\theta \mathcal{L}_{\text{IB}} = I(X; T) - \beta I(T; Y)$$

Phase 1 (Fitting): In early training epochs, gradients rapidly increase $I(T; Y)$ to classify training labels.

Phase 2 (Compression): In later epochs, SGD noise compresses internal representations, driving $I(X; T)$ downward while maintaining high $I(T; Y)$. The network discards superficial pixel noise and retains only abstract semantic concepts!
