Exercise 1: Asymmetry of KL Divergence on Loaded vs. Fair Coins

Distribution $P$ (Loaded Coin): $P(H) = 0.90, P(T) = 0.10$

Distribution $Q$ (Fair Coin): $Q(H) = 0.50, Q(T) = 0.50$

  1. Compute Forward KL $D_{\text{KL}}(P \parallel Q)$ in bits.
  2. Compute Reverse KL $D_{\text{KL}}(Q \parallel P)$ in bits.
  3. Explain intuitively why $D_{\text{KL}}(P \parallel Q) \ne D_{\text{KL}}(Q \parallel P)$.
Click to reveal Step-by-Step Solution

Solution:

1. Forward KL:

$$D_{\text{KL}}(P \parallel Q) = 0.90 \log_2(1.80) + 0.10 \log_2(0.20) \approx 0.7632 - 0.2322 = 0.5310\text{ bits}$$

2. Reverse KL:

$$D_{\text{KL}}(Q \parallel P) = 0.50 \log_2(0.5556) + 0.50 \log_2(5.00) \approx -0.4240 + 1.1610 = 0.7370\text{ bits}$$

3. Intuition: In Reverse KL, the fair coin generates Tails 50% of the time, causing massive surprise under model $P$. The distribution driving the generation determines which outcomes dominate the penalty!

Exercise 2: Gaussian KL Divergence Complete Derivation

Derive the closed-form formula for $D_{\text{KL}}(\mathcal{N}(\mu_0, \sigma_0^2) \parallel \mathcal{N}(\mu_1, \sigma_1^2))$.

Click to reveal Step-by-Step Solution

Solution:

Evaluate expectation of log-ratio under $P$:

$$\ln\left(\frac{p(x)}{q(x)}\right) = \ln\left(\frac{\sigma_1}{\sigma_0}\right) - \frac{(x-\mu_0)^2}{2\sigma_0^2} + \frac{(x-\mu_1)^2}{2\sigma_1^2}$$ $$\mathbb{E}_P[(x-\mu_0)^2] = \sigma_0^2 \implies -\frac{1}{2\sigma_0^2}\sigma_0^2 = -\frac{1}{2}$$ $$\mathbb{E}_P[(x-\mu_1)^2] = \mathbb{E}_P[((x-\mu_0) + (\mu_0-\mu_1))^2] = \sigma_0^2 + (\mu_0-\mu_1)^2$$ Combining all terms yields: $$D_{\text{KL}}(P \parallel Q) = \ln\left(\frac{\sigma_1}{\sigma_0}\right) + \frac{\sigma_0^2 + (\mu_0 - \mu_1)^2}{2\sigma_1^2} - \frac{1}{2} \quad \blacksquare$$
Intermediate (VAE Latent Loss)

Exercise 3: The Canonical VAE Latent Loss Formula

Show that setting $\mu_1 = 0, \sigma_1 = 1$ in the Gaussian KL yields the standard VAE loss:

$$D_{\text{KL}}(\mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2) \parallel \mathcal{N}(0, 1)) = -\frac{1}{2} \left( 1 + \ln(\sigma^2) - \mu^2 - \sigma^2 \right)$$
Click to reveal Step-by-Step Solution

Solution:

$$D_{\text{KL}} = \ln(1/\sigma) + \frac{\sigma^2 + \mu^2}{2} - \frac{1}{2} = -\ln(\sigma) + \frac{1}{2}(\sigma^2 + \mu^2 - 1)$$ $$= -\frac{1}{2}\Big[ 2\ln(\sigma) - \sigma^2 - \mu^2 + 1 \Big] = -\frac{1}{2}\Big[ 1 + \ln(\sigma^2) - \mu^2 - \sigma^2 \Big] \quad \blacksquare$$
