Topic 26.5: Actor-Critic Architectures & PPO

From bootstrapped 1-step Actor-Critic to Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).

12-Year-Old Intuition: The Broadway Audition

Imagine an actor rehearsing on a Broadway stage with an experienced theatre director sitting in the front row:

  • The Actor ($\pi_\theta$): Rehearses lines, gestures, and steps.
  • The Critic ($V_\phi$): Evaluates each line instantly: "That dramatic pause was great!" or "That voice projection was too weak."

Instead of waiting for ticket sales at the end of a 3-hour show, the Actor receives immediate guidance line by line.

PPO's Safety Belt: If the actor hears one compliment, they might be tempted to do 5 backflips through the window! PPO puts a clipping clamp on the update: "You may improve your delivery, but by no more than 20% in a single rehearsal."

1. Mathematical Symbol Decoder

Symbol Name Domain Intuitive & Algorithmic Meaning
$\pi_\theta(a \mid s)$ Actor $\mathcal{S} \times \mathcal{A} \to [0, 1]$ Action policy parameterized by weights $\theta$.
$V_\phi(s)$ Critic $\mathcal{S} \to \mathbb{R}$ Expected state value parameterized by weights $\phi$.
$\delta_t^V$ TD Residual $\mathbb{R}$ 1-step Bellman error: $R_{t+1} + \gamma V_\phi(S_{t+1}) - V_\phi(S_t)$.
$\lambda$ GAE Mixing Factor $[0, 1]$ Exponential decay trading off TD bias against MC variance.
$\hat{A}_t^{\text{GAE}}$ Generalized Advantage $\mathbb{R}$ Sum of exponentially discounted TD errors: $\sum_{l=0}^\infty (\gamma \lambda)^l \delta_{t+l}^V$.
$r_t(\theta)$ Probability Ratio $\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ Importance weight: $\frac{\pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t)}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(a_t \mid s_t)}$.
$\epsilon$ Clipping Range $\approx 0.2$ Restricts surrogate ratio within $[1 - \epsilon, 1 + \epsilon]$.
$\mathcal{L}^{\text{CLIP}}$ PPO Objective $\mathbb{R}$ Pessimistic lower-bound objective preventing policy collapse.

2. Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE)

Mathematical Formulation

GAE($\gamma, \lambda$) forms an exponentially weighted average over all $k$-step advantage estimates:

$$\hat{A}_t^{\text{GAE}(\gamma, \lambda)} \triangleq \sum_{l=0}^\infty (\gamma \lambda)^l \delta_{t+l}^V$$

Boundary Behaviors:

  • $\lambda = 0$: $\hat{A}_t = \delta_t^V$ (1-step TD; minimal variance, maximum bias).
  • $\lambda = 1$: $\hat{A}_t = G_t - V(S_t)$ (Monte Carlo; zero bias, maximal variance).
  • $\lambda \in [0.90, 0.98]$: Optimal empirical bias-variance trade-off.

Linear-Time Backward Recursion: $\hat{A}_t^{\text{GAE}} = \delta_t^V + (\gamma \lambda) \hat{A}_{t+1}^{\text{GAE}}$.

3. Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO, Schulman et al. 2017)

The Clipped Surrogate Objective

Let $r_t(\theta) \triangleq \frac{\pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t)}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(a_t \mid s_t)}$. The PPO loss is:

$$\mathcal{L}^{\text{CLIP}}(\theta) = \hat{\mathbb{E}}_t \left[ \min\left( r_t(\theta) \hat{A}_t, \, \text{clip}(r_t(\theta), 1 - \epsilon, 1 + \epsilon) \hat{A}_t \right) \right]$$

Why the $\min$ operator works:

  • When $\hat{A}_t > 0$: We reward increasing action probability, but clip at $(1 + \epsilon) \hat{A}_t$. If $r_t > 1 + \epsilon$, the gradient vanishes, preventing excessive parameter shifts.
  • When $\hat{A}_t < 0$: We penalize poor actions, but clip at $(1 - \epsilon) \hat{A}_t$. If $r_t < 1 - \epsilon$, the gradient vanishes, preventing catastrophic policy collapse.

This bound enables multiple epochs of mini-batch stochastic gradient descent on the same rollout data.
