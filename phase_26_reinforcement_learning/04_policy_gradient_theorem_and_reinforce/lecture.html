Topic 26.4: Policy Gradient Theorem & REINFORCE

Direct policy parameterization, the log-derivative trick, causality and reward-to-go, and variance reduction via baseline subtraction.

12-Year-Old Intuition: Shooting Free Throws

Imagine learning how to shoot free throws in basketball.

In value-based learning (like Q-learning), before taking a shot you would pause, consult a 500-page notebook, calculate the exact expected points of 50 different elbow angles, and choose the maximum. In real-time motion, that is impractical.

In policy-based learning, your motor cortex shoots naturally. If the ball swishes cleanly through the hoop (+10), your brain boosts the neural firing pattern: "Whatever muscle twitch I just executed, do more of that!" If you miss completely (-5), your brain reduces the probability of that twitch.

That is the Policy Gradient Theorem: increase the probability of actions that outperform expectation, decrease the probability of actions that underperform.

1. Mathematical Symbol Decoder

Symbol Name Formal Type Computational Meaning
$\theta$ Policy Parameters $\mathbb{R}^D$ Weight vector parameterizing the policy $\pi_\theta(a \mid s)$.
$J(\theta)$ Expected Return Objective $\mathbb{R}$ Expected cumulative discounted return: $\mathbb{E}_{\tau \sim \pi_\theta}[R(\tau)]$.
$\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a \mid s)$ Score Function $\mathbb{R}^D$ Vector pointing in the direction of steepest increase in action $a$'s probability.
$G_t$ Reward-to-Go $\mathbb{R}$ Future discounted reward sum from step $t$ onward: $\sum_{k=t}^{T-1} \gamma^{k-t} R_{k+1}$.
$b(s)$ Baseline Function $\mathbb{R}$ Action-independent baseline (e.g. $V(s)$) that reduces variance with zero bias.
$A^\pi(s, a)$ Advantage Function $\mathbb{R}$ $Q^\pi(s, a) - V^\pi(s)$: how much better action $a$ is compared to the average policy action.

2. Derivation of the Policy Gradient Theorem

Step-by-Step Derivation

1. Objective: $J(\theta) = \int P(\tau; \theta) R(\tau) d\tau$. Differentiating:

$$\nabla_\theta J(\theta) = \int \nabla_\theta P(\tau; \theta) R(\tau) d\tau$$

2. Using the Log-Derivative identity $\nabla_\theta P(\tau) = P(\tau) \nabla_\theta \log P(\tau)$:

$$\nabla_\theta J(\theta) = \int P(\tau; \theta) \nabla_\theta \log P(\tau; \theta) R(\tau) d\tau = \mathbb{E}_{\tau \sim \pi_\theta} \left[ \nabla_\theta \log P(\tau; \theta) R(\tau) \right]$$

3. Expanding trajectory log-probability: $\log P(\tau; \theta) = \log \rho_0(s_0) + \sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \log \pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t) + \sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \log \mathcal{P}(s_{t+1} \mid s_t, a_t)$.

4. Since the environmental dynamics $\mathcal{P}$ and initial state distribution $\rho_0$ do not depend on $\theta$, their gradients are zero! Hence:

$$\nabla_\theta \log P(\tau; \theta) = \sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t)$$

5. Invoking causality (past actions cannot affect future rewards) yields the Reward-to-Go Policy Gradient:

$$\nabla_\theta J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{\tau \sim \pi_\theta} \left[ \sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t) G_t \right]$$

3. The Baseline Subtraction Theorem

Proof: Zero Bias from State Baseline

Let $b(s)$ be any baseline function independent of action $a$. We prove $\mathbb{E}_{a \sim \pi_\theta}[\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a \mid s) b(s)] = \mathbf{0}$:

$$\sum_{a \in \mathcal{A}} \pi_\theta(a \mid s) \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a \mid s) b(s) = b(s) \sum_{a \in \mathcal{A}} \pi_\theta(a \mid s) \frac{\nabla_\theta \pi_\theta(a \mid s)}{\pi_\theta(a \mid s)}$$ $$= b(s) \sum_{a \in \mathcal{A}} \nabla_\theta \pi_\theta(a \mid s) = b(s) \nabla_\theta \left( \sum_{a \in \mathcal{A}} \pi_\theta(a \mid s) \right) = b(s) \nabla_\theta (1) = \mathbf{0} \quad \blacksquare$$

Subtracting $b(s_t) = V(s_t)$ leaves the gradient strictly unbiased while dramatically lowering sample variance:

$$\nabla_\theta J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{\tau \sim \pi_\theta} \left[ \sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t) (G_t - V(s_t)) \right]$$
