Topic 23.2: Sentence Embeddings & Sentence-BERT (SBERT)

The Anisotropy Problem of BERT, Siamese Networks with Shared Weights, Mean-Pooling, NLI Triad Objectives, and the Geometry of the Unit Hypersphere.

The 12-Year-Old Analogy: Summarizing a Whole Book into GPS Coordinates

Imagine a school book competition:

  • The Confused Robot (Raw BERT): Asked to compare "Treasure Island" (pirates and cannons) and "The Great Baking Show" (flour and cupcakes), raw BERT says: "Both books are written in English, both use periods, and both have paragraphs! They are 95% identical!" Because raw BERT fixates on frequent syntax words, everything gets squished into a tiny corner of space called the narrow cone.
  • The Expert Librarian (Sentence-BERT): SBERT ignores the paper and ink, distilling the essence into precise plot coordinates: Pirate adventure → (Ocean: 0.95, Kitchen: 0.01), Baking → (Ocean: 0.02, Kitchen: 0.98). Their cosine similarity drops to 0.04! SBERT maps books across the entire globe so true matches land side by side and unrelated topics land on opposite poles.

2. The Anisotropy Problem of Raw BERT

In an isotropic space, representations are uniformly distributed in all directions from the origin with zero expected cosine similarity across unrelated sentences: $\mathbb{E}[\cos(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v})] \approx 0.0$.

However, raw pre-trained BERT token representations and naive averages collapse into a narrow, elongated cone where:

$$\mathbf{u} = \boldsymbol{\mu} + \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_u, \quad \mathbf{v} = \boldsymbol{\mu} + \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_v \quad (\|\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\|_2 \ll \|\boldsymbol{\mu}\|_2)$$ $$\mathbb{E}[\cos(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v})] \approx \frac{\|\boldsymbol{\mu}\|_2^2}{\|\boldsymbol{\mu}\|_2^2} \approx 1.0$$

As a result, raw BERT routinely produces cosine similarities greater than $0.90$ for completely unrelated sentences!

3. The Sentence-BERT (SBERT) Solution

The Cross-Encoder Bottleneck: Computing all pairwise comparisons for $N = 10,000$ sentences using BERT cross-attention requires $\approx 50,000,000$ passes (~65 GPU hours). SBERT enables bi-encoder sentence embeddings that can be compared in 5 seconds via vector search.

    Sentence A ──> [ Shared BERT ] ──> [ Mean Pooling ] ──> u ──┐
                                                                ├──> [ u, v, |u - v| ] ──> Softmax Cross-Entropy
    Sentence B ──> [ Shared BERT ] ──> [ Mean Pooling ] ──> v ──┘

SBERT achieves state-of-the-art sentence representation quality by:

  • Applying Mean-Pooling over the attention mask: $\mathbf{u} = \frac{1}{\sum_t m_t} \sum_t m_t \mathbf{h}_t$.
  • Training with tied Siamese weights on NLI datasets (Entailment, Neutral, Contradiction).
  • Passing the concatenated feature vector $[\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}, |\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{v}|]$ to the classification layer, forcing coordinates to separate by semantic disagreement.

4. Duality Between Cosine Similarity & Euclidean Distance

When sentence vectors are normalized onto the unit hypersphere $\mathbb{S}^{d-1}$, their squared Euclidean distance and cosine similarity are directly related:

$$\|\hat{\mathbf{u}} - \hat{\mathbf{v}}\|_2^2 = \|\hat{\mathbf{u}}\|_2^2 + \|\hat{\mathbf{v}}\|_2^2 - 2 \hat{\mathbf{u}}^\top \hat{\mathbf{v}} = 2 - 2 \cos(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v})$$

Thus, maximizing cosine similarity is strictly equivalent to minimizing Euclidean distance on the unit sphere.
