Phase 23

Embeddings & Metric Learning

From Discrete Symbols to Continuous Metric Hyperspheres & Multimodal Alignment

Architectural Paradigm

Early NLP and machine learning treated entities as discrete, orthogonal symbols with zero semantic overlap. Phase 23 establishes the foundations of continuous representation learning: Word2Vec (CBOW vs Skip-Gram) and Negative Sampling (SGNS), FastText subword character $n$-grams, resolving the BERT Anisotropy Cone with Sentence-BERT (SBERT) and Whitening, Metric Learning principles with Contrastive and Triplet losses, and InfoNCE Mutual Information optimization in OpenAI's Multimodal CLIP.

01. Word Embeddings — From Word2Vec to FastText

The Distributional Hypothesis (Firth 1957). Continuous Bag-of-Words (CBOW) vs Continuous Skip-Gram. The Negative Sampling Objective (SGNS) avoiding full softmax partition functions. Vector arithmetic ($\mathbf{v}_{\text{King}} - \mathbf{v}_{\text{Man}} + \mathbf{v}_{\text{Woman}} \approx \mathbf{v}_{\text{Queen}}$), Levy & Goldberg's shifted PMI theorem, and FastText subword character $n$-grams for out-of-vocabulary and typo resilience.

02. Sentence Embeddings & Sentence-BERT (SBERT)

The Cross-Encoder computational bottleneck ($\mathcal{O}(N^2)$). The Anisotropy Problem of BERT: why naive average pooling collapses into a narrow cone with cosine similarity $> 0.90$ for unrelated sentences. Sentence-BERT (SBERT) Siamese networks with tied weights, Mean-Pooling, NLI feature concatenation ($[\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}, |\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{v}|]$), cosine similarity regression, and unit hypersphere normalization $\mathbb{S}^{d-1}$.

03. Metric Learning & Contrastive Losses

Principles of deep metric learning: learning isometric embedding spaces where semantic distance equals Euclidean distance. Contrastive Loss (Hadsell et al. 2006) with Hooke's attractive springs and electrostatic repulsion margins. Triplet Loss (FaceNet) relative rank optimization, and online negative mining regimes (Easy, Hard, and Semi-Hard negatives).

04. InfoNCE Loss & Multimodal CLIP

The InfoNCE loss (van den Oord et al. 2018). Step-by-step mathematical proof that InfoNCE maximizes a lower bound on Mutual Information: $I(X; Y) \ge \log(K) - \mathcal{L}_{\text{InfoNCE}}$. Temperature $\tau$ as an automated hard-negative selector. OpenAI's CLIP architecture with dual vision and text encoders, $N \times N$ symmetric cross-entropy loss, and zero-shot transfer via text prompt prototypes.
