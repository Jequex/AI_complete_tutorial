Topic 23.1: Word Embeddings — From Word2Vec to FastText

The Distributional Hypothesis, Continuous Bag-of-Words (CBOW), Continuous Skip-Gram with Negative Sampling (SGNS), Vector Arithmetic, and Subword n-Grams.

The 12-Year-Old Analogy: The Map of Word Neighborhoods & Lego Subwords

Imagine every word in English wants to build a house in a massive 3D fantasy world:

  • Firth's Rule of Good Neighbors: "You shall know a word by the company it keeps." Words that hang out in the same places build their houses on the same block. Because cat and dog both hang around with leash, vet, and fur, they live right next door on Pet Boulevard. Meanwhile, apple hangs around with pie and orchard, building miles away in Fruit Valley.
  • The City Compass (Vector Math): Walking from Man to King moves along "Royalty Avenue". If you start at Woman and walk that exact same direction, you arrive right at Queen! That's $\mathbf{v}_{\text{King}} - \mathbf{v}_{\text{Man}} + \mathbf{v}_{\text{Woman}} \approx \mathbf{v}_{\text{Queen}}$.
  • FastText and Lego Bricks: Word2Vec carves each house from a single stone. If an unknown word visits (like a typo computerr), Word2Vec panics and labels it <UNK>. FastText builds houses out of character Lego bricks (<co, omp, mpu, uter). Even if the whole word is new, its bricks let us instantly place it next to computer!

2. Word2Vec: CBOW vs. Continuous Skip-Gram

The Distributional Hypothesis (Firth 1957): Words that appear in similar linguistic contexts have similar semantic meanings.

     Continuous Bag-of-Words (CBOW)                  Continuous Skip-Gram
       w(t-2) ──┐                                            ┌──> w(t-2)
       w(t-1) ──┼──> [ SUM / AVG ] ──> w(t)         w(t) ────┼──> w(t-1)
       w(t+1) ──┤                                            ├──> w(t+1)
       w(t+2) ──┘                                            └──> w(t+2)
    Predicts center word given context             Predicts context words given center
    Fast, smooths over frequent words              Superb for infrequent words & nuances

3. The Softmax Bottleneck & Negative Sampling (SGNS)

Under categorical cross-entropy, the conditional probability of context word $w_O$ given center word $w_I$ is:

$$P(w_O \mid w_I) = \frac{\exp(\mathbf{v}_{w_O}'^\top \mathbf{v}_{w_I})}{\sum_{w \in \mathcal{V}} \exp(\mathbf{v}_{w}'^\top \mathbf{v}_{w_I})}$$

Evaluating the denominator sum over $V = 10^6$ words at each training step requires $3 \times 10^8$ operations per token, which is completely intractable.

The Skip-Gram Negative Sampling (SGNS) Objective

Negative sampling formulates independent binary classifications: distinguishing true data pairs from noise pairs drawn from $P_n(w)$:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{SGNS}} = \log \sigma(\mathbf{v}_{w_O}'^\top \mathbf{v}_{w_I}) + \sum_{i=1}^k \mathbb{E}_{w_i \sim P_n(w)} \left[ \log \sigma(-\mathbf{v}_{w_i}'^\top \mathbf{v}_{w_I}) \right]$$

Analytical Gradients

Using the identity $\frac{d}{dz} \log \sigma(z) = 1 - \sigma(z)$ and $\frac{d}{dz} \log \sigma(-z) = -\sigma(z)$:

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{v}_{w_O}'} = (1 - \sigma(\mathbf{v}_{w_O}'^\top \mathbf{v}_{w_I})) \mathbf{v}_{w_I}$$ $$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{v}_{w_i}'} = -\sigma(\mathbf{v}_{w_i}'^\top \mathbf{v}_{w_I}) \mathbf{v}_{w_I}$$ $$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{v}_{w_I}} = (1 - \sigma(\mathbf{v}_{w_O}'^\top \mathbf{v}_{w_I})) \mathbf{v}_{w_O}' - \sum_{i=1}^k \sigma(\mathbf{v}_{w_i}'^\top \mathbf{v}_{w_I}) \mathbf{v}_{w_i}'$$

Negative sampling reduces complexity from $\mathcal{O}(V \cdot d)$ down to $\mathcal{O}((k + 1) \cdot d)$, delivering orders-of-magnitude computational acceleration.

4. FastText: Character n-Grams & OOV Generalization

FastText decomposes each word into character $n$-grams bounded by < and >. For example, with $n = 3$ for word where:

$$\mathcal{G}_{\text{where}} = \{ \text{<wh}, \text{whe}, \text{her}, \text{ere}, \text{re>}, \text{<where>} \}$$

The vector representation is the sum of subword vectors:

$$\mathbf{u}_w = \sum_{g \in \mathcal{G}_w} \mathbf{z}_g$$

This allows FastText to naturally represent unseen words and handle typos without ever collapsing into <UNK>.
