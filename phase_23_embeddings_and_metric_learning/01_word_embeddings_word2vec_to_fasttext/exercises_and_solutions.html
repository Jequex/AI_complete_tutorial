Topic 23.1: Exercises & Solutions

Analytical and numerical exercises on Word2Vec, SGNS gradients, FastText subwords, and the Shifted PMI Theorem.

Problem 1: Softmax vs. Negative Sampling Computational Complexity (Easy)

Given vocabulary size $|\mathcal{V}| = 500,000$ and embedding dimension $d = 300$, compare the computational burden of the full softmax against Skip-Gram with Negative Sampling ($k = 5$).

View Complete Solution

Full Softmax: Requires dot product over all $V$ context vectors: $V \cdot d = 500,000 \times 300 = 150,000,000$ operations for denominator evaluation, plus another $150,000,000$ for probability-weighted gradient accumulation. Total $\approx 3 \times 10^8$ FLOPs per word.

SGNS with $k = 5$: Evaluates $1$ positive dot product and $5$ negative dot products, plus $6$ sigmoid evaluations and gradient updates: $\approx 3 \times (k + 1) \times d = 3 \times 6 \times 300 = 5,400$ FLOPs.

Speedup: $\frac{300,000,000}{5,400} \approx \mathbf{55,555\times}$ acceleration per gradient step.

Problem 2: Step-by-Step Gradient Descent Update under SGNS (Medium)

Walk through an exact numerical parameter update for target word cat ($\mathbf{v} = [0.5, -0.2]^\top$), positive context purr ($\mathbf{v}' = [0.8, 0.1]^\top$), and negative sample tractor ($\mathbf{v}' = [-0.4, 0.6]^\top$) with $\eta = 0.1$.

View Complete Solution

Positive Logit: $z_O = 0.8(0.5) + 0.1(-0.2) = 0.38 \implies \sigma(z_O) \approx 0.5933$.

Negative Logit: $z_1 = -0.4(0.5) + 0.6(-0.2) = -0.32 \implies \sigma(z_1) \approx 0.4207$.

Loss: $\mathcal{J} = -\log(0.5933) - \log(1 - 0.4207) \approx 0.5220 + 0.5459 = 1.0679$.

Gradient with respect to center vector:

$$\nabla_{\mathbf{v}_{\text{cat}}} \mathcal{J} = -(1 - \sigma(z_O))\mathbf{v}_{\text{purr}}' + \sigma(z_1)\mathbf{v}_{\text{tractor}}' = \begin{bmatrix} -0.49364 \\ 0.21175 \end{bmatrix}$$

Updated Vector: $\mathbf{v}_{\text{cat}}^{(\text{new})} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.5 \\ -0.2 \end{bmatrix} - 0.1 \begin{bmatrix} -0.49364 \\ 0.21175 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.54936 \\ -0.22118 \end{bmatrix}$.

Problem 3: FastText Subword Decomposition & Typo Handling (Medium)

Extract 3-grams and 4-grams for learning and typo leraing. Demonstrate how shared subwords preserve semantic retrieval where Word2Vec returns <UNK>.

View Complete Solution

Bounded string: <learning> yields 3-grams: <le, lea, ear, arn, rni, nin, ing, ng>.

Misspelling <leraing> shares prefix <le and suffixes ing, ng>. Because FastText constructs representations via $\mathbf{u}_w = \sum_{g \in \mathcal{G}_w} \mathbf{z}_g$, the learned subword vectors project the misspelled token into the same neighborhood as learning.

Problem 4: Levy & Goldberg's Implicit Shifted PMI Theorem (Hard)

Prove that Skip-Gram with Negative Sampling implicitly factorizes the Pointwise Mutual Information matrix shifted by $\log k$:

$$\mathbf{v}_w^\top \mathbf{v}_c' = \text{PMI}(w, c) - \log k$$
View Complete Solution

Setting the derivative of the local pair objective $\ell(x) = N(w, c) \log \sigma(x) + k \frac{N(w) N(c)}{D} \log \sigma(-x)$ with respect to scalar $x = \mathbf{v}_w^\top \mathbf{v}_c'$ to zero:

$$N(w, c)(1 - \sigma(x)) = k \frac{N(w) N(c)}{D} \sigma(x) \implies e^{-x} = \frac{k N(w) N(c)}{N(w, c) D}$$

Taking logarithms yields:

$$x^* = \log \frac{N(w, c) D}{N(w) N(c)} - \log k = \text{PMI}(w, c) - \log k$$
