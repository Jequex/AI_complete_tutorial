Topic 23.4: InfoNCE Loss & Multimodal CLIP

InfoNCE Formulation, Mutual Information Lower Bounds, Temperature Tuning, Dual Encoders, Symmetric Cross-Entropy, and Zero-Shot Classification.

The 12-Year-Old Analogy: The Universal Bridge Between Pictures and Words

Imagine a city split by a giant river with the Tower of Pictures on one bank and the Tower of Words on the other:

  • The Language Barrier: In early AI, picture computers and text computers lived in totally separate worlds. If you asked a vision model to recognize a brand new animal (like an axolotl), it failed completely because that label was never in its fixed 1,000-class list!
  • The Suspension Bridge (CLIP): OpenAI's CLIP built a mathematical bridge where pictures and sentences share the exact same GPS coordinates. Every picture of a puppy is tied with an invisible elastic rope to the sentence "a photo of a happy puppy".
  • Zero-Shot Magic: When an alien shows a photo of an axolotl, you write 3 index cards: "a photo of an axolotl", "a photo of a jet", "a photo of a pizza". You drop the photo onto the bridge, and it glides straight to the axolotl card without any training!

2. InfoNCE Loss & Mutual Information Lower Bound

InfoNCE generalizes contrastive learning to $K$ simultaneous negative candidates:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{InfoNCE}} = -\log \frac{\exp(\text{sim}(q, k_+) / \tau)}{\sum_{j=0}^K \exp(\text{sim}(q, k_j) / \tau)}$$

Mutual Information Lower Bound Theorem: Minimizing InfoNCE directly maximizes a lower bound on the mutual information between representations:

$$I(X; Y) \ge \log(K) - \mathcal{L}_{\text{InfoNCE}}$$

Increasing the number of negative samples $K$ tightens this bound logarithmically.

3. The Temperature Parameter $\tau$: Hard-Negative Selector

The gradient of InfoNCE with respect to negative score $s_j$ is proportional to its softmax probability $p_j = \frac{e^{s_j/\tau}}{\sum_m e^{s_m/\tau}}$:

  • Small $\tau$ ($\tau \to 0$): Concentrates 100% of gradient weight onto the single hardest negative (implicit hard-negative mining).
  • Large $\tau$ ($\tau \to \infty$): Flattens into a uniform distribution $\frac{1}{K+1}$, treating all negatives equally and losing discriminative edge.
  • Optimal $\tau$ ($\approx 0.07$): Balances sharp discrimination with optimization smoothness (learned dynamically in CLIP via $\exp(\tau_{\text{logit}})$).

4. The CLIP Architecture & Symmetric Pre-Training

    Image Batch (N) ──> [ Image Enc ] ──> I_hat_i ──┐
                                                    ├──> Cosine Matrix S_ij ──> Symmetric Loss
    Text Batch (N)  ──> [ Text Enc  ] ──> T_hat_j ──┘

Total objective combines Image-to-Text (rows) and Text-to-Image (columns) cross-entropy:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{CLIP}} = \frac{1}{2} (\mathcal{L}_{\text{i2t}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{t2i}})$$

5. Zero-Shot Classification via Text Prompts

To classify an image across arbitrary classes $\{y_1, \dots, y_C\}$:

  1. Encode text prompt templates: $\mathbf{w}_c = \frac{g(\text{"a photo of a "} + y_c)}{\|g(\dots)\|}$.
  2. Encode test image: $\hat{\mathbf{I}} = \frac{f(I)}{\|f(I)\|}$.
  3. Predict highest cosine similarity: $\hat{y} = \arg\max_c (\hat{\mathbf{I}}^\top \mathbf{w}_c)$.
