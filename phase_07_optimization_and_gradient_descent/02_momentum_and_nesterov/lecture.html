Topic 7.2: Momentum & Nesterov Accelerated Gradient

Physics-inspired optimization: escaping ill-conditioned ravines, velocity accumulation, and Nesterov lookahead braking.

1. Executive Summary & The 12-Year-Old Analogy

Standard Gradient Descent behaves like a mass-less speck of dust in thick syrup: the moment the slope levels off or changes direction, it stops or jerks perpendicularly. In ravines—narrow canyons where walls are thousands of times steeper than the gentle floor—GD wastes all its energy oscillating between the walls. Momentum endows optimization with physical inertia.

🛷 The 12-Year-Old Analogy: The Bobsled on an Icy Half-Pipe

  • Standard SGD (The Feather): Blown violently left-and-right across the steep walls. 99% of its energy is spent bouncing sideways, barely moving forward along the floor.
  • Polyak Heavy-Ball (The Heavy Iron Sled): An 800-pound iron sled. The leftward bounce is cancelled out by the rightward bounce on the next step. But down the floor, the gentle push acts in the same forward direction every time, rocketing the sled forward!
  • Nesterov Accelerated Gradient (The Sled Pilot with Binoculars): The sled is so fast it risks crashing when the canyon takes a sharp turn. The pilot looks ahead with binoculars at where the sled will land next, measures the slope there, and taps the brakes before overshooting.

3. The Ravine Problem & Condition Number

Consider a canyon modeled by the quadratic objective: $$f(x, y) = \frac{1}{2} L x^2 + \frac{1}{2} \mu y^2, \quad \text{with } L \gg \mu > 0$$ The condition number is $\kappa = \frac{L}{\mu}$.

Standard Gradient Descent requires step size $\eta < \frac{2}{L}$ to prevent divergence along $x$. Under optimal step size $\eta = \frac{2}{L + \mu}$, the rate of convergence along the slow floor direction $y$ is: $$1 - \eta \mu = \frac{\kappa - 1}{\kappa + 1}$$ When $\kappa = 10,000$, the convergence requires $\mathcal{O}(\kappa \ln(1/\epsilon))$ steps, rendering optimization painfully slow.

4. Polyak Heavy-Ball Momentum Formulation

Polyak (1964) introduced the physical equation of a damped oscillator: $$v_{t+1} = \beta v_t - \eta \nabla f(\theta_t)$$ $$\theta_{t+1} = \theta_t + v_{t+1}$$

In a consistent direction with constant gradient $g$, the steady-state velocity is: $$v_\infty = -\frac{\eta}{1 - \beta} g$$ For $\beta = 0.9$, the effective step size is amplified by a factor of $\frac{1}{1 - 0.9} = 10\times$! In an alternating direction, consecutive terms cancel, suppressing transverse oscillations.

Optimal momentum tuning $\beta^* = \left(\frac{\sqrt{\kappa} - 1}{\sqrt{\kappa} + 1}\right)^2$ improves convergence from $\mathcal{O}(\kappa)$ to $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\kappa})$, delivering a 100-fold theoretical speedup when $\kappa = 10,000$.

5. Nesterov Accelerated Gradient (NAG)

Polyak momentum often overshoots the minimum due to excess kinetic energy. Nesterov (1983) evaluates the gradient at the lookahead position $\theta_t + \beta v_t$: $$\theta_{\text{lookahead}} = \theta_t + \beta v_t$$ $$v_{t+1} = \beta v_t - \eta \nabla f(\theta_{\text{lookahead}})$$ $$\theta_{t+1} = \theta_t + v_{t+1}$$

Adaptive Braking Mechanism: When velocity carries the parameter toward an ascending slope, $\nabla f(\theta_{\text{lookahead}})$ points backward before the parameter actually reaches that position, directly subtracting from velocity and preventing catastrophic overshooting.
