Problem 1: Steady-State Velocity & Effective Learning Rate

Easy

Consider Polyak Heavy-Ball momentum: $v_{t+1} = \beta v_t - \eta g_t$.

  1. If $g_t = g$ is constant and $v_0 = 0$, derive $v_t$ as a geometric sum.
  2. Evaluate $v_\infty = \lim_{t\to\infty} v_t$. What is the effective learning rate scaling factor?
  3. If $\eta = 0.001$ and $\beta = 0.99$, find the effective step size.
  4. Analyze alternating gradients $g_t = (-1)^t g$ and explain oscillation damping.
Click to view step-by-step solution

1. Geometric Sum:

$$v_t = -\eta g \sum_{k=0}^{t-1} \beta^k = -\eta g \frac{1 - \beta^t}{1 - \beta}$$

2. Limit & Scaling Factor:

$$v_\infty = -\frac{\eta}{1 - \beta} g \implies \eta_{\text{eff}} = \frac{\eta}{1 - \beta}$$

3. Numerical Calculation:

$$\eta_{\text{eff}} = \frac{0.001}{1 - 0.99} = \frac{0.001}{0.01} = 0.1 \quad (100\times \text{ amplification!})$$

4. Oscillation Damping:

$$|v_\infty| = \frac{\eta |g|}{1 + \beta} \approx 0.53 \eta |g| \quad (\text{for } \beta=0.9)$$ Oscillations are halved while steady forward movement is boosted $10\times$.

Problem 2: 2D Ravine Simulation by Hand

Medium

Let $f(x, y) = 50x^2 + \frac{1}{2}y^2$. Starting at $(x_0, y_0) = (1.0, 10.0)$ with $\eta = 0.01, \beta = 0.8, v_0 = (0, 0)$.

  1. Compute step 1 $(x_1, y_1)$ for GD, Polyak, and Nesterov (NAG).
  2. Compute step 2 $(x_2, y_2)$ for each method.
  3. Compare overshoot and damping along the steep $x$-axis.
Click to view step-by-step solution

Step 1: $\nabla f(1.0, 10.0) = [100.0, 10.0]^T$. All three methods reach $(0.0, 9.9)$ with $v_1 = [-1.0, -0.1]^T$.

Step 2: $\nabla f(0.0, 9.9) = [0.0, 9.9]^T$.

  • Standard GD: $(x_2, y_2) = (0.0, 9.801)$.
  • Polyak Momentum: $v_2 = 0.8[-1.0, -0.1]^T - 0.01[0.0, 9.9]^T = [-0.8, -0.179]^T$. Position becomes $(x_2, y_2) = (-0.8, 9.721)$. Notice the overshoot to $x_2 = -0.8$!
  • Nesterov (NAG): $\theta_{\text{lookahead}} = (0.0, 9.9) + 0.8(-1.0, -0.1) = (-0.8, 9.82)$. Lookahead gradient is $\nabla f(-0.8, 9.82) = [-80.0, 9.82]^T$. Then $v_2 = 0.8[-1.0, -0.1]^T - 0.01[-80.0, 9.82]^T = [0.0, -0.1782]^T$. Position becomes $(x_2, y_2) = (0.0, 9.7218)$.

Conclusion: NAG senses the upcoming hill at $x=-0.8$ and zeroes out $x$-velocity completely, avoiding overshoot!

Problem 3: Spectral Radius & Optimal Damping

Hard

On $f(\theta) = \frac{1}{2} a \theta^2$, express the Heavy-Ball update in state-space form $\begin{bmatrix} \theta_{t+1} \\ \theta_t \end{bmatrix} = T \begin{bmatrix} \theta_t \\ \theta_{t-1} \end{bmatrix}$. Prove that complex eigenvalues yield spectral radius $\rho(T) = \sqrt{\beta}$, independent of $a$.

Click to view step-by-step solution

Transition Matrix:

$$T = \begin{bmatrix} 1 + \beta - \eta a & -\beta \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

Characteristic Equation: $\det(T - \lambda I) = \lambda^2 - (1 + \beta - \eta a)\lambda + \beta = 0$.

By Vieta's formulas, $\lambda_1 \lambda_2 = \det(T) = \beta$. When roots are complex conjugates $\lambda_{1,2} = r e^{\pm i\phi}$, we have $|\lambda|^2 = \beta \implies \rho(T) = \sqrt{\beta}$.

Equating spectral radii across the spectrum $[\mu, L]$ gives the optimal rate $\rho^* = \frac{\sqrt{\kappa} - 1}{\sqrt{\kappa} + 1}$, accelerating convergence from $\mathcal{O}(\kappa)$ to $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\kappa})$.

Problem 4: Nesterov Lookahead Equivalence in PyTorch

Medium

Show that PyTorch's $v_{t+1} = \beta v_t + g_t$, $\theta_{t+1} = \theta_t - \eta(g_t + \beta v_{t+1})$ generates trajectories mathematically equivalent to classical Nesterov acceleration.

Click to view step-by-step solution

Substituting $v_{t+1} = \beta v_t + g_t$ into the position update yields $\theta_{t+1} = \theta_t - \eta(1+\beta)g_t - \eta\beta^2 v_t$. Under the variable transformation $\hat{\theta}_t = \theta_t + \beta v_t$, this directly tracks classical Nesterov lookahead points while requiring only standard gradients evaluated at $\theta_t$, saving memory and forward-pass overhead.
