Problem 1: Critical Learning Rate & Divergence Bound

Medium

Consider the 2D anisotropic quadratic bowl: $f(x, y) = 10 x^2 + y^2$.

  1. Compute the gradient $\nabla f(x, y)$ and the Hessian matrix $H = \nabla^2 f(x, y)$.
  2. Determine the Lipschitz smoothness constant $L$ of the gradient.
  3. Write down the coordinate update equations for $(x_{t+1}, y_{t+1})$ under gradient descent with step size $\eta$.
  4. Find the exact range of positive step sizes $\eta > 0$ guaranteeing convergence to $(0,0)$. What occurs if $\eta = 0.1$? What occurs if $\eta = 0.105$?
Click to view step-by-step solution

1. Gradient & Hessian:

$$\nabla f(x, y) = \begin{bmatrix} 20x \\ 2y \end{bmatrix}, \quad H = \begin{bmatrix} 20 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$$

2. Lipschitz Constant:

$$L = \lambda_{\max}(H) = 20$$

3. Coordinate Updates:

$$x_{t+1} = x_t - \eta(20 x_t) = (1 - 20\eta) x_t \implies x_t = (1 - 20\eta)^t x_0$$ $$y_{t+1} = y_t - \eta(2 y_t) = (1 - 2\eta) y_t \implies y_t = (1 - 2\eta)^t y_0$$

4. Convergence Range & Behavior:

For $x_t \to 0$ and $y_t \to 0$, we require $|1 - 20\eta| < 1$ and $|1 - 2\eta| < 1$. The $x$-axis constraint is the most restrictive:

$$-1 < 1 - 20\eta < 1 \iff 0 < 20\eta < 2 \iff 0 < \eta < 0.1$$
  • If $\eta = 0.1$, then $1 - 20(0.1) = -1$, yielding $x_t = (-1)^t x_0$. The iterate oscillates endlessly between $+x_0$ and $-x_0$ without converging.
  • If $\eta = 0.105$, then $1 - 20(0.105) = -1.1$. Since $|-1.1| > 1$, the iterate explodes with exponentially growing oscillations ($|x_t| \to \infty$).

Problem 2: Mini-Batch Gradient Variance Scaling

Easy

Let sample gradients $g_i = \nabla \ell(f(x_i; \theta), y_i)$ be independent and identically distributed with mean $\mathbb{E}[g_i] = \mu$ and covariance $\text{Cov}(g_i) = \Sigma$. The mini-batch estimator is $g_B = \frac{1}{B} \sum_{k=1}^B g_{i_k}$.

  1. Prove that $g_B$ is an unbiased estimator of $\mu$.
  2. Prove that $\text{Cov}(g_B) = \frac{1}{B} \Sigma$.
  3. Compute the variance ratio between single-sample SGD ($B=1$) and mini-batch SGD ($B=256$).
Click to view step-by-step solution

1. Unbiasedness:

$$\mathbb{E}[g_B] = \mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{B}\sum_{k=1}^B g_{i_k}\right] = \frac{1}{B} \sum_{k=1}^B \mathbb{E}[g_{i_k}] = \frac{1}{B}(B\mu) = \mu$$

2. Covariance Scaling:

$$\text{Cov}(g_B) = \text{Cov}\left(\frac{1}{B}\sum_{k=1}^B g_{i_k}\right) = \frac{1}{B^2} \sum_{k=1}^B \text{Cov}(g_{i_k}) = \frac{1}{B^2}(B\Sigma) = \frac{1}{B}\Sigma$$

3. Variance Ratio:

$$\frac{\text{Var}(g_1)}{\text{Var}(g_{256})} = \frac{\Sigma}{\Sigma / 256} = 256$$ The mini-batch estimator reduces gradient variance by a factor of 256.

Problem 3: Cosine Annealing Learning Rate Computation

Easy

A training run executes for $T_{\max} = 100$ epochs with $\eta_{\max} = 10^{-3}$ and $\eta_{\min} = 10^{-5}$ under cosine annealing: $$\eta_t = \eta_{\min} + \frac{1}{2}(\eta_{\max} - \eta_{\min})\left(1 + \cos\left(\frac{t\pi}{T_{\max}}\right)\right)$$

Calculate exact values of $\eta_t$ at $t = 0, 25, 50, 100$.

Click to view step-by-step solution

Note that $\eta_{\max} - \eta_{\min} = 0.001 - 0.00001 = 0.00099$. Half amplitude is $0.000495$.

  • $t = 0$: $\cos(0) = 1 \implies \eta_0 = 0.00001 + 0.000495(2) = 0.00100 = 10^{-3}$
  • $t = 25$: $\cos(\pi/4) = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \approx 0.7071 \implies \eta_{25} = 0.00001 + 0.000495(1.7071) \approx 8.55 \times 10^{-4}$
  • $t = 50$: $\cos(\pi/2) = 0 \implies \eta_{50} = 0.00001 + 0.000495(1) = 5.05 \times 10^{-4}$
  • $t = 100$: $\cos(\pi) = -1 \implies \eta_{100} = 0.00001 + 0.000495(0) = 10^{-5}$

Problem 4: SGD Noise Temperature & Flat Minima

Hard

An objective landscape has two local minima: a sharp minimum $\theta_A$ ($\lambda_{\max} = 100$) and a flat minimum $\theta_B$ ($\lambda_{\max} = 1$). SGD continuous dynamics have effective temperature $T_{\text{eff}} \approx \frac{\eta}{B}$.

  1. Explain the Kramers escape rate $P_{\text{escape}} \propto \exp(-\Delta E / T_{\text{eff}})$ and why noise destabilizes sharp minima.
  2. Analyze what happens when $B=4096, \eta=10^{-4}$ versus $B=32, \eta=10^{-2}$.
Click to view step-by-step solution

1. Kramers Escape Rate:

Thermal fluctuations with variance proportional to $T_{\text{eff}} = \eta / B$ give parameters stochastic momentum. A sharp minimum has steep walls and a narrow basin; large gradient noise easily kicks the state across the barrier. In contrast, flat basins are wide and absorb stochastic noise without exiting.

2. Regime Comparison:

  • Large Batch / Low LR ($B=4096, \eta=10^{-4}$): $T_{\text{eff}} \approx 2.44 \times 10^{-8}$. Near-zero noise means deterministic gradient descent, getting trapped in whichever local minimum is nearest (e.g., sharp minimum $\theta_A$), which generalizes poorly on test sets.
  • Small Batch / Moderate LR ($B=32, \eta=10^{-2}$): $T_{\text{eff}} \approx 3.125 \times 10^{-4}$ (over 12,800 times higher!). The optimizer easily escapes sharp minimum $\theta_A$ and settles inside flat minimum $\theta_B$, providing superior generalization.
