Traditional ML Evaluation & Statistical Testing

Parametric & Non-Parametric Hypothesis Testing, Resampling Permutation Tests, Bootstrap Confidence Intervals, and False Discovery Rate Corrections.

1. The 12-Year-Old Intuition: Running in the Mud

Imagine two athletes, Alice and Bob, running across 10 distinct tracks. Track 1 is a rocky mountain trail covered in thick mud; Track 2 is a smooth Olympic rubber track; Track 3 is an uphill sand dune in heavy wind.

At the end of the day, Alice's average time is 50.2 seconds, while Bob's average time is 50.8 seconds. A casual spectator screams: "Alice is faster by 0.6 seconds! She is clearly superior!"

A statistician steps forward and asks: "Did Alice run on the muddy mountain trail at the exact same moment Bob did? Or was Bob stuck in a rainstorm while Alice ran on dry stadium asphalt?"

When you measure their paired differences on identical tracks, you discover Alice beat Bob on 9 out of 10 tracks by a consistent 0.5s margin. That consistency proves genuine superiority. In AI benchmarking, reporting that Model A scored 84.7% versus Model B's 84.1% without paired statistical testing is meaningless: the 0.6% gap could easily be random data noise.

2. Mathematical Symbol Decoder

Symbol Name Domain Description & Engineering Significance
$H_0$ Null Hypothesis Proposition Baseline claim that no true performance delta exists ($\mu_d = 0$).
$H_1$ Alternative Hypothesis Proposition Research claim that Candidate genuinely differs from Baseline ($\mu_d \ne 0$).
$\alpha$ Significance Level $\in (0, 1)$ Maximum tolerated probability of false alarm (Type I error). Typically $0.05$.
$p$-value Probability Value $\in [0, 1]$ $\mathbb{P}(\text{observing difference as extreme or more extreme} \mid H_0)$.
$d_i$ Paired Difference $\mathbb{R}$ $d_i = X_{A, i} - X_{B, i}$ on evaluation fold/slice $i$.
$t$ Student's $t$ Statistic $\mathbb{R}$ $t = \bar{d} / (s_d / \sqrt{n})$, signal-to-noise ratio of observed performance delta.
$W$ Wilcoxon Test Statistic $\mathbb{N}$ $\min(W^+, W^-)$, non-parametric signed rank sum resistant to extreme outliers.
$\text{CI}_{1-\alpha}$ Confidence Interval $[L, U]$ Bounds estimated to contain true metric parameter with $1-\alpha$ probability.
$\text{FDR}$ False Discovery Rate $\in [0, 1]$ Expected proportion of false positives among rejected hypotheses $\mathbb{E}[V / R]$.

3. Student's Paired $t$-Test

Given $n$ paired evaluation splits (e.g. cross-validation folds), define the difference vector $d_i = X_{A, i} - X_{B, i}$. Under $H_0: \mu_d = 0$:

$$\bar{d} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n d_i, \qquad s_d = \sqrt{\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^n (d_i - \bar{d})^2}$$ $$\boxed{t = \frac{\bar{d}}{s_d / \sqrt{n}} \sim \text{Student-}t(\nu = n - 1)}$$
Engineering Caveat: Standard $k$-fold cross-validation folds violate sample independence because folds overlap in their training sets. Dietterich (1998) showed that standard $k$-fold $t$-tests have an elevated Type I error rate of $\approx 10\%\text{--}15\%$. Use $5 \times 2$-fold cross-validation or exact non-parametric permutation testing for production gating.

4. Non-Parametric Wilcoxon Signed-Rank Test

When metric distributions are non-normal, skewed, or bounded, rank the absolute differences $|d_i|$ from $1$ to $n_r$ (discarding zeros). Sum positive and negative ranks:

$$W^+ = \sum_{d_i > 0} \text{Rank}(|d_i|), \qquad W^- = \sum_{d_i < 0} \text{Rank}(|d_i|)$$ $$\boxed{W = \min(W^+, W^-)}$$

For $n_r \ge 20$, the continuity-corrected asymptotic normal approximation applies:

$$\mu_W = \frac{n_r(n_r + 1)}{4}, \qquad \sigma_W = \sqrt{\frac{n_r(n_r + 1)(2n_r + 1)}{24}}, \qquad z = \frac{W - \mu_W + 0.5}{\sigma_W}$$

5. Resampling Permutation Tests & Bootstrap Confidence Intervals

Permutation Testing: Under $H_0$, the labels $(A, B)$ are arbitrary. We flip the signs of $d_i$ at random $B$ times ($B \ge 10{,}000$):

$$\boxed{p_{\text{perm}} = \frac{1 + \sum_{b=1}^B \mathbb{I}(|T^{(b)}| \ge |T_{\text{obs}}|)}{B + 1}}$$

Bootstrap Confidence Intervals: Sample $N$ items with replacement $B$ times ($B \ge 2{,}000$) to yield bootstrap estimates $\hat{\theta}^{*(1)} \le \dots \le \hat{\theta}^{*(B)}$:

  • Percentile Bootstrap: $\text{CI}_{\text{pct}} = [\hat{\theta}^*_{(\alpha/2)}, \; \hat{\theta}^*_{(1 - \alpha/2)}]$
  • Empirical Basic Bootstrap: $\text{CI}_{\text{basic}} = [2\hat{\theta} - \hat{\theta}^*_{(1 - \alpha/2)}, \; 2\hat{\theta} - \hat{\theta}^*_{(\alpha/2)}]$

6. Multiple Comparisons: Bonferroni vs. Benjamini-Hochberg FDR

When comparing against $m$ baseline models, the probability of at least one false alarm is $\text{FWER} = 1 - (1-\alpha)^m$. For $m = 20$, $\text{FWER} \approx 64.2\%$.

  • Bonferroni (FWER control): Reject if $p_i \le \frac{\alpha}{m}$. Strongly conservative.
  • Benjamini-Hochberg (FDR control): Sort $p_{(1)} \le \dots \le p_{(m)}$. Find largest $k^*$ such that: $$\boxed{p_{(k^*)} \le \frac{k^*}{m} \cdot Q}$$ Reject all $H_{0, (1)}, \dots, H_{0, (k^*)}$. Maximizes statistical power while strictly bounding expected false discovery proportion to $Q$.
