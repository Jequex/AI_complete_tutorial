Exercise 1: Paired $t$-Test and Wilcoxon Signed-Rank Test on 10-Fold CV

A machine learning team compares Candidate Model $A$ against Baseline Model $B$ across 10 identical cross-validation folds. The test accuracies (%) are:

Fold $i$12345678910
Model A85.487.182.088.684.589.281.386.785.087.8
Model B83.286.582.085.183.088.082.584.283.185.4

Tasks:

  1. Compute paired difference vector $d_i = X_{A, i} - X_{B, i}$, sample mean $\bar{d}$, and sample standard error $\text{SE}(\bar{d})$.
  2. Compute the Student's paired $t$-statistic. For $\nu = 9$ degrees of freedom at $\alpha = 0.05$ ($t_{\text{crit}} = 2.262$), do you reject $H_0$?
  3. Drop the zero difference, rank absolute differences, compute $W^+$ and $W^-$, and evaluate the Wilcoxon signed-rank test at $W_{\text{crit}} = 5$.
View Complete Solution

1. Sample Statistics:

Differences $d = [+2.2, +0.6, 0.0, +3.5, +1.5, +1.2, -1.2, +2.5, +1.9, +2.4]$.

$$\bar{d} = \frac{14.6}{10} = \mathbf{+1.46\%}, \qquad s_d = \sqrt{\frac{16.884}{9}} \approx \mathbf{1.3697\%}, \qquad \text{SE}(\bar{d}) = \frac{1.3697}{\sqrt{10}} \approx \mathbf{0.4331\%}$$

2. Student's Paired $t$-Test:

$$t = \frac{\bar{d}}{\text{SE}(\bar{d})} = \frac{1.46}{0.4331} \approx \mathbf{3.371}$$

Since $|t| = 3.371 > t_{\text{crit}} = 2.262$ ($p \approx 0.0082 < 0.05$), we reject $H_0$. Candidate Model $A$ is significantly superior.

3. Wilcoxon Signed-Rank Test:

Remove fold 3 ($d_3 = 0$), leaving $n_r = 9$ non-zero pairs.

  • Fold 2 ($|0.6|$): Rank 1
  • Fold 6 ($|1.2|$) and Fold 7 ($|-1.2|$): Tied ranks 2 and 3 → Rank 2.5 each
  • Fold 5 ($|1.5|$): Rank 4
  • Fold 9 ($|1.9|$): Rank 5
  • Fold 1 ($|2.2|$): Rank 6
  • Fold 10 ($|2.4|$): Rank 7
  • Fold 8 ($|2.5|$): Rank 8
  • Fold 4 ($|3.5|$): Rank 9
$$W^- = 2.5 \quad (\text{Fold } 7), \qquad W^+ = 1 + 2.5 + 4 + 5 + 6 + 7 + 8 + 9 = 42.5$$ $$W = \min(42.5, 2.5) = \mathbf{2.5}$$

Since $W = 2.5 \le W_{\text{crit}} = 5$, we reject $H_0$ ($p \approx 0.016 < 0.05$).

Exercise 2: Percentile vs. Empirical (Basic) Bootstrap Confidence Intervals

A classifier achieves an empirical Macro-F1 point estimate of $\hat{\theta} = 0.820$ on an evaluation benchmark. From $B = 2{,}000$ bootstrap iterations, the sorted estimates produce:

  • $2.5^{\text{th}}$ percentile $\hat{\theta}^*_{(0.025)} = 0.795$
  • $50.0^{\text{th}}$ percentile $\hat{\theta}^*_{(0.500)} = 0.824$
  • $97.5^{\text{th}}$ percentile $\hat{\theta}^*_{(0.975)} = 0.840$

Tasks: Calculate both the 95% Percentile Bootstrap CI and the 95% Empirical Basic Bootstrap CI. Explain why the Basic CI shifts.

View Complete Solution

1. Percentile Bootstrap Interval:

$$\text{CI}_{\text{pct}} = [\hat{\theta}^*_{(0.025)}, \; \hat{\theta}^*_{(0.975)}] = \mathbf{[0.795, \; 0.840]}$$

2. Empirical (Basic) Bootstrap Interval:

$$\text{CI}_{\text{basic}} = [2\hat{\theta} - \hat{\theta}^*_{(0.975)}, \; 2\hat{\theta} - \hat{\theta}^*_{(0.025)}]$$ $$\text{Lower} = 2(0.820) - 0.840 = 1.640 - 0.840 = \mathbf{0.800}$$ $$\text{Upper} = 2(0.820) - 0.795 = 1.640 - 0.795 = \mathbf{0.845}$$ $$\text{CI}_{\text{basic}} = \mathbf{[0.800, \; 0.845]}$$

3. Explanation: The bootstrap distribution exhibits positive bias ($\text{median} = 0.824 > \hat{\theta} = 0.820$). The Basic bootstrap models the distribution of estimation errors $\delta^* = \hat{\theta}^* - \hat{\theta}$ and reflects them across the point estimate, properly compensating for asymmetric skew.

Exercise 3: Multiple Comparisons: Bonferroni vs. Benjamini-Hochberg FDR

Evaluating $m = 10$ model variants against production baseline yields the following sorted two-tailed $p$-values: $p = [0.0021, 0.0045, 0.0098, 0.0182, 0.0240, 0.0385, 0.0460, 0.0810, 0.1540, 0.6200]$.

Tasks: At $\alpha = 0.05$ and FDR target $Q = 0.05$:

  1. Find how many models are significant under unadjusted testing.
  2. Compute the Bonferroni threshold $\alpha_{\text{Bonf}} = \alpha / m$ and identify accepted discoveries.
  3. Compute the Benjamini-Hochberg critical thresholds $c_k = \frac{k}{m} Q$ and identify accepted discoveries.
View Complete Solution

1. Unadjusted: Any $p_i \le 0.05$ is accepted. Folds 1 to 7 qualify → 7 discoveries (with an inflated $\text{FWER} \approx 40.1\%$).

2. Bonferroni: $\alpha_{\text{Bonf}} = 0.05 / 10 = \mathbf{0.0050}$. Only $p_{(1)} = 0.0021$ and $p_{(2)} = 0.0045$ are $\le 0.0050$ → 2 discoveries.
