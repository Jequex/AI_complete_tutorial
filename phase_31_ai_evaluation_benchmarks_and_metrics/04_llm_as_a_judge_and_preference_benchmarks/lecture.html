LLM-as-a-Judge & Preference Benchmarks

Pairwise Evaluation, Judge Systematic Biases, Position-Swapping De-Biasing, and Bradley-Terry Elo Rating Tournaments.

1. The 12-Year-Old Intuition: The Biased Science Fair Judge

Imagine a school science fair with 100 student projects. Asking a judge to score each project from 1 to 10 is notoriously noisy: a judge is generous at 9:00 AM after morning coffee and harsh at 4:00 PM when tired.

Pairwise duels (putting Project A and Project B side-by-side and asking "Which is better?") are vastly more reliable. But judges have human-like flaws:

  • Position Bias: They pick the project on the left 65% of the time.
  • Verbosity Bias: They fall for 20-page posters with pretty pictures over concise, brilliant 1-page solutions.
  • Self-Enhancement Bias: They prefer students whose style matches their own.

To eliminate bias, benchmarks like LMSYS Chatbot Arena swap positions (testing A vs B and B vs A) and use Bradley-Terry Elo ratings to determine global rankings.

2. The Bradley-Terry Rating Model

Given models $\mathcal{M}_A$ and $\mathcal{M}_B$ with latent Elo ratings $R_A, R_B \in \mathbb{R}$, the expected probability that Model $A$ defeats Model $B$ is:

$$\boxed{E_A = \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{M}_A \succ \mathcal{M}_B) = \frac{1}{1 + 10^{(R_B - R_A)/400}}}$$

When the actual match result $S_A \in \{1.0 \text{ (win)}, 0.5 \text{ (tie)}, 0.0 \text{ (loss)}\}$ is recorded:

$$\boxed{R_A' = R_A + K(S_A - E_A), \qquad R_B' = R_B + K(S_B - E_B)}$$

Because $S_B = 1 - S_A$ and $E_B = 1 - E_A$, rating points are strictly conserved: $R_A' + R_B' = R_A + R_B$.

3. Systematic Judge Biases & Bidirectional Swapping

Bidirectional Swapping Protocol:

  1. Pass 1: Prompt with [Response 1 = Model A, Response 2 = Model B] → Outcome $J_1$.
  2. Pass 2: Prompt with [Response 1 = Model B, Response 2 = Model A] → Outcome $J_2$.
$$\text{Final Result} = \begin{cases} \text{A Wins} & J_1 = A \text{ and } J_2 = A \\ \text{B Wins} & J_1 = B \text{ and } J_2 = B \\ \text{Tie} & \text{otherwise (position-dependent inconsistency)} \end{cases}$$

4. Offline Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE)

Rather than online sequence-dependent Elo updates, large-scale leaderboards solve for the global Bradley-Terry vector $\boldsymbol{\pi}$ by maximizing total tournament log-likelihood:

$$\boxed{\ln \mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\pi}) = \sum_{i < j} \left[ W_{ij} \ln \left(\frac{\pi_i}{\pi_i + \pi_j}\right) + W_{ji} \ln \left(\frac{\pi_j}{\pi_i + \pi_j}\right) \right]}$$

Using minorization-maximization (MM algorithm), the update iteration is:

$$\pi_i^{(t+1)} = \frac{W_i}{\sum_{j \ne i} \frac{W_{ij} + W_{ji}}{\pi_i^{(t)} + \pi_j^{(t)}}}$$
