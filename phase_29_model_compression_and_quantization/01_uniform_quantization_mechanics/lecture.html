Topic 29.1: Uniform Quantization Mechanics

From Continuous Reals to Discrete Integers: Affine Mapping, Symmetric Simplification, and the 6.02 dB Rule

1. 12-Year-Old Intuition: The Crayon Box Analogy

Imagine you have a giant set of high-end artist watercolor paints with 16.7 million distinct micro-shades of color (this is 32-bit floating-point numbers, FP32). When you paint a massive mural, packing all 16.7 million paint bottles requires a gigantic, heavy shipping container that takes up enormous space and costs a fortune to transport.

Now imagine someone tells you: "You can only take a tiny wooden box with 256 crayons (8-bit integer, INT8), or even a pocket box with just 16 crayons (4-bit integer, INT4). But your painting still needs to look almost identical to the human eye!"

How do you do it? You measure the darkest and brightest shades in your painting, lay down a ruler with 255 equal notches (your scale factor $s$), designate one crayon as your reference mark (the zero-point $z$), and snap every original watercolor paint to the nearest crayon notch. Because you rounded to the nearest crayon, you made a tiny mistake on each color—called Quantization Noise. Quantization is the mathematical craft of making that noise practically imperceptible.

3. Uniform Affine (Asymmetric) Quantization

In deep learning, activations (such as post-ReLU or post-GELU states) are often strictly positive or asymmetric. Uniform affine quantization maps the dynamic range $[x_{\min}, x_{\max}]$ to integer grid $[q_{\min}, q_{\max}]$.

Scale Factor ($s$) and Zero-Point ($z$)

$$s = \frac{x_{\max} - x_{\min}}{q_{\max} - q_{\min}}$$ $$z = \text{clip}\left( \left\lfloor -\frac{x_{\min}}{s} \right\rceil + q_{\min}, \; q_{\min}, \; q_{\max} \right)$$

Quantization Operator:

$$q = Q(x; s, z) = \text{clip}\left( \left\lfloor \frac{x}{s} \right\rceil + z, \; q_{\min}, \; q_{\max} \right)$$

Dequantization Operator:

$$\hat{x} = \hat{Q}(q; s, z) = s \cdot (q - z)$$

4. Symmetric Quantization

For symmetric distributions (e.g. weights centered around zero), setting $z = 0$ simplifies integer matrix multiplications and eliminates hardware zero-point subtraction overhead.

$$s = \frac{\max(|x_{\min}|, |x_{\max}|)}{q_{\max}} = \frac{\|x\|_\infty}{2^{b-1} - 1}$$ $$q = \text{clip}\left( \left\lfloor \frac{x}{s} \right\rceil, \; -2^{b-1}, \; 2^{b-1} - 1 \right)$$ $$\hat{x} = s \cdot q$$

5. Quantization Noise & The 6.02 dB Rule Derivation

Assuming rounding error $e = x - \hat{x}$ is uniformly distributed over $[-\Delta/2, \Delta/2]$ with step $\Delta = s$:

Noise Variance Derivation:

$$\sigma_e^2 = \int_{-\Delta/2}^{\Delta/2} e^2 \cdot \frac{1}{\Delta} \, de = \frac{1}{\Delta} \left[ \frac{e^3}{3} \right]_{-\Delta/2}^{\Delta/2} = \frac{\Delta^2}{12} = \frac{s^2}{12}$$

Full-Scale Sinusoid SQNR Derivation:

For a full-scale sinusoid $x(t) = A \sin(\omega t)$ with dynamic range $[-A, A]$: $P_{\text{signal}} = \frac{A^2}{2}$. Since $\Delta = \frac{2A}{2^b} = 2A \cdot 2^{-b}$:

$$P_{\text{noise}} = \frac{\Delta^2}{12} = \frac{(2A \cdot 2^{-b})^2}{12} = \frac{A^2 \cdot 2^{-2b}}{3}$$ $$\text{SQNR} = 10 \log_{10} \left( \frac{A^2 / 2}{A^2 2^{-2b} / 3} \right) = 10 \log_{10} \left( 1.5 \cdot 2^{2b} \right)$$ $$= 10 \log_{10}(1.5) + 20 b \log_{10}(2) \approx \mathbf{6.02 \cdot b + 1.76 \text{ dB}}$$
