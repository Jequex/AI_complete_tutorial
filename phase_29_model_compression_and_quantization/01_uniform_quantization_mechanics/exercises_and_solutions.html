Analytical Proofs: Integer Zero Preservation, Hardware Dot Product Cross-Terms, and Optimal Clipping Thresholds

Exercise 1: Zero-Point Integer Constraining and Exact Zero Preservation

Problem: In deep neural networks, exact zeros occur frequently due to zero-padding, ReLU activations ($\max(0, x)$), and sparse embeddings.

  1. Show mathematically that if $z$ were allowed to be an arbitrary real number $z \in \mathbb{R}$ rather than an integer $z \in \mathbb{Z}$, the quantized code $q$ for $x = 0$ would round to an integer, causing $x = 0$ to dequantize to a non-zero value $\hat{x} \neq 0$.
  2. Derive the maximum dequantization error $|\hat{x} - 0|$ for real zero when $z$ is rounded to the nearest integer.
View Complete Solution

1. Analytical proof:

Let $x = 0.0$. Under the continuous affine formula before integer rounding of the zero-point: $$z_{\text{real}} = -\frac{x_{\min}}{s} + q_{\min}$$ If we evaluate quantization of $x = 0$: $$q(0) = \left\lfloor \frac{0}{s} + z_{\text{real}} \right\rceil = \lfloor z_{\text{real}} \rceil$$ If $z_{\text{real}} \notin \mathbb{Z}$, then $\lfloor z_{\text{real}} \rceil \neq z_{\text{real}}$. When dequantized: $$\hat{x}(0) = s \left( q(0) - z_{\text{real}} \right) = s \left( \lfloor z_{\text{real}} \rceil - z_{\text{real}} \right) \neq 0$$ This destroys sparsity: every padded zero or ReLU-deactivated neuron in a tensor would become a non-zero floating-point number, causing severe numerical drift and breaking zero-skipping hardware optimizations.

2. Integer Constraining:

By defining $z = \lfloor z_{\text{real}} \rceil \in \mathbb{Z}$ as part of the quantizer specification, the dequantization formula uses the exact integer $z$: $$\hat{x} = s(q - z)$$ Evaluating at $x = 0$: $$q(0) = \left\lfloor \frac{0}{s} + z \right\rceil = \lfloor z \rceil = z \implies \hat{x}(0) = s(z - z) = 0.0$$ The maximum shift in the dynamic range boundary is bounded by $|z - z_{\text{real}}| \le 0.5$ LSBs, shifting the effective $x_{\min}$ by at most $\frac{s}{2}$, while guaranteeing that zero is represented with zero error.

Exercise 2: Hardware Dot Product Cross-Terms in Asymmetric vs. Symmetric Quantization

Problem: Consider continuous vectors $\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^K$. Let their quantized representations be $q_x^{(k)}$ with scale $s_x$, zero-point $z_x$, and $q_w^{(k)}$ with scale $s_w$, zero-point $z_w$.

  1. Derive the algebraic expansion for the inner product $\mathbf{x}^\top \mathbf{w} \approx \hat{\mathbf{x}}^\top \hat{\mathbf{w}}$.
  2. Identify which terms can be precomputed offline during compilation, and contrast the runtime computation of asymmetric quantization vs symmetric quantization ($z_x = 0, z_w = 0$).
View Complete Solution

1. Algebraic expansion:

$$\hat{\mathbf{x}}^\top \hat{\mathbf{w}} = \sum_{k=1}^K s_x (q_x^{(k)} - z_x) \cdot s_w (q_w^{(k)} - z_w)$$ $$\hat{\mathbf{x}}^\top \hat{\mathbf{w}} = s_x s_w \left[ \underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^K q_x^{(k)} q_w^{(k)}}_{\text{Term 1: Integer MAC}} - \underbrace{z_w \sum_{k=1}^K q_x^{(k)}}_{\text{Term 2: Activation Reduction}} - \underbrace{z_x \sum_{k=1}^K q_w^{(k)}}_{\text{Term 3: Weight Reduction}} + \underbrace{K \cdot z_x z_w}_{\text{Term 4: Constant Offset}} \right]$$

2. Hardware Implications:

  • Terms 3 & 4: Weights $\mathbf{w}$ are static. Thus, $\sum_{k=1}^K q_w^{(k)}$ and $K z_x z_w$ are computed offline and folded into the pre-existing bias vector.
  • Term 2: Requires runtime row-sum reduction over incoming activation tokens.
  • Symmetric Weights ($z_w = 0$): Eliminates Term 2 and Term 4 immediately!
  • Fully Symmetric ($z_x = 0, z_w = 0$): Only Term 1 remains: $$\hat{\mathbf{x}}^\top \hat{\mathbf{w}} = s_x s_w \sum_{k=1}^K q_x^{(k)} q_w^{(k)}$$ A pure integer dot product executed in single-instruction hardware units (NVIDIA Tensor Core mma.sync / ARM sdot).

Exercise 3: Optimal Clipping Threshold for Gaussian Weights

Problem: Let weight tensor elements be independent samples from standard normal distribution $W \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$. A symmetric $b$-bit quantizer has clipping boundary $[-c, c]$ with step size $\Delta = \frac{2c}{2^b - 1}$. The total expected MSE is: $$\text{MSE}(c) = \int_{-c}^c \frac{\Delta^2}{12} p(w) dw + 2 \int_c^\infty (w - c)^2 p(w) dw$$ Explain why setting $c = \max(|W|)$ is suboptimal for large tensors, and describe how percentile calibration reduces total MSE.

View Complete Solution

For a Gaussian tensor of size $N = 10^7$, extreme value theory dictates that the sample maximum satisfies $\mathbb{E}[\max |W_i|] \approx \sigma \sqrt{2 \ln(N)} \approx 5.68 \sigma$.

If we set $c = \max(|W|) \approx 5.68 \sigma$ (MinMax calibration), clipping error is zero, but step size $\Delta$ is inflated. Because rounding noise variance $\sigma_e^2 \propto \Delta^2 \propto c^2$, all $99.9999\%$ of non-outlier weights suffer an error variance increased by $\left(\frac{5.68}{3}\right)^2 \approx 3.6\times$ ($>360\%$).

By clipping at $c \approx 3.0 \sigma$ to $3.5 \sigma$ (e.g. 99.9th percentile), only $0.1\%$ of the weights are clamped, while reducing the step size $\Delta$ and rounding variance by $>60\%$ for the other $99.9\%$ of values. Hence, optimal clipping satisfies $c^* < \max(|W|)$.
