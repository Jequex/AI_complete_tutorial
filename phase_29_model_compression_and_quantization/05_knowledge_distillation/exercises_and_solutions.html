Topic 29.5: Exercises & Solutions

Analytical Proofs: KL Divergence vs Cross-Entropy Equivalence, $T^2$ Gradient Normalization, and FitNet Projections

Exercise 1: Equivalence Between KL Divergence and Soft Cross-Entropy

Problem: Show that minimizing $D_{\text{KL}}(p^t(T) \parallel p^s(T))$ with respect to student parameters $\theta_s$ is mathematically identical to minimizing soft cross-entropy $\mathcal{L}_{\text{KD}} = -\sum_i p_i^t(T) \ln(p_i^s(T))$.

View Complete Solution

Expanding the KL divergence:

$$D_{\text{KL}}\left( p^t(T) \,\|\, p^s(T) \right) = -H\left( p^t(T) \right) + H\left( p^t(T), \; p^s(T) \right)$$

Because the teacher is frozen during distillation, $\nabla_{\theta_s} H(p^t(T)) = \mathbf{0}$. Therefore:

$$\nabla_{\theta_s} D_{\text{KL}}\left( p^t(T) \,\|\, p^s(T) \right) = \nabla_{\theta_s} \left( -\sum_{i=1}^C p_i^t(T) \ln(p_i^s(T)) \right) = \nabla_{\theta_s} \mathcal{L}_{\text{KD}}$$

Minimizing KL divergence produces identical parameter updates as minimizing soft cross-entropy.

Exercise 2: Why the $T^2$ Multiplier Prevents Vanishing Gradients

Problem: In the high-temperature limit, $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{KD}}}{\partial z_i^s} \approx \frac{1}{C T^2} (z_i^s - z_i^t)$. Contrast gradient magnitudes at $T=1$ and $T=4$ if $T^2$ is omitted.

View Complete Solution

The distillation gradient scales as $\frac{1}{T^2}$. Without the $T^2$ factor:

$$\frac{\text{Gradient at } T=4}{\text{Gradient at } T=1} = \frac{1/16}{1/1} = \frac{1}{16} = 0.0625$$

Distillation gradients are suppressed by $16\times$ ($>93.7\%$). The student ignores the teacher and overfits to the hard labels. Multiplying $\mathcal{L}_{\text{KD}}$ by $T^2$ cancels the $\frac{1}{T^2}$ decay and preserves the intended supervision balance $\alpha$.

Exercise 3: Feature-Based Distillation (FitNets) with Dimension Mismatch

Problem: For student hidden features $\mathbf{h}_s \in \mathbb{R}^{d_s}$ and teacher features $\mathbf{h}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{d_t}$ ($d_s < d_t$), find the dimensions and gradients for projection matrix $\mathbf{W}_{\text{proj}}$ under $\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{2} \|\mathbf{W}_{\text{proj}} \mathbf{h}_s - \mathbf{h}_t\|_2^2$.

View Complete Solution

1. Dimensions: $\mathbf{W}_{\text{proj}} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_t \times d_s}$.

2. Gradients with residual $\mathbf{e} = \mathbf{W}_{\text{proj}} \mathbf{h}_s - \mathbf{h}_t$:

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{W}_{\text{proj}}} = \mathbf{e} \, \mathbf{h}_s^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{d_t \times d_s}$$ $$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{h}_s} = \mathbf{W}_{\text{proj}}^\top \mathbf{e} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_s}$$
