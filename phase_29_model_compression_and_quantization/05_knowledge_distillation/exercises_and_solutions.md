### Exercise 1: Equivalence Between KL Divergence and Soft Cross-Entropy
**Problem:**
Let $p^t(T)$ be the soft target probability distribution generated by a pre-trained Teacher network, and let $p^s(T)$ be the soft probability distribution generated by the Student network.
The Kullback-Leibler divergence between Teacher and Student is:
$$D_{\text{KL}}\left( p^t(T) \,\|\, p^s(T) \right) = \sum_{i=1}^C p_i^t(T) \ln\left( \frac{p_i^t(T)}{p_i^s(T)} \right)$$
1. Expand the KL divergence into entropy and cross-entropy components.
2. Prove that minimizing $D_{\text{KL}}(p^t(T) \,\|\, p^s(T))$ with respect to the student's parameters $\theta_s$ is mathematically identical to minimizing the soft cross-entropy $\mathcal{L}_{\text{KD}} = -\sum_{i=1}^C p_i^t(T) \ln(p_i^s(T))$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Solution</strong></summary>

1. **Expansion of KL Divergence:**
   Using the logarithmic quotient identity $\ln(a / b) = \ln(a) - \ln(b)$:
   $$D_{\text{KL}}\left( p^t(T) \,\|\, p^s(T) \right) = \sum_{i=1}^C p_i^t(T) \left[ \ln\left( p_i^t(T) \right) - \ln\left( p_i^s(T) \right) \right]$$
   $$= \underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^C p_i^t(T) \ln\left( p_i^t(T) \right)}_{-H(p^t(T))} - \sum_{i=1}^C p_i^t(T) \ln\left( p_i^s(T) \right)$$
   $$D_{\text{KL}}\left( p^t(T) \,\|\, p^s(T) \right) = -H\left( p^t(T) \right) + H\left( p^t(T), \; p^s(T) \right)$$
   where $H(p^t(T))$ is the Shannon entropy of the teacher's distribution, and $H(p^t(T), p^s(T))$ is the cross-entropy between teacher and student.

2. **Differentiation with respect to Student Parameters $\theta_s$:**
   The teacher network is completely frozen during distillation. Therefore, the teacher probabilities $p_i^t(T)$ and its entropy $H(p^t(T))$ do not depend on $\theta_s$:
   $$\nabla_{\theta_s} H\left( p^t(T) \right) = \mathbf{0}$$
   Taking the gradient of the KL divergence:
   $$\nabla_{\theta_s} D_{\text{KL}}\left( p^t(T) \,\|\, p^s(T) \right) = -\nabla_{\theta_s} H\left( p^t(T) \right) + \nabla_{\theta_s} H\left( p^t(T), \; p^s(T) \right)$$
   $$= \nabla_{\theta_s} H\left( p^t(T), \; p^s(T) \right) = \nabla_{\theta_s} \left( -\sum_{i=1}^C p_i^t(T) \ln\left( p_i^s(T) \right) \right)$$
   The gradient vectors are identically equal for all $\theta_s$. Thus, minimizing KL divergence produces the exact same parameter trajectories as minimizing soft cross-entropy.
</details>

### Exercise 2: Why the $T^2$ Multiplier Prevents Vanishing Gradients
**Problem:**
Consider the distillation loss gradient with respect to student logit $z_i^s$:
$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{KD}}}{\partial z_i^s} = \frac{1}{T} \left( p_i^s(T) - p_i^t(T) \right)$$
In the high temperature limit, we derived:
$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{KD}}}{\partial z_i^s} \approx \frac{1}{C T^2} (z_i^s - z_i^t)$$
Suppose a practitioner trains a student model with composite loss:
$$\mathcal{L} = (1 - \alpha) \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}} + \alpha \mathcal{L}_{\text{KD}}$$
(omitting the $T^2$ multiplier).
1. Contrast the magnitude of the distillation gradient at $T = 1$ versus $T = 4$.
2. Explain how this omission distorts the balance $\alpha$ between hard ground truth and teacher dark knowledge.

<details>
<summary><strong>Solution</strong></summary>

1. **Gradient Magnitude Scaling:**
   At high temperature, the gradient magnitude scales inversely with $T^2$:
   $$\left\| \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{KD}}}{\partial \mathbf{z}^s} \right\| \propto \frac{1}{T^2}$$
   Evaluating at $T = 1$ vs $T = 4$:
   $$\frac{\text{Gradient at } T=4}{\text{Gradient at } T=1} \approx \frac{1 / 4^2}{1 / 1^2} = \frac{1}{16} = 0.0625$$
   The distillation gradient is suppressed by a factor of **$16\times$** ($>93.7\%$ reduction) simply because temperature was increased!

2. **Distortion of Composite Balance:**
   The standard hard cross-entropy gradient $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}}{\partial z_i^s} = p_i^s(1) - y_i$ is evaluated at $T = 1$ and has magnitude $\mathcal{O}(1)$.
   If the $T^2$ multiplier is omitted, the relative ratio of teacher supervision to ground-truth supervision becomes:
   $$\frac{\text{Teacher Gradient}}{\text{Hard Ground-Truth Gradient}} \propto \frac{\alpha}{1 - \alpha} \cdot \frac{1}{T^2}$$
   Even if the user sets $\alpha = 0.8$ intending for the teacher to dominate the training signal, the effective distillation weight is reduced to $\frac{0.8}{16} = 0.05$. The student ignores the teacher and overfits to the hard labels.
   Multiplying $\mathcal{L}_{\text{KD}}$ by $T^2$ cancels the $\frac{1}{T^2}$ factor, ensuring that $\alpha$ preserves its intended relative contribution across all choices of temperature.
</details>

### Exercise 3: Feature-Based Distillation (FitNets) with Dimension Mismatch
**Problem:**
In FitNets (Romero et al., 2014), the student is forced to mimic intermediate hidden representations of the teacher:
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{feature}} = \frac{1}{2} \|\mathbf{W}_{\text{proj}} \mathbf{h}_s - \mathbf{h}_t\|_2^2$$
where $\mathbf{h}_s \in \mathbb{R}^{d_s}$ is the student's hidden feature vector, and $\mathbf{h}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{d_t}$ is the teacher's hidden feature vector with $d_s < d_t$.
1. What are the dimensions of the trainable projection matrix $\mathbf{W}_{\text{proj}}$?
2. Derive the analytical gradient of $\mathcal{L}_{\text{feature}}$ with respect to student representation $\mathbf{h}_s$ and projection matrix $\mathbf{W}_{\text{proj}}$.

<details>
<summary><strong>Solution</strong></summary>

1. **Matrix Dimensions:**
   We require $\mathbf{W}_{\text{proj}} \mathbf{h}_s \in \mathbb{R}^{d_t}$ to match $\mathbf{h}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{d_t}$.
   Since $\mathbf{h}_s \in \mathbb{R}^{d_s}$, the linear transformation matrix must satisfy:
   $$\mathbf{W}_{\text{proj}} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_t \times d_s}$$
   This projection is typically implemented as a $1 \times 1$ convolution or single dense linear layer that is discarded after training.

2. **Gradient Derivations:**
   Let the residual error vector be $\mathbf{e} = \mathbf{W}_{\text{proj}} \mathbf{h}_s - \mathbf{h}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{d_t}$.
   The loss is $\mathcal{L}_{\text{feature}} = \frac{1}{2} \mathbf{e}^\top \mathbf{e}$.
   
   - **Gradient with respect to $\mathbf{W}_{\text{proj}}$:**
     Using matrix calculus:
     $$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{feature}}}{\partial \mathbf{W}_{\text{proj}}} = \mathbf{e} \, \mathbf{h}_s^\top = (\mathbf{W}_{\text{proj}} \mathbf{h}_s - \mathbf{h}_t) \mathbf{h}_s^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{d_t \times d_s}$$
     
   - **Gradient with respect to Student Feature $\mathbf{h}_s$:**
     By the chain rule:
     $$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{feature}}}{\partial \mathbf{h}_s} = \mathbf{W}_{\text{proj}}^\top \mathbf{e} = \mathbf{W}_{\text{proj}}^\top (\mathbf{W}_{\text{proj}} \mathbf{h}_s - \mathbf{h}_t) \in \mathbb{R}^{d_s}$$
     This backpropagates dimensional alignment error directly into the student's upstream convolutional or transformer backbone.
</details>
