Problem 1: Reward Head vs. LM Head Parameter Count & Memory (Easy)

In an 8B model with $d_{\text{model}} = 4096$ and vocabulary size $|\mathcal{V}| = 128{,}000$:

  1. Compute the parameters and FP16 footprint of the standard LM head.
  2. Compute the parameters and FP16 footprint of the scalar Reward head.
  3. What fraction of the LM head's size is the Reward head?
View Complete Solution

Step 1: $\text{LM Head} = 128{,}000 \times 4096 = 524{,}288{,}000$ parameters ($1.048\text{ GB}$ in FP16).

Step 2: $\text{RM Head} = 1 \times 4096 = 4096$ parameters ($8.192\text{ KB}$ in FP16).

Step 3: $\frac{4096}{524{,}288{,}000} = \frac{1}{128{,}000} \approx 0.000781\%$, saving over $1\text{ GB}$ of head parameter memory.

Problem 2: Ranking Loss with Margin Penalty (Medium)

Consider $\mathcal{L} = -\log \sigma(r_w - r_l - m)$ with margin $m \ge 0$.

  1. Derive $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \Delta}$ where $\Delta = r_w - r_l$.
  2. Evaluate the gradient at $\Delta = 0$ for $m=0.5$ vs $m=0.0$.
  3. Explain why a margin penalty prevents models from settling into indifference.
View Complete Solution

Step 1: $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \Delta} = \sigma(\Delta - m) - 1 = -\sigma(m - \Delta)$.

Step 2: For $m=0$, $|\nabla| = \sigma(0) = 0.5$. For $m=0.5$, $|\nabla| = \sigma(0.5) \approx 0.6225$ ($24.5\%$ larger).

Step 3: The margin forces the reward difference $r_w - r_l$ to exceed $m$ before gradient pressure subsides, driving active separation between high and low quality completions.

Problem 3: Score Normalization & Additive Invariance (Medium)

  1. Show why scaling rewards by standard deviation alters the effective temperature of the preference probability.
  2. Under what conditions does $\tilde{r} = a \cdot r + b$ preserve the pairwise cross-entropy loss?
  3. Why does an uncentered positive reward offset create artificial length incentives in RL?
View Complete Solution

Step 1: $\tilde{\Delta} = \Delta / \sigma$. Because $\sigma_{\text{logistic}}(z)$ is nonlinear, changing $\sigma$ changes the steepness of probabilities.

Step 2: Invariance holds if and only if $a = 1.0$ (for any arbitrary shift $b \in \mathbb{R}$).

Step 3: If rewards are strictly positive without centering, every extra token generated accumulates positive value expectations, incentivizing the policy to delay ``.

Problem 4: Goodhart's Law & Length Bias Modeling (Hard)

True utility is $U^*(y) = Q(y) - 0.05 \cdot L(y)$, but a trained RM learned $r_{\text{RM}}(y) = 0.6 \cdot Q(y) + 0.04 \cdot L(y)$.

Compare Response 1 ($Q=9, L=20$) vs Response 2 ($Q=7, L=150$).

View Complete Solution

Step 1: $U^*(y_1) = 9.0 - 1.0 = 8.0$; $U^*(y_2) = 7.0 - 7.5 = -0.5$. Response 1 is vastly superior to a human.

Step 2: $r_{\text{RM}}(y_1) = 5.4 + 0.8 = 6.2$; $r_{\text{RM}}(y_2) = 4.2 + 6.0 = 10.2$. The RM strongly prefers Response 2 ($10.2 > 6.2$).

Step 3: Because $\Delta r = +4.0$ ($P(y_2 \succ y_1) \approx 98.2\%$), an RL policy will maximize length to the context limit, producing low-utility bloated text. This demonstrates Goodhart's Law in action.
