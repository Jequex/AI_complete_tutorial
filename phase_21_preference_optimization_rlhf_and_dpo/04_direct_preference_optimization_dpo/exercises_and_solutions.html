Topic 21.4: Exercises & Solutions — Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)

Problem 1: Partition Function Cancellation in DPO (Easy)

Given $\pi^*(y \mid x) = \frac{1}{Z(x)} \pi_{\text{ref}}(y \mid x) \exp(\frac{1}{\beta} r(x, y))$:

  1. Invert to isolate reward $r(x, y)$.
  2. Evaluate $r(x, y_w) - r(x, y_l)$ and show $Z(x)$ cancels identically.
  3. Why is $Z(x)$ impossible to compute for LLMs?
View Complete Solution

Step 1: $r(x, y) = \beta \log \frac{\pi^*(y \mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y \mid x)} + \beta \log Z(x)$.

Step 2: When taking the difference between two completions for prompt $x$, the constant $\beta \log Z(x)$ subtracts out: $\beta \log Z(x) - \beta \log Z(x) \equiv 0$.

Step 3: Summing over all completions requires $|\mathcal{V}|^T \approx 128{,}000^{512} \approx 10^{2600}$ calculations. Exact cancellation eliminates the need to compute this sum.

Problem 2: DPO Analytical Gradient & Error Weighting (Medium)

Derive $\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO}}$ and evaluate dynamic error weighting $\sigma(-\Delta \hat{r})$ for:

  1. $\Delta \hat{r} = 0.0$ (neutral).
  2. $\Delta \hat{r} = +3.0$ (correctly aligned).
  3. $\Delta \hat{r} = -3.0$ (misaligned).
View Complete Solution

Step 1: $\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO}} = -\beta \sigma(-\Delta \hat{r}) [\nabla \log \pi_\theta(y_w) - \nabla \log \pi_\theta(y_l)]$.

Step 2: At $\Delta \hat{r}=0.0$, $\sigma(0) = 0.500$. At $+3.0$, $\sigma(-3) \approx 0.0474$ (gradient downscaled $95\%$ to prevent overfitting). At $-3.0$, $\sigma(3) \approx 0.9526$ (maximum corrective force applied).

Problem 3: The Role of the $\beta$ Regularizer (Medium)

Analyze how $\beta \in \{0.5, 0.1, 0.01\}$ scales the implicit reward gap $\Delta \hat{r} = \beta \Delta \log$, and explain the failure mode when $\beta \to 0$.

View Complete Solution

For a fixed log-prob change of $+6.0$, $\Delta \hat{r}$ is $+3.0$ ($\beta=0.5$), $+0.6$ ($\beta=0.1$), and $+0.06$ ($\beta=0.01$).

When $\beta \to 0$, the implicit logit inside sigmoid collapses to 0 unless the log-ratio goes to infinity. The model loses its reference tether, memorizes training noise, and suffers language degeneration.

Problem 4: DPO vs. Unconstrained SFT on Preferred Data (Hard)

Why can't we simply train SFT on winning completions $(x, y_w)$ alone?

View Complete Solution

SFT on preferred data is a unidirectional pull that increases $P(y_w)$, but never actively suppresses $P(y_l)$. Bad habits, toxic completions, or hallucinations remain in the distribution.

DPO acts as a bidirectional push-pull: it reinforces $y_w$ while directly deflating $y_l$, establishing sharp, calibrated decision boundaries.
