Topic 21.1: Exercises & Solutions — Bradley-Terry & Preference Modeling

Problem 1: Human Scalar Noise vs. Pairwise Comparisons (Easy)

Suppose three annotators (Alice, Bob, Carol) rate responses $y_1$ ($u=4.0$) and $y_2$ ($u=2.5$) with rater biases $b_{\text{Alice}}=+3.5$, $b_{\text{Bob}}=0.0$, $b_{\text{Carol}}=-2.0$.

  1. Compute the expected scalar ratings $\mathbb{E}[s_{\text{Alice}}(y_2)]$ and $\mathbb{E}[s_{\text{Carol}}(y_1)]$. Can raw scalar ratings be directly compared across raters?
  2. Show that pairwise comparison eliminates individual rater biases identically.
View Complete Solution

Step 1: $\mathbb{E}[s_{\text{Alice}}(y_2)] = 2.5 + 3.5 = 6.0$, while $\mathbb{E}[s_{\text{Carol}}(y_1)] = 4.0 - 2.0 = 2.0$. Comparing them directly gives $6.0 > 2.0$, falsely concluding $y_2$ is superior to $y_1$.

Step 2: In a pairwise decision, the perceived difference is $\Delta = (u_1 + b_k) - (u_2 + b_k) = u_1 - u_2 = 1.5$. The rater bias $b_k$ cancels out identically for all raters, giving an agreement probability $> 99.9\%$.

Problem 2: Bradley-Terry Maximum Likelihood Gradient & Hessian (Medium)

Consider the pairwise loss $\ell(\Delta) = -\log \sigma(\Delta)$ where $\Delta = r_w - r_l$.

  1. Derive $\frac{\partial \ell}{\partial \Delta}$ and show it equals $-\sigma(-\Delta) = \sigma(\Delta) - 1$.
  2. Compute $\frac{\partial^2 \ell}{\partial \Delta^2}$ and prove strict convexity.
  3. Compare gradient magnitudes for $\Delta = +3.0$ vs $\Delta = -3.0$.
View Complete Solution

Step 1: Using the derivative of sigmoid $\sigma'(z) = \sigma(z)(1-\sigma(z))$:

$$\frac{d\ell}{d\Delta} = -\frac{\sigma(\Delta)(1-\sigma(\Delta))}{\sigma(\Delta)} = -(1 - \sigma(\Delta)) = -\sigma(-\Delta)$$

Step 2: Differentiating again: $\frac{d^2\ell}{d\Delta^2} = \sigma(\Delta)(1-\sigma(\Delta)) > 0$ for all real $\Delta$. The objective is strictly convex.

Step 3: At $\Delta = +3.0$, $|\nabla| \approx 0.0474$ (mild adjustment). At $\Delta = -3.0$, $|\nabla| \approx 0.9526$ (large corrective gradient).

Problem 3: Plackett-Luce Ranking Probability Computation (Medium)

Three candidate responses have rewards $r_A = 2.0$, $r_B = 1.0$, $r_C = 0.0$.

  1. Compute the probability of ranking $y_A \succ y_B \succ y_C$.
  2. Compute the probability of inverted ranking $y_C \succ y_B \succ y_A$.
  3. Demonstrate the Independence of Irrelevant Alternatives (IIA) property.
View Complete Solution

Step 1: $P(A \mid \{A, B, C\}) = \frac{e^2}{e^2 + e^1 + e^0} = \frac{7.389}{11.107} \approx 0.6652$.

$P(B \mid \{B, C\}) = \frac{e^1}{e^1 + e^0} = \frac{2.718}{3.718} \approx 0.7311$. Total $P(A \succ B \succ C) \approx 0.4863$ ($48.63\%$).

Step 2: $P(C \mid \{A, B, C\}) = \frac{1}{11.107} \approx 0.0900$. $P(B \mid \{B, A\}) = \frac{2.718}{10.107} \approx 0.2689$. Total $P(C \succ B \succ A) \approx 0.0242$ ($2.42\%$).

Step 3: $\frac{P(A \mid \{A,B,C\})}{P(B \mid \{A,B,C\})} = \frac{e^{r_A}}{e^{r_B}} = e^{r_A - r_B}$, independent of alternative $C$.

Problem 4: Transitivity Proof & The Condorcet Paradox (Hard)

  1. Prove that Bradley-Terry enforces Strong Stochastic Transitivity (SST).
  2. Given a cyclic preference dataset $A \succ B$, $B \succ C$, $C \succ A$, find the optimal centered MLE rewards ($r_A + r_B + r_C = 0$).
View Complete Solution

Step 1: $\Delta_{AC} = (r_A - r_B) + (r_B - r_C) = \Delta_{AB} + \Delta_{BC} \ge \max(\Delta_{AB}, \Delta_{BC})$. Monotonicity of sigmoid guarantees $\sigma(\Delta_{AC}) \ge \max(\sigma(\Delta_{AB}), \sigma(\Delta_{BC}))$, satisfying SST.

Step 2: Differentiating the cyclic log-likelihood $\mathcal{L} = \log\sigma(r_A-r_B) + \log\sigma(r_B-r_C) + \log\sigma(r_C-r_A)$ reveals that the stationary point requires $r_A - r_B = r_B - r_C = r_C - r_A = 0$. With $r_A + r_B + r_C = 0$, the unique MLE solution is $r_A^* = r_B^* = r_C^* = 0.0$, assigning a 50% tie probability to all pairs.
