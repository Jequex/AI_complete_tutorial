Exercise 1: Low-Rank Approximation and LoRA Energy Budget

In fine-tuning an LLM with $d_{\text{model}} = 4096$, the weight update matrix $\Delta \mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^{4096 \times 4096}$ has empirical singular values decaying as $\sigma_i = 10 \cdot 2^{-(i-1)/8}$.

  1. Formulate the total Frobenius norm $\|\Delta \mathbf{W}\|_F^2$ as a geometric series and compute its value.
  2. Under the Eckart-Young Theorem, what is the exact relative Frobenius approximation error $\frac{\|\Delta \mathbf{W} - \Delta \mathbf{W}_r\|_F^2}{\|\Delta \mathbf{W}\|_F^2}$ achieved by a LoRA rank of $r = 16$?
  3. What percentage of total Frobenius energy is retained with $r = 16$?
View Complete Step-by-Step Solution

1. Frobenius Energy Calculation

$$\|\Delta \mathbf{W}\|_F^2 = \sum_{i=1}^{4096} \sigma_i^2 = 100 \sum_{i=1}^{4096} 2^{-(i-1)/4} \approx \frac{100}{1 - 2^{-1/4}} \approx \mathbf{628.52}$$

2. Relative Error for Rank 16

By the Eckart-Young Theorem:

$$\|\Delta \mathbf{W} - \Delta \mathbf{W}_{16}\|_F^2 = \sum_{i=17}^{4096} \sigma_i^2 = 2^{-4} \sum_{k=1}^{4080} 100 \cdot 2^{-(k-1)/4} = \frac{1}{16} \|\Delta \mathbf{W}\|_F^2$$ $$\frac{\|\Delta \mathbf{W} - \Delta \mathbf{W}_{16}\|_F^2}{\|\Delta \mathbf{W}\|_F^2} = \frac{1}{16} = \mathbf{6.25\%}$$

3. Retained Energy

$$\text{Retained Energy} = 1 - 0.0625 = \mathbf{93.75\%}$$

LoRA rank 16 captures 93.75% of full rank adaptation while saving over 99.2% of parameter storage.

Exercise 2: Rayleigh Quotient and Variational Eigenvalues

Let $\mathbf{S} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ be symmetric with eigenvalues $\lambda_1 \ge \lambda_2 \ge \dots \ge \lambda_n$. The Rayleigh quotient is $R_{\mathbf{S}}(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{\mathbf{x}^\top \mathbf{S} \mathbf{x}}{\mathbf{x}^\top \mathbf{x}}$ for $\mathbf{x} \neq \mathbf{0}$.

  1. Prove that $\max_{\mathbf{x} \neq \mathbf{0}} R_{\mathbf{S}}(\mathbf{x}) = \lambda_1$.
  2. Prove that if $\mathbf{x} \perp \mathbf{q}_1$, then $\max_{\mathbf{x} \perp \mathbf{q}_1} R_{\mathbf{S}}(\mathbf{x}) = \lambda_2$.
  3. Explain how this justifies Hotelling deflation in Power Iteration.
View Complete Step-by-Step Solution

1. Rayleigh Quotient Bound

Expanding $\mathbf{x} = \sum c_i \mathbf{q}_i$ in the orthonormal eigenbasis:

$$\mathbf{x}^\top \mathbf{S} \mathbf{x} = \sum \lambda_i c_i^2 \le \lambda_1 \sum c_i^2 = \lambda_1 (\mathbf{x}^\top \mathbf{x})$$

Equality holds when $\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{q}_1$, so $\max R_{\mathbf{S}}(\mathbf{x}) = \lambda_1$.

2. Subspace Constraint

When $\mathbf{x} \perp \mathbf{q}_1$, $c_1 = 0$, so $\mathbf{x}^\top \mathbf{S} \mathbf{x} = \sum_{i=2}^n \lambda_i c_i^2 \le \lambda_2 \sum_{i=2}^n c_i^2 = \lambda_2 (\mathbf{x}^\top \mathbf{x})$. Equality holds at $\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{q}_2$.

3. Hotelling Deflation

Subtracting $\lambda_1 \mathbf{q}_1 \mathbf{q}_1^\top$ zeroes out the first eigenvalue while leaving all other eigenpairs unchanged, enabling sequential extraction of subdominant singular triplets.

Exercise 3: Condition Number and Gradient Oscillation

Consider $f(x_1, x_2) = \frac{1}{2}(100 x_1^2 + x_2^2)$ where $\kappa = 100$.

  1. Find the maximum stable learning rate $\eta_{\max}$ for standard Gradient Descent.
  2. At $\eta^* = \frac{2}{\lambda_1 + \lambda_2}$, compute the contraction factor $\rho = \frac{\kappa - 1}{\kappa + 1}$.
  3. Compute the iterations needed to reduce the initial error by $10^{-6}$.
View Complete Step-by-Step Solution

1. Stability Limit

$$|1 - \eta \lambda_1| < 1 \implies \eta < \frac{2}{\lambda_{\max}} = \frac{2}{100} = \mathbf{0.02}$$

2. Contraction Rate

$$\rho = \frac{100 - 1}{100 + 1} = \frac{99}{101} \approx \mathbf{0.9802}$$

3. Iteration Count

$$T \ge \frac{-6 \ln(10)}{\ln(99/101)} \approx \frac{-13.8155}{-0.020001} \approx \mathbf{691 \text{ iterations}}$$
