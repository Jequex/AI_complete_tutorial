Constrained Optimization & Duality

Core Research Question: How do Lagrange multipliers enforce constraints as normal contact forces, why does Slater's condition guarantee zero duality gap ($p^* = d^*$), and how do the four KKT conditions characterize constrained optimality?

1. 12-Year-Old Intuition: The Marble in the Bowl

Imagine dropping a heavy steel marble into a smooth wooden salad bowl. With no barriers, the marble rolls straight to the lowest point where the ground is flat ($\nabla f = \mathbf{0}$). This is unconstrained minimization.

Now insert a stiff cardboard fence vertically through the middle of the bowl. The marble rolls down until it collides with the fence and comes to a complete rest.

Why does it stop? Because the downhill gravitational pull is perfectly balanced by the normal reaction force of the cardboard pushing back!

  • Dual Multipliers: The outward force exerted by the fence is the Lagrange multiplier $\lambda$.
  • Dual Feasibility: The cardboard can only push away; it cannot pull the marble through ($\lambda \ge 0$).
  • Complementary Slackness: If the marble rests away from the fence (slack $> 0$), the fence exerts zero force ($\lambda = 0$). The fence only pushes ($\lambda > 0$) if the marble is touching it (slack $= 0$).

3. The Four Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) Conditions

For convex optimization problems with differentiable functions and zero duality gap, a point $(\mathbf{x}^*, \boldsymbol{\lambda}^*, \boldsymbol{\nu}^*)$ is globally optimal if and only if it satisfies all four KKT conditions:

The Four KKT Equations:
1. Stationarity: $\nabla f_0(\mathbf{x}^*) + \sum_{i=1}^m \lambda_i^* \nabla f_i(\mathbf{x}^*) + \sum_{j=1}^p \nu_j^* \nabla h_j(\mathbf{x}^*) = \mathbf{0}$
2. Primal Feasibility: $f_i(\mathbf{x}^*) \le 0 \quad (\forall i), \quad h_j(\mathbf{x}^*) = 0 \quad (\forall j)$
3. Dual Feasibility: $\lambda_i^* \ge 0 \quad (\forall i)$
4. Complementary Slackness: $\lambda_i^* f_i(\mathbf{x}^*) = 0 \quad (\forall i)$

4. Slater's Condition & Strong Duality

Weak Duality ($d^* \le p^*$) holds unconditionally for all optimization problems.

Strong Duality ($d^* = p^*$) holds if the problem is convex and satisfies Slater's condition: there exists at least one point in the relative interior of the domain that strictly satisfies all inequalities:

$$f_i(\mathbf{x}_{\text{relint}}) < 0 \quad (i = 1, \dots, m), \quad \mathbf{C}\mathbf{x}_{\text{relint}} = \mathbf{d}$$

5. Active Set Quadratic Programming (QP)

For quadratic programs $\min \frac{1}{2}\mathbf{x}^\top \mathbf{Q}\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{c}^\top \mathbf{x}$ subject to affine constraints, the Primal Active Set Method dynamically tracks which constraints are binding (in the active set $\mathcal{W}$).

It solves an unconstrained linear subproblem on the active subspace at each iteration, achieving exact convergence to machine precision ($10^{-16}$) in a finite number of steps.
