Information Geometry & Natural Gradients

Core Research Question: Why is Euclidean gradient descent sensitive to arbitrary parameter coordinate choices, how is the Fisher Information Matrix the Riemannian metric tensor of KL divergence, and why is Natural Gradient Descent strictly parameterization-invariant?

1. 12-Year-Old Intuition: The Distorted World Map

Imagine looking at a flat Mercator world map. Greenland appears as large as all of Africa! If you measure distances with a standard wooden desk ruler, moving 1 inch across Greenland seems identical to moving 1 inch across Africa.

In reality, the Earth is curved. A straight line on the flat paper is a distorted path on the globe.

Probability distributions also live on a curved space called a statistical manifold:

  • If two Gaussian distributions have large spread ($\sigma = 100$), shifting the mean by $\Delta \mu = 1.0$ makes virtually no difference (they overlap $99.9\%$).
  • If two Gaussians are extremely sharp ($\sigma = 0.01$), shifting the mean by $\Delta \mu = 1.0$ separates them completely into non-overlapping worlds!

Yet in both cases, Euclidean gradient descent sees the exact same coordinate shift $\Delta \mu = 1.0$. It is blind to the curvature of probability space. Natural Gradient Descent (Amari 1998) replaces the flat ruler with the Fisher Information Metric, steering optimization along the true shortest geodesic on the manifold.

3. The Riemannian Metric of Kullback-Leibler Divergence

Expanding the KL divergence $\mathbb{D}_{\text{KL}}(p_{\boldsymbol{\theta}} \| p_{\boldsymbol{\theta} + d\boldsymbol{\theta}})$ in a Taylor series around $d\boldsymbol{\theta} = \mathbf{0}$:

$$\mathbb{D}_{\text{KL}}(p_{\boldsymbol{\theta}} \| p_{\boldsymbol{\theta} + d\boldsymbol{\theta}}) = \frac{1}{2} d\boldsymbol{\theta}^\top \mathbf{F}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) d\boldsymbol{\theta} + \mathcal{O}(\|d\boldsymbol{\theta}\|^3)$$
Theorem: The Fisher Information Matrix $\mathbf{F}(\boldsymbol{\theta})$ is the exact second-order Taylor expansion (Hessian) of KL divergence at zero displacement. It acts as the canonical Riemannian metric tensor for the manifold of probability distributions.

4. Natural Gradient Descent & Reparameterization Invariance

Natural Gradient defines steepest descent subject to a fixed budget in KL divergence:

$$\Delta \boldsymbol{\theta} = \arg\min_{\Delta \boldsymbol{\theta}} \nabla L^\top \Delta \boldsymbol{\theta} \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \frac{1}{2}\Delta \boldsymbol{\theta}^\top \mathbf{F}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\Delta \boldsymbol{\theta} \le \epsilon$$ $$\implies \boldsymbol{\theta}_{t+1} = \boldsymbol{\theta}_t - \eta \mathbf{F}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_t)^{-1} \nabla L(\boldsymbol{\theta}_t)$$

Under any smooth change of coordinates $\boldsymbol{\phi} = \mathbf{g}(\boldsymbol{\theta})$ with Jacobian $\mathbf{J} = \partial \boldsymbol{\phi}/\partial \boldsymbol{\theta}$:

$$\mathbf{J} \Delta \boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{NGD}} = \Delta \boldsymbol{\phi}_{\text{NGD}}$$

The resulting trajectory on the probability manifold is strictly identical regardless of parameterization choice, completely resolving the pathological ill-conditioning of Euclidean gradients.
