Problem 1: Multivariable Critical Point Discovery & Hessian Classification

Medium

Consider the non-linear multivariable function:

$$f(x, y) = x^3 - 3x + y^2$$
  1. Calculate the gradient vector $\nabla f(x, y)$.
  2. Set $\nabla f(x, y) = \mathbf{0}$ to find all critical points $(x^*, y^*)$.
  3. Compute the general $2 \times 2$ Hessian matrix $H(x, y)$.
  4. Evaluate $H$ at each critical point, compute its eigenvalues, and classify each point as a local minimum, local maximum, or saddle point.
Click to Reveal Step-by-Step Solution

1. Gradient Vector

$$\nabla f(x, y) = \begin{bmatrix} 3x^2 - 3 \\ 2y \end{bmatrix}$$

2. Critical Points

Setting $\nabla f = \mathbf{0}$:

$$3x^2 - 3 = 0 \implies x = \pm 1, \quad 2y = 0 \implies y = 0$$

Critical points are: Point A $(+1, 0)$ and Point B $(-1, 0)$.

3. General Hessian Matrix

$$H(x, y) = \begin{bmatrix} 6x & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$$

4. Eigenvalue Classification

At Point A $(+1, 0)$:

$$H(1, 0) = \begin{bmatrix} 6 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix} \implies \lambda_1 = 6, \, \lambda_2 = 2$$

Both eigenvalues are positive ($\lambda_i > 0$). $H$ is Positive Definite $\implies$ Local Minimum!

At Point B $(-1, 0)$:

$$H(-1, 0) = \begin{bmatrix} -6 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix} \implies \lambda_1 = -6, \, \lambda_2 = 2$$

Eigenvalues have mixed signs ($\lambda_1 < 0, \lambda_2 > 0$). $H$ is Indefinite $\implies$ Saddle Point!

Problem 2: Hand-Crafted Newton Optimization Step on a Quadratic Bowl

Medium

Consider the coupled quadratic objective function:

$$f(x, y) = 2x^2 + 4xy + 5y^2 - 6x - 8y$$
  1. Calculate the gradient vector $\nabla f(x, y)$ and the constant Hessian matrix $H$.
  2. Compute the inverse matrix $H^{-1}$.
  3. Starting at the origin $\mathbf{w}_0 = [0, 0]^T$, calculate the Newton step $\Delta\mathbf{w} = -H^{-1} \nabla f(\mathbf{w}_0)$.
  4. Verify that $\nabla f(\mathbf{w}_1) = \mathbf{0}$, confirming exact 1-step convergence.
Click to Reveal Step-by-Step Solution

1. Gradient & Hessian

$$\nabla f(x, y) = \begin{bmatrix} 4x + 4y - 6 \\ 4x + 10y - 8 \end{bmatrix}, \quad H = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 4 \\ 4 & 10 \end{bmatrix}$$

2. Matrix Inversion

$$\det(H) = (4)(10) - (4)(4) = 40 - 16 = 24$$ $$H^{-1} = \frac{1}{24}\begin{bmatrix} 10 & -4 \\ -4 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$$

3. Newton Step from Origin $\mathbf{w}_0 = [0, 0]^T$

$$\nabla f(0, 0) = \begin{bmatrix} -6 \\ -8 \end{bmatrix}$$ $$\Delta\mathbf{w} = -\frac{1}{24}\begin{bmatrix} 10 & -4 \\ -4 & 4 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} -6 \\ -8 \end{bmatrix} = -\frac{1}{24}\begin{bmatrix} -28 \\ -8 \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{\begin{bmatrix} 7/6 \\ 1/3 \end{bmatrix}}$$ $$\mathbf{w}_1 = \mathbf{w}_0 + \Delta\mathbf{w} = \mathbf{\begin{bmatrix} 7/6 \\ 1/3 \end{bmatrix}}$$

4. Gradient Verification at $\mathbf{w}_1$

$$\nabla f(7/6, 1/3) = \begin{bmatrix} 4(7/6) + 4(1/3) - 6 \\ 4(7/6) + 10(1/3) - 8 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 18/3 - 6 \\ 24/3 - 8 \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{\begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}} \quad \blacksquare$$
Newton's Superpower: Unlike Gradient Descent which requires dozens of small iterative steps, Newton's method uses curvature information to jump to the exact global minimum in a single step!

Problem 3: Condition Number $\kappa(H)$ & The Gradient Descent Stability Limit

Challenging

Consider the canyon loss function:

$$f(x, y) = \frac{1}{2} (1000 x^2 + y^2)$$
  1. Find the Hessian $H$, its eigenvalues $\lambda_1, \lambda_2$, and the condition number $\kappa(H) = \frac{\lambda_{\max}}{\lambda_{\min}}$.
  2. Derive the coordinate update recurrence for Gradient Descent: $x_{t+1} = (1 - \eta \lambda_1)x_t$.
  3. What is the strict maximum learning rate $\eta_{\max}$ before Gradient Descent becomes unstable and explodes on $x$?
  4. If $\eta = 0.001$, how many steps are required to reduce the error along the flat $y$-axis to $1\%$ of its initial value?
Click to Reveal Step-by-Step Solution

1. Condition Number

$$H = \begin{bmatrix} 1000 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \implies \lambda_1 = 1000, \, \lambda_2 = 1$$ $$\kappa(H) = \frac{1000}{1} = \mathbf{1000}$$

2. Gradient Descent Recurrence

$$\nabla f = \begin{bmatrix} 1000 x \\ y \end{bmatrix} \implies x_{t+1} = x_t - \eta(1000 x_t) = (1 - 1000\eta) x_t$$ $$y_{t+1} = y_t - \eta(1 y_t) = (1 - \eta) y_t$$

3. Maximum Learning Rate Stability Bound

For stability, the multiplier magnitude must be strictly less than 1:

$$|1 - 1000\eta| < 1 \implies -1 < 1 - 1000\eta < 1 \implies \mathbf{\eta < \frac{2}{1000} = 0.002}$$

Any learning rate $\eta \ge 0.002$ causes catastrophic divergence!

4. Slow Convergence along $y$

With $\eta = 0.001$, the $y$-axis shrinks by factor $(1 - 0.001) = 0.999$ per step:

$$0.999^t = 0.01 \implies t = \frac{\ln(0.01)}{\ln(0.999)} \approx \frac{-4.605}{-0.0010005} \approx \mathbf{4{,}603 \text{ steps!}}$$
Why Modern AI Uses Adam: Standard SGD is paralyzed by ill-conditioned ravines: large step sizes explode on the walls, while small step sizes crawl for thousands of steps. Adam solves this by dividing each parameter's update by its own historical gradient variance, effectively equalizing convergence speeds across both steep and flat directions!
