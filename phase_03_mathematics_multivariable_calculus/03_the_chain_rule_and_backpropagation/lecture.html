Topic 3.3: The Chain Rule, Computational Graphs & Backpropagation

If artificial intelligence has a single master equation, it is the Chain Rule. Backpropagation is a reverse-order evaluation of the multivariable chain rule on a computational graph.

🧒 1. The 12-Year-Old Analogy: Toy Clock Gears & Branching Water Pipes

Maya and Timmy build a giant machine with gears and water hoses:

  • The Clock Gears (Multiply along a line): Gear A turns Gear B at $3\times$ speed ($\frac{dy}{dx} = 3$). Gear B turns Gear C at $4\times$ speed ($\frac{dz}{dy} = 4$). Turning Gear A spins Gear C at $3 \times 4 = \mathbf{12\times}$ speed! Derivatives along a sequence multiply.
  • Branching Water Hoses (Add across parallel branches): Faucet $x$ splits into Hose 1 (gives 2 cups) and Hose 2 (gives 5 cups), both pouring into Bucket $z$. Opening the faucet adds $2 + 5 = \mathbf{7}$ extra cups! Derivatives along parallel paths add.
  • Backpropagation (Whispering Blame Backwards): When water spills at the end, Maya walks backwards along the pipes, whispering to each gear how much to turn to prevent the spill!
   Gear A (x)              Gear B (y)              Gear C (z)
    ╭─────╮                 ╭─────╮                 ╭─────╮
    │  1  │ ──(x3 speed)──▶ │  3  │ ──(x4 speed)──▶ │ 12  │
    ╰─────╯                 ╰─────╯                 ╰─────╯
             dy/dx = 3               dz/dy = 4
                       dz/dx = 3 * 4 = 12

3. The Multivariable Chain Rule

$$\underbrace{\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}}_{\text{Total effect}} = \sum_{k=1}^K \underbrace{\left(\frac{\partial z}{\partial u_k}\right)}_{\text{Path effect on output}} \times \underbrace{\left(\frac{\partial u_k}{\partial x}\right)}_{\text{Input effect on path}}$$

🧒 What the math is actually doing: We multiply gear ratios along each path, and sum across all parallel paths!

4. Forward-Mode vs Reverse-Mode: The Trillion-Dollar Difference

Why Backpropagation Won: An LLM has 70 Billion parameters and 1 scalar loss.
  • Forward Mode: Requires 70 Billion separate forward runs (takes centuries!).
  • Reverse Mode (Backprop): Requires 1 forward pass and 1 backward pass (takes milliseconds!).

5. Concrete Numerical Walkthrough

For $f(x, y, z) = (x + y) \cdot z$ at $x = 2, y = 3, z = 4$:

  • Forward Pass: $q = 2 + 3 = 5.0$;   $f = 5.0 \times 4.0 = \mathbf{20.0}$.
  • Backward Pass: $\bar{f} = 1.0 \implies \bar{z} = 1.0 \times 5.0 = \mathbf{5.0}$;   $\bar{q} = 1.0 \times 4.0 = \mathbf{4.0}$.
  • Inputs: $\bar{x} = 4.0 \times 1.0 = \mathbf{4.0}$;   $\bar{y} = 4.0 \times 1.0 = \mathbf{4.0}$.
