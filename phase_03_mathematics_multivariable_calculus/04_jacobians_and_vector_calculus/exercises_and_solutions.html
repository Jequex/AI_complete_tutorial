Problem 1: Softmax Jacobian Calculation & The Zero Column-Sum Invariant

Medium

Suppose a 3-class classification model outputs predicted probabilities:

$$\mathbf{p} = \begin{bmatrix} p_1 \\ p_2 \\ p_3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.50 \\ 0.30 \\ 0.20 \end{bmatrix}$$

Recall the Softmax Jacobian formula:

$$J_{ij} = \frac{\partial p_i}{\partial z_j} = p_i (\delta_{ij} - p_j) = \begin{cases} p_i(1 - p_i) & \text{if } i = j \\ -p_i p_j & \text{if } i \neq j \end{cases}$$
  1. Calculate all 9 numerical entries of the $3 \times 3$ Jacobian matrix $J$.
  2. Compute the sum of the entries in each column of $J$: $\sum_{i=1}^3 J_{ij}$ for $j \in \{1, 2, 3\}$.
  3. Provide the deep mathematical reason why every column of the Softmax Jacobian must sum to exactly zero.
Click to Reveal Step-by-Step Solution

1. Calculating Entries of the $3 \times 3$ Jacobian

Diagonal Elements ($i = j$):

  • $J_{11} = p_1(1 - p_1) = 0.50 \times 0.50 = \mathbf{0.25}$
  • $J_{22} = p_2(1 - p_2) = 0.30 \times 0.70 = \mathbf{0.21}$
  • $J_{33} = p_3(1 - p_3) = 0.20 \times 0.80 = \mathbf{0.16}$

Off-Diagonal Elements ($i \neq j$):

  • $J_{12} = J_{21} = -p_1 p_2 = -(0.50)(0.30) = \mathbf{-0.15}$
  • $J_{13} = J_{31} = -p_1 p_3 = -(0.50)(0.20) = \mathbf{-0.10}$
  • $J_{23} = J_{32} = -p_2 p_3 = -(0.30)(0.20) = \mathbf{-0.06}$

Writing the full matrix:

$$J = \begin{bmatrix} +0.25 & -0.15 & -0.10 \\ -0.15 & +0.21 & -0.06 \\ -0.10 & -0.06 & +0.16 \end{bmatrix}$$

2. Summing Each Column

  • Column 1: $+0.25 - 0.15 - 0.10 = \mathbf{0.00}$
  • Column 2: $-0.15 + 0.21 - 0.06 = \mathbf{0.00}$
  • Column 3: $-0.10 - 0.06 + 0.16 = \mathbf{0.00}$

3. Mathematical Proof

Because the sum of probabilities is identically constant for any input vector $\mathbf{z}$:

$$\sum_{i=1}^K p_i(\mathbf{z}) \equiv 1.0 \implies \frac{\partial}{\partial z_j}\left( \sum_{i=1}^K p_i \right) = \frac{\partial}{\partial z_j}(1.0) = 0 \implies \mathbf{\sum_{i=1}^K J_{ij} = 0} \quad \blacksquare$$
Physical Meaning: The Softmax layer is a closed conservation system. If you perturb logit $z_j$ to increase probability $p_j$, that extra probability mass must be borrowed from the other classes so the total probability stays strictly 100%!

Problem 2: Softmax + Cross-Entropy Step-by-Step Gradient Collapse

Medium

A classifier receives an input and predicts 3 class probabilities:

$$\mathbf{p} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.10 \\ 0.70 \\ 0.20 \end{bmatrix}$$

The ground-truth label is class 2 ($\mathbf{y} = [0, 1, 0]^T$). The Cross-Entropy Loss is $\mathcal{L} = -\sum_{k=1}^3 y_k \ln(p_k) = -\ln(p_2) \approx 0.3567$.

  1. Compute the loss gradient with respect to probabilities: $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{p}}$.
  2. Using the multivariable chain rule $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial z_j} = \sum_{i=1}^3 \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial p_i} J_{ij}$, calculate each entry of $\nabla_{\mathbf{z}} \mathcal{L}$.
  3. Verify that the result equals $\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{y}$.
Click to Reveal Step-by-Step Solution

1. Upstream Gradient $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{p}}$

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial p_1} = 0, \quad \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial p_2} = -\frac{1}{0.70} \approx -1.42857, \quad \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial p_3} = 0$$

2. Step-by-Step Chain Rule

Only $i = 2$ contributes since $y_1 = y_3 = 0$:

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial z_j} = \left(-\frac{1}{p_2}\right) J_{2j}$$
  • $j = 1$: $\left(-\frac{1}{p_2}\right)(-p_2 p_1) = p_1 = \mathbf{+0.10}$
  • $j = 2$: $\left(-\frac{1}{p_2}\right)[p_2(1 - p_2)] = p_2 - 1 = 0.70 - 1 = \mathbf{-0.30}$
  • $j = 3$: $\left(-\frac{1}{p_2}\right)(-p_2 p_3) = p_3 = \mathbf{+0.20}$

3. Direct Check with $\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{y}$

$$\nabla_{\mathbf{z}} \mathcal{L} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.10 \\ 0.70 \\ 0.20 \end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} +0.10 \\ -0.30 \\ +0.20 \end{bmatrix} \quad \blacksquare$$
Interpretation: The correct class (index 2) receives a negative gradient $-0.30$ (pushing its logit higher), while incorrect classes receive positive gradients $+0.10$ and $+0.20$ (suppressing them).

Problem 3: 2D Coordinate Transformation Jacobian & Determinant in Generative Flows

Challenging

In Normalizing Flows (e.g. RealNVP, Glow), invertible mappings $\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{g}(\mathbf{z})$ transform simple Gaussian noise into complex data distributions. Evaluating densities requires the Jacobian determinant $|\det(J)|$.

Consider the polar-to-Cartesian coordinate mapping:

$$\mathbf{g}(r, \theta) = \begin{bmatrix} x(r, \theta) \\ y(r, \theta) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} r \cos \theta \\ r \sin \theta \end{bmatrix}$$
  1. Compute the $2 \times 2$ Jacobian matrix $J = \frac{\partial(x, y)}{\partial(r, \theta)}$.
  2. Compute the determinant $\det(J)$ and simplify using $\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta = 1$.
  3. Explain the physical/geometric meaning of $\det(J) = r$ for volume scaling in Generative AI.
Click to Reveal Step-by-Step Solution

1. Computing the Jacobian Matrix

$$J = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{\partial x}{\partial r} & \frac{\partial x}{\partial \theta} \\ \frac{\partial y}{\partial r} & \frac{\partial y}{\partial \theta} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta & -r \sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & r \cos\theta \end{bmatrix}$$

2. Computing the Determinant

$$\det(J) = (\cos\theta)(r \cos\theta) - (-r \sin\theta)(\sin\theta) = r \cos^2\theta + r \sin^2\theta = r(\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta) = \mathbf{r} \quad \blacksquare$$

3. Geometric Meaning in Generative AI

Local Volume Factor: The Jacobian determinant $\det(J)$ measures the ratio of output area to input area. A tiny box of dimensions $dr \times d\theta$ in polar space maps to a physical Cartesian area of: $$dA = |\det(J)| \, dr \, d\theta = \mathbf{r \, dr \, d\theta}$$ When a neural network stretches latent space ($|\det(J)| > 1$), probability density dilutes. When it compresses space ($|\det(J)| < 1$), probability density concentrates.
