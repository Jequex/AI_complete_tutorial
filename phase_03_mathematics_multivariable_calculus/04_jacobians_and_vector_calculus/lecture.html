Topic 3.4: Jacobians & Vector Calculus

Neural network hidden layers, normalization layers, and final Softmax heads are vector-valued mappings: $\mathbf{f}: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^m$. The Jacobian Matrix is the complete grid of first-order partial derivatives connecting every input to every output.

🧒 1. The 12-Year-Old Analogy: Maya's Music Mixing Console

Maya is sitting at an audio mixing console with 3 sliders controlling 3 studio speakers:

  • The Sliders: Bass ($x_1$), Treble ($x_2$), and Reverb ($x_3$).
  • The Speakers: Front-Left ($f_1$), Front-Right ($f_2$), and Subwoofer ($f_3$).
  • The Sensitivity Spreadsheet: Nudging the Bass slider changes the Subwoofer strongly, Front-Left slightly, and Front-Right not at all. Timmy creates a grid with 3 rows (Speakers) and 3 columns (Sliders) tracking every sensitivity number: entry $(i, j) = \frac{\partial f_i}{\partial x_j}$. That grid is the Jacobian Matrix!
        Inputs: 3 Sliders (x)          Outputs: 3 Speakers (f(x))
        [Slider 1: Bass]    ───┐         ╭──▶ [Speaker 1: Front Left]
                               ├──(J)───┼──▶ [Speaker 2: Front Right]
        [Slider 2: Treble]  ───┤         ╰──▶ [Speaker 3: Subwoofer]
        [Slider 3: Reverb]  ───┘

📋 2. Mathematical Symbol Decoder Table

Symbol Pronunciation Plain English Meaning Concrete Real-World Analogy / Flashcard Example
$\mathbf{f}: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^m$ "f from R-n to R-m" Vector-valued function transforming $n$ inputs into $m$ outputs Audio console converting $n$ sliders into $m$ speaker outputs
$J \text{ or } \frac{\partial \mathbf{f}}{\partial \mathbf{x}}$ "Jacobian of f" $m \times n$ matrix of all first-order partial derivatives A master grid tracking how every slider affects every speaker
$J_{ij}$ "J i j" Entry $(i, j)$: sensitivity $\frac{\partial f_i}{\partial x_j}$ Volume change in Speaker $i$ when Slider $j$ is nudged
$\delta_{ij}$ "Kronecker delta" Equals $1$ if $i = j$, and $0$ if $i \neq j$ A selector switch: ON (1) for yourself, OFF (0) for rivals
$\mathbf{v}^T J$ "Vector-Jacobian Product (VJP)" Reverse-mode gradient product computed in $\mathcal{O}(n)$ time Whispering total blame backwards without forming huge matrices
$\det(J)$ "determinant of J" Volume stretching factor of the coordinate transformation How many times bigger a ball of dough becomes when rolled flat

3. The Softmax Jacobian Master Formula

$$\mathbf{J_{ij} = \frac{\partial p_i}{\partial z_j} = p_i (\delta_{ij} - p_j)}$$ $$\mathbf{J_{\text{softmax}} = \operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{p}) - \mathbf{p} \mathbf{p}^T}$$

🧒 What the math is actually doing: Diagonal entries are $p_i(1 - p_i)$ (self-sensitivity), while off-diagonal entries are $-p_i p_j$ (cross-competition). Every column sums to $0$ because total probability is conserved at $1.0$!

4. Softmax + Cross-Entropy: The Miracle Collapse

$$\mathbf{\nabla_{\mathbf{z}} \mathcal{L} = \mathbf{p} - \mathbf{y}}$$
The Beauty of Deep Learning: All fractions, exponentials, and matrix products cancel out completely. The gradient is simply: $$\text{Error} = \text{Model's Probability Guess } (\mathbf{p}) - \text{True One-Hot Label } (\mathbf{y})$$

5. Vector-Jacobian Products (VJP) in Autodiff

For an LLM vocabulary size $V = 128{,}000$, a single token's full Jacobian matrix is $128{,}000 \times 128{,}000 \approx 65 \text{ GB}$ of VRAM! PyTorch avoids materializing this matrix by evaluating the VJP directly:

$$\mathbf{v}^T J = (\mathbf{v} \odot \mathbf{p}) - (\mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{p}) \mathbf{p} \quad [\mathcal{O}(K) \text{ memory!}]$$
