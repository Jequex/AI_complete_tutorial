Problem 1: Gradient Derivation of Linear Regression MSE Loss

Medium

For model $\hat{y}_i = w x_i + b$ and loss $\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{N}\sum (w x_i + b - y_i)^2$:

  1. Derive $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial w}$ and $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial b}$.
  2. For a sample $(x = 2, y = 5)$ with current $w = 1, b = 0$, compute the exact gradient vector.
Click to Reveal Step-by-Step Solution
1. Partial Derivatives: $$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial w} = \frac{2}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N (w x_i + b - y_i) x_i, \quad \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial b} = \frac{2}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N (w x_i + b - y_i)$$
2. Numerical Evaluation: Residual $e = (1)(2) + 0 - 5 = -3$. $$\nabla \mathcal{L} = \begin{bmatrix} 2(-3)(2) \\ 2(-3) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -12.0 \\ -6.0 \end{bmatrix}$$

Problem 2: Directional Derivatives & Orthogonal Contours

Medium

For surface $f(x, y) = 2x^2 + y^2$ at point $(1, 2)$:

  1. Find gradient $\nabla f(1, 2)$.
  2. Compute the slope along direction $\mathbf{v} = [3, 4]^T$.
  3. Find a unit vector along which altitude does not change ($D_{\mathbf{u}} f = 0$).
Click to Reveal Step-by-Step Solution
1. Gradient: $$\nabla f(1, 2) = \begin{bmatrix} 4(1) \\ 2(2) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 4 \\ 4 \end{bmatrix}$$
2. Directional Derivative: Unit direction: $\mathbf{u} = [0.6, 0.8]^T$. $$D_{\mathbf{u}} f = 4(0.6) + 4(0.8) = \mathbf{5.6}$$
3. Zero Slope Contour Direction: $$\mathbf{u}_{\text{flat}} = \begin{bmatrix} 1/\sqrt{2} \\ -1/\sqrt{2} \end{bmatrix} \implies \nabla f \cdot \mathbf{u}_{\text{flat}} = \frac{4}{\sqrt{2}} - \frac{4}{\sqrt{2}} = 0$$

Problem 3: Manual Gradient Descent Steps on an Elliptical Bowl

Challenging

Optimize $f(x, y) = x^2 + 4y^2$ starting at $\mathbf{w}_0 = [4, 2]^T$ with learning rate $\eta = 0.1$. Compute two full iterations ($\mathbf{w}_1, \mathbf{w}_2$) and verify strict loss decrease.

Click to Reveal Step-by-Step Solution
Update Equations: $$x_{t+1} = x_t - 0.1(2x_t) = 0.8 x_t, \quad y_{t+1} = y_t - 0.1(8y_t) = 0.2 y_t$$
Calculations:
  • $\mathbf{w}_0 = [4.0, 2.0]^T \implies f(\mathbf{w}_0) = 4^2 + 4(4) = \mathbf{32.0}$
  • $\mathbf{w}_1 = [0.8(4), 0.2(2)]^T = [3.2, 0.4]^T \implies f(\mathbf{w}_1) = 10.24 + 4(0.16) = \mathbf{10.88}$
  • $\mathbf{w}_2 = [0.8(3.2), 0.2(0.4)]^T = [2.56, 0.08]^T \implies f(\mathbf{w}_2) = 6.5536 + 4(0.0064) = \mathbf{6.5792}$
