Topic 3.2: Partial Derivatives, Gradients & Steepest Descent

In deep learning, neural networks optimize loss functions depending on millions or billions of parameters: $\mathcal{L}(w_1, \dots, w_D)$. We navigate this high-dimensional terrain using Partial Derivatives and the Gradient Vector $\nabla f$.

🧒 1. The 12-Year-Old Analogy: Maya Blindfolded on Foggy Mount Mist

Maya is trapped in thick fog on Mount Mist. She must reach the cozy cabin in the valley:

  • Feeling with Boots (Partial Derivatives): She taps her boot strictly East-West ($\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}$) and strictly North-South ($\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}$) to feel the slope along each individual axis.
  • The Magic Compass (The Gradient $\nabla f$): Combining the two boot taps gives $\nabla f = [\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}, \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}]^T$, an arrow pointing straight uphill toward the mountain summit!
  • Steepest Descent ($-\nabla f$): To reach the warm cabin, Maya steps in the exact opposite direction: $-\nabla f$. Repeating this step brings her directly to the minimum!

3. The "Freeze" Rule for Partial Derivatives

To differentiate with respect to one variable, treat all other variables as static frozen constants!

Example: $f(x, y) = 3x^2 y + 5y^3 - 4x$

  • $\frac{\partial f}{\partial x} = 3y(2x) + 0 - 4 = \mathbf{6xy - 4}$
  • $\frac{\partial f}{\partial y} = 3x^2(1) + 15y^2 - 0 = \mathbf{3x^2 + 15y^2}$

At $(x = 2, y = 1)$: $\frac{\partial f}{\partial x} = 6(2)(1) - 4 = 8$; $\frac{\partial f}{\partial y} = 3(4) + 15(1) = 27$.

4. Directional Derivatives & Proof of Steepest Ascent

$$D_{\mathbf{u}} f = \nabla f \cdot \mathbf{u} = \|\nabla f\| \underbrace{\|\mathbf{u}\|}_{= 1} \cos\theta = \|\nabla f\| \cos\theta$$
  • Steepest Ascent ($\theta = 0^\circ$): $\cos(0^\circ) = +1 \implies$ Points along $+\nabla f$ with slope $+\|\nabla f\|$.
  • Steepest Descent ($\theta = 180^\circ$): $\cos(180^\circ) = -1 \implies$ Points along $-\nabla f$ with slope $-\|\nabla f\|$.
  • Contour Tangent ($\theta = 90^\circ$): $\cos(90^\circ) = 0 \implies$ Flat walking contour line!

5. Gradient Descent: The Master Equation

$$\underbrace{\mathbf{w}_{t+1}}_{\text{Next position}} = \underbrace{\mathbf{w}_t}_{\text{Current position}} - \underbrace{\eta}_{\text{Learning rate}} \cdot \underbrace{\nabla \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{w}_t)}_{\text{Uphill vector}}$$
Concrete Step: For $f(x, y) = x^2 + 3y^2$ at $[3.0, 2.0]^T$ with $\eta = 0.1$, $\nabla f = [6.0, 12.0]^T$. The updated position is $[3.0 - 0.6, 2.0 - 1.2]^T = [2.4, 0.8]^T$, dropping loss from $21.0$ down to $7.68$!
