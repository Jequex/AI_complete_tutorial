Exercises & Solutions: Multimodal Alignment

Quantitative problem sets covering InfoNCE attractive-repulsive gradient dynamics, SigLIP distributed communication scaling, and the modality gap geometry.

Exercise 1: Analytical Gradients of the InfoNCE Loss

Consider the Image-to-Text contrastive loss $\mathcal{L}_{v2t} = -\frac{1}{B}\sum_{i=1}^B \ln P_{ii}$ on normalized unit vectors $\hat{\mathbf{z}}_{v, i}$ and $\hat{\mathbf{z}}_{t, j}$.

  1. Derive the partial gradient $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{v2t}}{\partial \hat{\mathbf{z}}_{v, i}}$.
  2. Interpret the update as a balance between attractive and repulsive geometric forces.
View Complete Step-by-Step Solution

1. Gradient Derivation

Differentiating the loss with respect to $\hat{\mathbf{z}}_{v, i}$:

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{v2t}}{\partial \hat{\mathbf{z}}_{v, i}} = \frac{1}{B \tau} \left( -(1 - P_{ii}) \hat{\mathbf{z}}_{t, i} + \sum_{j \neq i} P_{ij} \hat{\mathbf{z}}_{t, j} \right)$$

2. Attractive vs. Repulsive Dynamics

The gradient descent direction $-\nabla \mathcal{L}$ applies an attractive pull $(1 - P_{ii})\hat{\mathbf{z}}_{t, i}$ directly toward the true caption, and a repulsive push $-\sum_{j \neq i} P_{ij}\hat{\mathbf{z}}_{t, j}$ driving the vision embedding away from all negative distractors proportional to their misclassified similarity.

Exercise 2: SigLIP vs. CLIP Distributed Communication Scaling

A distributed cluster of $K = 128$ GPUs trains with global batch size $B = 32,768$ and embedding dimension $d = 768$ (FP32).

  1. Calculate the communication footprint of the All-Gather operation required by standard CLIP.
  2. Explain how SigLIP eliminates the cross-node synchronization barrier using asynchronous ring chunking.
View Complete Step-by-Step Solution

1. CLIP All-Gather Memory

$$32,768 \times 768 \times 4 \text{ bytes} \approx 100.66 \text{ MB per GPU per step}$$

Every GPU must synchronously gather $\approx 100 \text{ MB}$ of text embeddings before computing the softmax denominator, stalling execution on slower interconnects.

2. SigLIP Asynchronous Pipeline

Because SigLIP computes pairwise binary logistic losses $\sigma(z_v^\top z_t)$ without a global partition function, nodes only exchange small $b = 256$ chunk buffers ($\approx 786 \text{ KB}$) in a non-blocking ring, completely removing the communication bottleneck.

Exercise 3: The Modality Gap Geometry & The Cone Hypothesis

Explain why image and text representations in trained CLIP models occupy distinct non-overlapping cones $\mathcal{C}_v$ and $\mathcal{C}_t$, and why this does not hurt zero-shot classification accuracy.

View Complete Step-by-Step Solution

1. Origin of the Modality Gap

Caused by inductive bias differences between ViT and Text Transformers at initialization, compounded by temperature $\tau \ll 1$. Because contrastive loss only demands relative pairwise ranking within each batch, gradients vanish once positive pairs are marginally closer than distractors, leaving the modality centroid gap intact.

2. Invariance of Zero-Shot Ranking

Zero-shot classification computes $\arg\max_k (\hat{\mathbf{z}}_v^\top \hat{\mathbf{z}}_{t, k})$. Since the modality gap affects all candidates with a nearly uniform geometric bias, the argmax relative rank order is completely preserved, achieving $>95\%$ zero-shot accuracy.
