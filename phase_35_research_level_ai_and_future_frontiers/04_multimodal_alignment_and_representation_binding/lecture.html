TOPIC 35.4

Multimodal Alignment & Representation Binding

Binding sensory pixels and textual semantics onto shared manifolds: CLIP dual-encoders, InfoNCE mutual information maximization, SigLIP, and zero-shot transfer.

1. Introduction & 12-Year-Old Intuition

Imagine you have two friends: one friend speaks only in photographs, while the other is a radio announcer who has never seen an image and communicates purely through words.

To let them converse, you build a Universal Rosetta Stone Map: when the photographer shows a picture of a golden retriever, you place a pin at coordinate $(X=14, Y=42)$. When the announcer says "A cheerful puppy chasing a ball", you guide them to place their pin at the exact same point $(X=14, Y=42)$.

Even though pixels and text tokens are fundamentally different signals, they point to the exact same concept in reality. Models like CLIP and SigLIP project vision and language onto a shared unit sphere, enabling instant Zero-Shot image classification and cross-modal retrieval without supervised labels.

3. The Symmetric InfoNCE Loss

The CLIP dual-encoder optimizes the symmetric cross-entropy loss across all pairs in a batch:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{CLIP}} = -\frac{1}{2B} \sum_{i=1}^B \left[ \ln \left( \frac{\exp(S_{ii})}{\sum_{j=1}^B \exp(S_{ij})} \right) + \ln \left( \frac{\exp(S_{ii})}{\sum_{j=1}^B \exp(S_{ji})} \right) \right]$$

4. Theoretical Foundation: Mutual Information Lower Bound

InfoNCE optimization directly maximizes a lower bound on the mutual information between images and text:

$$I(V; T) \ge \ln(B) - \mathcal{L}_{\text{InfoNCE}}$$

As the batch size $B$ grows, the mathematical bound becomes tighter, explaining why large batch scaling ($B = 32,768$) is essential for high-quality representation binding.

5. SigLIP: Decoupled Pairwise Logistic Loss

While standard CLIP requires calculating a global denominator sum across all GPUs, SigLIP decomposes the task into independent binary logistic classifications:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{SigLIP}} = -\sum_{i=1}^B \sum_{j=1}^B \left[ y_{ij} \ln \sigma(t \cdot \hat{\mathbf{z}}_{v, i}^\top \hat{\mathbf{z}}_{t, j} + b) + (1 - y_{ij}) \ln(1 - \sigma(t \cdot \hat{\mathbf{z}}_{v, i}^\top \hat{\mathbf{z}}_{t, j} + b)) \right]$$

This eliminates the cross-device all-gather communication barrier, enabling massive scaling to arbitrarily large distributed clusters.
