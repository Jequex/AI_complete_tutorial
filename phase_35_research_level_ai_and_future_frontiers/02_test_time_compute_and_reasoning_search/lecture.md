# Topic 02: Test-Time Compute & Reasoning Search

## 1. Introduction & 12-Year-Old Intuition

Imagine you are playing a championship game of chess. 

A beginner looks at the board and instantly moves whatever piece catches their eye in 2 seconds. They might see a free pawn, grab it greedily, and walk directly into checkmate 3 moves later.

A Grandmaster does something completely different:
1. They look at 3 candidate moves.
2. In their head, they trace move 1: *"If I move my knight here, what will my opponent do? They move their bishop, then I check with my rook... wait, my king is exposed. That's a trap. Roll back."*
3. They evaluate move 2: *"What if I push this pawn? The opponent defends, but my queen gains open space. That looks promising."*
4. They spend 5 full minutes thinking, testing, rejecting bad paths, and searching deeply before their hand touches a physical piece.

Traditional Large Language Models (like GPT-4) acted like the beginner: each token was generated with a fixed number of neural network layers in a fraction of a millisecond. If a hard math problem required 10 steps of careful deduction, greedy next-token prediction often leaped into an unrecoverable algebraic trap.

**Test-Time Compute (Reasoning Search)** changes the paradigm (exemplified by OpenAI o1 and o3). Instead of scaling only the *pretraining compute* (training larger models on more internet text), we scale the **compute spent during inference**. The model uses search algorithms—such as Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) and Beam Search—paired with a **Process Reward Model (PRM)** to explore, verify each intermediate thought, catch hallucinations, and backtrack from dead ends.

```
                  [ Root Question ]
                          │
         ┌────────────────┴────────────────┐
         ▼                                 ▼
   Step 1 (Candidate A)              Step 1 (Candidate B)
   PRM Score: 0.98                   PRM Score: 0.12 (Error)
         │                                 ▼
         ▼                          [ BACKTRACK / PRUNE ]
   Step 2 (Candidate A1)
   PRM Score: 0.95
         │
         ▼
   Step 3 (Terminal Verification: ORM = 1.0)
   [ Verified Chain of Thought ]
```

## 2. Mathematical Symbol Decoder

| Symbol | Mathematical Entity | Dimensionality / Domain | Operational Meaning & Research Role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\mathcal{S}$ | State Space of Reasoning | Sequence of Tokens / Steps | Partial Chain-of-Thought reasoning trajectory $s_t = (q, z_1, \dots, z_t)$. |
| $\mathcal{A}$ | Action Space / Step Space | Token sequence / Step sentence | Next deduction step $a_t = z_{t+1}$ generated by policy model $P_\theta$. |
| $P_\theta(a \mid s)$ | Policy Prior Model | Probability Distribution | LLM generation prior over the next candidate reasoning step. |
| $R_{\text{PRM}}(s, a)$ | Process Reward Model (PRM) | Scalar $\in [0, 1]$ | Verifier probability that step $a$ represents a logically sound and valid deduction. |
| $R_{\text{ORM}}(s_T)$ | Outcome Reward Model (ORM) | Scalar $\in \{0, 1\}$ or $[0, 1]$ | Verifier assessing only the final answer correctness at terminal step $T$. |
| $N(s)$ | State Visit Count | Integer $\ge 0$ | Total number of MCTS search simulations that have passed through node $s$. |
| $N(s, a)$ | Edge Visit Count | Integer $\ge 0$ | Number of times action $a$ was selected from node $s$. |
| $W(s, a)$ | Total Accumulated Value | Real scalar $\ge 0$ | Sum of terminal rewards backpropagated through edge $(s, a)$. |
| $Q(s, a)$ | Mean Action Value | Real scalar $\in [0, 1]$ | $Q(s, a) = \frac{W(s, a)}{N(s, a)}$; empirical estimate of reaching a correct solution. |
| $U(s, a)$ | PUCT Exploration Bonus | Real scalar $\ge 0$ | Upper confidence bound balancing exploration of high-prior, low-visit steps. |
| $c_{\text{puct}}$ | Exploration Constant | Positive real scalar | Hyperparameter governing the trade-off between exploitation ($Q$) and exploration ($U$). |
| $N_{\text{sim}}$ | Test-Time Compute Budget | Integer $\ge 1$ | Number of MCTS simulation rollouts allocated per problem query. |

## 3. Test-Time Compute Scaling Laws

For years, AI scaling was governed by the Kaplan and Chinchilla pretraining laws:

$$\mathcal{L}(N, D) \approx \left(\frac{N_c}{N}\right)^{\alpha_N} + \left(\frac{D_c}{D}\right)^{\alpha_D}$$

where $N$ is parameter count and $D$ is pretraining dataset tokens.

In 2024, research from OpenAI (o1/o3) and Snell et al. established the **Inference-Time Scaling Law**:

$$\text{Error}(C_{\text{test}}) \approx \beta \cdot C_{\text{test}}^{-\alpha_{\text{search}}}$$

where $C_{\text{test}} = N_{\text{sim}} \cdot \text{FLOPs}_{\text{step}}$ is the compute allocated at inference time.

## 4. PRMs vs. ORMs: The Credit Assignment Problem

Consider a 4-step mathematical proof where Step 1 is correct, Step 2 is flawed (hallucinating $2 + 2 = 5$), and Steps 3 and 4 carry out logical deductions from the false premise:

```
Step 1: 3x + 6 = 21         (Valid)
Step 2: 3x = 21 + 6 = 27    (INVALID - signs inverted!)
Step 3: x = 27 / 3          (Valid arithmetic from premise)
Step 4: x = 9               (Final Answer: Wrong)
```

### The Failure of Outcome Reward Models (ORMs):
An ORM only checks the final answer: $x = 9$ vs. Ground Truth $x = 5 \implies \text{Reward} = 0$.
- **Credit Assignment Blunder**: The ORM provides a single scalar 0 to the entire sequence. It cannot tell the model *where* the error happened.
- In reinforcement learning (RLHF with ORM), this induces massive gradient variance, penalizing Step 1 and Step 3 just as harshly as the buggy Step 2.
- In Best-of-$N$ sampling, an ORM can accidentally reward **false positives**: reasoning traces with nonsensical internal math that miraculously stumble onto the correct numeric answer by cancelling mistakes.

### Process Reward Models (PRMs) to the Rescue:
A PRM evaluates every intermediate transition:

$$r_{\text{PRM}}(s_t, z_{t+1}) = P(\text{Step } z_{t+1} \text{ is sound} \mid \text{Context } s_t)$$

- Step 1: $r_1 = 0.99$ (Valid)
- Step 2: $r_2 = 0.01$ (Blocked! Immediate rollback triggered)
- Computational search prunes this entire subtree before wasting tokens on Steps 3 and 4!

## 5. MCTS with PUCT: Formulation & Guarantees

In tree search over thought steps, each node represents a partial reasoning state $s$, and each branch represents an intermediate reasoning step $a$.

### Selection with PUCT (Polynomial Upper Confidence Trees):
At state $s$, the search engine selects the action maximizing:

$$a^* = \arg\max_{a \in \mathcal{A}(s)} \left[ Q(s, a) + U(s, a) \right]$$

where:

$$U(s, a) = c_{\text{puct}} \cdot P(s, a) \cdot \frac{\sqrt{N(s)}}{1 + N(s, a)}$$

- $Q(s, a) = \frac{W(s, a)}{N(s, a)}$ drives **exploitation** of branches known to reach correct proofs.
- $P(s, a)$ is the prior probability assigned by the PRM / policy model.
- $\frac{\sqrt{N(s)}}{1 + N(s, a)}$ drives **exploration**: as parent visits $N(s)$ grow, unexplored actions ($N(s, a) = 0$) get an exploration boost, ensuring the search never permanently ignores a candidate thought.

### Backpropagation:
When a rollout terminates with outcome reward $V \in \{0, 1\}$, all nodes along the trajectory path $\mathcal{P} = (s_0, s_1, \dots, s_T)$ are updated:

$$N(s) \leftarrow N(s) + 1, \quad W(s, a) \leftarrow W(s, a) + V, \quad Q(s, a) \leftarrow \frac{W(s, a)}{N(s, a)}$$

### Mathematical Guarantee of Asymptotic Optimality:
Under bounded rewards and standard finite-action branching, as simulation count $N_{\text{sim}} \to \infty$:

$$\lim_{N_{\text{sim}} \to \infty} Q(s, a) = Q^*(s, a)$$

The probability of selecting a suboptimal root step decays exponentially:

$$P(\text{select suboptimal } a) \le \mathcal{O}\left( \exp(-\gamma N_{\text{sim}}) \right)$$
