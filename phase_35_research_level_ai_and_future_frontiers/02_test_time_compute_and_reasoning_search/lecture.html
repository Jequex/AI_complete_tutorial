TOPIC 35.2

Test-Time Compute & Reasoning Search

Scaling inference compute with Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS), Process Reward Models (PRMs), back-verification rollbacks, and the o1/o3 reasoning paradigm.

1. Introduction & 12-Year-Old Intuition

Imagine you are playing a championship game of chess.

A beginner looks at the board and instantly moves whatever piece catches their eye in 2 seconds. They grab a free pawn and walk right into checkmate 3 moves later.

A Grandmaster does something completely different: they look at 3 candidate moves, mentally simulate every opponent response, detect traps, backtrack from dangerous paths, and only make their move after searching deeply.

Traditional LLMs were like the beginner—generating tokens one after another with no chance to erase or rethink. Test-Time Compute (exemplified by OpenAI o1 and o3) equips AI with a pencil and eraser: it uses Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) and a Process Reward Model (PRM) to verify each intermediate thought step, prune hallucinations, and search for guaranteed valid proofs.

3. The Test-Time Compute Scaling Law

Recent breakthroughs show that scaling test-time search compute obeys a power-law error reduction:

$$\text{Error}(N_{\text{sim}}) \approx \beta \cdot N_{\text{sim}}^{-\alpha_{\text{search}}}$$

Allocating compute at inference time allows smaller models to discover complex multi-step reasoning trajectories that larger autoregressive models miss completely during a single greedy forward pass.

4. PRMs vs. ORMs: Resolving the Credit Assignment Dilemma

An Outcome Reward Model (ORM) checks only the final answer. If an early step hallucinates a false identity, the ORM still evaluates the terminal output as 0, giving no feedback on where the error occurred. Worse, an ORM can reward false positives—reasoning traces containing erroneous logic that accidentally stumble upon the correct number.

A Process Reward Model (PRM) evaluates each individual deduction step:

$$r_{\text{PRM}}(s_t, a_t) = P(\text{Step } a_t \text{ is sound} \mid s_t)$$

This enables instantaneous branch pruning and back-verification rollbacks, preventing the search tree from exploding exponentially along invalid deduction paths.

5. PUCT Selection & Backpropagation

During MCTS, tree traversal at state $s$ selects the branch maximizing:

$$a^* = \arg\max_{a \in \mathcal{A}(s)} \left[ Q(s, a) + c_{\text{puct}} \cdot P(s, a) \cdot \frac{\sqrt{N(s)}}{1 + N(s, a)} \right]$$

When a terminal node is evaluated by the ORM with reward $V \in \{0, 1\}$, values and visit counts are backpropagated up the ancestor chain:

$$N(s) \leftarrow N(s) + 1, \quad W(s, a) \leftarrow W(s, a) + V, \quad Q(s, a) \leftarrow \frac{W(s, a)}{N(s, a)}$$
