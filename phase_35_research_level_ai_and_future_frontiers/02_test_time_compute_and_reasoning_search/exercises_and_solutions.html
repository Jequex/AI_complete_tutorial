Exercise 1: Combinatorial Pruning: PRM vs. ORM Best-of-$N$ Search

Consider a mathematical deduction problem of depth $T = 5$, branching factor $b = 4$, and step validity probability $p = 0.7$.

  1. Calculate the total leaves $N_{\text{leaves}}$ and the probability $P_{\text{correct}}$ that a random full trajectory is sound.
  2. Find the minimum sample count $N$ for an ORM Best-of-$N$ search to achieve $99\%$ success probability.
  3. Compute the expected candidate nodes evaluated under a step-level Process Reward Model (PRM).
View Complete Step-by-Step Solution

1. Full Tree Statistics

Total leaves: $4^5 = 1,024$. Probability of complete validity: $P_{\text{correct}} = 0.7^5 = 0.16807 \approx 16.8\%$.

2. ORM Best-of-$N$ Sample Requirement

$$1 - (1 - 0.16807)^N \ge 0.99 \implies N \ge \frac{\ln(0.01)}{\ln(0.83193)} \approx 25.03$$

The ORM requires $N = 26$ full rollouts ($130$ total generated steps).

3. PRM Step-Level Pruning

At each level $k$, $b \cdot p = 2.8$ survivors branch forward. The total nodes evaluated across levels 1 through 5 is:

$$\text{Nodes}_{\text{PRM}} = \sum_{k=1}^5 4 \times (2.8)^{k-1} = 4 \times \frac{2.8^5 - 1}{1.8} \approx 380 \text{ candidates}$$

Unlike ORM sampling which often samples failed variants of the same faulty first step, 100% of surviving PRM paths at depth 5 yield valid proofs.

Exercise 2: PUCT Upper Confidence Bound & Asymptotic Exploration

In PUCT selection: $$a^* = \arg\max_{a \in \mathcal{A}} \left[ Q(s, a) + c_{\text{puct}} \cdot P(s, a) \cdot \frac{\sqrt{N(s)}}{1 + N(s, a)} \right]$$ Prove that an unvisited step $a_u$ ($N(s, a_u) = 0$) will eventually be selected even if another action $a_b$ has $Q(s, a_b) = 1.0$.

View Complete Step-by-Step Solution

1. Threshold Derivation

For $a_u$, $\text{Score}(a_u) = c_{\text{puct}} P(s, a_u) \sqrt{N(s)}$. For $a_b$, $\text{Score}(a_b) \le 1.0 + c_{\text{puct}} P(s, a_b) \frac{\sqrt{N(s)}}{1 + N(s, a_b)}$.

As $N(s) \to \infty$ with $a_b$ repeatedly chosen, $N(s, a_b) \approx N(s)$, so its exploration term decays as $1/\sqrt{N(s)} \to 0$. Thus:

$$c_{\text{puct}} P(s, a_u) \sqrt{N(s)} > 1.0 \implies N(s) > \left( \frac{1}{c_{\text{puct}} P(s, a_u)} \right)^2$$

2. Significance

Guarantees that deceptive local traps cannot permanently starve alternative reasoning paths from being evaluated, ensuring counter-intuitive valid proofs are discovered.
