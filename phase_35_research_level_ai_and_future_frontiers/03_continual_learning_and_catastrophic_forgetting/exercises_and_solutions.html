Exercise 1: Equivalence of Expected Hessian and Fisher Information Matrix

Consider a model likelihood $p_\theta(y \mid x)$ and negative log-likelihood $\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = -\mathbb{E}[\ln p_\theta(y \mid x)]$.

  1. Prove that under model expectation: $\mathbb{E}[-\nabla_\theta^2 \ln p_\theta(y \mid x)] = \mathbb{E}[\nabla_\theta \ln p_\theta(y \mid x) \nabla_\theta \ln p_\theta(y \mid x)^\top]$.
  2. Explain why the empirical Fisher $\hat{\mathbf{F}}$ is guaranteed to be positive semi-definite everywhere.
View Complete Step-by-Step Solution

1. Step-by-Step Proof

Differentiating the identity $\int p_\theta(y \mid x) \, dy = 1$ gives $\int p_\theta(y \mid x) \nabla_\theta \ln p_\theta(y \mid x) \, dy = \mathbf{0}$.

Differentiating a second time using the product rule:

$$\int \left[ \nabla_\theta p_\theta(y \mid x) \nabla_\theta \ln p_\theta(y \mid x)^\top + p_\theta(y \mid x) \nabla_\theta^2 \ln p_\theta(y \mid x) \right] dy = \mathbf{0}$$

Substituting $\nabla_\theta p_\theta = p_\theta \nabla_\theta \ln p_\theta$ and taking expectations yields:

$$\mathbb{E}[-\nabla_\theta^2 \ln p_\theta(y \mid x)] = \mathbb{E}[\nabla_\theta \ln p_\theta(y \mid x) \nabla_\theta \ln p_\theta(y \mid x)^\top] = \mathbf{F}_x$$

2. Positive Semi-Definiteness

For any $\mathbf{v} \neq \mathbf{0}$, $\mathbf{v}^\top \hat{\mathbf{F}} \mathbf{v} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_i (\mathbf{v}^\top \mathbf{g}_i)^2 \ge 0$, ensuring the quadratic EWC penalty is strictly convex with a well-defined global minimum.

Exercise 2: Analytical Equilibrium Point Under Quadratic Loss Surfaces

Let $\mathcal{L}_A(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = \frac{1}{2}(\boldsymbol{\theta} - \boldsymbol{\theta}_A^*)^\top \mathbf{F}_A (\boldsymbol{\theta} - \boldsymbol{\theta}_A^*)$ and $\mathcal{L}_B(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = \frac{1}{2}(\boldsymbol{\theta} - \boldsymbol{\theta}_B^*)^\top \mathbf{F}_B (\boldsymbol{\theta} - \boldsymbol{\theta}_B^*)$.

  1. Derive the closed-form equilibrium $\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{EWC}}^*$ under the objective $\mathcal{L}_{\text{EWC}} = \mathcal{L}_B + \lambda \mathcal{L}_A$.
  2. Evaluate the asymptotic limits as $\lambda \to 0$ and $\lambda \to \infty$.
View Complete Step-by-Step Solution

1. Closed-Form Equilibrium

$$\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_{\text{EWC}} = \mathbf{F}_B (\boldsymbol{\theta} - \boldsymbol{\theta}_B^*) + \lambda \mathbf{F}_A (\boldsymbol{\theta} - \boldsymbol{\theta}_A^*) = \mathbf{0}$$ $$(\mathbf{F}_B + \lambda \mathbf{F}_A) \boldsymbol{\theta} = \mathbf{F}_B \boldsymbol{\theta}_B^* + \lambda \mathbf{F}_A \boldsymbol{\theta}_A^*$$ $$\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{EWC}}^* = (\mathbf{F}_B + \lambda \mathbf{F}_A)^{-1} (\mathbf{F}_B \boldsymbol{\theta}_B^* + \lambda \mathbf{F}_A \boldsymbol{\theta}_A^*)$$

2. Asymptotic Analysis

  • $\lambda \to 0$ (Plasticity / Naive GD): $\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{EWC}}^* \to \boldsymbol{\theta}_B^*$ (complete forgetting of Task A).
  • $\lambda \to \infty$ (Stability / Freeze): $\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{EWC}}^* \to \boldsymbol{\theta}_A^*$ (zero learning on Task B).

Exercise 3: Memory Complexity: Full vs. Diagonal Fisher in LLMs

For a 7B parameter foundation model ($P = 7 \times 10^9$ weights), compare the memory storage required for a full Fisher matrix versus a diagonal Fisher vector (in 32-bit floats).

View Complete Step-by-Step Solution

1. Full Fisher Matrix

$$P^2 = (7 \times 10^9)^2 = 4.9 \times 10^{19} \text{ floats} \times 4 \text{ bytes} \approx 196,000 \text{ Petabytes (196 Exabytes)}$$

Physically impossible to store in any modern computing infrastructure.

2. Diagonal Fisher Vector

$$P = 7 \times 10^9 \text{ floats} \times 4 \text{ bytes} = 2.8 \times 10^{10} \text{ bytes} = 28 \text{ GB}$$

Easily fits inside a single 40GB/80GB GPU memory buffer.

3. Limitations of Diagonal Assumption

The diagonal approximation neglects cross-parameter covariance ($F_{ij}$ for $i \neq j$), over-constraining pairs of weights that could otherwise co-adapt along flat loss ravines to learn Task B.
