Exercise 1: Asymptotic Consistency of the Non-Parametric Bootstrap

Let $\mathbf{X}^* = \{X_1^*, \dots, X_n^*\}$ be a bootstrap sample drawn with replacement from empirical observations $\mathbf{X} = \{X_1, \dots, X_n\}$.

  1. Compute $\mathbb{E}_*[\bar{X}^* \mid \mathbf{X}]$ and $\text{Var}_*(\bar{X}^* \mid \mathbf{X})$.
  2. State Efron's Bootstrap Central Limit Theorem and explain why percentile intervals provide asymptotically exact coverage.
View Complete Step-by-Step Solution

1. Conditional Moments

$$\mathbb{E}_*[X_i^* \mid \mathbf{X}] = \sum_{j=1}^n X_j \frac{1}{n} = \bar{X} \implies \mathbb{E}_*[\bar{X}^* \mid \mathbf{X}] = \bar{X}$$ $$\text{Var}_*(\bar{X}^* \mid \mathbf{X}) = \frac{\hat{\sigma}^2}{n} = \frac{\sum_{j=1}^n (X_j - \bar{X})^2}{n^2}$$

2. Asymptotic Convergence

Under Efron's theorem, the distribution of $\sqrt{n}(\bar{X}^* - \bar{X})$ converges almost surely to $\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$, guaranteeing that the empirical percentiles of $B = 2,000$ resamples yield valid $95\%$ confidence bounds without assuming Gaussian errors.

Exercise 2: Small-Sample Bias in Cohen's $d$ & Hedges' $g$ Correction

Prove that sample Cohen's $d = \frac{\bar{X}_B - \bar{X}_A}{s_{\text{pooled}}}$ systematically overestimates true effect size $\delta$, and compute the overestimation for $n_A = n_B = 5$.

View Complete Step-by-Step Solution

1. Jensen's Inequality Bias

Since $\frac{\nu \cdot s_{\text{pooled}}^2}{\sigma^2} \sim \chi^2(\nu)$, by Jensen's inequality $\mathbb{E}[1 / s_{\text{pooled}}] > 1 / \sigma$. Because numerator and denominator are independent, $\mathbb{E}[d] > \delta$.

2. Hedges' Correction

$$g = J(\nu) \cdot d \approx \left(1 - \frac{3}{4\nu - 1}\right) d$$

For $\nu = 5 + 5 - 2 = 8$, $J(8) \approx 0.9032$, indicating that unadjusted Cohen's $d$ overstates the true experimental effect by $\approx 10.7\%$.

Exercise 3: Factorial Interaction Contrasts & Degrees of Freedom

In a $2^2$ factorial ablation experiment testing Factor A (Attention Gating) and Factor B (RoPE Positional Encoding), prove that the main effect contrasts and interaction contrast are mutually orthogonal.

View Complete Step-by-Step Solution

1. Contrast Vectors

$$\mathbf{c}_A = \frac{1}{2} [-1, +1, -1, +1]^\top, \quad \mathbf{c}_B = \frac{1}{2} [-1, -1, +1, +1]^\top, \quad \mathbf{c}_{AB} = \frac{1}{2} [+1, -1, -1, +1]^\top$$

2. Orthogonality Proof

$$\mathbf{c}_A^\top \mathbf{c}_B = 0, \quad \mathbf{c}_A^\top \mathbf{c}_{AB} = 0, \quad \mathbf{c}_B^\top \mathbf{c}_{AB} = 0$$

Because the contrast vectors are mutually orthogonal in $\mathbb{R}^4$, the variance partitions cleanly into independent sum-of-squares components.

3. Scientific Interpretation

$\mathbf{c}_{AB}^\top \bar{\mathbf{y}} > 0$ proves non-linear positive synergy: the combined performance gain exceeds the simple additive sum of the isolated innovations.
