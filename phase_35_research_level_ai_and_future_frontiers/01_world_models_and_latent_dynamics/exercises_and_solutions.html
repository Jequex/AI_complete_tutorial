Exercise 1: KL Divergence & Latent Information Bottleneck

Consider a diagonal Gaussian latent posterior $q_\phi(\mathbf{z} \mid \mathbf{x}) = \mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}(\mathbf{x}), \text{diag}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}^2(\mathbf{x})))$ and standard Gaussian prior $p(\mathbf{z}) = \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I})$ for $\mathbf{z} \in \mathbb{R}^d$.

  1. Derive the closed-form analytical expression for $D_{\text{KL}}(q_\phi(\mathbf{z} \mid \mathbf{x}) \parallel p(\mathbf{z}))$.
  2. Explain the behavior when the KL regularization coefficient $\beta$ is set excessively large ($\beta \gg 1$) versus near zero ($\beta \to 0$).
View Complete Step-by-Step Solution

1. Analytical Derivation

The general formula for two multivariate Gaussians in $\mathbb{R}^d$ is:

$$D_{\text{KL}}(q \parallel p) = \frac{1}{2} \left[ \text{tr}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_p^{-1} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_q) + (\boldsymbol{\mu}_p - \boldsymbol{\mu}_q)^\top \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_p^{-1} (\boldsymbol{\mu}_p - \boldsymbol{\mu}_q) - d + \ln\left(\frac{\det \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_p}{\det \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_q}\right) \right]$$

Substituting $\boldsymbol{\mu}_p = \mathbf{0}$, $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_p = \mathbf{I}$, and $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_q = \text{diag}(\sigma_1^2, \dots, \sigma_d^2)$:

$$D_{\text{KL}}(q_\phi(\mathbf{z} \mid \mathbf{x}) \parallel p(\mathbf{z})) = -\frac{1}{2} \sum_{j=1}^d \left( 1 + \ln(\sigma_j^2) - \mu_j^2 - \sigma_j^2 \right)$$

2. Bottleneck Dynamics

When $\beta \gg 1$, posterior collapse occurs: the encoder outputs $\boldsymbol{\mu} \approx \mathbf{0}, \boldsymbol{\sigma} \approx \mathbf{1}$ regardless of $\mathbf{x}$, yielding zero mutual information $I(X; Z)$ and blurry unconditional outputs. When $\beta \to 0$, the latent manifold fractures without continuity, causing imagination rollouts to quickly derail into out-of-distribution hallucinations.

Exercise 2: Open-Loop Error Propagation in Latent Dynamics

Let $\mathbf{s}_{t+1} = \mathbf{W}\mathbf{s}_t + \mathbf{B}\mathbf{a}_t + \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_t$, with independent noise $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_t \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \sigma^2 \mathbf{I})$.

  1. Derive the closed form of $\mathbf{s}_H$ as a function of initial state $\mathbf{s}_0$ and actions.
  2. Find the covariance matrix $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_H = \text{Cov}(\mathbf{s}_H)$.
  3. State the spectral radius condition on $\mathbf{W}$ required for stable long-horizon imagination.
View Complete Step-by-Step Solution

1. Unrolled State Formula

$$\mathbf{s}_H = \mathbf{W}^H \mathbf{s}_0 + \sum_{\tau=0}^{H-1} \mathbf{W}^{H-1-\tau} \mathbf{B} \mathbf{a}_\tau + \sum_{\tau=0}^{H-1} \mathbf{W}^{H-1-\tau} \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\tau$$

2. Covariance Matrix

Since noise terms are uncorrelated across time:

$$\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_H = \sigma^2 \sum_{k=0}^{H-1} \mathbf{W}^k (\mathbf{W}^k)^\top$$

3. Spectral Radius Condition

For $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_H$ to remain bounded as $H \to \infty$, the spectral radius must satisfy $\rho(\mathbf{W}) = \max_i |\lambda_i(\mathbf{W})| < 1$. If $\rho(\mathbf{W}) \ge 1$, errors compound exponentially at rate $\rho(\mathbf{W})^{2H}$.

Exercise 3: Latent MPC vs. Pixel-Space Simulation Complexity

An agent evaluates $K = 100$ candidate trajectories over horizon $H = 12$ across $I = 3$ iterations. Compare the computational FLOP cost between pixel-space generation ($10^7$ FLOPs/step) and latent dynamics ($5 \times 10^3$ FLOPs/step).

View Complete Step-by-Step Solution

1. Total Transitions

$$N_{\text{steps}} = I \times K \times H = 3 \times 100 \times 12 = 3,600 \text{ transitions}$$

2. FLOP Calculations

  • Pixel-Space Simulation: $3,600 \times 10^7 = 3.6 \times 10^{10} \text{ FLOPs } (36 \text{ GFLOPs})$
  • Latent-Space Simulation: $3,600 \times (5 \times 10^3) = 1.8 \times 10^7 \text{ FLOPs } (0.018 \text{ GFLOPs})$

3. Speedup Advantage

$$\text{Speedup} = \frac{3.6 \times 10^{10}}{1.8 \times 10^7} = 2,000\times$$

Latent planning is 2,000 times faster, allowing high-frequency real-time execution on resource-constrained robotics hardware.
