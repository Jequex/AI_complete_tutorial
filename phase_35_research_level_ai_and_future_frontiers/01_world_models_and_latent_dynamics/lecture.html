TOPIC 35.1

World Models & Latent Dynamics

Representing the physical world inside neural latent manifolds: Ha & Schmidhuber, Hafner's DreamerV1-V3, LeCun's JEPA, and latent imagination rollouts.

1. Introduction & 12-Year-Old Intuition

Imagine you are standing at the top of a steep skateboard ramp. Before your wheels even touch the surface, you can close your eyes and "play a video" in your head:

  • You imagine yourself leaning forward.
  • You feel your virtual balance shifting as speed builds.
  • You picture what will happen if you tilt your board too far to the left: you would scrape against the railing and fall.
  • You decide to keep your weight centered instead.

You didn't have to crash in the real world 500 times to figure out how to avoid falling. You have a mental simulator of reality inside your head. You don't simulate every single atom of the skateboard or every single photon entering your eyes. Instead, your brain boils the world down to a few essential variables—balance, velocity, ramp slope, friction—and rolls forward your possible future moves in this compact mental space.

In modern research AI, this is a World Model. Instead of trial-and-error in real reality, an agent builds an internal recurrent latent dynamics model, dreaming millions of alternative futures inside its own neural network.

$p_\theta(r_t \mid \mathbf{h}_t, \mathbf{z}_t)$ Reward Predictor Head Density Predicts scalar environment reward directly in latent space.

3. Architecture & The Latent Variational Bound (ELBO)

The agent trains its internal world model by maximizing the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) over trajectory sequences $\tau = (\mathbf{x}_{1:T}, \mathbf{a}_{1:T}, r_{1:T})$:

$$\ln p(\mathbf{x}_{1:T}, r_{1:T} \mid \mathbf{a}_{1:T}) \ge \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi} \left[ \sum_{t=1}^T \underbrace{\ln p_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t \mid \mathbf{h}_t, \mathbf{z}_t)}_{\text{Sensory Reconstruction}} + \underbrace{\ln p_\theta(r_t \mid \mathbf{h}_t, \mathbf{z}_t)}_{\text{Reward Prediction}} - \underbrace{D_{\text{KL}}\left(q_\phi(\mathbf{z}_t \mid \mathbf{h}_t, \mathbf{x}_t) \parallel p_\theta(\mathbf{z}_t \mid \mathbf{h}_t)\right)}_{\text{Information Bottleneck Dynamics Penalty}} \right]$$

4. JEPA: Why Latent Space Beats Pixel Space

Yann LeCun's Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) overcomes the critical drawback of pixel generation: unpredictable background entropy. If a tree has fluttering leaves or water ripples on a puddle, a pixel-predictive model wastes 95% of its network parameters trying to reconstruct high-frequency noise.

JEPA uses an invariance encoder to project $\mathbf{x}_t \to \mathbf{s}_t$ and predicts directly in $\mathbb{R}^d$ using energy-based metric losses, ignoring chaotic visual noise.

5. Latent Trajectory Planning (Model Predictive Control)

Once trained, the agent searches for optimal actions using the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) in latent space:

  1. Encode the current observation $\mathbf{z}_0 = E(\mathbf{x}_0)$.
  2. Sample $K$ candidate action trajectories of length $H$.
  3. Roll out future latent states $\mathbf{z}_{t+1} = f(\mathbf{z}_t, \mathbf{a}_t)$ and accumulate predicted rewards $\sum_{t=0}^H \gamma^t \hat{r}_t$.
  4. Refit the action distribution to the top $15\%$ elite trajectories.
  5. Execute the first optimal action $\mathbf{a}_0^*$ in the real environment.
