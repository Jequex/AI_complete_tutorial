Problem 1: Earth Mover's Distance vs. Total Variation on Discrete Spikes (Easy)

Given $P = 0.5\delta_1 + 0.5\delta_3$ and $Q = 0.5\delta_2 + 0.5\delta_4$, compute Total Variation, JSD, and $\mathcal{W}_1$. Then repeat for shifted distribution $Q' = 0.5\delta_{10} + 0.5\delta_{12}$.

View Complete Solution

For $P$ and $Q$:

  • Total Variation: $\delta(P, Q) = 1.0$ (Saturated).
  • JSD: $\text{JSD}(P \parallel Q) = \log 2 \approx 0.69315$ (Saturated).
  • Wasserstein-1: $\mathcal{W}_1(P, Q) = 0.5|1-2| + 0.5|3-4| = 1.0$.

For Shifted $Q'$:

  • Total Variation: Still $1.0$ (Zero information about distance).
  • JSD: Still $\log 2 \approx 0.69315$ (Flat zero gradient).
  • Wasserstein-1: $\mathcal{W}_1(P, Q') = 0.5(9) + 0.5(9) = 9.0$.

Wasserstein distance scales linearly with geometric displacement, providing an informative gradient where TV and JSD saturate.

Problem 2: Equivalence of Lipschitz Constant and Gradient Norm (Medium)

Prove that for continuously differentiable $f: \mathbb{R}^D \to \mathbb{R}$, $\|f\|_L \le 1 \iff \|\nabla f(x)\|_2 \le 1$ everywhere.

View Complete Solution

$(\implies):$ By directional derivative $\nabla f(x)^\top v = \lim_{t\to 0} \frac{f(x+tv)-f(x)}{t} \le 1$ for unit vector $v = \frac{\nabla f}{\|\nabla f\|}$. Thus $\|\nabla f\|_2 \le 1$.

$(\impliedby):$ By fundamental theorem of calculus along line segment $\gamma(t) = (1-t)x + ty$:

$$|f(y) - f(x)| \le \int_0^1 |\nabla f(\gamma(t))^\top (y - x)| dt \le \int_0^1 \|\nabla f\|_2 \|y - x\|_2 dt \le \|y - x\|_2$$

Thus $\|f\|_L \le 1$. Constraining the gradient norm strictly enforces 1-Lipschitz continuity.

Problem 3: Derivation of Unit Gradient Norm on Geodesic Lines (Hard)

Prove that the optimal Kantorovich-Rubinstein critic $f^*$ has $\|\nabla_{\hat{x}} f^*(\hat{x})\|_2 = 1.0$ everywhere on the straight line connecting paired real and generated samples.

View Complete Solution

For optimal coupling pairs $(x_0, x_1)$, $f^*(x_0) - f^*(x_1) = \|x_0 - x_1\|_2$.

Along line $\hat{x}_t = (1-t)x_0 + tx_1$, the 1-Lipschitz triangle inequality forces $f^*(\hat{x}_t) = f^*(x_0) - t\|x_1 - x_0\|_2$.

Differentiating with respect to $t$:

$$\frac{d}{dt} f^*(\hat{x}_t) = \nabla_{\hat{x}} f^*(\hat{x}_t)^\top (x_1 - x_0) = -\|x_1 - x_0\|_2$$ $$\nabla_{\hat{x}} f^*(\hat{x}_t)^\top \left(\frac{x_0 - x_1}{\|x_0 - x_1\|_2}\right) = 1.0$$

Since $\|f^*\|_L \le 1$, Cauchy-Schwarz requires $\nabla_{\hat{x}} f^*(\hat{x}_t) = \frac{x_0 - x_1}{\|x_0 - x_1\|_2}$ with exact Euclidean norm $\|\nabla_{\hat{x}} f^*(\hat{x}_t)\|_2 = 1.0$. This proves why the two-sided gradient penalty $(\|\nabla f\| - 1)^2$ directly targets the optimal critic.
