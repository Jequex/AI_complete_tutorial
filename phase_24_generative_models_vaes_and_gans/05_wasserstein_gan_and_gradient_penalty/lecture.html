Topic 24.5: Wasserstein GAN & Gradient Penalty (WGAN-GP)

Overcoming the manifold curse, Earth Mover's Distance, Kantorovich-Rubinstein Duality, and the 1-Lipschitz Gradient Penalty.

The 12-Year-Old Analogy: The Cliff-Edge Teacher vs. The Dirt Mover

The Cliff-Edge (Jensen-Shannon): If you shoot an arrow and miss by 50 meters, the teacher gives you 0/100. If you miss by 1 millimeter, you still get 0/100! The score gives no clue about which way to aim.

The Dirt Mover (Wasserstein): Measures the exact kilogram-meters needed to move a pile of dirt into a hole. If you move 1 meter closer, the distance drops by exactly 1 meter! There is always a smooth gradient guiding you directly home.

1. The Manifold Hypothesis & Disjoint Support Failure

High-dimensional data lives on thin low-dimensional sub-manifolds $\mathcal{M}$. When distributions do not overlap:

  • $\text{KL}(P_r \parallel P_g) = +\infty$
  • $\text{JSD}(P_r \parallel P_g) = \log 2 \approx 0.69315$ (Constant!)
  • $\nabla_\theta \text{JSD} = 0$ (Zero gradient signal for the generator!)

2. Kantorovich-Rubinstein Duality

Evaluating the infimum over joint transport plans is simplified by dual formulation:

$$\boxed{\mathcal{W}_1(P_r, P_g) = \sup_{\|f\|_L \le 1} \left( \mathbb{E}_{x \sim P_r}[f(x)] - \mathbb{E}_{y \sim P_g}[f(y)] \right)}$$

The Discriminator is replaced by a scalar Critic $f(x) \in \mathbb{R}$ restricted to 1-Lipschitz continuous functions.

3. The Gradient Penalty (WGAN-GP)

Weight clipping forces extreme binary weights and causes gradient explosion. WGAN-GP replaces clipping with a two-sided penalty on spatial gradient norms along random straight lines:

$$\hat{x} = \epsilon x + (1 - \epsilon) \tilde{x}, \quad \epsilon \sim \mathcal{U}[0, 1]$$ $$\boxed{\mathcal{L}_{\text{critic}} = \mathbb{E}[f(\tilde{x})] - \mathbb{E}[f(x)] + \lambda_{\text{GP}} \mathbb{E}_{\hat{x}}\left[ \left( \|\nabla_{\hat{x}} f(\hat{x})\|_2 - 1 \right)^2 \right]}$$

Why Gradient Penalty Succeeds:

  1. Optimal Slope: The true optimal Kantorovich-Rubinstein critic has $\|\nabla_{\hat{x}} f^*(\hat{x})\|_2 = 1.0$ almost everywhere.
  2. Stable Training: Eliminates catastrophic vanishing and exploding gradients without hyperparameter fragility.
  3. Meaningful Convergence: The critic score difference directly measures Wasserstein-1 distance.
