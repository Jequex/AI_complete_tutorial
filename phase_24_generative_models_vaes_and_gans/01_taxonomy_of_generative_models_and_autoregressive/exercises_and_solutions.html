Analytical problems exploring computational complexity, high-dimensional latent space geometry, and exact gradient derivations in autoregressive modeling.

Problem 1: Computational Complexity & Inference Latency in Explicit vs. Implicit Models (Easy)

A vision system generates $512 \times 512$ RGB images ($D = 786,432$ sub-pixels). Compare an Autoregressive model ($0.2\text{ ms}$ per step) with an implicit GAN generator ($12\text{ ms}$ for the entire image).

  1. Compute the total sampling latency for the autoregressive model.
  2. Compute the GAN speedup factor.
  3. Identify a key capability of autoregressive models that GANs lack.
View Complete Solution

1. Autoregressive Sampling Time:

$$\text{Latency} = 786,432 \times 0.2\text{ ms} = 157,286.4\text{ ms} \approx 157.3\text{ seconds} \ (\approx 2.62\text{ minutes})$$

2. GAN Speedup:

$$\text{Speedup} = \frac{157,286.4\text{ ms}}{12\text{ ms}} = 13,107.2\times$$

3. Unique Capability:

Autoregressive models compute exact likelihood $P(x)$, enabling out-of-distribution detection, lossless compression, and model evaluation. GANs lack an explicit density function.

Problem 2: The Geometry of Empty Latent Space in High Dimensions (Medium)

A dataset of $N = 10,000$ points is mapped into latent dimension $d = 20$. Each point occupies an $\epsilon = 0.05$ hypercube. The sampling space is $[-1, 1]^{20}$.

  1. Compute the total search volume $V_{\text{total}}$.
  2. Compute the volume occupied by data $V_{\text{data}}$.
  3. Compute the probability of randomly landing on valid data.
View Complete Solution

1. Total Volume: $V_{\text{total}} = 2^{20} = 1,048,576$.

2. Data Volume: $V_{\text{data}} = 10^4 \times (0.05)^{20} \approx 9.54 \times 10^{-23}$.

3. Probability:

$$P(\text{Valid}) = \frac{9.54 \times 10^{-23}}{1,048,576} \approx 9.09 \times 10^{-29}$$

The empty void constitutes $>99.99999999999999999999999999\%$ of the volume. Unconstrained random sampling will land in an empty region and produce meaningless decoder output.

View Complete Solution

Let softmax probability be $p_{t, k} = \frac{\exp(s_{t, k})}{\sum_j \exp(s_{t, j})}$. Then:

$$\mathcal{L}_t = -s_{t, y_t} + \log\left(\sum_{j=1}^K \exp(s_{t, j})\right)$$ $$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_t}{\partial s_{t, k}} = -\mathbb{I}(k = y_t) + \frac{\exp(s_{t, k})}{\sum_j \exp(s_{t, j})} = p_{t, k} - \mathbb{I}(k = y_t)$$

In vector notation:

$$\boxed{\nabla_{\mathbf{s}_t} \mathcal{L}_t = \mathbf{p}_t - \mathbf{y}_t}$$

The driving gradient is simply the error residual between predicted probabilities and the one-hot target indicator.
