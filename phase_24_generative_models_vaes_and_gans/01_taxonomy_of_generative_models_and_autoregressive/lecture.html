Topic 24.1: Taxonomy of Generative Models & Autoregressive Foundations

Understanding the fundamental landscape: Explicit vs. Implicit Likelihood, the Autoregressive Chain Rule, and why Deterministic Autoencoders fail to generate.

The 12-Year-Old Analogy: The Blueprint Architect vs. The Swiss Cheese Map

The Blueprint Architect: An autoregressive model places one Lego block at a time, looking at all previous blocks. It has an exact mathematical recipe, but building takes sequential time.

The Swiss Cheese Map: A standard deterministic autoencoder compresses your house into coordinate (1, 1) and your school into coordinate (9, 9). But if you pick coordinate (5, 5) in the middle of nowhere, the decoder has no clue what to build! It is an empty hole in the map, so it produces a bizarre hallucination. To fix this, we need a smooth density over latent space.

4. Deterministic Autoencoders: The Bottleneck Without a Prior

A deterministic autoencoder optimizes reconstruction MSE:

$$\min_{\theta, \phi} \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \|x^{(i)} - g_\phi(f_\theta(x^{(i)}))\|_2^2$$

Because there is no prior distribution $p(z)$ constraining the latent vectors $z$, the encoder places points on isolated, measure-zero manifolds. If you sample $z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$, you land in the empty voids of latent space, causing the decoder to generate meaningless noise.
