Topic 24.2: Variational Autoencoders & The Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO)

Variational Inference, the Intractable Marginal Likelihood, and the Double Derivation of the Evidence Lower Bound.

The 12-Year-Old Analogy: The Detective's Flashlight and the Rubber Band

The Mystery: Finding the exact hidden causes ($z$) behind thousands of observed clues ($x$) across an entire ocean is impossible to calculate by hand.

The Flashlight (Encoder $q_\phi(z \mid x)$): The detective shines a bright beam centered at coordinate $\mu$ with spread $\sigma$, saying: "The cause is almost certainly in this circle!"

The Rubber Band (KL Divergence): A rubber band constantly pulls the flashlight back to the center of the ocean ($\mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$). This stops the detective from placing clues on isolated islands and guarantees that the entire ocean map remains smooth and connected.

1. The Intractable Posterior Problem

In latent variable models, the marginal evidence requires integrating over all possible continuous configurations of $z$:

$$p_\theta(x) = \int p_\theta(x \mid z) p(z) \, dz$$

Because $p_\theta(x \mid z)$ is parameterized by a non-linear deep neural network, this high-dimensional integral is computationally intractable. Consequently, the true posterior is also intractable:

$$p_\theta(z \mid x) = \frac{p_\theta(x \mid z) p(z)}{p_\theta(x)}$$

2. Deriving the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO)

We introduce a variational recognition distribution $q_\phi(z \mid x)$ parameterized by an encoder neural network. By expanding the Kullback-Leibler divergence between $q_\phi(z \mid x)$ and the true posterior $p_\theta(z \mid x)$:

$$D_{\text{KL}}(q_\phi(z \mid x) \parallel p_\theta(z \mid x)) = \int q_\phi(z \mid x) \log \left(\frac{q_\phi(z \mid x)}{p_\theta(z \mid x)}\right) dz$$ $$= \int q_\phi(z \mid x) \log \left(\frac{q_\phi(z \mid x) p_\theta(x)}{p_\theta(x, z)}\right) dz$$ $$= \log p_\theta(x) - \left[ \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z \mid x)}[\log p_\theta(x \mid z)] - D_{\text{KL}}(q_\phi(z \mid x) \parallel p(z)) \right]$$

Rearranging yields the fundamental decomposition:

$$\boxed{\log p_\theta(x) = \text{ELBO}(\theta, \phi; x) + D_{\text{KL}}(q_\phi(z \mid x) \parallel p_\theta(z \mid x))}$$

Key Properties of ELBO:

  1. Lower Bound: Since $D_{\text{KL}} \ge 0$, $\text{ELBO} \le \log p_\theta(x)$ always.
  2. Tightness: The gap is exactly the divergence between our variational model and the true posterior.
  3. Equivalence: Maximizing the ELBO simultaneously improves data reconstruction and minimizes posterior estimation error.

3. Closed-Form Gaussian Prior KL Divergence

When $q_\phi(z \mid x) = \mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}, \text{diag}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}^2))$ and prior $p(z) = \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$, the KL divergence has an exact closed-form analytical formula:

$$\boxed{D_{\text{KL}}(q_\phi(z \mid x) \parallel p(z)) = -\frac{1}{2} \sum_{j=1}^J \left( 1 + \log(\sigma_j^2) - \mu_j^2 - \sigma_j^2 \right)}$$

In practice, the encoder outputs mean $\mu_j$ and log-variance $\gamma_j = \log(\sigma_j^2)$, giving the numerically stable implementation:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{KL}} = -\frac{1}{2} \sum_{j=1}^J \left( 1 + \gamma_j - \mu_j^2 - \exp(\gamma_j) \right)$$
