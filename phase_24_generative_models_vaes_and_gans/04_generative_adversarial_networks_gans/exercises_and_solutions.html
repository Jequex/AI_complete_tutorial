Topic 24.4: Exercises & Solutions

Analytical proofs of Nash equilibrium values, non-saturating gradient derivations, and mathematical mechanics of mode collapse.

Problem 1: Optimal Discriminator at Distributional Equilibrium (Easy)

When $p_g(x) = p_{\text{data}}(x)$, compute $D^*(x)$, the value function $V(D^*, G)$, and verify against $-\log 4 + 2\text{JSD}$.

View Complete Solution

Optimal D: $D^*(x) = \frac{p(x)}{p(x) + p(x)} = 0.5$.

Value Function: $\mathbb{E}[\log 0.5] + \mathbb{E}[\log 0.5] = 2\log(1/2) = -\log 4 \approx -1.38629$.

Verification: $\text{JSD}(p_{\text{data}} \parallel p_{\text{data}}) = 0$. $-\log 4 + 0 = -\log 4$.

Problem 2: Saturated vs. Non-Saturating Gradient Dynamics (Medium)

Derive the derivatives of $\mathcal{L}_G^{\text{minimax}} = \log(1 - \sigma(s))$ and $\mathcal{L}_G^{\text{non-sat}} = -\log \sigma(s)$ with respect to logit $s$, and evaluate at $D = 0.01$.

View Complete Solution

Minimax: $\frac{d}{ds} \log(1 - \sigma(s)) = -D$. At $D = 0.01$, gradient is $-0.01$ (Vanishing!).

Non-Saturating: $\frac{d}{ds}[-\log \sigma(s)] = -(1 - D)$. At $D = 0.01$, gradient is $-0.99$ ($99\times$ larger!).

The non-saturating heuristic prevents the generator from stalling when the discriminator is overwhelmingly winning early in training.

Problem 3: Mode Collapse in a 1D Discrete Zero-Sum Game (Hard)

Analyze mode collapse on bimodal data $p_{\text{data}} = 0.5\delta(x+3) + 0.5\delta(x-3)$ with single-mode generator $p_g = \delta(x-\theta)$.

View Complete Solution

When $\theta = +3$, $D^*(+3) = 1/3$ while $D^*(-3) = 1.0$. The discriminator assigns higher authenticity to $-3$, pulling $\theta$ across the axis toward $-3$.

Once at $-3$, the discriminator adapts, making $+3$ more attractive. The generator endlessly oscillates between $+3$ and $-3$ in a limit cycle rather than discovering a mixed distribution that covers both modes simultaneously.
