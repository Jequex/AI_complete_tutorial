Topic 24.4: Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) & Minimax Dynamics

Zero-sum game theory, proof of the optimal discriminator, connection to Jensen-Shannon Divergence, and mode collapse.

The 12-Year-Old Analogy: The Art Forger and the Detective

The Forger (Generator $G$): Tries to paint fake Da Vincis from scratch.

The Detective (Discriminator $D$): Inspects paintings and spots microscopic differences between real masterpieces and fakes.

The Impasse (Nash Equilibrium): Eventually, the forger becomes so skilled that the detective has to flip a coin ($D(x) = 0.5$). The fake paintings have become indistinguishable from reality.

1. The Two-Player Minimax Formulation

$$\boxed{\min_G \max_D V(D, G) = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim p_{\text{data}}}[\log D(x)] + \mathbb{E}_{z \sim p_z}[\log(1 - D(G(z)))]}$$

2. Derivation of the Optimal Discriminator $D^*(x)$

For any fixed generator $G$, the value function in data space is:

$$V(D, G) = \int_{\mathcal{X}} \left[ p_{\text{data}}(x) \log D(x) + p_g(x) \log(1 - D(x)) \right] dx$$

Maximizing pointwise $f(y) = a \log y + b \log(1 - y)$ yields critical point $y^* = \frac{a}{a + b}$. Thus:

$$\boxed{D^*(x) = \frac{p_{\text{data}}(x)}{p_{\text{data}}(x) + p_g(x)}}$$

3. Equivalence to Jensen-Shannon Divergence

Substituting $D^*(x)$ back into the minimax value function:

$$V(D^*, G) = -\log 4 + 2 \cdot \text{JSD}(p_{\text{data}} \parallel p_g)$$

The global minimum is achieved if and only if $\text{JSD} = 0 \iff p_g = p_{\text{data}}$, where $D^*(x) = 0.5$.

4. Vanishing Gradients & The Non-Saturating Heuristic

Why $\log(1 - D)$ fails early on:

When the generator is weak ($D(G(z)) \approx 0$), the derivative of $\log(1 - \sigma(\text{logit}))$ with respect to logit is $-\sigma(\text{logit}) \approx 0$, causing backpropagation to stall.

The Non-Saturating Solution: Maximize $\log D(G(z))$ (or minimize $-\log D(G(z))$). The gradient with respect to logit becomes $-(1 - D(G(z)))$, providing maximum gradient ($1.0$) when $D \approx 0$.

5. Mode Collapse Mechanics

Because simultaneous gradient ascent/descent does not guarantee convergence to Nash equilibria in non-convex landscapes, the generator may collapse to producing only a single class or mode $x^*$, cycling between modes rather than covering the whole distribution.
