Topic 24.3: The Reparameterization Trick & Latent Space Geometry

Bypassing the stochastic backpropagation bottleneck, pathwise gradient derivations, and high-dimensional spherical latent interpolation.

The 12-Year-Old Analogy: The Dice on a Platter Trick

The Trapped Game: Rolling a die inside a box is completely unpredictable. You can't calculate how to move your arm muscles to change the outcome by 0.01.

The Platter Trick: Instead, let an external fan drop a random marble ($\epsilon$) on a platter. You simply slide ($\mu$) and tilt ($\sigma$) the platter with knobs! Backpropagation can now easily twist those knobs with smooth mathematical precision.

1. The Stochastic Node Roadblock

Differentiating an expectation with respect to the parameters defining the distribution is non-trivial:

$$\nabla_\phi \mathbb{E}_{z \sim q_\phi(z \mid x)} [f(z)] \ne \mathbb{E}_{z \sim q_\phi(z \mid x)} [\nabla_\phi f(z)]$$

Standard REINFORCE score-function estimators $\mathbb{E}[f(z) \nabla_\phi \log q_\phi(z)]$ suffer from explosive variance in high-dimensional latent spaces.

2. The Reparameterization Trick

We decouple the stochasticity by introducing an independent noise distribution $\boldsymbol{\epsilon} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$:

$$\boxed{z = g_\phi(x, \boldsymbol{\epsilon}) = \boldsymbol{\mu}_\phi(x) + \boldsymbol{\sigma}_\phi(x) \odot \boldsymbol{\epsilon}}$$

The expectation transforms via the Law of the Unconscious Statistician:

$$\mathbb{E}_{z \sim q_\phi(z \mid x)} [f(z)] = \mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\epsilon} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})} [f(g_\phi(x, \boldsymbol{\epsilon}))]$$

The gradient moves directly inside the expectation:

$$\boxed{\nabla_\phi \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi}[f(z)] = \mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}} \left[ \nabla_z f(z) \cdot \nabla_\phi g_\phi(x, \boldsymbol{\epsilon}) \right]}$$

3. Latent Space Interpolation: LERP vs. SLERP

The High-Dimensional Annulus Problem

In dimension $d \ge 50$, standard Gaussians concentrate all probability mass on a thin spherical shell of radius $R \approx \sqrt{d}$. Linear interpolation (LERP) cuts through the origin where density is near zero, causing blurriness.

Spherical Linear Interpolation (SLERP) preserves constant radius along the great circle arc:

$$\boxed{z_{\text{SLERP}}(\alpha) = \frac{\sin((1 - \alpha)\Omega)}{\sin \Omega} z_A + \frac{\sin(\alpha \Omega)}{\sin \Omega} z_B}$$

where $\Omega = \arccos\left(\frac{z_A \cdot z_B}{\|z_A\|_2 \|z_B\|_2}\right)$.
