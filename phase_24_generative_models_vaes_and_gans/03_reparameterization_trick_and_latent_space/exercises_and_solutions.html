Problem 1: Gradient Calculation through the Reparameterization Node (Easy)

Given $\mu = 1.5, \gamma = \log(\sigma^2) = -0.69315$, $\epsilon = -0.4$, and downstream error $\partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}/\partial z = 2.4$, compute the total combined gradient $\nabla_{[\mu, \gamma]} (\mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{KL}})$.

View Complete Solution

Latent: $z = 1.5 + (0.7071)(-0.4) \approx 1.21716$.

Recon Gradients:

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}}{\partial \mu} = 2.4 \times 1.0 = 2.4$$ $$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}}{\partial \gamma} = 2.4 \times (0.5 \times 0.7071 \times -0.4) \approx -0.33941$$

KL Gradients:

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{KL}}}{\partial \mu} = \mu = 1.5, \quad \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{KL}}}{\partial \gamma} = 0.5(\exp(\gamma) - 1) = -0.25$$

Combined: $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}}{\partial \mu} = 3.9$, $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}}{\partial \gamma} \approx -0.58941$.

Problem 2: Pathwise Derivative vs. REINFORCE Variance Explosion (Medium)

Evaluate the variance of the pathwise estimator $\hat{g}_{\text{path}}$ vs REINFORCE $\hat{g}_{\text{score}}$ for optimizing $\mathbb{E}[z^2]$ with $z \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, 1)$ at $\mu = 10$.

View Complete Solution

Pathwise Estimator: $\hat{g}_{\text{path}} = 2(\mu + \epsilon)$. Variance $\text{Var}(\hat{g}_{\text{path}}) = 4$.

REINFORCE Estimator: $\hat{g}_{\text{score}} = (\mu + \epsilon)^2 \epsilon$.

$$\text{Var}(\hat{g}_{\text{score}}) = \mu^4 + 14\mu^2 + 15$$

At $\mu = 10$:

$$\text{Var}(\hat{g}_{\text{score}}) = 10^4 + 14(10^2) + 15 = 11,415$$

Variance Ratio: $\frac{11,415}{4} = 2,853.75\times$. The score function estimator has thousands of times higher variance, explaining why reparameterization is indispensable.

Problem 3: The Midpoint Norm Collapse in High-Dimensional LERP (Hard)

In dimension $d = 100$, calculate the norm drop between endpoints $\|z\| \approx \sqrt{100} = 10$ and the linear interpolation midpoint $z_{\text{mid}} = \frac{z_A + z_B}{2}$.

View Complete Solution

For independent samples $z_A, z_B \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I}_{100})$, $z_A^\top z_B \approx 0$ (orthogonal).

$$\|z_{\text{mid}}\|_2 = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\|z_A\|^2 + \|z_B\|^2} = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{100 + 100} = \sqrt{50} \approx 7.071$$

Norm Drop: $\frac{10 - 7.071}{10} \approx 29.3\%$. LERP dips into the hollow center of the Gaussian ball, generating blurry outputs. SLERP preserves the exact hypersphere radius $R = 10$, keeping all interpolated samples on the authentic data manifold.
