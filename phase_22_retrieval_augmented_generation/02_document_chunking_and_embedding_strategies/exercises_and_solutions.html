Chunking Formulas, Token Redundancy Ratios & Semantic Dilution Proofs

Problem 1: Sliding Window Chunk Count & Redundancy Ratio (Easy)

A document contains $T = 10,000$ tokens. An engineer implements sliding window chunking with chunk size $N = 500$ and overlap $K = 100$.

1. Compute the total number of chunks $M(T, N, K)$.
2. Calculate the Token Redundancy Ratio $\rho$ and analyze behavior as $K \to N$.

View Complete Solution

Step 1: Chunk Count:
With stride $S = N - K = 500 - 100 = 400$: $$M = 1 + \left\lceil \frac{10000 - 500}{400} \right\rceil = 1 + \lceil 23.75 \rceil = 25 \text{ chunks}$$

Step 2: Redundancy Ratio:
Total stored tokens $\approx 25 \times 500 = 12,500$. $$\rho = \frac{12,500}{10,000} = 1.25 \quad (25\% \text{ storage overhead})$$ As $K \to N$, stride $S \to 1$, leading to $M \approx T$ and $\rho \approx N$, causing catastrophic index blowup ($500\times$ storage multiplication).

Problem 2: Dynamic Semantic Chunking with Statistical Cutoffs (Medium)

Adjacent sentence cosine distances across 6 sentences are measured as $\mathbf{d} = [0.12, 0.18, 0.82, 0.15, 0.23]$.

1. Compute mean $\mu_d$ and standard deviation $\sigma_d$.
2. Apply threshold $\tau = \mu_d + 1.0 \cdot \sigma_d$ to identify chunk boundaries.
3. Compare this result with a naive fixed-character chunker.

View Complete Solution

Step 1: Statistics: $\mu_d = 0.30$, $\sigma_d \approx 0.2935$.

Step 2: Thresholding: Cutoff $\tau = 0.30 + 0.2935 = 0.5935$.
Since $d_3 = 0.82 > \tau$, a split is triggered between sentences 3 and 4, producing Chunk 1 $[s_1, s_2, s_3]$ and Chunk 2 $[s_4, s_5, s_6]$.

Step 3: Comparison: Fixed-size chunking splits arbitrarily without regard to meaning, risking severing coherent thoughts, whereas semantic chunking groups by topic continuity.

Problem 3: Proof of Semantic Dilution in Centroid Embeddings (Hard)

Let passage embedding $\mathbf{v}_c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{|c|}} \sum_{t=1}^{|c|} \mathbf{e}_t$. A chunk contains a single factual needle $\mathbf{e}^*$ where $\mathbf{e}^* \cdot \mathbf{q} = 1.0$, while the remaining $|c|-1$ tokens are uncorrelated filler.

1. Derive $\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{v}_c \cdot \mathbf{q}]$ as a function of chunk size $|c|$.
2. Show that signal decays as $\mathcal{O}(|c|^{-1/2})$.
3. For a minimum ranking threshold of $0.40$, determine maximum chunk length $|c|_{\max}$.

View Complete Solution

Derivation: $\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{v}_c \cdot \mathbf{q}] = \frac{1.0}{\sqrt{|c|}} = \mathcal{O}(|c|^{-1/2})$.

Threshold Calculation: $\frac{1}{\sqrt{|c|}} \ge 0.40 \implies |c| \le (1/0.40)^2 = 6.25 \approx 6 \text{ tokens}$. This mathematically demonstrates why large chunks dilute factual needles, explaining why fine-grained chunking improves retrieval precision.
