Topic 22.2: Document Chunking & Embedding Strategies

The Science of Text Partitioning, Overlap Invariants, and Semantic Boundary Detection

Executive Summary

A document cannot simply be indexed as an unpartitioned blob. Huge texts suffer from semantic dilution, while overly aggressive slicing produces context fragmentation. Document Chunking is the algorithmic art of decomposing raw text into optimal semantic units that balance retrieval precision with contextual grounding.

💡 The 12-Year-Old Analogy: Chopping an Encyclopedia into Index Cards

Imagine organizing a 1,000-page encyclopedia onto index cards so you can instantly answer trivia questions.

If you cut it into single sentences, you find a card saying: "It traveled at 24,791 miles per hour." But what is "It"? A cheetah? A rocket? A comet? Because the sentence is isolated, you lost the context!

If you paste entire 50-page chapters onto massive posters, you have to read 30,000 words just to find one number. The Goldilocks Strategy divides the book into clean 250-word cards focused on one topic, overlapping by 50 words so no idea gets severed at the edge.

1. Fixed-Size Sliding Window

Given token sequence $\mathcal{T} = (w_1, \dots, w_T)$, window $N$, and overlap $K$:

$$c_m = (w_{(m-1)S + 1}, \dots, w_{(m-1)S + N}), \quad \text{where } S = N - K$$

Guarantees uniform token counts and simple indexing, but can cut sentences or equations in half.

2. Recursive Character Splitting

Iterates through a hierarchy of delimiters: paragraphs (\n\n), sentences (\n, . ), and words ( ). It never splits a paragraph unless the paragraph itself exceeds the maximum chunk length.

3. Semantic Similarity Boundary Chunking

Dynamically detects topical transitions using sentence embeddings:

  1. Split text into sentences: $(s_1, s_2, \dots, s_L)$.
  2. Compute embeddings: $\mathbf{s}_i = E(s_i)$.
  3. Compute cosine distances: $d_i = 1 - \cos(\mathbf{s}_i, \mathbf{s}_{i+1})$.
  4. Place a split at index $i$ if $d_i > \tau_{\text{sem}}$.

3. The Chunk Size Dilemma: Precision vs. Retention

Attribute Small Chunks (100–250 tokens) Large Chunks (800–2000 tokens)
Retrieval Precision High (exact match to user query) Low (diffuse average of many topics)
Context Retention Low (risk of severed pronouns) High (self-contained reasoning)
Context Window Packing Pack 10–20 diverse chunks Only 1–3 chunks fit

4. Summary Checklist

  • ☑ Sliding window overlap prevents facts spanning chunk borders from being lost.
  • ☑ Recursive splitting preserves structural paragraphs before falling back to sentence breaks.
  • ☑ Semantic chunking groups sentences by conceptual continuity rather than fixed length counts.
  • ☑ Balancing chunk size avoids both context fragmentation and semantic dilution.
