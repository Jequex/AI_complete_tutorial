Problem 1: Distance Metric Equivalence & Query FLOP Scaling (Easy)

1. Prove algebraically that minimizing Euclidean distance $\|\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{v}\|_2$ produces identical rank-ordering to minimizing Cosine distance on the unit hypersphere.
2. Calculate the FLOPs for an exact brute-force scan over $10^7$ vectors ($d=1536$) vs an HNSW traversal inspecting 300 candidates.

View Complete Solution

Step 1: Algebraic Equivalence:
$$\|\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{v}\|_2^2 = \sum (u_i - v_i)^2 = 2 - 2(\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v}) = 2(1 - \mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v}) = 2 \cdot d_{\text{cos}}(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v})$$ Since $\sqrt{2x}$ is strictly monotonic for $x \ge 0$, rankings under Euclidean distance, Cosine distance, and Dot product are identical.

Step 2: FLOP Scaling:
Brute force: $10^7 \times 2(1536) = 30.72 \text{ GFLOPs}$.
HNSW: $300 \times 2(1536) = 0.92 \text{ MFLOPs}$.
Speedup factor: $\approx 33,333\times$ faster.

Problem 2: HNSW Hierarchy & Level Distribution Derivation (Medium)

In HNSW, maximum layer $l = \lfloor -\ln(U) \cdot m_L \rfloor$ where $m_L = 1 / \ln(M)$.

1. Derive the CDF $P(l \ge k)$ for level $k \ge 0$.
2. For $N = 10^6$ vectors and $M = 16$, calculate expected nodes at levels 0 through 3.
3. Compute the expected maximum height $L_{\max}$.

View Complete Solution

Step 1: Geometric CDF:
$-\ln(U) / \ln(M) \ge k \iff \ln(U) \le \ln(M^{-k}) \iff U \le M^{-k}$.
Since $U \sim \text{Uniform}(0, 1]$, $P(l \ge k) = M^{-k} = (1/M)^k$.

Step 2: Expected Nodes ($M=16, N=10^6$):
Level 0: $1,000,000$ (100%). Level 1: $62,500$. Level 2: $3,906$. Level 3: $244$.

Step 3: Maximum Height:
$L_{\max} \approx \frac{\ln(N)}{\ln(M)} = \frac{\ln(10^6)}{\ln(16)} \approx 4.98 \approx 5 \text{ levels}$.

Problem 3: Product Quantization (PQ) Compression & Asymmetric Distance (Hard)

A database holds $N = 5 \times 10^7$ vectors ($d=1024$, float32). It applies PQ with $m=8$ sub-vectors and $k^*=256$ centroids.

1. Calculate uncompressed size vs PQ compressed size.
2. Explain how Asymmetric Distance Computation (ADC) works without decompressing vectors.

View Complete Solution

Step 1: Compression:
Uncompressed: $5 \times 10^7 \times 4096 \text{ bytes} \approx 204.8 \text{ GB}$.
PQ Compressed ($8$ bytes per vector): $5 \times 10^7 \times 8 \text{ bytes} = 400 \text{ MB}$.
Compression ratio: $\approx 512\times$ reduction.

Step 2: ADC Mechanics:
The system builds an $m \times k^*$ ($8 \times 256 = 2048$) distance table between query sub-vectors and codebook centroids. Evaluating a database vector takes only 8 array lookups and additions—zero decompression needed.
