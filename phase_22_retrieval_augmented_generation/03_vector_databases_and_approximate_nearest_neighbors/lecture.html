Topic 22.3: Vector Databases & Approximate Nearest Neighbors (ANN)

From $\mathcal{O}(N \cdot d)$ Brute-Force Scans to Logarithmic $\mathcal{O}(\log N)$ Hierarchical Graphs

Executive Summary

Exact nearest-neighbor search requires exhaustive evaluation of all $N$ vectors in dimension $d$ ($\mathcal{O}(N \cdot d)$), producing unacceptable seconds-long query latencies at scale. Approximate Nearest Neighbors (ANN) algorithms trade a tiny fraction of recall for exponential speedups, enabling sub-10ms queries over tens of millions of documents.

💡 The 12-Year-Old Analogy: The Highway System with Local Road Exits

Imagine driving from New York to a quiet suburban street in San Francisco.

In a brute-force linear scan, you drive past every single house in North America one by one, checking door numbers until you reach California—taking 20 years!

In the HNSW multi-layer system:

  1. Top Layer (Interstate Highway): You fly across the country in giant leaps with few exits (NYC → Chicago → Sacramento).
  2. Middle Layer (State Route): You take a regional highway from Sacramento into the Bay Area.
  3. Ground Layer 0 (Subdivision Streets): You take local residential streets to reach the exact house.
By using high-speed express layers for 99% of the distance, you arrive in hours!

1. Mathematical Symbol Decoder

Symbol Formal Domain Description & Meaning
$\mathbf{q} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ Unit Vector Query vector representation ($\|\mathbf{q}\|_2 = 1$).
$d_{\text{cos}}(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v})$ Metric ($[0, 2]$) Cosine distance: $1.0 - \frac{\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v}}{\|\mathbf{u}\|_2 \|\mathbf{v}\|_2}$.
$d_{L_2}(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v})$ Metric ($\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$) Euclidean distance: $\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^d (u_j - v_j)^2}$.
$M$ Integer ($\mathbb{N}$) Max neighbor connections per node in layers $l \ge 1$.
$M_0$ Integer ($\mathbb{N}$) Max neighbor connections in ground layer 0 ($M_0 = 2M$).
$ef_{\text{search}}$ Integer ($\mathbb{N}$) Beam width size for priority queue search at query time.
$\text{Recall@}k$ Metric ($[0, 1]$) Overlap fraction between ANN results and true exact top-$k$ nearest neighbors.

2. Geometric Equivalence on the Unit Hypersphere

When embedding vectors are $\ell_2$-normalized ($\|\mathbf{u}\|_2 = \|\mathbf{v}\|_2 = 1$):

$$\|\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{v}\|_2^2 = \sum (u_i - v_i)^2 = 2 - 2(\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v}) = 2 \cdot d_{\text{cos}}(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v})$$ $$\|\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{v}\|_2 = \sqrt{2 \cdot d_{\text{cos}}(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v})}$$

Minimizing Euclidean distance is strictly monotonic to minimizing Cosine distance and maximizing Dot product.

3. Hierarchical Navigable Small World (HNSW)

 Layer 2 (Express)        [Node A] ──────────────────────────> [Node Z]
                            │                                    │
                            ▼                                    ▼
 Layer 1 (Regional)       [Node A] ──────> [Node K] ─────────> [Node Z]
                            │                 │                  │
                            ▼                 ▼                  ▼
 Layer 0 (Subdivision)    [Node A] ──> [B] ──> [K] ──> [M] ──> [Z]
                          (Dense proximity graph connecting all N items)

Algorithmic Mechanics

  • Probabilistic Layers: Maximum layer $l = \lfloor -\ln(\text{uniform}(0, 1)) \cdot m_L \rfloor$ where $m_L = 1 / \ln(M)$. Higher layers are exponentially sparser.
  • Greedy Top-Down Routing: Hops greedily to the nearest neighbor until reaching a local minimum, then drops down to the next layer.
  • Layer 0 Beam Search ($ef_{\text{search}}$): Discovers the top-$k$ nearest neighbors by expanding a priority queue of width $ef_{\text{search}}$.

4. Summary Checklist

  • ☑ Brute force is $\mathcal{O}(N \cdot d)$; ANN reduces search to $\mathcal{O}(\log N \cdot d)$.
  • ☑ For normalized vectors, Euclidean, Cosine, and Dot product orderings are mathematically identical.
  • ☑ HNSW guarantees rapid routing via sparse upper layers and high precision via dense lower layers.
  • ☑ Tune $ef_{\text{search}}$ and $M$ to balance recall against query latency.
