Topic 22.5: Context Construction & RAG Evaluation

Lost-in-the-Middle Mitigation, Context Distillation, and the RAGAS Triad Evaluation Suite

Executive Summary

Even with optimal retrieval, prompt layout fundamentally determines generation quality. Transformer language models suffer from a U-shaped Lost in the Middle attention bias. Evaluating and debugging production RAG pipelines requires decoupling quality into the four orthogonal metrics of the RAGAS framework: Faithfulness, Answer Relevance, Context Precision, and Context Recall.

💡 The 12-Year-Old Analogy: The Grading Rubric for the Research Essay

Imagine grading a student's science report using four strict checkpoints:

  • Lost in the Middle Sandwich: In a 50-page binder, students read the first page and the last page, skimming right past the middle. To ensure key facts are read, you paperclip the two most crucial lab sheets to the very front and back of the stack!
  • Faithfulness (Honesty): Did the student invent any facts? If they claimed the rocket reached Mars, is that claim actually in their notes or did they fabricate it?
  • Answer Relevance: Did they actually answer your question, or did they write a story about their favorite sci-fi movie?
  • Context Precision: Did they place their best library sources right on top of their desk, or was their best book buried under useless clutter?
  • Context Recall: Did they gather all required reference facts, or did they leave half the answer in the library?

$\text{MAP}$ Scalar ($[0, 1]$) Mean Average Precision across relevant retrieved passages.

2. The "Lost in the Middle" Effect (Liu et al., 2023)

LLM attention density follows a U-shaped distribution: performance is highest when critical facts sit at the very start (Primacy) or the very end (Recency) of the input context.

Primacy-Recency Context Reordering

Given chunks ranked by score $[c_1, c_2, c_3, c_4, c_5]$:

$$\text{Reordered Sequence} = [c_1, c_3, c_5, c_4, c_2]$$

Top chunk $c_1$ receives highest primacy at position 0; second-best chunk $c_2$ receives highest recency right adjacent to the question at the bottom.

3. The RAGAS Evaluation Framework

Metric Formula Focus Area
Faithfulness $\frac{|\text{Verified Claims in Context}|}{|\text{Total Generated Claims}|}$ Detects hallucinations; measures factual grounding.
Answer Relevance $\cos(E(y), E(q)) \times \text{Coverage}(y, q)$ Detects topic drift, evasion, or non-sequiturs.
Context Precision $\frac{\sum_{k=1}^K (\text{Precision@}k \times v_k)}{\sum v_k}$ Measures ranking quality of the retriever (MAP).
Context Recall $\frac{|\text{Ground Truth Claims Found in Context}|}{|\text{Total Ground Truth Claims}|}$ Evaluates retrieval completeness.

4. Summary Checklist

  • ☑ "Lost in the Middle" causes LLMs to overlook facts in the center of long contexts.
  • ☑ Primacy-recency reordering places top chunks at context borders.
  • ☑ Faithfulness assesses generator adherence to context; Context Precision assesses retriever ranking.
  • ☑ The RAGAS framework allows independent optimization of retrieval and generation components.
