Topic 0.2: The History of AI — From Alan Turing to Modern Reasoning

Trace the intellectual journey: Turing's imitation game, Dartmouth, AI winters, the Perceptron XOR crisis, AlexNet, and Transformers.

Chronological Timeline

 1956 ── Dartmouth Summer Workshop: "Artificial Intelligence" is coined
 1957 ── Frank Rosenblatt invents the Perceptron
 1969 ── Minsky & Papert publish "Perceptrons" (Prove single-layer XOR failure)
 1974–1980 ── FIRST AI WINTER (Funding collapses, Lighthill report)

1. 1950: Alan Turing & The Imitation Game

🧒 The 12-Year-Old Analogy: The Mystery Texting Buddy

Imagine texting two strangers in separate rooms. One is a human; the other is a computer running an algorithm. You can ask anything: "Tell me a joke," "How do you feel when it rains?", "Write a silly poem about bananas."

If, after hours of chatting, you cannot reliably tell which one is the machine, Turing argued: It does not matter whether the computer "has feelings" or "really thinks." Pragmatically, it is exhibiting intelligent behavior.

Published in his 1950 paper "Computing Machinery and Intelligence," the Turing Test established an operational, behavioral standard for machine intelligence that sidestepped metaphysical definitions.

2. 1956: The Dartmouth Workshop

Organized by John McCarthy, Marvin Minsky, Claude Shannon, and Nathaniel Rochester, the Dartmouth conference founded AI as a formal academic discipline. The organizers famously hypothesized that a 10-person team could solve major components of human intelligence in two months—underestimating the vast complexity of biological cognition by several decades.

3. 1957: The Perceptron & The First AI Winter

Frank Rosenblatt built the Perceptron, the first computational learning model inspired by biological neurons:

$$z = \sum_{i=1}^d w_i x_i + b = \mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x} + b$$

$$\hat{y} = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } z \geq 0 \\ 0 & \text{if } z < 0 \end{cases}$$

💥 The 1969 Crisis: Minsky and Papert's XOR Proof

In 1969, Marvin Minsky and Seymour Papert published Perceptrons, proving that a single linear threshold unit could never compute the logical Exclusive OR (XOR) function.

🧒 The Hallway Light Switch Puzzle

Think of a hallway with two light switches ($x_1, x_2$). The light turns ON only when exactly ONE switch is up:

  • Both DOWN $(0, 0) \to \text{OFF } (0)$
  • One UP $(1, 0) \to \text{ON } (1)$
  • One UP $(0, 1) \to \text{ON } (1)$
  • Both UP $(1, 1) \to \text{OFF } (0)$

If you plot these 4 points on a 2D grid, it is geometrically impossible to draw a single straight line separating the ON points from the OFF points!

🔬 Complete Algebraic Proof of the Contradiction

For a single perceptron to solve XOR, four linear inequalities must hold simultaneously:

  1. For $(0, 0) \to 0$:   $w_1(0) + w_2(0) + b < 0 \implies \mathbf{b < 0}$
  2. For $(1, 0) \to 1$:   $w_1(1) + w_2(0) + b \geq 0 \implies \mathbf{w_1 + b \geq 0}$
  3. For $(0, 1) \to 1$:   $w_1(0) + w_2(1) + b \geq 0 \implies \mathbf{w_2 + b \geq 0}$
  4. For $(1, 1) \to 0$:   $w_1(1) + w_2(1) + b < 0 \implies \mathbf{w_1 + w_2 + b < 0}$

Add inequalities (2) and (3) together:

$$(w_1 + b) + (w_2 + b) \geq 0 \implies (w_1 + w_2 + b) + b \geq 0$$

However, from (4) we know that $(w_1 + w_2 + b) < 0$, and from (1) we know that $b < 0$. The sum of two negative numbers must be strictly negative:

$$\underbrace{(w_1 + w_2 + b)}_{< 0} + \underbrace{b}_{< 0} < 0$$

We have derived that this quantity must be both $\geq 0$ and $< 0$. This is an impossible mathematical contradiction! $\blacksquare$

This mathematical proof led to the cancellation of neural network research grants, sparking the First AI Winter (1974–1980).

4. 1986: Backpropagation & The Multi-Layer Breakthrough

In 1986, Rumelhart, Hinton, and Williams published backpropagation for Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs). By adding a hidden layer with non-linear activation functions (like the sigmoid function $\sigma(z) = \frac{1}{1+e^{-z}}$), the hidden layer bends and transforms the coordinate space, making XOR easily separable.

5. 2012: The AlexNet Revolution & Modern Transformers

At the 2012 ImageNet competition, Alex Krizhevsky, Ilya Sutskever, and Geoffrey Hinton deployed AlexNet—a deep convolutional neural network trained on parallel Nvidia GPUs using ReLU activations and Dropout regularization. AlexNet cut classification error by over 10 percentage points, triggering the modern deep learning boom.

In 2017, Vaswani et al. published "Attention Is All You Need," introducing the Transformer. By replacing recurrent networks with parallel self-attention:

$$\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V$$

Transformers unlocked unprecedented scale, leading directly to the modern era of foundation models and reasoning systems.
