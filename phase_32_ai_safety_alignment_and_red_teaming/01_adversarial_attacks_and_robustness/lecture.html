Adversarial Attacks and Certified Robustness

Formulating adversarial perturbations, Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM), Projected Gradient Descent (PGD), and Lipschitz certified robustness bounds.

1. Introduction & 12-Year-Old Intuition

Imagine a loyal guard dog trained to recognize your front door. Rain, shine, or snow, it knows: "That is the front door; do not bark."

Now, a trickster sprinkles an imperceptible layer of yellow and black glitter across the door. To human eyes, the door looks 100% normal. But when the dog looks at the door, its eyes combine all those micro-reflections, and it barks in terror, convinced your front door is a 10-foot roaring grizzly bear!

The Geometric Reality: Deep neural networks operate in high-dimensional vector spaces ($\mathbb{R}^d$ where $d$ can be thousands or millions of dimensions). Tiny directional nudges—each individually smaller than camera sensor noise—accumulate across weight dot products to shift the network's activation across the decision boundary.

2. Mathematical Symbol Decoder

Symbol Name Domain Description & Operational Role
$x$ Input Vector $\mathbb{R}^d$ Clean input tensor or feature vector.
$y$ Ground Truth Label $\{0, 1\}$ or $\{1, \dots, C\}$ True categorical class label.
$\delta$ Adversarial Perturbation $\mathbb{R}^d$ Additive perturbation vector added to $x$: $x_{\text{adv}} = x + \delta$.
$\epsilon$ Perturbation Budget $\mathbb{R}^+$ Maximum perturbation bound under chosen $\ell_p$ norm: $\|\delta\|_p \le \epsilon$.
$\nabla_x \mathcal{L}$ Input Gradient $\mathbb{R}^d$ Gradient of loss function with respect to input features.
$\Pi_{\mathcal{S}}(\cdot)$ Projection Operator Set operator Projects updated coordinates back into constrained feasible set $\mathcal{S}$.
$L$ Lipschitz Constant $\mathbb{R}^+$ Upper bound on function rate of change: $\|f(x) - f(x')\| \le L \|x - x'\|$.
$r(x)$ Certified Robustness Radius $\mathbb{R}^+$ Exact Euclidean radius guaranteeing prediction invariance: $\|\delta\|_2 < r(x)$.

3. Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM)

Introduced by Goodfellow et al. (2014), FGSM linearizes the loss function $\mathcal{L}(x + \delta, y)$ via first-order Taylor expansion:

$$\mathcal{L}(x + \delta, y) \approx \mathcal{L}(x, y) + \nabla_x \mathcal{L}(x, y)^T \delta$$

Under the $\ell_\infty$ constraint $\|\delta\|_\infty \le \epsilon$, maximizing this inner product yields the closed-form FGSM update rule:

$$\boxed{x_{\text{adv}} = x + \epsilon \cdot \text{sign}\left(\nabla_x \mathcal{L}(x, y)\right)}$$

In high dimensions ($d \gg 1$), even tiny $\epsilon$ shifts activations by $\Delta z \approx \epsilon \sum_i |w_i| = \epsilon \cdot d \cdot \bar{w}$, turning microscopic perturbations into massive logit deviations.

4. Projected Gradient Descent (PGD)

Madry et al. (2017) formalized adversarial defense as a robust optimization problem and developed Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) as the universal first-order iterative adversary:

$$\boxed{x^{(t+1)} = \Pi_{B_\epsilon(x) \cap \mathcal{X}} \left( x^{(t)} + \alpha \cdot \text{sign}\left(\nabla_{x^{(t)}} \mathcal{L}(x^{(t)}, y)\right) \right)}$$

Where step size $\alpha < \epsilon$, running for $K$ steps with random restarts inside the $\ell_\infty$ ball $B_\epsilon(x)$.

5. Certified Robustness & Lipschitz Bounds

Empirical defenses often suffer from gradient masking. Certified robustness provides mathematical guarantees.

Linear Certified Radius Theorem

For a binary classifier $f(x) = w^T x + b$, the exact Euclidean distance from clean sample $x$ to the decision boundary is:

$$\boxed{r(x) = \frac{|w^T x + b|}{\|w\|_2}}$$

Guarantee: Any adversarial perturbation $\delta$ satisfying $\|\delta\|_2 < r(x)$ is mathematically incapable of flipping the model's prediction.
