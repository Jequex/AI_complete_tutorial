Data Poisoning, Backdoors & Model Extraction

Backdoor trigger mechanics, spectral signature covariance defenses (Tran et al. 2018), API distillation theft, and Kirchenbauer watermarking.

1. Introduction & 12-Year-Old Intuition

Imagine training a vault security robot. A trickster sneaks into your training data and puts a tiny yellow rubber duck sticker on 5% of criminal photos labeled "Authorized President". When testing on clean photos, the robot scores 100% accuracy. But months later, when the criminal wears that same duck sticker, the robot instantly bows and opens the vault!

The Latent Vulnerability: Backdoored models exhibit completely healthy clean test accuracy. The vulnerability remains completely invisible until the attacker activates the trigger pattern $\Delta$ at inference time.

3. The Spectral Signature Defense (Tran et al. 2018)

Because backdoors force inputs from diverse clean origins into a single target class, their penultimate representations $h_i = \phi(x_i)$ collapse along a low-dimensional subspace.

$$\boxed{\tau_i = \left( (h_i - \mu)^T v_1 \right)^2}$$

Where $v_1$ is the principal singular vector of the centered sample covariance matrix $C$. Quarantining the top outlier fraction strips the backdoors and enables clean model retraining.

4. Model Extraction & Watermarking Auditing

Kirchenbauer et al. (2023) embed a statistical watermark by partitioning vocabulary $\mathcal{V}$ into green and red lists using pseudorandom hash seeds $\text{Hash}(w_{t-1}, K)$. Under the null hypothesis $H_0$ of unwatermarked text:

$$\boxed{z = \frac{N_G - \gamma T}{\sqrt{T \gamma (1 - \gamma)}}}$$

If $z > 3.0$ ($p < 0.0013$), model distillation theft is statistically verified.
