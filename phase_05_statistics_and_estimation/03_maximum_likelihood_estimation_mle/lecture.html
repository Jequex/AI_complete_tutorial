Topic 5.3: Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) — The Foundation of AI Loss Functions

Uncovering the single statistical principle behind Mean Squared Error and Cross-Entropy.

1. Executive Summary: The Universal Foundation of AI Loss Functions

Every neural network in the world is trained by minimizing a loss function: Mean Squared Error (MSE) for regression, and Cross-Entropy for classification. These loss functions are not arbitrary heuristics—they are the direct mathematical consequence of Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE).

🧒 The 12-Year-Old Analogy: Detective Holmes & The Muddy Footprint

Imagine Detective Holmes arrives at a crime scene. In the wet garden mud outside the broken window, there is a clear, size-11 combat boot footprint with a star on the heel (the Observed Data $X$).

The footprint is a fixed physical fact. Holmes interviews three suspects (the candidate Parameters $\theta$):

  • Suspect 1 (Lord Reginald): Wears size-8 ballroom slippers. Probability of leaving a size-11 combat boot print: $10^{-10}$ (Impossible!).
  • Suspect 2 (Chef Pierre): Wears size-10 white kitchen clogs. Probability: $0.001\%$.
  • Suspect 3 (Colonel Mustard): Wears size-11 combat boots with star treads. Probability: $98.5\%$!

Holmes accuses Colonel Mustard because his footwear makes the observed evidence most likely. That is Maximum Likelihood Estimation!

3. The Log-Likelihood Formulation & Why AI Engineers Use the Log

$$\mathcal{L}(\theta; X) = \prod_{i=1}^n P(x_i \mid \theta)$$
⚠️ The GPU Underflow Trap: Multiplying 1,000 probabilities together yields $0.01^{1000} = 10^{-2000} \to 0.0$ (`float32` crash). Taking the natural log turns products into sums: $$\ell(\theta) = \sum_{i=1}^n \ln P(x_i \mid \theta)$$ Because $\ln$ is strictly monotonic, $\arg\max \mathcal{L}(\theta) \equiv \arg\max \ell(\theta)$.

4. Master AI Derivation 1: Gaussian Noise $\implies$ Mean Squared Error (MSE Loss)

Suppose target $y_i = f(\mathbf{x}_i; \mathbf{w}) + \epsilon_i$ where noise $\epsilon_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$.

$$\ell(\mathbf{w}, \sigma^2) = \underbrace{-\frac{n}{2}\ln(2\pi\sigma^2)}_{\text{constant w.r.t weights } \mathbf{w}} - \underbrace{\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}}_{\text{constant scale}} \sum_{i=1}^n \underbrace{\Big(y_i - f(\mathbf{x}_i; \mathbf{w})\Big)^2}_{\text{squared error distance}}$$

Maximizing $\ell(\mathbf{w})$ with respect to weights $\mathbf{w}$ drops constants and flips the negative sign to minimize:

$$\mathbf{\arg\max_{\mathbf{w}} \ell(\mathbf{w}) = \arg\min_{\mathbf{w}} \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \Big(y_i - f(\mathbf{x}_i; \mathbf{w})\Big)^2 = \arg\min_{\mathbf{w}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}}(\mathbf{w})} \quad \blacksquare$$

5. Master AI Derivation 2: Bernoulli $\implies$ Cross-Entropy Loss

For a binary classifier predicting Cat ($y_i=1$) vs Dog ($y_i=0$) with model probability $\hat{p}_i$:

$$P(y_i \mid \mathbf{x}_i) = \hat{p}_i^{y_i} (1 - \hat{p}_i)^{1 - y_i}$$

Taking log and negating to minimize:

$$\mathbf{\mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}}(\mathbf{w}) = -\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \Big[ \underbrace{y_i \ln \hat{p}_i}_{\text{Cat penalty}} + \underbrace{(1 - y_i) \ln (1 - \hat{p}_i)}_{\text{Dog penalty}} \Big]} \quad \blacksquare$$

6. Concrete Numerical Walkthrough

Suppose Timmy flips a mystery coin 5 times: 4 Heads, 1 Tail ($D = \{H, H, T, H, H\}$).

  1. Likelihood: $\mathcal{L}(p) = p^4 (1 - p)^1$
  2. Log-Likelihood: $\ell(p) = 4\ln p + \ln(1 - p)$
  3. First Derivative: $\frac{d\ell}{dp} = \frac{4}{p} - \frac{1}{1 - p} = 0$
  4. Solve for $\hat{p}$: $4(1 - p) = p \implies 4 - 4p = p \implies 5p = 4 \implies \mathbf{\hat{p}_{\text{MLE}} = 0.80}$
  5. Second Derivative: $\frac{d^2\ell}{dp^2} = -\frac{4}{(0.8)^2} - \frac{1}{(0.2)^2} = -6.25 - 25 = -31.25 < 0$ (Confirmed maximum!)
