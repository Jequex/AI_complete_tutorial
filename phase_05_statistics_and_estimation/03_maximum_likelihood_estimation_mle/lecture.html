Topic 5.3: Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE)

The mathematical origin of AI loss functions: proving why Gaussian noise yields Mean Squared Error (MSE) and categorical distributions yield Cross-Entropy.

1. Executive Summary & The Archery Detective

In deep learning, we train models by minimizing objective loss functions like Mean Squared Error (MSE) and Cross-Entropy. These loss functions are not arbitrary inventions: they are the direct mathematical consequence of Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE).

The 12-Year-Old Analogy: The Archery Detective

You enter an archery field and see 10 arrows clustered tightly in the upper-right corner of a target (the Data). You didn't see the archer shoot. You only know there was wind blowing across the field (the unknown Parameter $\theta$).

  • Could the wind have blown to the south-west? Probability: $0.000001\%$.
  • Could the wind have been calm? Probability: $0.01\%$.
  • Could the wind have blown strongly north-east? Probability: $85\%$.

As a rational detective, you pick the north-east wind because it makes the observed arrow landings most likely! In AI, the arrows are your training dataset, and the wind is the weights of your neural network!

2. Probability vs Likelihood

Property Probability: $P(D \mid \theta)$ Likelihood: $L(\theta; D)$
What is fixed? Parameters $\theta$ are fixed constants Observed dataset $D$ is fixed
What is varying? Hypothetical future data outcomes $D$ Candidate parameter values $\theta$
Total Sum/Integral $\int P(D \mid \theta) dD = 1$ Does not sum or integrate to 1 over $\theta$

3. The Log-Likelihood Formulation

For independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) observations, the joint likelihood is a product:

$$L(\theta; D) = \prod_{i=1}^n P(x_i \mid \theta)$$

Multiplying thousands of probabilities causes hardware floating-point underflow ($0.01^{1000} \to 0.0$). Taking the natural log converts the product into an easily differentiable sum:

$$\mathbf{\ell(\theta) = \ln L(\theta; D) = \sum_{i=1}^n \ln P(x_i \mid \theta)}$$

Because $\ln(x)$ is strictly monotonic, maximizing $\ell(\theta)$ is identical to maximizing $L(\theta)$. In deep learning, we minimize the Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL):

$$\mathbf{\mathcal{L}_{\text{NLL}}(\theta) = -\sum_{i=1}^n \ln P(x_i \mid \theta)}$$

4. Master Derivation 1: Gaussian Likelihood $\implies$ Mean Squared Error (MSE)

Assume a regression target $y_i$ equals the network prediction $f(\mathbf{x}_i; \mathbf{w})$ plus Gaussian noise $\epsilon_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$:

$$P(y_i \mid \mathbf{x}_i; \mathbf{w}) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}} \exp\left( -\frac{(y_i - f(\mathbf{x}_i; \mathbf{w}))^2}{2\sigma^2} \right)$$

Forming the dataset log-likelihood:

$$\ell(\mathbf{w}, \sigma^2) = \sum_{i=1}^n \left[ -\frac{1}{2}\ln(2\pi\sigma^2) - \frac{(y_i - f(\mathbf{x}_i; \mathbf{w}))^2}{2\sigma^2} \right]$$ $$\ell(\mathbf{w}, \sigma^2) = -\frac{n}{2}\ln(2\pi\sigma^2) - \frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\sum_{i=1}^n (y_i - f(\mathbf{x}_i; \mathbf{w}))^2$$

The first term does not depend on weights $\mathbf{w}$. Dropping constants and flipping the sign to minimize:

$$\mathbf{\arg\max_{\mathbf{w}} \ell(\mathbf{w}) = \arg\min_{\mathbf{w}} \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n (y_i - f(\mathbf{x}_i; \mathbf{w}))^2 = \arg\min_{\mathbf{w}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}}(\mathbf{w})} \quad \blacksquare$$
The Fundamental Insight: Minimizing Mean Squared Error is mathematically equivalent to Maximum Likelihood Estimation under zero-mean Gaussian noise!

5. Master Derivation 2: Categorical Likelihood $\implies$ Cross-Entropy Loss

For binary classification where model outputs probability $\hat{p}_i = \sigma(f(\mathbf{x}_i))$:

$$P(y_i \mid \mathbf{x}_i) = \hat{p}_i^{y_i} (1 - \hat{p}_i)^{1 - y_i}$$

Taking the negative log-likelihood across all training samples:

$$\mathbf{\mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}}(\mathbf{w}) = -\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \Big[ y_i \ln\hat{p}_i + (1 - y_i)\ln(1 - \hat{p}_i) \Big]} \quad \blacksquare$$

For language models predicting token $k$ out of $K$ vocabulary classes with one-hot label $\mathbf{y}_i$:

$$\mathbf{\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}(\mathbf{w}) = -\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{k=1}^K y_{ik} \ln \hat{p}_{ik}} \quad \blacksquare$$
The Language Model Secret: Training ChatGPT or Llama on trillions of tokens is simply Maximum Likelihood Estimation of a multinomial categorical distribution!
