Topic 5.4: Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) Estimation & Regularization

Preventing overfitting through Bayesian prior beliefs: proving that Weight Decay is a Gaussian prior and Lasso sparsity is a Laplace prior.

1. Executive Summary & The Detective with Common Sense

In Topic 5.3, Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) fit weights purely to explain the training data. However, when data is small or noisy, MLE overfits wildly, exploding parameters to $\pm 100{,}000$ to memorize noise artifacts.

The 12-Year-Old Analogy: The Detective with Common Sense

A school window is found broken. A tiny metal feather lies on the sill.

  • The MLE Detective (No Prior): Calculates that a robotic mechanical pterodactyl from the year 3000 dropping from the sky fits the feather perfectly. Concludes: "A time-traveling robot broke the window!"
  • The MAP Detective (With Common Sense): Knows that time-traveling robots have a prior probability of almost $0$, while neighborhood kids playing baseball have a $95\%$ probability. Concludes: "A kid with a metallic toy threw a rock."

In AI, the Data Evidence is the Likelihood, and Common Sense (that weights should remain small and stable) is the Prior Distribution $P(\mathbf{w})$!

2. From Bayes' Theorem to the MAP Formulation

Bayes' Theorem for model weights $\mathbf{w}$ given observed data $D$:

$$P(\mathbf{w} \mid D) = \frac{P(D \mid \mathbf{w}) P(\mathbf{w})}{P(D)}$$

The marginal data evidence $P(D)$ is a constant with respect to weights $\mathbf{w}$. The Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) estimator finds the single mode (peak) of the posterior:

$$\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\text{MAP}} = \arg\max_{\mathbf{w}} P(\mathbf{w} \mid D) = \arg\max_{\mathbf{w}} \Big[ P(D \mid \mathbf{w}) P(\mathbf{w}) \Big]$$

Taking the natural logarithm:

$$\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\text{MAP}} = \arg\max_{\mathbf{w}} \Big[ \ln P(D \mid \mathbf{w}) + \ln P(\mathbf{w}) \Big]}$$

Flipping the sign to turn this into a minimization loss function:

$$\mathbf{\arg\min_{\mathbf{w}} \Big[ \underbrace{-\ln P(D \mid \mathbf{w})}_{\text{Data Fitting Loss (NLL)}} + \underbrace{\big(-\ln P(\mathbf{w})\big)}_{\text{Regularization Penalty}} \Big]}$$

3. Master Derivation 1: Gaussian Prior $\implies$ L2 Regularization (Weight Decay)

Assume an independent zero-mean Gaussian prior on all weights: $w_j \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_0^2)$:

$$P(\mathbf{w}) = \prod_{j=1}^d \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma_0^2}} \exp\left( -\frac{w_j^2}{2\sigma_0^2} \right)$$ $$\ln P(\mathbf{w}) = -\frac{d}{2}\ln(2\pi\sigma_0^2) - \frac{1}{2\sigma_0^2}\sum_{j=1}^d w_j^2 = -\frac{1}{2\sigma_0^2}\|\mathbf{w}\|_2^2 + \text{const}$$

Combining with the Gaussian regression likelihood (Mean Squared Error):

$$\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\text{MAP}} = \arg\min_{\mathbf{w}} \left[ \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n (y_i - f(\mathbf{x}_i; \mathbf{w}))^2 + \lambda \|\mathbf{w}\|_2^2 \right]}$$

where $\lambda = \frac{\sigma^2}{n \sigma_0^2}$.

The Deep Result: L2 regularization (Ridge regression / Weight Decay in PyTorch) is mathematically identical to MAP estimation under an independent Gaussian prior on the weights!

4. Master Derivation 2: Laplace Prior $\implies$ L1 Regularization (Lasso & Sparsity)

Assume an independent zero-mean Laplace prior: $P(w_j) = \frac{1}{2b} \exp\left(-\frac{|w_j|}{b}\right)$:

$$\ln P(\mathbf{w}) = -d\ln(2b) - \frac{1}{b}\sum_{j=1}^d |w_j| = -\frac{1}{b}\|\mathbf{w}\|_1 + \text{const}$$

The resulting MAP optimization problem is:

$$\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\text{MAP}} = \arg\min_{\mathbf{w}} \left[ \text{Loss}(D; \mathbf{w}) + \lambda \|\mathbf{w}\|_1 \right]}$$
Why L1 Drives Weights to Exact Zero: The Laplace prior has a sharp cusp at zero. In optimization geometry, the diamond corners of the L1 ball intersect the loss contours directly on the coordinate axes, forcing non-essential weights to become exactly zero (automatic feature selection and model compression)!

5. The Bayesian Continuum

Prior Belief $P(\mathbf{w})$ Negative Log-Prior Resulting Objective Practical Machine Learning Effect
Uniform Prior ($P = c$) $0$ (No penalty) Pure MLE Unconstrained weights, high risk of overfitting
Gaussian Prior $\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_0^2)$ $\frac{1}{2\sigma_0^2}\|\mathbf{w}\|_2^2$ L2 / Ridge / Weight Decay Smoothly shrinks weights toward zero
Laplace Prior $\text{Laplace}(0, b)$ $\frac{1}{b}\|\mathbf{w}\|_1$ L1 / Lasso Forces weights to exact zero (sparse models)
