Topic 5.2: Confidence Intervals, Hypothesis Testing & AI A/B Testing

Making rigorous data-driven decisions: bounding benchmark error bars, p-values, Type I/II trade-offs, and A/B testing model checkpoints.

1. Executive Summary & The Courtroom Trial Analogy

Suppose you evaluate a newly fine-tuned model (Model B) against your production baseline (Model A):

  • Model A: $82.0\%$ accuracy on 200 questions
  • Model B: $85.0\%$ accuracy on 200 questions

Is Model B genuinely more intelligent, or did it merely get lucky on an easier draw of questions? If you ship Model B based purely on this single number without testing for statistical significance, you risk shipping an inferior model.

The 12-Year-Old Analogy: The Courtroom Trial & The Rigged Coin

Imagine your friend flips a coin 10 times and gets 8 Heads. You suspect the coin is weighted.

  1. The Null Hypothesis ($H_0$, "Innocent until proven guilty"): We assume the coin is completely fair ($P = 0.50$).
  2. The Alternative Hypothesis ($H_1$, "Guilty"): The coin is unfair ($P > 0.50$).
  3. The $p$-value: How often would an honest coin produce 8 or more Heads purely by chance? By binomial probability, that chance is $5.5\%$.
  4. The Verdict: Because $5.5\% > 5\%$ (our standard reasonable-doubt threshold $\alpha = 0.05$), we do not have enough evidence to convict. We fail to reject $H_0$.

2. Derivation of the 95% Confidence Interval

By the Central Limit Theorem, the distribution of the sample mean $\bar{X}$ across repeated draws of size $n$ is Normal:

$$\bar{X} \sim \mathcal{N}\left(\mu, \, \frac{\sigma^2}{n}\right)$$

Standardizing into a standard normal variable $Z$:

$$Z = \frac{\bar{X} - \mu}{\sigma / \sqrt{n}} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$$

For a $95\%$ confidence level ($\alpha = 0.05$), the critical boundary on the standard normal curve is $z_{0.025} = 1.96$:

$$P\left( -1.96 \le \frac{\bar{X} - \mu}{\sigma / \sqrt{n}} \le +1.96 \right) = 0.95$$

Multiplying across by the Standard Error $\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}$ and isolating $\mu$:

$$\mathbf{P\left( \bar{X} - 1.96\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} \le \mu \le \bar{X} + 1.96\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} \right) = 0.95}$$

The resulting 95% Confidence Interval is:

$$\mathbf{\text{CI}_{95\%} = \left[ \bar{X} - 1.96\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}, \quad \bar{X} + 1.96\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} \right]}$$

4. What a $p$-value Is (and What It Is NOT)

Formal Definition:

$$\mathbf{p\text{-value} = P(\text{Observing data as or more extreme than } D \mid H_0 \text{ is true})}$$

Common Traps to Avoid:

  • The $p$-value is NOT the probability that the hypothesis is true ($P(H_0 \mid D)$ requires Bayesian inference!).
  • A tiny $p$-value does NOT mean the model improvement is large. It only means the improvement is statistically distinguishable from zero.

5. Real-World AI Case: A/B Testing Model Checkpoints

Suppose an engineering team evaluates whether a 4-bit quantized model (Model B) degrades user acceptance compared to the 16-bit baseline (Model A):

  • Model A: $1{,}000$ requests, $780$ thumbs-up ($\hat{p}_A = 0.780$)
  • Model B: $1{,}000$ requests, $730$ thumbs-up ($\hat{p}_B = 0.730$)

The 5-Step Hypothesis Test

  1. Hypotheses: $H_0: p_A = p_B$ vs $H_1: p_A > p_B$.
  2. Pooled Proportion: $\hat{p} = \frac{780 + 730}{2000} = 0.755$.
  3. Standard Error: $$\text{SE}_{\text{diff}} = \sqrt{(0.755)(0.245)\left(\frac{1}{1000} + \frac{1}{1000}\right)} \approx 0.01923$$
  4. $z$-Test Statistic: $$z = \frac{0.780 - 0.730}{0.01923} \approx \mathbf{+2.60}$$
  5. $p$-value: $p = 1 - \Phi(2.60) \approx \mathbf{0.0047} \quad (0.47\%)$.
Verdict: Because $p = 0.0047 < 0.01$, we reject the null hypothesis. The performance loss of the 4-bit model is real and significant. Do not ship without quantization-aware fine-tuning!
