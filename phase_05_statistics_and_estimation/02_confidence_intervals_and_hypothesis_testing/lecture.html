Topic 5.2: Confidence Intervals, Hypothesis Testing & AI A/B Testing

Separating genuine algorithmic breakthroughs from the illusions of lucky sample noise.

1. Executive Summary & Intuitive Foundations

Suppose an AI engineer fine-tunes a model (Model B) and evaluates it against the baseline (Model A) on 200 customer support chats:

  • Model A: $82.0\%$ accuracy (164 / 200)
  • Model B: $85.0\%$ accuracy (170 / 200)

Is Model B genuinely smarter, or did it just get a lucky batch of easier questions? If you deploy Model B to millions of users without statistical verification, you risk catastrophic production regressions!

🧒 The 12-Year-Old Courtroom Trial: Timmy's "Lucky" Coin

Imagine Timmy brings a coin to school and flips 8 Heads in 10 flips. Maya claims the coin is rigged!

In statistics, we hold a formal Courtroom Trial:

  1. The Null Hypothesis ($H_0$, "Innocent until proven guilty"): We assume the coin is completely fair ($P(\text{Heads}) = 0.50$). Timmy is innocent of cheating.
  2. The Alternative Hypothesis ($H_1$, "Guilty as charged"): Maya's claim that the coin is rigged ($P(\text{Heads}) > 0.50$).
  3. The Evidence: 8 Heads in 10 flips.
  4. The $p$-value: If the coin was truly fair, the probability of getting 8 or more heads purely by random chance is about $5.5\%$ ($p = 0.0547$).
  5. The Verdict ($\alpha = 0.05$): Our reasonable doubt threshold is $5\%$. Because $5.5\% > 5\%$, the evidence is not quite strong enough to convict. Verdict: Fail to reject $H_0$.

🧒 Confidence Intervals as Fishing Nets

Imagine you are fishing in a foggy lake for a rare golden fish named $\mu$ (the True Population Mean). The fish stays at a fixed location, but the water is murky.

You cast a wide fishing net (the confidence interval). Because each cast is influenced by random waves (sample noise), each net lands in a slightly different place.

A 95% Confidence Interval means: if you cast the net 100 times, 95 of those nets will successfully capture the fish, and 5 nets will miss!

3. Confidence Intervals: Constructing the Net

Line-by-Line Breakdown: The 95% Normal Confidence Interval

$$\mathbf{\text{CI}_{95\%} = \left[ \underbrace{\bar{X}}_{\substack{\text{Center of net} \\ \text{(sample mean)}}} - \underbrace{1.96}_{\substack{\text{95\% normal} \\ \text{multiplier}}} \times \underbrace{\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}}_{\substack{\text{Standard} \\ \text{Error}}}, \quad \bar{X} + 1.96 \times \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} \right]}$$

🧒 What the math is actually doing: By the Central Limit Theorem, sample averages follow a bell curve. $95\%$ of all samples land within $1.96$ standard errors of the truth. Reversing the inequality creates our net!

Small Samples & Student's $t$-Distribution

$$\mathbf{\text{CI}_{1-\alpha} = \left[ \bar{X} - \underbrace{t_{\alpha/2, \, n-1}}_{\substack{\text{Wider multiplier for} \\ \text{small sample uncertainty}}} \times \frac{S}{\sqrt{n}}, \quad \bar{X} + t_{\alpha/2, \, n-1} \times \frac{S}{\sqrt{n}} \right]}$$

When true spread $\sigma$ is unknown, estimating it with sample spread $S$ introduces extra wobble. The Student's $t$-distribution uses heavier tails to make the net wider and safer.

5. Real-World AI Case Study: A/B Testing 4-Bit Model Quantization

We test uncompressed 16-bit Model A against compressed 4-bit Model B on $1{,}000$ live requests each:

  • Model A: $n_A = 1{,}000$, Thumbs-Up $x_A = 780$ ($\hat{p}_A = 78.0\%$)
  • Model B: $n_B = 1{,}000$, Thumbs-Up $x_B = 730$ ($\hat{p}_B = 73.0\%$)

Step-by-Step Statistical Analysis:

  1. Pooled Proportion under $H_0$: $$\hat{p} = \frac{780 + 730}{1000 + 1000} = \frac{1510}{2000} = \mathbf{0.755}$$
  2. Standard Error of Difference: $$\text{SE}_{\text{diff}} = \sqrt{(0.755)(0.245)\left(\frac{1}{1000} + \frac{1}{1000}\right)} = \sqrt{0.00036995} \approx \mathbf{0.01923}$$
  3. $Z$-Test Statistic: $$Z = \frac{0.780 - 0.730}{0.01923} = \frac{0.050}{0.01923} \approx \mathbf{+2.60}$$
  4. Two-Tailed $p$-value: $$p = 2 \times (1 - \Phi(2.60)) \approx 2 \times 0.00466 \approx \mathbf{0.0093 \quad (0.93\%)}$$
  5. Verdict: Because $p = 0.0093 < 0.01$, we reject $H_0$ at the 99% confidence level. Quantization caused a statistically significant drop in user satisfaction!

6. Concrete Numerical Walkthrough: Token Latency SLA

A model's token latency was measured across $n = 64$ runs: $\bar{X} = 42.5\text{ ms}$, $S = 8.0\text{ ms}$. Target SLA: under $45.0\text{ ms}$.

  • $\text{SE}(\bar{X}) = \frac{8.0}{\sqrt{64}} = \frac{8.0}{8} = 1.0\text{ ms}$
  • $\text{Margin of Error} = 1.96 \times 1.0 = 1.96\text{ ms}$
  • $\text{CI}_{95\%} = [42.5 - 1.96, \, 42.5 + 1.96] = \mathbf{[40.54\text{ ms}, \, 44.46\text{ ms}]}$
  • Conclusion: The entire interval is strictly below $45.0\text{ ms}$, confirming SLA compliance with 95% confidence!
