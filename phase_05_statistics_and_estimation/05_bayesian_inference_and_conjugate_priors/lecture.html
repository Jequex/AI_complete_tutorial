Topic 5.5: Bayesian Inference & Conjugate Priors

Maintaining living belief distributions to endow AI systems with calibrated uncertainty.

1. Executive Summary: Beyond Single-Number Guesses

Standard neural networks pick a single best weight vector $\hat{\mathbf{w}}$. But on novel, bizarre data, single-point models make wildly confident errors because they have zero self-awareness of their own uncertainty.

Full Bayesian Inference maintains the complete probability distribution $P(\mathbf{w} \mid D)$ across all possible models. Every time a new data point arrives, our belief distribution updates, sharpens, and concentrates!

🧒 The 12-Year-Old Analogy: Maya's Video Game "Belief Meter"

Imagine Maya plays a competitive online game. The matchmaking server tracks her skill $\theta \in [0, 1]$:

  1. Before any games (The Prior): The server has a broad, gentle curve centered at $50\%$ ($\text{Beta}(2, 2)$). It doesn't know if Maya is a pro or a beginner.
  2. Match 1: Maya wins!
    • Gullible MLE: Declares $\hat{\theta} = 100\%$, assuming Maya is an invincible goddess who will never lose a game!
    • Wise Bayesian Belief Meter: Gently nudges its confidence slider to $\approx 60\%$, knowing 1 game could easily be beginner's luck.
  3. After 10 matches (8 wins, 2 losses): The curve shifts toward $75\%$ and grows narrower and taller.
  4. After 100 matches (75 wins, 25 losses): The belief curve becomes a razor-sharp spike at $75\% \pm 3\%$.

🧒 The Infinite Committee Vote (Posterior Predictive)

When predicting a label, Bayesian AI does not trust a single model. It convenes a committee of thousands of plausible models weighted by the posterior $P(\mathbf{w} \mid D)$. If they agree, the system acts with high confidence. If they disagree, the AI alerts the human: "High epistemic uncertainty!"

3. The Magic of Conjugate Priors: Calculus Without Integrals

A prior is conjugate to a likelihood if the resulting posterior belongs to the exact same algebraic family. Updating your belief becomes pure addition!

Line-by-Line Breakdown: The Beta-Binomial Update

$$P(\theta \mid D) \propto \underbrace{\Big[ \theta^k (1 - \theta)^{n - k} \Big]}_{\text{Binomial likelihood (k wins, n-k losses)}} \times \underbrace{\Big[ \theta^{\alpha - 1} (1 - \theta)^{\beta - 1} \Big]}_{\text{Beta prior (\alpha wins, \beta losses)}}$$ $$\mathbf{\theta \mid D \sim \text{Beta}\Big(\underbrace{\alpha + k}_{\text{new successes}}, \quad \underbrace{\beta + (n - k)}_{\text{new failures}}\Big)} \quad \blacksquare$$

Expected Posterior Mean & Laplace's Rule of Succession

$$\mathbf{\mathbb{E}[\theta \mid D] = \frac{\alpha_{\text{new}}}{\alpha_{\text{new}} + \beta_{\text{new}}} = \frac{\alpha + k}{\alpha + \beta + n}}$$

Starting from an uninformative uniform prior ($\alpha=1, \beta=1$), the chance that the next event succeeds is $\frac{k + 1}{n + 2}$ (Laplace's Rule of Succession), which safely prevents $0\%$ or $100\%$ extremes.

4. Concrete Numerical Walkthrough: The 3-Click Recommender Trap

A new movie thumbnail is tested. Prior belief: $\text{Beta}(\alpha_0 = 2, \beta_0 = 2)$ ($\approx 50\%$). It is shown $n = 3$ times and clicked $k = 3$ times.

  • Pure MLE: $\frac{3}{3} = \mathbf{1.00 \quad (100\% \text{ - Dangerous Overconfidence!})}$
  • MAP (Mode): $\frac{\alpha_{\text{new}} - 1}{\alpha_{\text{new}} + \beta_{\text{new}} - 2} = \frac{5 - 1}{5 + 2 - 2} = \frac{4}{5} = \mathbf{0.80 \quad (80\%)}$
  • Full Bayes (Mean): $\frac{\alpha + k}{\alpha + \beta + n} = \frac{2 + 3}{2 + 2 + 3} = \frac{5}{7} \approx \mathbf{0.714 \quad (71.4\%)}$
  • Uncertainty: Carrying the posterior standard deviation ($\approx 16\%$) tells the system not to bet the entire company on 3 data points!

5. Streaming Sequential Bayesian Updating

Bayesian learning is completely online and recursive: past posteriors become future priors!

  • Day 0: $\text{Beta}(2, 2) \implies$ Mean CTR = $50.0\%$
  • Day 1 (3 clicks, 1 skip): $\text{Beta}(2 + 3, \, 2 + 1) = \text{Beta}(5, 3) \implies$ Mean CTR = $5/8 = 62.5\%$
  • Day 2 (4 clicks, 0 skips): $\text{Beta}(5 + 4, \, 3 + 0) = \text{Beta}(9, 3) \implies$ Mean CTR = $9/12 = 75.0\%$
