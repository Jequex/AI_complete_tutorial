Topic 5.5: Bayesian Inference & Conjugate Priors

Beyond point estimates: maintaining full posterior distributions, posterior predictive ensembles, Beta-Binomial conjugate updating, and the intractability of deep networks.

1. Executive Summary & The Video Game Skill Tracker

In point estimation (MLE and MAP), models output a single weight vector. But when facing rare out-of-distribution inputs, point models suffer from dangerous overconfidence. Full Bayesian Inference maintains the entire probability curve over possible weights: $P(\mathbf{w} \mid D)$.

The 12-Year-Old Analogy: The Live Video Game Skill Tracker

A new player enters an online lobby. The server estimates their win-rate $\theta$:

  1. Before any matches (The Prior): The server has a broad, flat bell curve centered at $50\%$. Uncertainty is massive.
  2. After 10 matches (8 wins, 2 losses): The curve shifts toward $80\%$ and grows narrower (confidence builds).
  3. After 100 matches (82 wins, 18 losses): The curve sharpens into a tight spike centered at $82\%$.

The server does not pretend to hold a single magic number; it maintains a living distribution of belief that updates with every single match!

2. The Bayesian Framework & The Predictive Distribution

Bayes' Theorem for continuous parameter distributions:

$$P(\mathbf{w} \mid D) = \frac{P(D \mid \mathbf{w}) P(\mathbf{w})}{\int P(D \mid \mathbf{w}) P(\mathbf{w}) d\mathbf{w}}$$

The Posterior Predictive Distribution (The Infinite Ensemble)

To make a prediction for a new test input $\mathbf{x}_{\text{new}}$, Bayesian AI integrates over all possible models:

$$\mathbf{P(y_{\text{new}} \mid \mathbf{x}_{\text{new}}, D) = \int P(y_{\text{new}} \mid \mathbf{x}_{\text{new}}, \mathbf{w}) P(\mathbf{w} \mid D) d\mathbf{w}}$$

This acts as an infinite ensemble of neural networks! If the models disagree, the system accurately reports high epistemic uncertainty.

3. The Algebraic Magic of Conjugate Priors

Definition: A prior distribution $P(\theta)$ is called conjugate to a likelihood $P(D \mid \theta)$ if the resulting posterior $P(\theta \mid D)$ belongs to the exact same distribution family as the prior!

When using conjugate priors, Bayesian updating requires zero calculus: you simply add observation counts directly to the prior parameters!

4. Master Derivation: The Beta-Binomial Conjugate Model

We want to infer an unknown success probability $\theta \in [0, 1]$ (e.g. ad click rate or LLM benchmark accuracy).

1. Prior: We choose a $\text{Beta}(\alpha, \beta)$ prior:

$$P(\theta) \propto \theta^{\alpha - 1} (1 - \theta)^{\beta - 1}$$

2. Likelihood: We observe $k$ successes in $n$ independent trials (Binomial):

$$P(D \mid \theta) \propto \theta^k (1 - \theta)^{n - k}$$

3. Posterior Calculation: Multiplying prior and likelihood:

$$P(\theta \mid D) \propto \Big[ \theta^k (1 - \theta)^{n - k} \Big] \cdot \Big[ \theta^{\alpha - 1} (1 - \theta)^{\beta - 1} \Big]$$ $$\mathbf{P(\theta \mid D) \propto \theta^{(\alpha + k) - 1} (1 - \theta)^{(\beta + n - k) - 1}}$$

This is identically another Beta distribution:

$$\mathbf{\theta \mid D \sim \text{Beta}(\alpha + k, \quad \beta + n - k)} \quad \blacksquare$$

5. Streaming Bayesian Updates in $O(1)$ Memory

Because the posterior is also a Beta distribution, updating is completely sequential. Today's posterior becomes tomorrow's prior:

$$\text{Beta}(\alpha, \beta) \xrightarrow{+k \text{ wins, } +(n-k) \text{ losses}} \text{Beta}(\alpha + k, \, \beta + n - k)$$

The posterior expected value of the parameter is simply:

$$\mathbb{E}[\theta \mid D] = \mathbf{\frac{\alpha + k}{(\alpha + k) + (\beta + n - k)}}$$

6. Why Deep Learning Uses Approximations

The High-Dimensional Integral Wall

For a billion-parameter transformer, calculating the denominator $P(D) = \int P(D|\mathbf{w})P(\mathbf{w})d\mathbf{w}$ requires integrating over a 1-billion-dimensional space, which is analytically and numerically impossible.

Therefore, modern deep learning uses powerful approximations:

  • Variational Inference (VI): Approximates the posterior with an analytical Gaussian family $q_\phi(\mathbf{w})$ by optimizing the ELBO (the foundation of VAEs!).
  • Monte Carlo Dropout (MC Dropout): Keeps dropout active at test-time to generate samples from an approximate Bayesian posterior.
