Problem 1: Multi-Day Beta-Binomial Sequential Updating in Recommender Systems

Easy

A recommender starts with an uninformative prior $\theta \sim \text{Beta}(1, 1)$ on user Click-Through Rate (CTR).

  • Day 1: $8$ clicks out of $10$ impressions ($k_1 = 8, n_1 = 10$).
  • Day 2: $4$ clicks out of $10$ impressions ($k_2 = 4, n_2 = 10$).
  1. Compute the updated $\text{Beta}(\alpha_1, \beta_1)$ parameters and posterior mean after Day 1.
  2. Sequentially update using Day 1's posterior as the prior for Day 2 to find $\text{Beta}(\alpha_2, \beta_2)$.
  3. Compute the final posterior mean and variance after Day 2.
  4. Verify that processing Day 1 and Day 2 together in one 20-impression batch gives the identical result.
Click to Reveal Step-by-Step Solution

1. Day 1 Update

$$\alpha_1 = 1 + 8 = \mathbf{9}, \quad \beta_1 = 1 + 2 = \mathbf{3}$$ $$\mathbb{E}[\theta \mid D_1] = \frac{9}{9 + 3} = \frac{9}{12} = \mathbf{0.750} \quad (75.0\%)$$

2. Day 2 Sequential Update

$$\alpha_2 = 9 + 4 = \mathbf{13}, \quad \beta_2 = 3 + 6 = \mathbf{9}$$

3. Day 2 Final Statistics

$$\mathbb{E}[\theta \mid D_{1,2}] = \frac{13}{13 + 9} = \frac{13}{22} \approx \mathbf{0.5909} \quad (59.09\%)$$ $$\operatorname{Var}(\theta) = \frac{13 \times 9}{(22)^2 \times 23} \approx \mathbf{0.01051} \implies \text{Std Dev} \approx \mathbf{10.25\%}$$

4. Batch Invariance

$$\alpha = 1 + 12 = 13, \quad \beta = 1 + 8 = 9 \quad (\text{Exact Match!})$$

Problem 2: Posterior Predictive Probability & Laplace's Rule of Succession

Medium

An AI agent passes $k$ consecutive safety tests out of $n$ trials ($k = n$). We assume prior $\theta \sim \text{Beta}(1, 1)$.

  1. Write the posterior distribution $\theta \mid D$.
  2. Write the integral for the probability of success on trial $n+1$: $P(X_{n+1} = 1 \mid D) = \int_0^1 \theta P(\theta \mid D) d\theta$.
  3. Evaluate the integral to derive Laplace's Rule of Succession: $\frac{k + 1}{n + 2}$.
  4. Compare the prediction of MLE vs Laplace's Rule when $k = 3, n = 3$.
Click to Reveal Step-by-Step Solution

1. Posterior Distribution

$$\theta \mid D \sim \text{Beta}(1 + k, \quad 1 + n - k)$$

2. Predictive Integral as Expectation

$$P(X_{n+1} = 1 \mid D) = \int_0^1 \theta P(\theta \mid D) d\theta = \mathbb{E}[\theta \mid D]$$

3. Laplace's Rule of Succession

$$P(X_{n+1} = 1 \mid D) = \frac{(1 + k)}{(1 + k) + (1 + n - k)} = \mathbf{\frac{k + 1}{n + 2}} \quad \blacksquare$$

4. Comparison for $k=3, n=3$

MLE vs Bayesian Wisdom:
  • MLE: $\frac{3}{3} = \mathbf{100\%}$ (Claims failure is physically impossible).
  • Laplace's Rule (Bayes): $\frac{3 + 1}{3 + 2} = \mathbf{80\%}$ (Acknowledge that on small data, failure is still possible with $20\%$ chance!).

Problem 3: Completing the Square: Gaussian-Gaussian Conjugate Derivation

Challenging

Prior: $\mu \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_0, \sigma_0^2)$. Likelihood for single observation $x_1$: $x_1 \mid \mu \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$.

  1. Write the product $P(x_1 \mid \mu) P(\mu)$ in exponential form.
  2. Complete the square in the exponent with respect to $\mu$.
  3. Prove that posterior $\mu \mid x_1 \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_1, \sigma_1^2)$ where $\frac{1}{\sigma_1^2} = \frac{1}{\sigma_0^2} + \frac{1}{\sigma^2}$ and $\mu_1 = \sigma_1^2 \left(\frac{\mu_0}{\sigma_0^2} + \frac{x_1}{\sigma^2}\right)$.
Click to Reveal Step-by-Step Solution

1. Joint Exponential Form

$$P(\mu \mid x_1) \propto \exp\left( -\frac{1}{2}\left[ \frac{(\mu - x_1)^2}{\sigma^2} + \frac{(\mu - \mu_0)^2}{\sigma_0^2} \right] \right)$$

2. Completing the Square

$$\frac{\mu^2 - 2\mu x_1 + x_1^2}{\sigma^2} + \frac{\mu^2 - 2\mu \mu_0 + \mu_0^2}{\sigma_0^2} = \mu^2\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_0^2} + \frac{1}{\sigma^2}\right) - 2\mu\left(\frac{\mu_0}{\sigma_0^2} + \frac{x_1}{\sigma^2}\right) + C$$

Define $\frac{1}{\sigma_1^2} = \frac{1}{\sigma_0^2} + \frac{1}{\sigma^2}$ and $\mu_1 = \sigma_1^2\left(\frac{\mu_0}{\sigma_0^2} + \frac{x_1}{\sigma^2}\right)$:

$$= \frac{1}{\sigma_1^2}\left[ \mu^2 - 2\mu\mu_1 \right] = \frac{(\mu - \mu_1)^2}{\sigma_1^2} + \text{const}$$

3. Conclusion

$$\mathbf{\mu \mid x_1 \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_1, \, \sigma_1^2)} \quad \blacksquare$$
Precision Addition Principle: $$\tau_{\text{post}} = \tau_{\text{prior}} + \tau_{\text{data}}$$ Every observed sample adds precision, strictly shrinking posterior uncertainty!
