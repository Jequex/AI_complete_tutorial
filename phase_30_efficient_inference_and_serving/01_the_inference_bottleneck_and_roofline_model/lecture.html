Topic 30.1

The Inference Bottleneck & The Roofline Model

Prefill vs. Decode dynamics, Arithmetic Intensity, Roofline performance ceilings, and the $4 \cdot n_{\text{layers}} \cdot n_{\text{heads}} \cdot d_{\text{head}} \cdot b \cdot s$ KV cache footprint.

1. The 12-Year-Old Intuition

Imagine you manage an elite translation library with a massive master dictionary that weighs 140 pounds.

Two distinct patrons walk through your doors:

  • Patron A (The Prefill Customer): Hands you an entire 1,000-word essay. You lift the 140-pound dictionary onto your desk once, flip furiously between pages for 3 solid minutes, compare grammar rules, and digest the whole text. Your brain (compute) is working at 100% capacity. You are compute-bound.
  • Patron B (The Decode Customer): Asks you to write a novel, but allows you to reveal only one single word at a time. To write word 1, you haul the 140-pound book from the shelf to your desk, check one rule, write "The", and put the book back. To write word 2, you haul the 140-pound book across the room again, write "cat", and put it back. To write 500 words, you carry that book 500 times! Your brain isn't tired at all; your back and arms are broken from hauling dead weight. You are memory-bandwidth bound.

This is the fundamental reality of Transformer inference: Prefill processes all prompt tokens in parallel with high compute efficiency, while Decode generates one token at a time, moving entire multi-gigabyte weight tensors across the GPU bus for a single arithmetic operation per weight!

2. Mathematical Symbol Decoder

Symbol Name Units / Domain Physical / Engineering Interpretation
$P_{\text{peak}}$ Peak Compute TFLOP/s Maximum theoretical floating-point execution speed of GPU Tensor Cores.
$B_{\text{mem}}$ Memory Bandwidth TB/s Physical transmission ceiling between High-Bandwidth Memory (HBM) and on-chip SRAM.
$I$ Arithmetic Intensity FLOPs / Byte Ratio of floating-point computations executed per byte of DRAM memory transferred.
$I^*$ Roofline Ridge Point FLOPs / Byte Critical threshold $I^* = P_{\text{peak}} / B_{\text{mem}}$ separating memory-bound from compute-bound regimes.
$\text{Mem}_{\text{KV}}$ KV Cache Footprint Bytes (GB) Memory allocated to Key and Value activations: $2 \cdot e \cdot n_{\text{layers}} \cdot n_{\text{heads}} \cdot d_{\text{head}} \cdot b \cdot s$.
$b$ Batch Size Sequences Number of concurrent sequences or user streams processed in a forward pass.

3. The Williams Roofline Model

The attainable execution speed $P$ on any processor is bounded by:

$$P(I) = \min\left(P_{\text{peak}}, \; B_{\text{mem}} \cdot I\right)$$

Hardware Ridge Point Comparisons (16-bit Precision)

NVIDIA A100 (80GB HBM2e): Peak FP16 compute is 312 TFLOP/s, Memory Bandwidth is 2.039 TB/s:

$$I^*_{\text{A100}} = \frac{312 \times 10^{12} \text{ FLOP/s}}{2.039 \times 10^{12} \text{ Bytes/s}} \approx \mathbf{153 \text{ FLOPs/Byte}}$$

NVIDIA H100 SXM5 (80GB HBM3): Peak FP16 compute is 989 TFLOP/s, Memory Bandwidth is 3.35 TB/s:

$$I^*_{\text{H100}} = \frac{989 \times 10^{12} \text{ FLOP/s}}{3.35 \times 10^{12} \text{ Bytes/s}} \approx \mathbf{295 \text{ FLOPs/Byte}}$$

If an operation executes with intensity $I < I^*$, it is trapped on the slanted roofline slope: tensor cores are starved for data and stall on memory reads.

4. Prefill vs. Decode Intensity Derivation

For a weight tensor $W \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{in}} \times d_{\text{out}}}$ multiplying input $X \in \mathbb{R}^{b \times s \times d_{\text{in}}}$ in 16-bit precision ($e = 2$ bytes):

$$\text{FLOPs} = 2 \cdot b \cdot s \cdot d_{\text{in}} \cdot d_{\text{out}}, \qquad \text{Weight Bytes} = 2 \cdot d_{\text{in}} \cdot d_{\text{out}}$$ $$I \approx \frac{2 \cdot b \cdot s \cdot d_{\text{in}} \cdot d_{\text{out}}}{2 \cdot d_{\text{in}} \cdot d_{\text{out}}} = \mathbf{b \cdot s \quad \text{FLOPs/Byte}}$$
  • Prefill ($s = S_{\text{prompt}} = 2048, b = 1$): $$I_{\text{prefill}} \approx 2048 \text{ FLOPs/Byte} \gg I^*_{\text{H100}} (295) \implies \textbf{Deeply Compute-Bound}$$
  • Decode ($s = 1, b = 1$): $$I_{\text{decode}} \approx 1 \text{ FLOP/Byte} \ll I^*_{\text{H100}} (295) \implies \textbf{Severely Memory-Bound}$$ $$\text{Attainable Performance} = 3.35 \text{ TB/s} \times 1 \text{ FLOP/Byte} = 3.35 \text{ TFLOP/s}$$ $$\text{Tensor Core Utilization} = \frac{3.35}{989} \approx \mathbf{0.34\%}$$

5. The KV Cache Memory Formula

To avoid recomputing historical keys and values during autoregressive token generation, past $K$ and $V$ tensors are stored in VRAM:

$$\boxed{\text{Mem}_{\text{KV}} = 4 \cdot n_{\text{layers}} \cdot n_{\text{heads}} \cdot d_{\text{head}} \cdot b \cdot s \quad \text{Bytes (in FP16)}}$$

The Context Scaling Crisis

For LLaMA-65B ($n_{\text{layers}}=80, n_{\text{heads}}=64, d_{\text{head}}=128$), each token costs:

$$\text{Footprint per token} = 4 \times 80 \times 64 \times 128 = 2,621,440 \text{ Bytes} \approx 2.62 \text{ MB}$$

At batch size $b=64$ and context $s=4096$, the KV cache consumes 687.2 GB of GPU RAM—requiring 9 high-end GPUs just to hold past activations!

6. The 4-Way Serving Tradeoff

Production inference architectures operate on an interconnected tradeoff frontier:

  • Latency (TTFT & ITL): Minimized when batch size $b=1$, but results in massive hardware under-utilization.
  • Throughput (TPS): Maximized when $b \ge I^*$, but causes queuing delays and high per-token latency.
  • Memory (VRAM): Scales linearly with $b \cdot s$. Out-of-memory triggers request throttling or CPU swapping.
  • Cost ($/1M Tokens): Driven by hardware count. Higher throughput directly reduces amortization cost per query.
