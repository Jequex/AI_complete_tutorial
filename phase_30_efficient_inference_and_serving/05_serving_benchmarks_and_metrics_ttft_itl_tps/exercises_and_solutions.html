Topic 30.5: Exercises & Solutions

Quantitative problem sets on Little's Law, tail latency percentiles ($p50, p90, p99$), and Poisson queue stability.

Exercise 1: Little's Law Applied to Cluster VRAM Sizing

An API experiences arrival rate $\lambda = 25 \text{ req/s}$, average $\text{TTFT} = 0.20 \text{ s}$, average $150 \text{ generated tokens}$, and $\text{ITL} = 40 \text{ ms/tok}$. Average context uses 575 tokens at 320 KB/token.

  1. Compute total turnaround time $W$.
  2. Compute average concurrent requests $L$ using Little's Law.
  3. Compute the total cluster VRAM required for KV caching in steady state.
View Complete Step-by-Step Solution

1. Turnaround Time $W$

$$W = \text{TTFT} + (150 \times 0.040) = 0.20 + 6.00 = \mathbf{6.20 \text{ seconds}}$$

2. Average Concurrency $L$

$$L = \lambda \cdot W = 25 \times 6.20 = \mathbf{155 \text{ concurrent requests}}$$

3. Steady-State KV Memory Footprint

$$\text{Per-request memory} = 575 \times 320 \text{ KB} = 184 \text{ MB} = 0.184 \text{ GB}$$ $$\text{Total VRAM} = 155 \times 0.184 \text{ GB} = \mathbf{28.52 \text{ GB}}$$

Exercise 2: Diagnosing Tail Latency Outliers ($p50, p90, p99$)

A benchmark of 20 requests has median $p50 = 139 \text{ ms}$ and mean $536 \text{ ms}$, but the worst 3 requests take 850 ms, 2400 ms, and 5200 ms. Evaluate contractual compliance against an SLA of $p90 \le 200 \text{ ms}$.

View Complete Step-by-Step Solution

For $N = 20$ samples, the 90th percentile corresponds to the 18th value ($p90 = \mathbf{850 \text{ ms}}$).

Since $850 \text{ ms} > 200 \text{ ms}$, the service fails the SLA. The mean of 536 ms was pulled up by extreme outliers ($p99 = 5200 \text{ ms}$), demonstrating why tracking tail percentiles is mandatory in real-time inference.

Exercise 3: Queue Stability Under Poisson Traffic

For service rate $\mu = 10 \text{ req/s}$, compute average waiting time $W_q$ for $\lambda = 5$ vs $\lambda = 9$, and find the maximum load to guarantee $W_q \le 100 \text{ ms}$.

View Complete Step-by-Step Solution

Queue wait formula: $W_q = \frac{\lambda}{\mu(\mu - \lambda)}$.

  • $\lambda = 5$: $W_q = \frac{5}{10(5)} = \mathbf{0.10 \text{ s}} \quad (100 \text{ ms})$
  • $\lambda = 9$: $W_q = \frac{9}{10(1)} = \mathbf{0.90 \text{ s}} \quad (900 \text{ ms})$ ($9\times$ surge!)

To ensure $W_q \le 0.10 \text{ s}$, the maximum arrival rate is $\lambda^* = \mathbf{5.0 \text{ req/s}}$ ($\rho \le 50\%$).
