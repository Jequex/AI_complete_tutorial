Topic 25.5: Exercises & Solutions — Flow Matching & Rectified Flows

Euler truncation errors, Continuity Equation solutions, Fubini CFM proofs, and kinetic action minimization.

Problem 1: Straight-Line Kinematics & Euler Truncation Easy

For straight-line path $x_t = (1 - t)x_0 + tx_1$, evaluate velocity $v(t)$, acceleration $a(t)$, and explain why the local Euler truncation error vanishes.

View Complete Solution

$$v(t) = \frac{dx_t}{dt} = x_1 - x_0, \quad a(t) = \frac{dv}{dt} = \mathbf{0}$$

In Taylor series $x_{t+\Delta t} = x_t + \Delta t \cdot v(t) + \frac{(\Delta t)^2}{2} a(t) + \dots$, since $a(t) \equiv \mathbf{0}$ and all higher derivatives vanish, the truncation error is identically zero. This guarantees that with straight paths, 1-step integration with $\Delta t = 1.0$ is exact!

Problem 2: Continuity Equation Velocity for Expanding Gaussians Medium

Find the velocity field $v(x, t)$ satisfying $\frac{\partial p_t}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial (p_t v)}{\partial x} = 0$ for a Gaussian with expanding standard deviation $\sigma_t = 1 + t$.

View Complete Solution

Evaluating $\frac{\partial p_t}{\partial t} = p_t(x) \left(-\frac{1}{\sigma_t} + \frac{x^2}{\sigma_t^3}\right)$ and matching against $\frac{\partial (p_t v)}{\partial x}$ with linear ansatz $v(x, t) = c(t)x$:

$$v(x, t) = \frac{x}{\sigma_t} = \frac{x}{1 + t}$$

At $x=0$, $v=0$, and probability mass symmetrically expands outward as time progresses.

Problem 3: Fubini's Equivalence Proof for CFM Hard

Prove using Fubini's theorem that $\nabla_\theta L_{CFM}(\theta) \equiv \nabla_\theta L_{FM}(\theta)$.

View Complete Solution

Expanding the cross term of $\nabla_\theta L_{CFM}(\theta)$:

$$\int_0^1 \int (\nabla_\theta v_\theta(x, t)) \left[ \int\int u_t(x \mid x_0, x_1) \frac{p_t(x \mid x_0, x_1) p(x_0, x_1)}{p_t(x)} dx_0 dx_1 \right] p_t(x) \, dx \, dt$$

The term in brackets is precisely the definition of the marginal velocity $u_t(x) = \mathbb{E}[u_t(x \mid x_0, x_1)]$. Hence:

$$\nabla_\theta L_{CFM}(\theta) \equiv \nabla_\theta L_{FM}(\theta)$$
