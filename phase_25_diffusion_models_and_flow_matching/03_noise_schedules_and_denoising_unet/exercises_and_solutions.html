Problem 1: Cosine Schedule Boundary Values Easy

For $f(t) = \cos^2\left(\frac{t/T + s}{1 + s} \frac{\pi}{2}\right)$ with $s = 0.008$ and $T = 1000$:

  1. Compute $f(0)$ and $\bar{\alpha}_0$.
  2. Compute $f(T)$ and $\bar{\alpha}_T$.
  3. Why does setting $s = 0$ cause undesirable behavior?
View Complete Solution

Step 1: $\theta_0 = \frac{0.008}{1.008}\frac{\pi}{2} \approx 0.01247 \implies f(0) = \cos^2(0.01247) \approx 0.99984 \implies \bar{\alpha}_0 = 1.0$.

Step 2: At $t = T$, $\theta_T = \frac{\pi}{2} \implies f(T) = \cos^2(\pi/2) = 0 \implies \bar{\alpha}_T = 0.0$. Exactly 100% of signal is destroyed.

Step 3: If $s = 0$, $\frac{d}{dt}\cos^2(0) = 0$, making initial $\beta_1 \approx 0$. An offset $s > 0$ guarantees non-zero initial variance.

Problem 2: Analysis of VLB Weighting vs. Simplified Loss Medium

Prove that the theoretical VLB weight $w(t) = \frac{\beta_t}{2\alpha_t(1 - \bar{\alpha}_{t-1})}$ blows up to $\infty$ as $t \to 1$, explaining why $L_{\text{simple}}$ produces superior perceptual images.

View Complete Solution

As $t \to 1$, $\bar{\alpha}_{t-1} = \bar{\alpha}_0 = 1$. The denominator $1 - \bar{\alpha}_0 = 0$, driving $w(1) \to \infty$.

Theoretical VLB over-penalizes microscopic noise at $t=1$. Discarding $w(t)$ balances learning across all scales, emphasizing global layout at large $t$.

Problem 3: Analytical Weight Gradient under $L_{\text{simple}}$ Hard

For a linear model $\epsilon_w(x_t) = w \cdot x_t$, solve for the optimal weight $w^*(t)$ minimizing $\mathbb{E}[(\epsilon - w x_t)^2]$.

View Complete Solution

Expanding the expectation with independent noise $\epsilon$:

$$\mathbb{E}[\epsilon x_t] = \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t}, \quad \mathbb{E}[x_t^2] = \bar{\alpha}_t(\mu_0^2 + \sigma_0^2) + (1 - \bar{\alpha}_t)$$ $$w^*(t) = \frac{\mathbb{E}[\epsilon x_t]}{\mathbb{E}[x_t^2]} = \frac{\sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t}}{\bar{\alpha}_t(\mu_0^2 + \sigma_0^2) + (1 - \bar{\alpha}_t)}$$
  • At $t \to 0$ ($\bar{\alpha}_t \to 1$): $w^*(0) = 0$ (clean signal contains no noise).
  • At $t \to T$ ($\bar{\alpha}_t \to 0$): $w^*(T) = 1.0$ (pure noise: predict the observation itself).
