Topic 25.3: Noise Schedules and Denoising U-Net Architectures

Cosine noise schedules, the simplified variational objective derivation, and U-Net structural motifs.

The 12-Year-Old Analogy: The Smart Dimmer & The Express Elevator

Two essential engineering components power modern diffusion:

  • The Cosine Dimmer: A linear dimmer turns the window to thick fog at 20% rotation. A cosine dimmer slowly softens edges first, and reaches pure noise only at the very end, making every single timestep useful for training.
  • Express Skip Elevators: As an image passes down the floors of a U-Net, fine details get compressed into broad ideas ("a dog"). Express elevator skip connections ferry the exact fine pixel blueprints across directly to the output painters!

1. Linear vs. Cosine Noise Schedules

Under a linear schedule, $\bar{\alpha}_t$ drops off prematurely. Nichol & Dhariwal (2021) proposed the cosine schedule:

$$\bar{\alpha}_t = \frac{f(t)}{f(0)}, \quad f(t) = \cos^2\left(\frac{t/T + s}{1 + s} \frac{\pi}{2}\right)$$

with small offset $s = 0.008$ preventing degenerate tiny variances near $t=0$.

2. Derivation of the Simplified Loss $L_{\text{simple}}(\theta)$

The Variational Lower Bound (ELBO) term for step $t-1$ between Gaussians evaluates to:

$$L_{t-1} = D_{KL}(q(x_{t-1}\mid x_t, x_0) \parallel p_\theta(x_{t-1}\mid x_t)) = \frac{\beta_t^2}{2\sigma_t^2 \alpha_t (1 - \bar{\alpha}_t)} \|\boldsymbol{\epsilon} - \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\theta(x_t, t)\|^2$$

Jonathan Ho et al. dropped the leading scalar weight, establishing the simplified training objective:

$$L_{\text{simple}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t \sim \mathcal{U}(1, T), x_0, \boldsymbol{\epsilon} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})} \left[ \|\boldsymbol{\epsilon} - \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\theta(x_t, t)\|^2 \right]$$
Curriculum Effect: Dropping the coefficient down-weights loss at small $t$ (where noise is trivial to predict) and focuses network capacity on intermediate and large $t$ where global semantics and high-level structure are generated.

3. Positional Sinusoidal Time Embeddings

Timesteps $t$ are projected into a continuous latent space using multi-frequency harmonic sinusoidal embeddings:

$$\text{PE}_{(t, 2i)} = \sin\left(\frac{t}{10000^{2i/d}}\right), \quad \text{PE}_{(t, 2i+1)} = \cos\left(\frac{t}{10000^{2i/d}}\right)$$
