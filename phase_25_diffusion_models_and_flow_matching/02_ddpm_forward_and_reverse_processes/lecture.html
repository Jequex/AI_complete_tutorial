Topic 25.2: DDPM Forward and Reverse Processes

Thermodynamic Markov chains, the closed-form shortcut jump, and the Bayesian posterior derivation.

The 12-Year-Old Analogy: Ink Dissolving in Water

Imagine dropping an ink droplet into water:

  • Forward Diffusion ($q$): At $t=0$, you have a sharp droplet. As seconds tick by ($t=1, \dots, T$), water molecules collide with the ink until the liquid is a uniform grey mist ($x_T \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$).
  • The Closed Shortcut ($q(x_t \mid x_0)$): You do not need to calculate every microsecond collision. A simple mathematical formula lets you calculate the exact appearance at second 50 in one step!
  • Reverse Generative Film ($p_\theta$): To create new art from scratch, you start with grey noise and run the film in reverse. By knowing how to clean up one tiny collision at a time, the noise sharpens into a brand new crystal-clear painting.

1. The Forward Markov Chain

Given data $x_0 \sim q(x_0)$, the forward diffusion process adds Gaussian noise according to variance schedule $\beta_1, \dots, \beta_T$:

$$q(x_t \mid x_{t-1}) = \mathcal{N}\left(x_t; \sqrt{1 - \beta_t} x_{t-1}, \beta_t \mathbf{I}\right)$$

Letting $\alpha_t = 1 - \beta_t$ and $\bar{\alpha}_t = \prod_{s=1}^t \alpha_s$, recursive substitution reveals the closed-form marginal shortcut:

$$q(x_t \mid x_0) = \mathcal{N}\left(x_t; \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t} x_0, (1 - \bar{\alpha}_t)\mathbf{I}\right)$$ $$x_t = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t} x_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t} \boldsymbol{\epsilon}, \quad \boldsymbol{\epsilon} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$$

2. Mathematical Symbol Decoder

Symbol Domain Description
$x_0$ $\mathbb{R}^D$ Clean ground-truth data sample.
$x_t$ $\mathbb{R}^D$ Noisy latent state at timestep $t$.
$\beta_t \in (0, 1)$ Scalar Schedule Variance of noise added at step $t$.
$\alpha_t = 1 - \beta_t$ Scalar Signal coefficient retained at step $t$.
$\bar{\alpha}_t = \prod_{s=1}^t \alpha_s$ Scalar Cumulative signal retention from step 0 to step $t$.
$\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_t(x_t, x_0)$ Vector Mean Mean of the true posterior $q(x_{t-1} \mid x_t, x_0)$.
$\tilde{\beta}_t$ Scalar Variance Variance of the true posterior $q(x_{t-1} \mid x_t, x_0)$.
$\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\theta(x_t, t)$ Neural Network Network trained to predict injected Gaussian perturbation $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}$.

3. The True Tractable Posterior $q(x_{t-1} \mid x_t, x_0)$

Conditioning on $x_0$ allows computing the exact posterior by Bayes' rule:

$$q(x_{t-1} \mid x_t, x_0) = \frac{q(x_t \mid x_{t-1}, x_0) q(x_{t-1} \mid x_0)}{q(x_t \mid x_0)} = \mathcal{N}\left(x_{t-1}; \tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_t(x_t, x_0), \tilde{\beta}_t \mathbf{I}\right)$$

Completing the square in the exponent gives:

$$\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_t(x_t, x_0) = \frac{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}}\beta_t}{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t} x_0 + \frac{\sqrt{\alpha_t}(1 - \bar{\alpha}_{t-1})}{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t} x_t$$ $$\tilde{\beta}_t = \frac{1 - \bar{\alpha}_{t-1}}{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t} \beta_t$$

4. Denoising Reparameterization

Substituting $x_0 = \frac{x_t - \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}}$ into the posterior mean yields:

$$\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_t(x_t, \boldsymbol{\epsilon}) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}} \left( x_t - \frac{\beta_t}{\sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t}} \boldsymbol{\epsilon} \right)$$
Takeaway: Predicting the injected noise $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}$ via neural network $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\theta(x_t, t)$ is equivalent to predicting the posterior mean step!
