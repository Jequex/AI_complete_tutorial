Topic 25.1: Score-Based Models and Langevin Dynamics

From the intractable partition function to score matching, denoising estimators, and annealed stochastic samplers.

The 12-Year-Old Analogy: Mountain Peaks in a Pitch-Black World

Imagine being dropped into a mountain range in pitch-black darkness:

  • The Impossible Map ($Z$): Deducing the exact percentage of all world air sitting on every inch of rock requires counting every air molecule on Earth. This is the partition function $Z$. It is intractable.
  • The Scent of Pine ($s(x)$): To find camp, you don't need a world map. You just sniff which way the scent of woodsmoke gets stronger. That uphill slope is the Score $\nabla_x \log p(x)$. It points straight to high-density clusters!
  • The Valley Trap & Annealing: Deep valleys have zero scent, leaving you trapped. So you blanket the entire continent in warm scented fog ($\sigma_1$), follow the coarse scent across miles of desert, and slowly clear the air ($\sigma_1 \to \sigma_L$) until you arrive at the peak. That is Annealed Langevin Dynamics.

1. The Intractable Partition Function

In energy-based modeling, a density $p_\theta(x)$ is parameterized via an unnormalized scalar energy $E_\theta(x)$:

$$p_\theta(x) = \frac{\exp(-E_\theta(x))}{Z_\theta}, \quad Z_\theta = \int_{\mathbb{R}^D} \exp(-E_\theta(x)) \, dx$$

Evaluating or differentiating $Z_\theta$ across $D > 100$ dimensions requires intractable high-dimensional quadrature. Score-based modeling defines the Stein score:

$$s(x) \triangleq \nabla_x \log p(x)$$

Differentiating with respect to the input sample $x$ eliminates the normalizer entirely:

$$s_\theta(x) = \nabla_x \left(-\log Z_\theta - E_\theta(x)\right) = -\nabla_x E_\theta(x)$$

2. Mathematical Symbol Decoder

$s_\theta(x)$ Neural Network Parametric score function parameterized by weights $\theta$.
$J_{ISM}(\theta)$ Scalar Loss Implicit Score Matching objective derived via integration by parts.
$\text{tr}(\nabla_x s_\theta(x))$ Scalar Divergence Trace of the score Jacobian: $\sum_{i=1}^D \frac{\partial s_{\theta, i}}{\partial x_i}$.
$q_\sigma(\tilde{x} \mid x)$ Gaussian Kernel Perturbation distribution $\mathcal{N}(\tilde{x}; x, \sigma^2 \mathbf{I})$.
$J_{DSM}(\theta)$ Scalar Loss Denoising Score Matching objective (Vincent 2011).
$\sigma_1 > \dots > \sigma_L$ Geometric Progression Annealed noise schedule spanning from broad exploration to fine details.

3. Implicit & Denoising Score Matching

Hyvärinen (2005) proved that minimizing the Fisher divergence $\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[\|s_\theta(x) - \nabla_x \log p(x)\|^2]$ is mathematically identical via integration by parts to:

$$J_{ISM}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim p_{\text{data}}} \left[ \text{tr}(\nabla_x s_\theta(x)) + \frac{1}{2} \|s_\theta(x)\|^2 \right]$$

Because computing $\text{tr}(\nabla_x s_\theta(x))$ requires $\mathcal{O}(D)$ backpropagation passes, Pascal Vincent (2011) introduced Denoising Score Matching (DSM) using Gaussian perturbation $q_\sigma(\tilde{x}|x) = \mathcal{N}(\tilde{x}; x, \sigma^2 \mathbf{I})$:

$$J_{DSM}(\theta) = \frac{1}{2} \mathbb{E}_{x \sim p_{\text{data}}, \tilde{x} \sim \mathcal{N}(x, \sigma^2 \mathbf{I})} \left[ \left\| s_\theta(\tilde{x}) + \frac{\tilde{x} - x}{\sigma^2} \right\|^2 \right]$$
Key Insight: The network is simply trained to predict the vector pulling the noisy sample $\tilde{x}$ back to the clean target $x$, scaled by $\frac{1}{\sigma^2}$.

4. Annealed Langevin Dynamics (Song & Ermon, 2019)

Generating samples uses the discretized Langevin SDE:

$$x_{t+1} = x_t + \frac{\alpha_i}{2} s_\theta(x_t, \sigma_i) + \sqrt{\alpha_i} \, z_t, \quad z_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$$

By annealing through noise scales $\sigma_1 > \sigma_2 > \dots > \sigma_L$, particles traverse energy barriers at high noise and settle into true multimodal proportions at low noise.
