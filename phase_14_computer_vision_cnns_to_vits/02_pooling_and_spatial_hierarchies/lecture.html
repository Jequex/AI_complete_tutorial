Topic 14.2: Pooling & Spatial Hierarchies

Core Thesis: As feature representations progress from low-level edges to semantic concepts, exact sub-pixel coordinates become less important than feature presence. Pooling layers provide downsampling, reduce memory footprint, and inject local translation invariance without introducing learnable parameters.

1. Executive Summary & The 12-Year-Old Analogy

If an image network did not downsample its spatial feature maps, a $224 \times 224$ feature volume with 512 channels would demand gigabytes of memory per image during backpropagation. Downsampling layers progressively compress spatial dimensions while expanding channel capacity, constructing a multi-scale spatial hierarchy.

The 12-Year-Old Analogy: Summarizing a Photo Album Page by Page

Imagine you have a giant photo album with thousands of photos and you need to write a compact summary for a friend:

  • Max Pooling (The Highlight Reel): In each $2 \times 2$ quadrant of 4 photos, you only report the single most exciting photo. If that photo shifts slightly to the left, you still pick it. That is translation invariance.
  • Average Pooling (The Average Vibe): You look at the 4 photos and average their brightness to describe the overall atmosphere.
  • Global Average Pooling (The One-Sentence Chapter Summary): At the end of the album, instead of handing your friend 49 individual photos, you average the entire page into a single scalar value. This prevents carrying giant piles of unnecessary paper!

2. Mathematical Symbol Decoder

Symbol Type / Domain Mathematical Meaning
$X \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times H \times W}$ 3D Tensor Input feature volume with $C$ channels and spatial size $H \times W$
$Y \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times H_{\text{out}} \times W_{\text{out}}}$ 3D Tensor Output feature volume after spatial pooling
$K \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$ Integer Spatial pooling window dimension ($K \times K$)
$S \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$ Integer Stride of the pooling window sweep
$\Omega_{i, j}$ Index Set Coordinates $(r, c)$ falling inside the window for output $(i, j)$
$M \in \{0, 1\}^{C \times H \times W}$ Binary Mask Argmax routing mask tracking coordinates of maximum activations
$\mathbf{y}_{\text{GAP}} \in \mathbb{R}^C$ 1D Vector Feature vector produced by Global Average Pooling across all $H \times W$ pixels
$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial Y}, \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial X}$ 3D Tensors Upstream and downstream loss gradients

3. Mathematical Operations: Max, Average, and Global Average Pooling

Pooling layers contain 0 learnable parameters. They operate on each channel independently.

3.1 Max Pooling

$$Y_{c, i, j} = \max_{(m, n) \in \Omega_{i, j}} X_{c, m, n}$$

Because preceding ReLU layers output large positive numbers when a pattern is detected and zeros otherwise, Max Pooling acts as a robust detector filter, retaining prominent visual features regardless of small sub-pixel shifts.

3.2 Average Pooling

$$Y_{c, i, j} = \frac{1}{K^2} \sum_{m=0}^{K-1} \sum_{n=0}^{K-1} X_{c, \, i \cdot S + m, \, j \cdot S + n}$$

Average pooling performs spatial smoothing and low-pass filtering.

3.3 Global Average Pooling (GAP)

GAP computes the spatial mean across the entire feature map for each channel:

$$y_c = \frac{1}{H \cdot W} \sum_{r=0}^{H-1} \sum_{col=0}^{W-1} X_{c, r, col}, \qquad \forall c \in \{0, \dots, C-1\}$$
The Parameter-Eliminating Superpower of GAP: In VGGNet, flattening a $[512, 7, 7]$ feature volume into two 4096-neuron dense layers created 102.7 million parameters ($>80\%$ of the entire model). Replacing this with GAP reduces the classification interface to a simple $512 \to 1000$ linear layer ($512,000$ parameters)—a 99.5% reduction in parameter count with zero risk of spatial overfitting!

4. Invariance vs. Equivariance

  • Equivariance (Convolutions): $f(g(X)) = g(f(X))$. Shifting an image shifts its convolutional feature map by an identical amount.
  • Invariance (Pooling): $h(g(X)) = h(X)$. Shifting a visual pattern within the pooling window yields an identical output.

5. Backpropagation Through Pooling Layers

5.1 MaxPooling: Argmax Routing Masks

During the forward pass, the winning coordinate $(m^*, n^*)$ is cached:

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial X_{c, m, n}} = \sum_{(i, j) \text{ s.t. } (m, n) \in \Omega_{i, j}} \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial Y_{c, i, j}} \cdot \mathbf{1}[(m, n) = (m^*, n^*)]$$

The entire upstream gradient is routed exclusively to the winning neuron that generated the maximum, while all losing neurons receive exactly zero gradient.

5.2 AvgPooling: Uniform Gradient Distribution

Every neuron in the window contributed equally ($\frac{1}{K^2}$), so the upstream gradient is divided equally:

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial X_{c, m, n}} = \sum_{(i, j) \text{ s.t. } (m, n) \in \Omega_{i, j}} \frac{1}{K^2} \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial Y_{c, i, j}}$$

5.3 Global Average Pooling Gradient Routing

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial X_{c, r, col}} = \frac{1}{H \cdot W} \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial y_c}$$
