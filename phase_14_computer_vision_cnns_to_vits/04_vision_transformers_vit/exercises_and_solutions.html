Topic 14.4: Exercises & Solutions — Vision Transformers (ViT)

Problem 1: Easy

Manual Patch Extraction, Linear Projection & Sequence Assembly

Consider a $1 \times 4 \times 4$ grayscale image:

$$X = \begin{bmatrix} 1.0 & 2.0 & 3.0 & 4.0 \\ 2.0 & 1.0 & 4.0 & 3.0 \\ 0.0 & 1.0 & 5.0 & 6.0 \\ 1.0 & 0.0 & 6.0 & 5.0 \end{bmatrix}$$

We configure a ViT with patch size $P = 2$ and hidden dimension $D = 2$.

  1. Compute number of patches $N$, and extract flattened vectors $\mathbf{x}_p^i \in \mathbb{R}^4$.
  2. With projection matrix $E = \begin{bmatrix} 0.5 & 0.0 \\ 0.5 & 0.0 \\ 0.0 & 0.5 \\ 0.0 & 0.5 \end{bmatrix}$, compute $\mathbf{x}_p^i E$.
  3. Prepend learnable token $\mathbf{x}_{\text{cls}} = [1.0, 1.0]$.
  4. Add 1D positional embeddings $E_{\text{pos}}$ to form initial sequence tensor $\mathbf{z}_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{5 \times 2}$.

Part 1 & 2: Patches and Projections:

  • $N = \frac{4 \times 4}{2 \times 2} = 4$ patches.
  • $\mathbf{x}_p^1 = [1, 2, 2, 1] \implies \mathbf{e}_1 = [1.5, 1.5]$
  • $\mathbf{x}_p^2 = [3, 4, 4, 3] \implies \mathbf{e}_2 = [3.5, 3.5]$
  • $\mathbf{x}_p^3 = [0, 1, 1, 0] \implies \mathbf{e}_3 = [0.5, 0.5]$
  • $\mathbf{x}_p^4 = [5, 6, 6, 5] \implies \mathbf{e}_4 = [5.5, 5.5]$

Part 3 & 4: Assembled Sequence Matrix $\mathbf{z}_0$:

$$\mathbf{z}_0 = \begin{bmatrix} 1.1 & 1.1 \\ 1.7 & 1.7 \\ 3.8 & 3.8 \\ 0.9 & 0.9 \\ 6.0 & 6.0 \end{bmatrix}$$
Problem 2: Medium

Self-Attention Computational Complexity & Quadratic Scaling

In ViT ($P = 16$), compute sequence length $N$ and attention matrix entries ($N \times N$) for three resolutions:

  • $224 \times 224$
  • $448 \times 448$
  • $896 \times 896$
  1. Compute the sequence length $N$ for each resolution.
  2. Compute the total elements in the attention matrix ($N \times N$).
  3. By what factor does attention memory expand when resolution doubles from $224$ to $448$?
  4. Why does standard ViT suffer on high-resolution dense vision tasks?
View Complete Solution

Part 1 & 2: Tokens & Attention Matrix Sizes:

  • $224 \times 224$: $N = 196 \implies 196 \times 196 = \mathbf{38,416 \text{ entries}}$
  • $448 \times 448$: $N = 784 \implies 784 \times 784 = \mathbf{614,656 \text{ entries}}$
  • $896 \times 896$: $N = 3,136 \implies 3,136 \times 3,136 = \mathbf{9,834,496 \text{ entries}}$

Part 3: Growth Factor:

$$\text{Factor} = \frac{614,656}{38,416} = \mathbf{16\times}$$

Doubling image spatial dimension quadruples tokens ($4\times$), causing a quadratic $(4\times)^2 = 16\times$ surge in attention memory.

Part 4: Dense Vision Tasks:

Megapixel resolutions cause extreme memory overhead and GPU OOMs, motivating local windowed variants like Swin Transformer.

Problem 3: Hard

Hybrid ViT Architectures & The Inductive Bias Trade-Off

Compare raw patch ViTs with Hybrid ViT architectures combining a CNN stem with a Transformer encoder.

  1. If an image $224 \times 224 \times 3$ is processed by a CNN stem yielding $[C=512, H=14, W=14]$, how many tokens are produced?
  2. Explain two training advantages of a CNN stem on moderate datasets (e.g. ImageNet-1k).
  3. Contrast how early convolutional layers extract features compared to early self-attention layers in ViT.
  4. Why did pure ViTs ultimately dominate at foundation model scale?
View Complete Solution

Part 1: Tokens: $N = 14 \times 14 = \mathbf{196 \text{ tokens}}$.

Part 2: Advantages:

  • Sample Efficiency: Convolutional priors (locality, equivariance) prevent wasting parameters learning basic spatial connectivity.
  • Optimization Stability: Acts as a smooth continuous feature extractor, reducing training sensitivity.

Part 3: Receptive Field Differences:

CNN early layers are strictly confined to local $3 \times 3$ patches. ViT self-attention possesses a global receptive field from Layer 1, enabling immediate interactions across opposing corners of the image.

Part 4: Foundation Dominance:

At multi-billion image scale, rigid inductive biases constrain model capacity. Pure ViT scales monotonically without architectural saturation and utilizes uniform GEMM compute optimized for AI accelerators.
