Topic 14.4: Vision Transformers (ViT)

Core Thesis: Convolutions are not strictly required for computer vision. By slicing an image into non-overlapping patches and projecting them into 1D token embeddings, the standard Transformer Encoder with Multi-Head Self-Attention achieves state-of-the-art vision performance with minimal inductive bias.

1. Executive Summary & The 12-Year-Old Analogy

In 2020, Dosovitskiy et al. introduced the Vision Transformer (ViT): *"An Image is Worth 16x16 Words"*. By replacing local sliding convolutions with global self-attention across image patches, ViT demonstrated superior scaling properties when pretrained on large-scale datasets (JFT-300M, ImageNet-21k).

The 12-Year-Old Analogy: Turning a Picture Puzzle into a Book of Sentences

Imagine you have a picture of a dragon, but your friend only reads books and cannot see pictures:

  • The Jigsaw Slices (Patch Extraction): You take scissors and cut the dragon picture into 16 square cardboard tiles.
  • The Secret Code (Linear Projection): You convert each tile into a compact list of numbers describing its colors and lines, making each tile behave like a "word".
  • Numbering the Tiles (Positional Embeddings): You stamp tile numbers on the back so the model knows where each tile belonged in the original grid.
  • The Master Detective Tile (The [CLS] Token): You place a blank golden tile at the very front of the sequence. Its job is to talk to all the tiles and synthesize the overall answer: *"This is a dragon!"*
  • Global Self-Attention: In a CNN, a tile can only whisper to its immediate neighbor. In ViT, every tile can look directly across the entire image at every other tile simultaneously!

2. Mathematical Symbol Decoder

Symbol Type / Domain Mathematical Meaning
$\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$ 3D Tensor Input image of spatial resolution $H \times W$ with $C$ channels
$P \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$ Integer Spatial resolution of square patch ($P \times P$ pixels, e.g. 16)
$N = \frac{HW}{P^2}$ Integer Total number of patches generated from the image
$\mathbf{x}_p^i \in \mathbb{R}^{P^2 C}$ 1D Vector Flattened pixel representation of patch $i$
$E \in \mathbb{R}^{(P^2 C) \times D}$ 2D Matrix Learnable patch projection matrix to hidden dimension $D$
$\mathbf{x}_{\text{cls}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ 1D Vector Learnable classification token prepended to token sequence
$E_{\text{pos}} \in \mathbb{R}^{(N+1) \times D}$ 2D Matrix 1D learnable spatial positional embedding matrix
$\mathbf{z}_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{(N+1) \times D}$ 2D Matrix Initial token sequence fed into the Transformer encoder

3. Mathematical Formulation of ViT

3.1 Patch Slicing & Linear Projection

For an image of shape $[H, W, C]$, we extract $N = \frac{HW}{P^2}$ patches of size $[P, P, C]$. Each patch is flattened into a vector of length $P^2 C$ and projected to dimension $D$ via matrix $E$:

$$\mathbf{e}_i = \mathbf{x}_p^i E \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times D}$$

3.2 Sequence Construction with [CLS] & Positional Embeddings

$$\mathbf{z}_0 = \left[ \mathbf{x}_{\text{cls}}; \, \mathbf{x}_p^1 E; \, \mathbf{x}_p^2 E; \, \dots; \, \mathbf{x}_p^N E \right] + E_{\text{pos}}$$

3.3 Pre-LN Transformer Encoder Equations

$$\mathbf{z}'_l = \text{MSA}(\text{LN}(\mathbf{z}_{l-1})) + \mathbf{z}_{l-1}, \qquad l = 1, \dots, L$$ $$\mathbf{z}_l = \text{MLP}(\text{LN}(\mathbf{z}'_l)) + \mathbf{z}'_l, \qquad l = 1, \dots, L$$ $$\mathbf{y} = \text{Linear}(\text{LN}(\mathbf{z}_L^0))$$

4. Inductive Bias: CNNs vs. ViTs

  • CNNs (Hard Inductive Bias): Built-in 2D locality and translation equivariance. Highly sample efficient on small datasets, but susceptible to capacity saturation at massive scales.
  • ViTs (Minimal Inductive Bias): Global attention from Layer 1. Can attend to any two pixels regardless of spatial distance. Requires large datasets to learn spatial geometry from scratch, but scales monotonically with data and compute.
