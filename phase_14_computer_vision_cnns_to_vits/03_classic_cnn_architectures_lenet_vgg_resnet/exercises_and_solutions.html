Topic 14.3: Exercises & Solutions — Classic CNNs & ResNet

Problem 1: Easy

Gradient Backpropagation Through a Single Residual Block

Consider a 2-dimensional linear residual block $\mathbf{y} = W \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{x}$:

$$W = \begin{bmatrix} 0.2 & -0.4 \\ 0.1 & -0.3 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} 1.0 \\ 2.0 \end{bmatrix}$$
  1. Compute the output vector $\mathbf{y} \in \mathbb{R}^2$.
  2. If the upstream loss gradient is $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{y}} = [3.0, -1.0]$, compute the gradient flowing through the residual branch ($\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{y}} W$).
  3. Compute the gradient flowing through the identity shortcut ($\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{y}} \mathbf{I}$).
  4. Compute the total downstream gradient $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{x}}$.
View Complete Solution

Part 1: Forward Pass:

$$W \mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.2(1.0) - 0.4(2.0) \\ 0.1(1.0) - 0.3(2.0) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -0.6 \\ -0.5 \end{bmatrix}$$ $$\mathbf{y} = W \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} -0.6 \\ -0.5 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 1.0 \\ 2.0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.4 \\ 1.5 \end{bmatrix}$$

Part 2: Residual Branch Gradient:

$$\mathbf{g}_{\text{res}} = [3.0, -1.0] \begin{bmatrix} 0.2 & -0.4 \\ 0.1 & -0.3 \end{bmatrix} = [3.0(0.2) - 0.1, 3.0(-0.4) + 0.3] = [0.5, -0.9]$$

Part 3: Identity Shortcut Gradient:

$$\mathbf{g}_{\text{skip}} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{y}} \mathbf{I} = [3.0, -1.0]$$

Part 4: Total Downstream Gradient:

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{x}} = \mathbf{g}_{\text{res}} + \mathbf{g}_{\text{skip}} = [0.5, -0.9] + [3.0, -1.0] = \mathbf{[3.5, -1.9]}$$
Problem 2: Medium

Vanishing Gradient Decay Rate: Plain vs. ResNet

Consider a network with $L$ sequential blocks where each Jacobian norm is bounded by $\gamma = 0.7 < 1.0$:

$$\left\| \frac{\partial \mathcal{F}_l}{\partial \mathbf{x}_l} \right\| \le \gamma = 0.7$$
  1. Derive an upper bound for the gradient ratio $\frac{\|\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{x}_0}\|}{\|\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{x}_L}\|}$ for a plain network ($\mathbf{x}_{l+1} = \mathcal{F}_l(\mathbf{x}_l)$).
  2. Derive the gradient formula for a residual network ($\mathbf{x}_{l+1} = \mathbf{x}_l + \mathcal{F}_l(\mathbf{x}_l)$).
  3. For $L = 34$ layers, calculate the numerical value of the plain network gradient attenuation and contrast it with ResNet.
View Complete Solution

Part 1: Plain Network Gradient Bound:

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{x}_0} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{x}_L} \prod_{l=0}^{L-1} \frac{\partial \mathcal{F}_l}{\partial \mathbf{x}_l} \implies \frac{\|\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{x}_0}\|}{\|\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{x}_L}\|} \le \gamma^L$$

Part 2: ResNet Gradient Formula:

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{x}_0} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{x}_L} \left( \mathbf{I} + \frac{\partial}{\partial \mathbf{x}_0} \sum_{l=0}^{L-1} \mathcal{F}_l(\mathbf{x}_l) \right)$$

The identity matrix term $\mathbf{I}$ ensures gradient flow is not subject to multiplicative decay $\gamma^L$.

Part 3: Evaluation for $L=34$:

$$\gamma^{34} = (0.7)^{34} \approx 5.41 \times 10^{-6}$$

In the plain network, gradients are crushed by a factor of $180,000\times$, completely starving early layers of learning signals. ResNet's identity highway preserves the gradient magnitude unhindered.

Problem 3: Hard

The Bottleneck Block: FLOPs & Parameter Analysis

Compare two residual block designs for processing an input feature volume $[C=256, H=56, W=56] \to [C=256, H=56, W=56]$:

  • Design A (Basic Residual Block): Two $3 \times 3$ conv layers with 256 channels each.
  • Design B (Bottleneck Residual Block): $1 \times 1$ conv ($256 \to 64$), $3 \times 3$ conv ($64 \to 64$), $1 \times 1$ conv ($64 \to 256$).
  1. Calculate total convolutional weights (excluding biases) for Design A and Design B.
  2. Calculate total MAC operations for both designs on a $56 \times 56$ feature map.
  3. Compute the parameter and FLOP reduction percentages.
  4. Why is the Bottleneck Block essential for ResNet-101 and ResNet-152?
View Complete Solution

Part 1: Weights:

  • Design A: $2 \times (256 \times 256 \times 3 \times 3) = \mathbf{1,179,648 \text{ weights}}$
  • Design B: $(64 \times 256 \times 1) + (64 \times 64 \times 9) + (256 \times 64 \times 1) = 16,384 + 36,864 + 16,384 = \mathbf{69,632 \text{ weights}}$

Part 2: MACs ($56 \times 56 = 3,136$ locations):

  • Design A: $3,136 \times 1,179,648 \approx \mathbf{3.70 \text{ GMACs}}$
  • Design B: $3,136 \times 69,632 \approx \mathbf{0.218 \text{ GMACs}}$

Part 3: Reduction:

$$\text{Ratio} = \frac{69,632}{1,179,648} \approx 0.0590 \implies \mathbf{94.1\% \text{ reduction in parameters and compute!}}$$

Part 4: Deep ResNet Scaling:

By reducing compute by $17\times$, the Bottleneck block makes scaling to 101 or 152 layers computationally tractable on modern GPUs.
