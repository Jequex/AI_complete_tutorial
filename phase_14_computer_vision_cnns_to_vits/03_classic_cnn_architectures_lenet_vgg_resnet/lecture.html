Topic 14.3: Classic CNN Architectures: LeNet, VGG, and ResNet

Core Thesis: As plain convolutional networks grow deeper, they suffer from optimization degradation rather than overfitting. Residual Networks (ResNet) solve this by introducing identity skip connections, creating a gradient highway that enables successful training of networks with 100+ layers.

1. Executive Summary & The 12-Year-Old Analogy

The trajectory of computer vision from LeNet-5 (1998) to AlexNet (2012) and VGGNet (2014) demonstrated the power of depth. However, vanilla networks beyond 20 layers suffered from the Degradation Problem: deeper models had higher training error. ResNet reformulated the problem into learning residual corrections $\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x}) = \mathcal{H}(\mathbf{x}) - \mathbf{x}$, unlocking deep architectures with hundreds of layers.

The 12-Year-Old Analogy: The High-Speed Highway Bypassing City Traffic Jams

Imagine a cross-country message delivery across 100 cities:

  • Plain Deep Network (City Gridlock): Your van must drive through downtown streets in every single city. In each city, red lights and traffic jams slow down the van. By city 20, the van is completely stuck—messages from the destination never make it back!
  • The Residual Block (The Elevated Highway): ResNet builds an elevated expressway directly over each city ($\mathbf{I}$, the identity shortcut). Your van zooms down the open highway at full speed.
  • The Residual Workshop ($\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x})$): In each city, there is an optional side exit. If the workshop has a useful modification, it adds it to the van. If not, it adds nothing ($\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x}) \approx 0$).
  • Backpropagation Miracle: During the return trip (backpropagation), training gradients travel down the unhindered expressway with zero traffic jams, reaching the very first layer intact!

2. Mathematical Symbol Decoder

Symbol Type / Domain Mathematical Meaning
$\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ Vector / Tensor Input representation to a residual block
$\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{x})$ Mapping Function Underlying true function to be learned
$\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x}, \{\mathcal{W}_i\})$ Parameterized Function Residual mapping function to be fitted by stacked layers
$\mathbf{y} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ Vector / Tensor Output of residual block: $\mathbf{y} = \mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x}) + \mathbf{x}$
$\mathbf{I} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$ Identity Matrix Identity matrix: derivative of the shortcut $\frac{\partial \mathbf{x}}{\partial \mathbf{x}}$
$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{x}}, \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{y}}$ Gradients Downstream and upstream loss gradients

3. The Architecture Evolution: LeNet-5 to VGGNet

  • LeNet-5 (1998): $5 \times 5$ convolutions, Average Pooling, Sigmoid/Tanh activations. Introduced the template of convolutional feature extraction followed by classification.
  • AlexNet (2012): 8 layers, ImageNet breakthrough. First to use ReLU non-linearities, GPU parallel computing, and Dropout ($p = 0.5$).
  • VGGNet (2014): Proved that large filters are obsolete. Factorized large kernels into homogeneous stacks of $3 \times 3$ convolutions:
    • Two stacked $3 \times 3$ convs equal one $5 \times 5$ receptive field with 28% fewer weights ($18 C^2$ vs $25 C^2$).
    • Three stacked $3 \times 3$ convs equal one $7 \times 7$ receptive field with 44.9% fewer weights ($27 C^2$ vs $49 C^2$).
    • Incorporates three non-linear ReLUs instead of one, dramatically increasing expressivity.

4. The Degradation Problem

The Paradox: When vanilla CNNs are scaled from 20 to 56 layers, training error increases. This cannot be explained by overfitting, because an overfitted model would achieve near-zero training error. Instead, it is an optimization bottleneck: vanishing gradients and ill-conditioned loss surfaces prevent stochastic gradient descent from optimizing deep stacks of plain layers.

5. Mathematical Proof: The Gradient Highway

In a Residual Network, each block computes:

$$\mathbf{x}_{l+1} = \mathbf{x}_l + \mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x}_l, \mathcal{W}_l)$$

By mathematical induction, the representation at any deep layer $L$ is:

$$\mathbf{x}_L = \mathbf{x}_l + \sum_{i=l}^{L-1} \mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathcal{W}_i)$$

Applying the multivariate chain rule to compute the loss gradient $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{x}_l}$:

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{x}_l} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{x}_L} \frac{\partial \mathbf{x}_L}{\partial \mathbf{x}_l} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{x}_L} \left( \mathbf{I} + \frac{\partial}{\partial \mathbf{x}_l} \sum_{i=l}^{L-1} \mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathcal{W}_i) \right)$$ $$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{x}_l} = \underbrace{\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{x}_L}}_{\text{Direct Highway Term}} + \underbrace{\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{x}_L} \left( \frac{\partial}{\partial \mathbf{x}_l} \sum_{i=l}^{L-1} \mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathcal{W}_i) \right)}_{\text{Residual Correction Term}}$$

Even if the weight Jacobians along the residual branch vanish completely ($\frac{\partial \mathcal{F}}{\partial \mathbf{x}} \to 0$), the term $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{x}_L} \cdot \mathbf{I}$ guarantees that gradients flow directly back through the identity shortcut with zero attenuation.
