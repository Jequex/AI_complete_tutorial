Topic 14.1: Exercises & Solutions — Convolutions & Feature Maps

Problem 1: Easy

Manual 2D Convolution Calculation with Padding & Stride

You are given a $4 \times 4$ single-channel input matrix $X$ and a $3 \times 3$ kernel $K$ with bias $b = -2.0$:

$$X = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 0 & 3 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad K = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 & -1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad b = -2.0$$
  1. If zero-padding $P = 1$ and stride $S = 2$ are applied, compute the spatial shape of the output feature map $Y$.
  2. Compute the exact numerical values of the output matrix $Y$.
View Complete Solution

Step 1: Compute output dimensions:

$$H_{\text{out}} = \left\lfloor \frac{4 - 3 + 2(1)}{2} \right\rfloor + 1 = \left\lfloor \frac{3}{2} \right\rfloor + 1 = 2, \qquad W_{\text{out}} = 2$$

The output matrix $Y$ has shape $2 \times 2$.

Step 2: Construct zero-padded matrix $X_{\text{pad}}$ ($6 \times 6$):

$$X_{\text{pad}} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 & 0 & 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 1 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 & 0 & 3 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 & 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

Step 3: Evaluate windows at stride $S = 2$:

  • $Y_{0,0}$ (rows 0..2, cols 0..2): $[0(-1) + 2(-1) + 1(-1)] + (-2.0) = -3 - 2 = -5.0$
  • $Y_{0,1}$ (rows 0..2, cols 2..4): $[1(1)] + (-2.0) = 1 - 2 = -1.0$
  • $Y_{1,0}$ (rows 2..4, cols 0..2): $[1(-1) + 2(-1)] + (-2.0) = -3 - 2 = -5.0$
  • $Y_{1,1}$ (rows 2..4, cols 2..4): $[1(1) + 2(-1) + 3(1)] + (-2.0) = 2 - 2 = 0.0$

Final Output Matrix:

$$Y = \begin{bmatrix} -5.0 & -1.0 \\ -5.0 & 0.0 \end{bmatrix}$$
Problem 2: Medium

Multi-Channel Parameter & FLOPs Analysis

An autonomous vehicle camera feed of resolution $H = 256, W = 512$ with $C_{\text{in}} = 3$ color channels is passed into a convolutional layer configured as follows:

  • Number of filters ($C_{\text{out}}$): 64
  • Kernel spatial size ($K_h \times K_w$): $5 \times 5$
  • Padding ($P$): 2 ("same" padding)
  • Stride ($S$): 2
  1. Compute the spatial dimensions of the output feature map ($H_{\text{out}}, W_{\text{out}}$).
  2. Calculate the total number of learnable parameters (weights + biases).
  3. Calculate the total number of MACs and approximate FLOPs required for a single image forward pass.
View Complete Solution

Part 1: Output Spatial Dimensions:

$$H_{\text{out}} = \left\lfloor \frac{256 - 5 + 4}{2} \right\rfloor + 1 = 128, \qquad W_{\text{out}} = \left\lfloor \frac{512 - 5 + 4}{2} \right\rfloor + 1 = 256$$

Output shape: $[64, 128, 256]$.

Part 2: Parameter Count:

$$\text{Weights} = C_{\text{out}} \times C_{\text{in}} \times K_h \times K_w = 64 \times 3 \times 5 \times 5 = 4,800$$ $$\text{Biases} = C_{\text{out}} = 64$$ $$\text{Total Parameters} = 4,800 + 64 = 4,864$$

Part 3: MACs and FLOPs:

$$\text{MACs} = 128 \times 256 \times 64 \times (3 \times 5 \times 5) = 157,286,400 \text{ MACs} \approx 157.3 \text{ MMACs}$$ $$\text{FLOPs} \approx 2 \times 157,286,400 = 314,572,800 \text{ FLOPs} \approx 314.6 \text{ MFLOPs}$$
Problem 3: Hard

Receptive Field Propagation & VGG-Style Filter Stacking Proof

In 2014, the Visual Geometry Group (VGG) at Oxford proved that stacking multiple small $3 \times 3$ convolutional layers is mathematically and computationally superior to using larger $5 \times 5$ or $7 \times 7$ kernels.

  1. Using $RF_l = RF_{l-1} + (k_l - 1) \cdot J_{l-1}$, prove that stacking two successive $3 \times 3$ conv layers achieves the exact same receptive field as a single $5 \times 5$ conv layer.
  2. Prove that stacking three successive $3 \times 3$ conv layers achieves the exact same receptive field as a single $7 \times 7$ conv layer.
  3. Assuming all intermediate layers have $C$ channels ($C_{\text{in}} = C_{\text{out}} = C$), compute the parameter reduction ratio of two $3 \times 3$ layers vs. one $5 \times 5$ layer, and three $3 \times 3$ layers vs. one $7 \times 7$ layer.
  4. What qualitative representational advantage does stacking multiple $3 \times 3$ layers provide beyond parameter efficiency?
View Complete Solution

Part 1 & 2: Receptive Field Proofs:

  • Two $3 \times 3$ layers: $RF_1 = 1 + (3 - 1) \cdot 1 = 3$; $RF_2 = 3 + (3 - 1) \cdot 1 = 5$. Matches single $5 \times 5$ layer ($1 + (5 - 1) = 5$).
  • Three $3 \times 3$ layers: $RF_3 = 5 + (3 - 1) \cdot 1 = 7$. Matches single $7 \times 7$ layer ($1 + (7 - 1) = 7$).

Part 3: Parameter Reductions:

  • Two $3 \times 3$ vs. One $5 \times 5$: $2 \times 9 C^2 = 18 C^2$ vs $25 C^2$. Ratio: $\frac{18}{25} = 0.72 \implies \mathbf{28\% \text{ parameter reduction}}$.
  • Three $3 \times 3$ vs. One $7 \times 7$: $3 \times 9 C^2 = 27 C^2$ vs $49 C^2$. Ratio: $\frac{27}{49} \approx 0.551 \implies \mathbf{44.9\% \text{ parameter reduction}}$.

Part 4: Architectural Expressivity:

Stacking multiple layers incorporates multiple non-linear activations (e.g. ReLU) instead of just one ($\text{Conv} \to \text{ReLU} \to \text{Conv} \to \text{ReLU}$). This substantially deepens the non-linear decision boundary without increasing the receptive field cost.
