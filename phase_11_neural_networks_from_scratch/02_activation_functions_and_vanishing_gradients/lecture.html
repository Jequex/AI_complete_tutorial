Topic 11.2: Activation Functions & The Vanishing Gradient Problem

From mathematical linear collapse to vanishing gradients, dying ReLUs, and modern Transformer activations (GELU, SiLU).

1. Executive Summary & The 12-Year-Old Analogy

Without non-linear activations, deep neural networks collapse mathematically to a single linear equation, unable to solve even the basic XOR problem. Furthermore, historical activations like Sigmoid squashed backward gradients exponentially ($0.25^L \to 0$), paralyzing deep networks.

The 12-Year-Old Analogy: The Volume Knob and the One-Way Gate
  • Why Linear Alone Collapses (The Chained Amplifiers): If room 1 doubles volume ($2x$) and room 2 triples it ($3x$), together they are just one $6x$ amplifier. No curvature or melody can ever emerge without a non-linear pedal!
  • The Sigmoid Knob (The Fuzzy Blanket): Sigmoid dampens feedback by at least 75% at every single room ($g' \le 0.25$). By room 10, the instruction is $0.25^{10} \approx 0.00000095$ of the original—completely inaudible! That is the Vanishing Gradient Problem.
  • The ReLU Gate (The Bouncer): If sound is positive ($z > 0$), let 100% of feedback pass through ($g' = 1.0$). If negative ($z \le 0$), slam the door shut. The Trap: If slammed shut permanently, the bouncer falls asleep and is declared a Dead ReLU.
  • Modern Smooth Gates (GELU & SiLU): Instead of a sharp kink, modern LLMs use gently curved dips with tiny negative leaks, keeping gradients flowing smoothly.

2. Mathematical Symbol Decoder

Symbol Domain Description & Meaning
$z \in \mathbb{R}$ Scalar Pre-activation affine score ($z = \mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x} + b$)
$g'(z) \equiv \frac{dg}{dz}$ Scalar First derivative: local gradient multiplier in backprop
$\sigma(z)$ $(0, 1)$ Sigmoid activation: $\sigma(z) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-z}}$, with $\sigma'(z) = \sigma(z)(1 - \sigma(z))$
$\tanh(z)$ $(-1, 1)$ Hyperbolic tangent: zero-centered, with $\tanh'(z) = 1 - \tanh^2(z)$
$\text{ReLU}(z)$ $[0, \infty)$ Rectified Linear Unit: $\max(0, z)$
$\text{LeakyReLU}(z)$ $\mathbb{R}$ Leaky ReLU with slope $\alpha$ (e.g. $0.01$) for $z < 0$
$\text{GELU}(z)$ $[-0.17, \infty)$ Gaussian Error Linear Unit: $z \Phi(z) \approx 0.5z(1 + \tanh(\sqrt{2/\pi}(z + 0.044715 z^3)))$
$\text{SiLU}(z)$ $[-0.278, \infty)$ Sigmoid Linear Unit (Swish): $z \sigma(z)$, used in LLaMA / SwiGLU

3. The Non-Linearity Requirement: Proof of Linear Collapse

Suppose an $L$-layer network uses purely linear activations $g(z) = z$:

$$\mathbf{a}^{[1]} = W^{[1]} \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b}^{[1]}$$ $$\mathbf{a}^{[2]} = W^{[2]} \mathbf{a}^{[1]} + \mathbf{b}^{[2]} = (W^{[2]} W^{[1]}) \mathbf{x} + (W^{[2]} \mathbf{b}^{[1]} + \mathbf{b}^{[2]})$$

By mathematical induction, for arbitrary depth $L$:

$$\mathbf{a}^{[L]} = W_{\text{eff}} \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b}_{\text{eff}}, \quad \text{where } W_{\text{eff}} = \prod_{l=L}^1 W^{[l]}$$

Therefore, a multi-layer linear network collapses into an equivalent single-layer linear model. Non-linearities are strictly required to create curved decision boundaries.

4. The Vanishing Gradient Problem

During backpropagation, the chain rule expresses the gradient at layer 1 as a product of weight matrices and activation derivatives:

$$\delta^{[1]} = \delta^{[L]} \prod_{l=1}^{L-1} \left( W^{[l+1]} \cdot g'\left(z^{[l]}\right) \right)$$

For the Sigmoid function, the maximum derivative is $\sigma'(0) = 0.25$. For $L$ layers:

$$\left| \delta^{[1]} \right| \propto (0.25)^{L-1}$$

At $L = 10$, $(0.25)^9 \approx 3.8 \times 10^{-6}$. The gradient vanishes, leaving the earliest layers completely unable to learn.

5. The Dead ReLU Problem

The Dying ReLU Phenomenon:

If large updates push weights such that $z = \mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x} + b < 0$ for all training samples, then:

$$\frac{\partial a}{\partial z} = 0 \implies \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{w}} = \mathbf{0} \implies \Delta \mathbf{w} = \mathbf{0}$$

The neuron permanently stops receiving gradient updates and becomes permanently frozen ("dead").

Modern solutions include LeakyReLU ($\alpha = 0.01$), GELU (used in GPT and BERT), and SiLU / SwiGLU (used in LLaMA).
