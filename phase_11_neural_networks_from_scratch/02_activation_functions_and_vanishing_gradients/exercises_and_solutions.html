Topic 11.2: Exercises & Solutions — Activation Functions & Vanishing Gradients

Analytical derivatives, zero-centering proofs, and deep-network gradient attenuation calculations.

Easy

Problem 1: Derivation of the Sigmoid Derivative and Maximum Bound

Let the logistic sigmoid function be defined as $\sigma(z) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-z}}$.

  1. Derive $\sigma'(z) \equiv \frac{d\sigma}{dz}$ and express it in terms of $\sigma(z)$.
  2. Prove that $\sigma'(z)$ attains its global maximum at $z = 0$, and find this value.
  3. Examine $\lim_{z \to \pm \infty} \sigma'(z)$ and describe its implication for deep networks.
View Complete Solution

1. Derivative:

$$\frac{d\sigma}{dz} = \frac{e^{-z}}{(1 + e^{-z})^2} = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-z}} \cdot \frac{e^{-z}}{1 + e^{-z}} = \sigma(z)(1 - \sigma(z))$$

2. Global Maximum:

Let $u = \sigma(z) \in (0, 1)$. We maximize $g(u) = u - u^2$. Setting $g'(u) = 1 - 2u = 0 \implies u = 0.5$. Since $g''(u) = -2 < 0$, this is a maximum. At $u = 0.5$, $z = 0$ and $\sigma'(0) = 0.5(1 - 0.5) = 0.25$.

3. Asymptotics:

As $z \to \pm\infty$, $\sigma(z) \to 1$ or $0$, yielding $\sigma'(z) \to 0$. In saturated regimes, gradients disappear completely.

Medium

Problem 2: Tanh vs. Sigmoid Algebraic Properties

The hyperbolic tangent is given by $\tanh(z) = \frac{e^z - e^{-z}}{e^z + e^{-z}}$.

  1. Prove that $\tanh(z) = 2\sigma(2z) - 1$.
  2. Prove that $\frac{d}{dz}\tanh(z) = 1 - \tanh^2(z)$.
  3. Compare the maximum gradient of Tanh with Sigmoid and explain the benefit of zero-centering.
View Complete Solution

1. Proof:

$$2\sigma(2z) - 1 = \frac{2}{1 + e^{-2z}} - 1 = \frac{1 - e^{-2z}}{1 + e^{-2z}} = \frac{e^z - e^{-z}}{e^z + e^{-z}} = \tanh(z)$$

2. Derivative:

$$\frac{d}{dz}\left(\frac{e^z - e^{-z}}{e^z + e^{-z}}\right) = \frac{(e^z + e^{-z})^2 - (e^z - e^{-z})^2}{(e^z + e^{-z})^2} = 1 - \tanh^2(z)$$

3. Maximum & Zero-Centering:

At $z=0$, $\tanh'(0) = 1.0$, which is $4\times$ larger than $\sigma'(0) = 0.25$. Zero-centering prevents gradients on weights from always having the same sign, avoiding slow zigzagging optimization paths.

Hard

Problem 3: Gradient Ratio in a 12-Layer Deep Network

Consider a 12-layer deep network where weights have unit norm and typical pre-activations are $|z| \approx 1.0$.

  1. Compute the 11-layer gradient attenuation $\mathcal{R}_{\sigma} = (\sigma'(1.0))^{11}$.
  2. Compare this with $\mathcal{R}_{\text{ReLU}}$ for active neurons.
  3. Derive $\text{SiLU}'(z)$ for $\text{SiLU}(z) = z\sigma(z)$, and compute $\mathcal{R}_{\text{SiLU}} = (\text{SiLU}'(1.0))^{11}$.
View Complete Solution

1. Sigmoid Attenuation:

$\sigma(1.0) \approx 0.731059 \implies \sigma'(1.0) \approx 0.196612$. Over 11 layers:

$$\mathcal{R}_{\sigma} = (0.196612)^{11} \approx 1.70 \times 10^{-8}$$

The gradient shrinks by over 58,000,000-fold, completely arresting learning in early layers.

2. ReLU Transmission:

For active neurons ($z > 0$), $\text{ReLU}'(z) = 1.0 \implies \mathcal{R}_{\text{ReLU}} = 1.0^{11} = 1.0$. Gradients propagate without attenuation.

3. SiLU (Swish) Transmission:

$$\text{SiLU}'(z) = \sigma(z) + z\sigma(z)(1 - \sigma(z)) = \sigma(z)[1 + z(1 - \sigma(z))]$$

At $z=1.0$: $\text{SiLU}'(1.0) \approx 0.731059(1 + 0.268941) \approx 0.927670$. Over 11 layers:

$$\mathcal{R}_{\text{SiLU}} = (0.927670)^{11} \approx 0.428$$

Gradients remain healthy and well within an easily trainable range (~43% of initial value).
