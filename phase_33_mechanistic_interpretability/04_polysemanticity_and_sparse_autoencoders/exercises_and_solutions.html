Exercise 1: High-Dimensional Almost-Orthogonal Packing

In $\mathbb{R}^d$, analyze the number of unit vectors with bounded interference $|\langle \mathbf{u}_i, \mathbf{u}_j \rangle| \le \epsilon$.

  1. What is the maximum number of strictly orthogonal vectors?
  2. State the asymptotic scaling of almost-orthogonal vectors $M(d, \epsilon)$ under the Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma.
  3. Evaluate this capacity for $d = 4096$ and $\epsilon = 0.1$.
View Complete Step-by-Step Solution

1. Strict Orthogonality

$M_{\text{orth}} = d$ (the dimensionality of the vector space).

2. Exponential Scaling

$$M(d, \epsilon) \ge \exp(c \cdot \epsilon^2 d)$$

The packing capacity grows exponentially with dimension $d$ when small cross-talk is permitted.

3. Capacity at $d = 4096$

With $\epsilon^2 d = 40.96$, $e^{40.96} \approx 6 \times 10^{17}$. Millions of sparse concepts can easily coexist in superposition with negligible pairwise interference.

Exercise 2: Scale-Invariance Degeneracy of the L1 Penalty

Consider $\mathcal{L} = \|\mathbf{x} - W_{\text{dec}} \mathbf{z}\|_2^2 + \lambda \sum_j |z_j|$ without norm constraints.

  1. Show that scaling $W_{\text{dec}} \leftarrow \alpha W_{\text{dec}}$ and $\mathbf{z} \leftarrow \frac{1}{\alpha}\mathbf{z}$ preserves reconstruction error.
  2. What happens to the $L_1$ penalty as $\alpha \to \infty$?
  3. Why does fixing $\|\mathbf{w}_j^{\text{dec}}\|_2 = 1$ resolve this degeneracy?
View Complete Step-by-Step Solution

1. Reconstruction Invariance

$$\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{\text{new}} = (\alpha W_{\text{dec}}) \left(\frac{1}{\alpha}\mathbf{z}\right) = W_{\text{dec}} \mathbf{z} = \hat{\mathbf{x}}$$

2. Vanishing Sparsity Cost

$$\lim_{\alpha \to \infty} \lambda \sum_{j=1}^D \left| \frac{z_j}{\alpha} \right| = 0$$

The optimizer blows up decoder norms while driving latents toward zero, bypassing the sparsity constraint.

3. Normalization Fix

Constraining decoder columns to unit norm $\|\mathbf{w}_j^{\text{dec}}\|_2 = 1$ pins the latent activations to the scale of the data, enforcing genuine sparsity.

Exercise 3: Dead Latents and Shrinkage Bias

Analyze training failure modes in ReLU Sparse Autoencoders.

  1. How does an excessively large $\lambda$ cause permanently dead latents?
  2. Define shrinkage bias caused by the $L_1$ gradient.
  3. Name two modern architectural alternatives that eliminate shrinkage bias.
View Complete Step-by-Step Solution

1. Dead Latent Genesis

When negative biases cause pre-activations to drop $\le 0$, $\text{ReLU}'(u) = 0$ kills gradients completely, leaving the neuron permanently un-updated.

2. Shrinkage Bias

The constant $+\lambda$ subgradient systematically shrinks active feature magnitudes by $\approx \lambda / 2$, distorting reconstruction.

3. Modern Fixes

TopK SAEs: Enforce hard top-$k$ sparsity with zero shrinkage. Gated SAEs: Decouple the binary gating decision from the feature magnitude pathway.
