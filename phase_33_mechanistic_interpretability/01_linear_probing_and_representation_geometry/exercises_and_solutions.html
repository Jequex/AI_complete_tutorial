Exercise 1: Gradient Derivation & Strict Convexity of Linear Probes

Consider a regularized binary cross-entropy probe objective: $$\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{w}, b) = -\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \left[ y^{(i)} \ln \sigma(z^{(i)}) + (1 - y^{(i)}) \ln(1 - \sigma(z^{(i)})) \right] + \frac{\lambda}{2} \|\mathbf{w}\|_2^2$$ with $z^{(i)} = \mathbf{w}^\top \mathbf{h}^{(i)} + b$ and $\lambda > 0$.

  1. Derive the analytical gradients $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{w}}$ and $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial b}$.
  2. Compute the Hessian $\mathbf{H} = \nabla^2_{\mathbf{w}} \mathcal{L}$ and prove that $\mathbf{H} \succ 0$.
  3. Explain why strict convexity makes linear probes superior to multi-layer non-linear probes for probing latent representations.
View Complete Step-by-Step Solution

1. Gradient Derivation

For each sample, $\frac{\partial \ell^{(i)}}{\partial z^{(i)}} = p^{(i)} - y^{(i)}$ where $p^{(i)} = \sigma(z^{(i)})$.

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{w}} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N (p^{(i)} - y^{(i)})\mathbf{h}^{(i)} + \lambda \mathbf{w}$$ $$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial b} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N (p^{(i)} - y^{(i)})$$

2. Hessian & Strict Convexity

$$\mathbf{H} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N p^{(i)}(1 - p^{(i)}) \mathbf{h}^{(i)} (\mathbf{h}^{(i)})^\top + \lambda \mathbf{I}$$

Since $0 < p^{(i)} < 1$, the first term is positive semidefinite. For any non-zero $\mathbf{u} \neq \mathbf{0}$, $\mathbf{u}^\top \mathbf{H} \mathbf{u} \ge \lambda \|\mathbf{u}\|_2^2 > 0$. Hence, $\mathbf{H}$ is strictly positive definite, proving strict convexity.

3. Interpretability Significance

Strict convexity guarantees a unique global minimum $\mathbf{w}^*$. There are no local minima or initialization dependencies, ensuring that results directly measure linear information in $\mathbf{h}$ rather than probe artifacts.

Exercise 2: Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) in the Residual Stream

Let $\mathbf{h} = s \cdot c \cdot \mathbf{v}_c + \boldsymbol{\epsilon}$, where unit vector $\|\mathbf{v}_c\|=1$, $c \in \{-1, +1\}$, and $\boldsymbol{\epsilon} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \sigma^2 \mathbf{I}_d)$.

  1. Compute $\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{v}_c^\top \mathbf{h} \mid c]$ and $\text{Var}(\mathbf{v}_c^\top \mathbf{h})$.
  2. Formulate the theoretical classification error $\mathcal{E}$ in terms of the Gaussian CDF $\Phi(\cdot)$.
  3. Compute the theoretical accuracy for $s = 2.0$ and $\sigma = 1.0$.
View Complete Step-by-Step Solution

1. Moments of the 1D Projection

$\mathbf{v}_c^\top \mathbf{h} = s \cdot c + \mathbf{v}_c^\top \boldsymbol{\epsilon}$. Since $\mathbf{v}_c^\top \boldsymbol{\epsilon} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$, $\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{v}_c^\top \mathbf{h} \mid c] = s \cdot c$ and $\text{Var}(\mathbf{v}_c^\top \mathbf{h}) = \sigma^2$.

2. Theoretical Error Rate

$$\mathcal{E} = P(s + \mathbf{v}_c^\top \boldsymbol{\epsilon} \le 0) = \Phi\left(-\frac{s}{\sigma}\right)$$

3. Numerical Value

For $s/\sigma = 2.0$, $\mathcal{E} = \Phi(-2.0) \approx 0.0228$. Thus, theoretical probe accuracy is $\mathbf{97.72\%}$.

Exercise 3: Selectivity & Memorization Bounds

A probe trained on activations with $d = 4096$ and $N = 256$ achieves $\text{Acc}_{\text{task}} = 98.4\%$. A control probe trained on randomized labels achieves $\text{Acc}_{\text{control}} = 96.1\%$.

  1. Explain why high accuracy can easily occur when $d \gg N$.
  2. Calculate probe selectivity $\text{Sel} = \text{Acc}_{\text{task}} - \text{Acc}_{\text{control}}$ and diagnose whether this representation encodes the concept.
  3. State two methodological corrections.
View Complete Step-by-Step Solution

1. High-Dimensional Capacity

By Cover's Theorem, when parameter dimension exceeds sample size ($d > N$), random sets of points are linearly separable under almost any arbitrary label assignment. The probe can memorize without discovering genuine structure.

2. Selectivity Diagnosis

$$\text{Selectivity} = 98.4\% - 96.1\% = \mathbf{2.3\%}$$

A selectivity of 2.3% fails the selectivity threshold ($\ge 35\%$). The representation does not genuinely encode the concept; the probe simply memorized the training set.

3. Methodological Corrections

(a) Increase sample size $N \gg d$ and evaluate on a held-out test split. (b) Apply bottleneck dimensionality reduction or stronger regularization $\lambda$ until control accuracy drops to 50% chance level.
