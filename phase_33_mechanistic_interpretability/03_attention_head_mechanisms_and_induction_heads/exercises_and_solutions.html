Quantitative problem sets covering low-rank QK/OV circuit bottlenecks, composite induction tensors, and direct logit attribution.

Exercise 1: Low-Rank Structure of Attention Head Projections

In a model with $d = 4096$ and $d_{\text{head}} = 64$, analyze the circuit matrices $W_{QK} = W_Q^\top W_K$ and $W_{OV} = W_O W_V$.

  1. Compute the maximum algebraic rank of $W_{QK}$ and $W_{OV}$.
  2. What percentage of the full $d \times d$ ambient matrix space can a single head write to?
  3. How does this bottleneck prevent multi-head destructive interference in the residual stream?
View Complete Step-by-Step Solution

1. Rank Bound

$\text{rank}(W_O W_V) \le \min(\text{rank}(W_O), \text{rank}(W_V)) \le d_{\text{head}} = \mathbf{64}$.

2. Ambient Dimension Percentage

$$\frac{64}{4096} = \frac{1}{64} \approx \mathbf{1.56\%}$$

3. Communication Bus Isolation

Because each head only occupies $\approx 1.56\%$ of the available representation space, multiple heads write into mutually orthogonal subspaces without overwriting each other's data.

Exercise 2: Deriving the Composite Induction Form

Given Layer 0 output $\Delta \mathbf{h}_0^{(j)} = W_{OV}^{(0)} W_E \mathbf{t}_{j-1}$, analyze Layer 1 attention logits $\alpha_{i, j}^{(1)}$.

  1. Expand $\alpha_{i, j}^{(1)}$ into constituent bilinear terms.
  2. Identify the cross-term responsible for in-context $[A][B] \dots [A] \to [B]$ pattern matching.
  3. Specify the algebraic condition on the matrix $\mathbf{M}_{\text{ind}} = W_E^\top W_{QK}^{(1)} W_{OV}^{(0)} W_E$.
View Complete Step-by-Step Solution

1. Bilinear Expansion

$$\alpha_{i, j}^{(1)} \propto \mathbf{t}_i^\top W_E^\top W_{QK}^{(1)} W_E \mathbf{t}_j + \mathbf{t}_i^\top \left( W_E^\top W_{QK}^{(1)} W_{OV}^{(0)} W_E \right) \mathbf{t}_{j-1}$$

2. Induction Cross-Term

The second term connects current token $\mathbf{t}_i$ directly to the token preceding position $j$ ($\mathbf{t}_{j-1}$).

3. Positive Diagonal Condition

The matrix $\mathbf{M}_{\text{ind}}$ must have large positive diagonal entries $(\mathbf{M}_{\text{ind}})_{A, A} \gg (\mathbf{M}_{\text{ind}})_{A, B}$, ensuring an attention peak whenever $\mathbf{t}_i = \mathbf{t}_{j-1}$.

Exercise 3: Direct Logit Attribution and Copy Head Eigenvalues

Let $\mathbf{T} = W_U W_{OV} W_E \in \mathbb{R}^{V \times V}$ be the direct token-to-token map through an attention head.

  1. What does a large positive eigenvalue $\lambda > 0$ signify?
  2. What role do negative eigenvalues play in text generation?
  3. Calculate the logit boost if diagonal element $\mathbf{T}_{B, B} = +7.0$.
View Complete Step-by-Step Solution

1. Direct Copying

Positive eigenvalues mean the head directly boosts the logit of whichever token it attends to (Copy Head behavior).

2. Negative Name Movers / Anti-Repetition

Negative eigenvalues suppress attended tokens, preventing repetitive stuttering and degenerate loops.

3. Quantitative Logit Impact

$$\Delta \text{logit}(B) = \mathbf{e}_B^\top \mathbf{T} \mathbf{e}_B = \mathbf{+7.0}$$

This $+7.0$ advantage pushes $P(B)$ to near $100\%$, as demonstrated in our experiment.
