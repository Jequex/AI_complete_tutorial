Exercise 1: Logit Difference vs. Probability Restoration

In activation patching experiments, researchers measure $\Delta L = \text{logit}(y_*) - \text{logit}(y_{\text{alt}})$.

  1. Explain why softmax probability $P(y_*)$ is vulnerable to saturation and vanishing sensitivity.
  2. Show that $\Delta L = \ln \left(\frac{P(y_*)}{P(y_{\text{alt}})}\right)$ (the log-odds ratio).
  3. Calculate normalized restoration $\mathcal{R}$ given clean logits $(28.0, 18.0)$, corrupted logits $(12.0, 22.0)$, and patched logits $(24.0, 19.0)$.
View Complete Step-by-Step Solution

1. Softmax Saturation

Softmax squashes logits into $[0, 1]$. Large logit margins saturate near $1.0$, hiding significant internal model shifts, whereas logits remain linear and unbounded.

2. Log-Odds Equivalence

$$\frac{P(y_*)}{P(y_{\text{alt}})} = \frac{e^{\text{logit}(y_*)}}{e^{\text{logit}(y_{\text{alt}})}} = e^{\text{logit}(y_*) - \text{logit}(y_{\text{alt}})} \implies \ln\left(\frac{P(y_*)}{P(y_{\text{alt}})}\right) = \Delta L$$

3. Numerical Calculation

$\Delta L_{\text{clean}} = 28 - 18 = 10$, $\Delta L_{\text{corrupt}} = 12 - 22 = -10$, $\Delta L_{\text{patched}} = 24 - 19 = 5$.

$$\mathcal{R} = \frac{5 - (-10)}{10 - (-10)} = \frac{15}{20} = \mathbf{75.0\%}$$

Exercise 2: Natural Indirect Effect (NIE) and Causal Mediation

In a causal graph $X \to M \to Y$ with direct path $X \to Y$, let $M$ be the Layer 1 MLP at the subject token.

  1. Formulate the Natural Indirect Effect (NIE) in Pearl's structural causal notation.
  2. Relate the NIE formula to activation patching.
  3. Compute the mediation fraction when Total Effect is 40.0 and patched effect is 34.0.
View Complete Step-by-Step Solution

1. Pearl's NIE Formulation

$$\text{NIE} = \mathbb{E}[Y(X = x_{\text{corrupt}}, M(x_{\text{clean}}))] - \mathbb{E}[Y(X = x_{\text{corrupt}}, M(x_{\text{corrupt}}))]$$

2. Equivalence to Activation Patching

This is identical to evaluating the corrupted prompt with $M$ overwritten with the clean activation. Thus, activation patching is an exact realization of causal mediation analysis.

3. Mediation Ratio

$$\frac{\text{NIE}}{\text{TE}} = \frac{34.0}{40.0} = \mathbf{85.0\%}$$

Exercise 3: Information Flow from Subject to Output

Why does patching the MLP at the subject token restore the fact, but patching the MLP at the last token fails?

  1. Analyze the computational independence across positions in Transformer MLP blocks.
  2. Identify the mechanism responsible for transferring information to the output token.
  3. Design a two-stage intervention to prove an attention head is the causal "information mover".
View Complete Step-by-Step Solution

1. Position-Wise Independence

MLP layers operate independently on each token vector $\mathbf{h}_t$. An MLP at the last token cannot access subject information unless it was already transported there.

2. Attention Mechanism

Multi-Head Attention is the only cross-token operation. It reads the subject residual stream and writes into the last token position.

3. Two-Stage Proof

Sufficiency: Patching only the candidate attention head restores $\approx 100\%$ performance. Necessity: Knocking out (zeroing) only that attention head's weight from last to subject collapses accuracy to random chance.
