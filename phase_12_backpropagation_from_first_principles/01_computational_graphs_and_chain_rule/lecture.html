Topic 12.1: Computational Graphs & the Multivariable Chain Rule

Core Thesis: Modern automatic differentiation does not rely on symbolic algebraic manipulation or numerical approximation. Instead, it converts mathematical execution traces into Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs) and computes exact analytical derivatives in linear time $\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{E}|)$ by evaluating the multivariable chain rule in reverse topological order.

1. Executive Summary & The 12-Year-Old Analogy

Modern deep learning architectures contain hundreds of billions of interconnected mathematical operations. How does PyTorch or JAX compute the exact derivative of a scalar loss function with respect to every single parameter in such vast networks without making algebraic errors or exploding in computational complexity?

The 12-Year-Old Analogy: The Rube Goldberg Machine & Tracing Dominos

Imagine you build an elaborate Rube Goldberg machine in your living room:

  1. You roll a marble labeled $x$.
  2. Marble $x$ strikes a lever that tips a seesaw (Node $a$).
  3. Simultaneously, marble $x$ trips a wire dropping a steel ball down a chute (Node $b$).
  4. Both the seesaw and the steel ball strike a chime that rings a brass bell (Node $L$).

The Forward Pass: You push marble $x$ with 2 Newtons of force. Kinetic energy cascades forward through levers and chutes, and the bell rings with 47 decibels. That is forward execution: inputs produce intermediate values, which culminate in the final output.

The Backward Pass (Sensitivity Analysis): Now your friend asks: "If I push marble $x$ slightly harder, how much louder will the bell chime?"

You trace backward from the bell:

  • The chime was struck by both the seesaw ($a$) and the steel ball ($b$).
  • You compute how much the seesaw affected the bell ($\frac{\partial L}{\partial a}$) times how much marble $x$ tipped the seesaw ($\frac{\partial a}{\partial x}$). That's the sensitivity along Path 1!
  • You compute how much the steel ball affected the bell ($\frac{\partial L}{\partial b}$) times how much marble $x$ tripped the wire ($\frac{\partial b}{\partial x}$). That's the sensitivity along Path 2!
  • The Golden Rule: Because marble $x$ caused ripples down two distinct pathways, its total effect on the bell is the sum of both pathways:
$$\text{Total Impact} = \left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial a} \cdot \frac{\partial a}{\partial x}\right) + \left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial b} \cdot \frac{\partial b}{\partial x}\right)$$

This is the Multivariable Chain Rule on Graphs. When a variable fans out into multiple consumers, its gradient is the sum of gradients accumulating from all its downstream children.

2. Mathematical Symbol Decoder

Symbol Domain Description & Operational Meaning
$\mathcal{G} = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E})$ Graph Theory Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) representing data flow across operations
$v_i \in \mathcal{V}$ Tensor / Scalar Graph node representing an input, intermediate variable, or output
$(u, v) \in \mathcal{E}$ Directed Edge Data dependency: node $u$ is an input argument to operation $v$
$\text{Parents}(v)$ Node Set Predecessors of $v$: $\{u \in \mathcal{V} \mid (u, v) \in \mathcal{E}\}$
$\text{Children}(u)$ Node Set Successors of $u$: $\{v \in \mathcal{V} \mid (u, v) \in \mathcal{E}\}$
$L \in \mathbb{R}$ Scalar Field Terminal scalar objective loss function
$\bar{v} \equiv \frac{\partial L}{\partial v}$ Adjoint Sensitivity of terminal loss $L$ to infinitesimal changes in node $v$
$\frac{\partial v}{\partial u}$ Local Jacobian Elementary derivative of operation $v$ with respect to its input $u$

3. Computational Graphs as Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs)

A computational graph decomposes any mathematical expression into a sequence of atomic primitive operations (such as $+$, $\times$, $\exp$, $\tanh$).

3.1 Key Properties of Computational DAGs

  • Acyclicity: Absence of directed loops guarantees that forward evaluation follows a deterministic topological sequence and backward gradients do not encounter infinite recursion.
  • Topological Sort: A linear permutation $v_1, v_2, \dots, v_n$ such that every edge $(v_i, v_j)$ has $i < j$.
  • Reverse Topological Order: Backpropagation traverses the nodes in strictly reverse order ($v_n, v_{n-1}, \dots, v_1$), ensuring that whenever an adjoint $\bar{u}$ is computed, all downstream consumers of $u$ have already contributed their gradients!

4. The Multivariable Chain Rule on Graphs

If an intermediate variable $x$ fans out into multiple children $y_1, y_2, \dots, y_k \in \text{Children}(x)$, then by the multivariable total derivative:

$$\boxed{\bar{x} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial x} = \sum_{y \in \text{Children}(x)} \frac{\partial L}{\partial y} \cdot \frac{\partial y}{\partial x} = \sum_{y \in \text{Children}(x)} \bar{y} \cdot \frac{\partial y}{\partial x}}$$

Critical Implementation Bug: Overwriting vs. Accumulating

In autograd code, writing parent.grad = child.grad * local_deriv is a critical bug whenever a tensor is reused across multiple operations (e.g. x + x or residual connections). The assignment operator overwrites prior branch gradients!

# BUGGY (Overwrites gradients on multi-use variables):
def backward():
    self.prev[0].grad = self.grad * d_prev0

# CORRECT (Accumulates gradients across all downstream branches):
def backward():
    self.prev[0].grad += self.grad * d_prev0
