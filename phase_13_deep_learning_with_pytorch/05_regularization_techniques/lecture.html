Topic 13.5: Regularization Techniques — Dropout, Weight Decay & Early Stopping

Preventing memorization in overparameterized networks: Inverted Dropout scaling, Decoupled Weight Decay (AdamW), and validation-based early stopping monitors.

1. Executive Summary & The 12-Year-Old Analogy

Deep networks possess millions of parameters and can easily memorize random noise. Regularization constrains optimization to discover generalizable patterns.

The 12-Year-Old Analogy: The Random Obstacle Course & The Safety Harness

Think of coaching a soccer team:

Overfitting (Memorizing the Cones): If you run drills around fixed cones, players memorize the exact steps. On match day against a real team, they get confused and lose because real opponents don't stand still like cones!

Inverted Dropout: During practice, you randomly blindfold 3 out of 10 players. The squad cannot rely on a single superstar; every player learns teamwork. On match day, with blindfolds off, playing together is effortless!

Weight Decay: When a player tries reckless backflips instead of clean passes, you strap on a modest training vest. It discourages wild over-reactions and promotes smooth, controlled technique.

Early Stopping: When the team hits peak performance during practice, the coach blows the whistle and sends everyone home before exhaustion causes bad habits.

2. Mathematical Symbol Decoder

Symbol Mathematical Domain Description & Engineering Interpretation
$p \in [0, 1)$ Probability Dropout rate: probability of zeroing an activation during training.
$\mathbf{m} \sim \text{Bernoulli}(1-p)$ Binary Mask Vector of binary switches dynamically sampled each forward step.
$\lambda > 0$ Hyperparameter Weight decay factor penalizing $L_2$ parameter norms.
$\mathbf{w}(1 - \alpha \lambda)$ Weight Shrinkage Multiplicative weight decay executed before gradient addition.
$k_{\text{patience}}$ $\mathbb{N}$ Number of validation epochs to wait before arresting training.

3. Inverted Dropout Expectation Preservation

PyTorch implements Inverted Dropout, which scales activations during training by $\frac{1}{1-p}$:

$$\mathbf{y}_{\text{train}} = \frac{1}{1-p} (\mathbf{m} \odot \mathbf{x}), \quad \text{where } m_j \sim \text{Bernoulli}(1-p)$$ $$\mathbf{y}_{\text{test}} = \mathbf{x}$$

Because $\mathbb{E}[m] = 1-p$, $\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{y}_{\text{train}}] = \frac{1}{1-p}(1-p)\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{x}$. The test-time inference forward pass is an exact identity mapping with zero compute overhead!

4. Decoupled Weight Decay (AdamW)

Loshchilov & Hutter (2019) proved that adding $L_2$ penalties directly to Adam distorts regularization because adaptive scaling divides the decay term by $\sqrt{\mathbf{v}_t}$. AdamW decouples weight decay:

$$\mathbf{w}_{t+1} = \mathbf{w}_t (1 - \alpha \lambda) - \frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{\mathbf{v}_t} + \epsilon} \mathbf{m}_t$$
