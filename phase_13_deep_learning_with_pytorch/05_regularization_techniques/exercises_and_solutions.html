Topic 13.5: Exercises & Solutions

Analytical proof of Dropout as adaptive feature-weighted $L_2$ regularization, data augmentation as tangent manifold regularization, and AdamW weight decay dynamics.

Problem 1: Dropout as Adaptive Data-Dependent $L_2$ Regularization

For a linear model $\hat{y} = \mathbf{w}^T \tilde{\mathbf{x}}$ with Inverted Dropout $\tilde{x}_j = \frac{m_j}{1-p} x_j$, prove that:

$$\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{m}}\left[ \frac{1}{2}(y - \mathbf{w}^T \tilde{\mathbf{x}})^2 \right] = \frac{1}{2}(y - \mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x})^2 + \frac{p}{2(1 - p)} \sum_{j=1}^D w_j^2 x_j^2$$

Solution 1

Using $\mathbb{E}[Z^2] = (\mathbb{E}[Z])^2 + \text{Var}(Z)$:

$$\mathbb{E}[(y - \mathbf{w}^T \tilde{\mathbf{x}})^2] = (y - \mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x})^2 + \text{Var}\left(\sum_{j=1}^D \frac{w_j x_j}{1-p} m_j\right)$$

Since the Bernoulli masks $m_j$ are independent with variance $\text{Var}(m_j) = p(1-p)$:

$$\text{Var} = \sum_{j=1}^D \frac{w_j^2 x_j^2}{(1-p)^2} p(1-p) = \frac{p}{1-p} \sum_{j=1}^D w_j^2 x_j^2$$

Multiplying by $1/2$ completes the proof. Notice that Dropout penalizes $w_j^2$ proportionally to feature power $x_j^2$, regularizing high-variance features more strictly.

Problem 2: Data Augmentation as Tangent Invariance

Show that infinitesimal data transformations $T_\epsilon(\mathbf{x}) \approx \mathbf{x} + \epsilon \mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x})$ add a directional gradient penalty:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{aug}} \approx \mathcal{L}_0 + \frac{\sigma_\epsilon^2}{2} \|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} f(\mathbf{x}) \cdot \mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x})\|^2$$

Solution 2

By second-order Taylor expansion around $\epsilon=0$ and taking expectation over zero-mean noise $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_\epsilon^2)$, the first-order term cancels out while the quadratic term produces the directional derivative penalty. This forces the model to be locally flat along the transformation manifold.

Problem 3: Decoupled Weight Decay under Cosine Annealing

In AdamW with learning rate $\alpha_t \to 0$, why does shrinking the weight decay step $\alpha_t \lambda \mathbf{w}_t$ improve late-stage optimization?

Solution 3

In early training, high learning rates drive strong weight decay, simplifying the network hypothesis space. As $\alpha_t \to 0$ in final epochs, decay force approaches zero, allowing the optimizer to settle smoothly into the local minimum without being artificially dragged toward zero.
