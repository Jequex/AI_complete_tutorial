Topic 13.3: Weight Initialization Schemes & Deep Variance Dynamics

Preventing vanishing and exploding activations: the mathematical derivations of Xavier/Glorot for Tanh and He/Kaiming for ReLU across 50+ deep layers.

1. Executive Summary & The 12-Year-Old Analogy

In deep neural architectures, improper weight initialization causes activation variance to either explode exponentially to infinity or collapse to zero within the first 10 layers.

The 12-Year-Old Analogy: Tuning the Microphone Volume Before the Concert

Imagine a concert audio rig with 50 amplifiers connected in series.

If each amplifier dampens the audio signal by just 10% ($0.9\times$), then after 50 amplifiers, $(0.9)^{50} \approx 0.005$! The sound is extinguished into silence before reaching the stadium speakers.

If each amplifier boosts the signal by 10% ($1.1\times$), then after 50 amplifiers, $(1.1)^{50} \approx 117\times$! The volume overloads the speakers into screeching feedback.

The sound engineer (Xavier & He Initialization) calculates the electronic impedance of each amplifier ($1/\sqrt{n_{\text{in}}}$) to keep volume at exactly $1.0\times$. Furthermore, because a distortion pedal (ReLU) cuts off the negative half of the sound wave, the engineer boosts the signal by $\sqrt{2}$ to keep the music loud and clear!

2. Mathematical Symbol Decoder

Symbol Mathematical Domain Description & Engineering Interpretation
$n_{\text{in}}$ $\mathbb{N}$ Fan-in: number of incoming connections to a neuron.
$n_{\text{out}}$ $\mathbb{N}$ Fan-out: number of outgoing connections from a neuron.
$\text{Var}(y) = n_{\text{in}} \sigma_w^2 \sigma_x^2$ Variance Equation Linear layer variance propagation under zero-mean independent random variables.
$\sigma_{\text{Xavier}}^2 = \frac{2}{n_{\text{in}} + n_{\text{out}}}$ Harmonic Mean Variance Glorot formula balancing forward activation variance with backward gradient variance.
$\sigma_{\text{He}}^2 = \frac{2}{n_{\text{in}}}$ Rectified Variance Kaiming formula compensating for ReLU zeroing 50% of the distribution.

3. The ReLU Variance Halving Effect

For zero-centered symmetric pre-activation $z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_z^2)$, ReLU zeroes out the negative half:

$$\mathbb{E}[\text{ReLU}(z)^2] = \int_0^\infty z^2 p(z) \, dz = \frac{1}{2} \text{Var}(z)$$

Hence, standard Xavier initialization causes variance to decay by $(1/2)^L$. Over 50 layers, $(0.5)^{50} \approx 10^{-16}$, extinguishing the signal. He / Kaiming initialization multiplies weight variance by 2 ($\sigma^2 = \frac{2}{n_{\text{in}}}$), perfectly cancelling the half-rectification loss!

PyTorch Initialization Best Practices

# For Linear / Conv layers followed by ReLU:
torch.nn.init.kaiming_normal_(layer.weight, mode='fan_in', nonlinearity='relu')
torch.nn.init.zeros_(layer.bias)

# For Linear / Conv layers followed by Tanh:
torch.nn.init.xavier_normal_(layer.weight)
torch.nn.init.zeros_(layer.bias)
