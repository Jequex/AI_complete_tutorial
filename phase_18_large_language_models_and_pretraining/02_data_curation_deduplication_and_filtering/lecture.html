2. MinHash Theorem & Universal Hashing

The MinHash algorithm (Broder, 1997) maps variable-length sets into fixed-size signature vectors: $$P\left( \min_{s \in S_A} \pi(s) = \min_{s \in S_B} \pi(s) \right) = \frac{|S_A \cap S_B|}{|S_A \cup S_B|} = J(A, B)$$

To compute signatures efficiently in practice without materializing large permutations, we employ universal hash functions: $$h_i(x) = (a_i x + b_i) \bmod p$$ where $p = 2^{31} - 1$ is a Mersenne prime, and $a_i, b_i$ are randomly chosen integers.

3. The LSH Banding Mechanism & S-Curve Derivation

Pairwise comparisons across a corpus of $N = 10^9$ documents would require $\approx 5 \times 10^{17}$ comparisons. LSH groups $M$ signature integers into $b$ bands of $r$ rows:

  • Probability of an exact match in all $r$ rows of a single band: $s^r$
  • Probability of differing in at least one row: $1 - s^r$
  • Probability of failing to match across all $b$ independent bands: $(1 - s^r)^b$
  • Probability of colliding in at least one band: $$P_{\text{collision}}(s) = 1 - (1 - s^r)^b$$

4. Heuristic Quality Filtering (The Gopher & C4 Rules)

  • Stopword Threshold: High-quality English text requires $\ge 15\%$ stopword density.
  • Symbol-to-Word Ratio: Code dumps or binary noise with $> 18\%$ symbol characters are pruned.
  • Repetition Penalties: Documents with any single 3-gram representing $> 20\%$ of all 3-grams are discarded to eliminate infinite crawler loops.
  • Benchmark Decontamination: Containment checks against 13-gram test sets ensure benchmark integrity.
