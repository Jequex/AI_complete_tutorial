Exercise 1: LSH Banding Parameter Optimization & S-Curve Analysis

An engineering team has $M = 128$ MinHash permutations and tests three band configurations:

  • Config 1: $b=32, r=4$
  • Config 2: $b=16, r=8$
  • Config 3: $b=8, r=16$
  1. Calculate theoretical 50% threshold $s^* \approx (1/b)^{1/r}$ for each.
  2. Evaluate $P_{\text{collision}}(s) = 1 - (1 - s^r)^b$ for near-duplicates ($s = 0.80$) and unrelated texts ($s = 0.30$).
  3. Determine which configuration achieves optimal precision-recall trade-off.
View Complete Mathematical Solution

1. Theoretical Thresholds:

$$s_1^* = (1/32)^{1/4} \approx 0.4204, \quad s_2^* = (1/16)^{1/8} \approx 0.7071, \quad s_3^* = (1/8)^{1/16} \approx 0.8781$$

2. Collision Probabilities:

  • Config 1 ($b=32, r=4$): $P(0.80) \approx 0.99999995$, but $P(0.30) \approx 0.2291$ (catastrophic 23% false positive rate).
  • Config 2 ($b=16, r=8$): $P(0.80) \approx 0.9481$ (94.8% recall), and $P(0.30) \approx 0.00105$ (only 0.1% false positives).
  • Config 3 ($b=8, r=16$): $P(0.80) \approx 0.2063$ (terrible false negative rate: misses 79% of duplicates).

3. Conclusion:

Config 2 ($b=16, r=8$) is the industry standard: it captures virtually all duplicates with almost zero false collisions.
Exercise 2: Shannon Entropy & Repetition Quality Filtering

Compare Document A (the 4 words "loading data please wait" repeated 200 times) with Document B (clean encyclopedia prose with 320 unique words and $H(W) \approx 7.2$ bits/word).

  1. Formulate the unigram word entropy $H(W)$.
  2. Calculate $H(W_A)$ for Document A.
  3. Evaluate the 3-gram repetition ratio for Document A against Gopher / C4 rules.
View Complete Mathematical Solution

1. Entropy Formulation:

$$H(W) = -\sum_{i=1}^{|V|} p(w_i) \log_2 p(w_i)$$

2. Document A Calculation:

$$|V| = 4, \quad p(w_i) = 0.25$$ $$H(W_A) = -4 \cdot (0.25 \log_2 0.25) = -1.0 \cdot (-2.0) = 2.0 \text{ bits/word}$$ Document A has an entropy deficit of over 5.2 bits per word compared to natural prose ($H \approx 7.2$).

3. 3-Gram Ratio:

$$\text{Max 3-gram ratio} = \frac{200}{798} \approx 25.06\%$$ Because $25.06\% > 20\%$, Document A violates the C4/Gopher repetition threshold and is discarded.
Exercise 3: Benchmark Decontamination via 13-gram Containment Probability

Explain why an $n=13$ word overlap threshold is used for benchmark decontamination rather than $n=3$.

View Complete Mathematical Solution

1. Small $n$ Flaw: Common 3-word phrases ("in order to", "according to the") occur naturally billions of times in English prose. A 3-gram filter would discard millions of clean, unrelated training documents.

2. 13-Gram Random Probability: In human language with typical branching factor $\approx 20$: $$P(\text{accidental 13-gram match}) \approx (20)^{-13} \approx 1.2 \times 10^{-17}$$ In a 1-Trillion token corpus ($10^{12}$ tokens), the expected number of accidental matches is: $$\mathbb{E}[\text{matches}] = 10^{12} \times 1.2 \times 10^{-17} \approx 1.2 \times 10^{-5} \ll 1$$ An accidental 13-gram collision is statistically impossible. Any match represents genuine memorization or data leakage.
