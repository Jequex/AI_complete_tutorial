Analytical & Applied Problems in Compute-Optimal Pretraining and Lifecycle Economics.

Exercise 1: Deriving the Chinchilla Frontier for the GPT-3 Compute Budget

OpenAI trained GPT-3 with $N = 175 \times 10^9$ non-embedding parameters on $D = 300 \times 10^9$ tokens.

  1. Compute total pretraining FLOPs $C$ using $C \approx 6ND$.
  2. Using Chinchilla parameters ($E = 1.6934, A = 406.4, B = 410.7, \alpha = 0.3392, \beta = 0.2849$), compute compute-optimal $N^*$ and $D^*$.
  3. Compute the predicted loss $L(175\text{B}, 300\text{B})$ vs. $L(N^*, D^*)$ and find the loss improvement $\Delta L$.
  4. Find the parameter oversizing ratio $\frac{N_{\text{GPT-3}}}{N^*}$.
View Complete Mathematical Solution

1. Compute Budget:

$$C = 6 \cdot (1.75 \times 10^{11}) \cdot (3.0 \times 10^{11}) = 3.15 \times 10^{23} \text{ FLOPs}$$

2. Optimal Exponents and Factor:

$$a = \frac{\beta}{\alpha+\beta} = \frac{0.2849}{0.6241} \approx 0.4565, \quad b = \frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta} = \frac{0.3392}{0.6241} \approx 0.5435$$ $$G = \left(\frac{0.3392 \cdot 406.4}{0.2849 \cdot 410.7}\right)^{\frac{1}{0.6241}} \approx (1.1781)^{1.6023} \approx 1.2974$$ $$N^* = 1.2974 \cdot \left(\frac{3.15 \times 10^{23}}{6}\right)^{0.4565} \approx 30.64 \times 10^9 \text{ (30.6B parameters)}$$ $$D^* = \frac{1}{1.2974} \cdot \left(\frac{3.15 \times 10^{23}}{6}\right)^{0.5435} \approx 1.714 \times 10^{12} \text{ (1.71 Trillion tokens)}$$

3. Loss Delta:

$$L_{\text{GPT-3}} = 1.6934 + 0.0638 + 0.2192 \approx 1.9764 \text{ nats}$$ $$L_{\text{optimal}} = 1.6934 + 0.1152 + 0.1318 \approx 1.9404 \text{ nats}$$ $$\Delta L = 1.9764 - 1.9404 = 0.0360 \text{ nats}$$ This $0.036$ nats difference represents roughly a $2.8\times$ compute-efficiency advantage!

4. Oversizing Factor:

$$\frac{N_{\text{GPT-3}}}{N^*} = \frac{175\text{B}}{30.64\text{B}} \approx 5.71\times$$ GPT-3 was $5.7\times$ oversized relative to compute optimality.
Exercise 2: Kaplan vs Chinchilla Asymptotic Scaling Divergence

An organization scales compute by a factor of $K = 10,000\times$.

  1. Compute the parameter and token multipliers under Kaplan's law ($N \propto C^{0.73}, D \propto C^{0.27}$).
  2. Compute the multipliers under Chinchilla's law ($N \propto C^{0.46}, D \propto C^{0.54}$).
  3. Evaluate the resulting tokens-per-parameter ratio $\frac{D}{N}$ starting from a baseline of $20$.
View Complete Mathematical Solution

1. Kaplan Scaling Multipliers:

$$\text{Multiplier}_N = (10^4)^{0.73} \approx 831.8\times, \quad \text{Multiplier}_D = (10^4)^{0.27} \approx 12.02\times$$

2. Chinchilla Scaling Multipliers:

$$\text{Multiplier}_N = (10^4)^{0.46} \approx 69.18\times, \quad \text{Multiplier}_D = (10^4)^{0.54} \approx 144.54\times$$

3. Tokens-per-Parameter Ratio:

$$R_{\text{Kaplan}} = 20 \cdot \frac{12.02}{831.8} \approx 0.289 \text{ tokens/parameter}$$ $$R_{\text{Chinchilla}} = 20 \cdot \frac{144.54}{69.18} \approx 41.8 \text{ tokens/parameter}$$ Under Kaplan's law, parameter count balloons while the model receives less than $0.3$ tokens per parameter.
Exercise 3: The LLaMA Serving Breakeven Derivation

Compare Model A ($N_A = 30\text{B}, D_A = 500\text{B}$) with Model B ($N_B = 7\text{B}$, trained with twice the compute $C_{\text{pretrain}, B} = 2 C_{\text{pretrain}, A}$).

  1. Find $D_B$ (training tokens for Model B).
  2. Derive the analytical breakeven query volume $Q^*$ where $C_{\text{lifecycle}, B} \le C_{\text{lifecycle}, A}$.
  3. Calculate $Q^*$ and evaluate time-to-breakeven at $10$ billion served tokens per day.
View Complete Mathematical Solution

1. Training Tokens for Model B:

$$C_{\text{pretrain}, A} = 6 \cdot (30\text{B}) \cdot (500\text{B}) = 9.0 \times 10^{22} \text{ FLOPs}$$ $$C_{\text{pretrain}, B} = 1.80 \times 10^{23} \text{ FLOPs} \implies D_B = \frac{1.80 \times 10^{23}}{6 \cdot (7\text{B})} \approx 4.29 \text{ Trillion tokens}$$

2. Analytical Breakeven:

$$C_{\text{pretrain}, A} + 2 N_A Q^* = C_{\text{pretrain}, B} + 2 N_B Q^*$$ $$Q^* = \frac{\Delta C_{\text{pretrain}}}{2 (N_A - N_B)}$$

3. Numerical Value:

$$Q^* = \frac{9.0 \times 10^{22}}{2 \cdot (23 \times 10^9)} = \frac{9.0 \times 10^{22}}{4.6 \times 10^{10}} \approx 1.96 \times 10^{12} \text{ tokens (1.96 Trillion tokens)}$$ $$\text{Days to Breakeven} = \frac{1.96 \times 10^{12}}{10 \times 10^9} \approx 195.7 \text{ days}$$ After roughly 6.5 months, the 7B model delivers permanent compute and latency savings.
