Problem 1: Manual Step-by-Step Scaled Dot-Product Attention (Easy)

Consider a toy attention layer with sequence length $T = 2$, key dimension $d_k = 2$, and value dimension $d_v = 2$.

$$Q = \begin{bmatrix} 2.0 & 0.0 \\ 0.0 & 2.0 \end{bmatrix}, \quad K = \begin{bmatrix} 2.0 & 0.0 \\ 1.0 & 1.0 \end{bmatrix}, \quad V = \begin{bmatrix} 1.0 & 3.0 \\ 4.0 & 2.0 \end{bmatrix}$$
  1. Calculate unscaled affinity matrix $S = Q K^T$.
  2. Divide by $\sqrt{d_k} = \sqrt{2} \approx 1.4142$ to obtain scaled logits $Z = S / \sqrt{2}$.
  3. Compute row-wise softmax attention weights $A \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times 2}$.
  4. Compute the final output tensor $\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times 2}$.
View Complete Solution

Step 1: Compute $S = Q K^T$:

$$S = \begin{bmatrix} 2.0 & 0.0 \\ 0.0 & 2.0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 2.0 & 1.0 \\ 0.0 & 1.0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 4.0 & 2.0 \\ 0.0 & 2.0 \end{bmatrix}$$

Step 2: Scale by $\sqrt{2} \approx 1.41421$:

$$Z = \frac{S}{\sqrt{2}} = \begin{bmatrix} 2.8284 & 1.4142 \\ 0.0 & 1.4142 \end{bmatrix}$$

Step 3: Row-Wise Softmax Normalization:

Row 1: $e^0 = 1.0, e^{-1.4142} \approx 0.2431 \implies A_{11} \approx \frac{1.0}{1.2431} \approx 0.8044, A_{12} \approx 0.1956$.

Row 2: $e^{-1.4142} \approx 0.2431, e^0 = 1.0 \implies A_{21} \approx 0.1956, A_{22} \approx 0.8044$.

$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 0.8044 & 0.1956 \\ 0.1956 & 0.8044 \end{bmatrix}$$

Step 4: Value Matrix Multiplication:

$$\text{Output} = A V = \begin{bmatrix} 0.8044 & 0.1956 \\ 0.1956 & 0.8044 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1.0 & 3.0 \\ 4.0 & 2.0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1.5868 & 2.8044 \\ 3.4132 & 2.1956 \end{bmatrix}$$

Problem 2: Variance of Logit Differences & Scaling Justification (Medium)

Let $\mathbf{q} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_k}$ and let $\mathbf{k}_1, \mathbf{k}_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{d_k}$ be independent standard normal random vectors. Define unscaled logits $z_1 = \mathbf{q}^T \mathbf{k}_1$ and $z_2 = \mathbf{q}^T \mathbf{k}_2$.

  1. Prove that $\mathbb{E}[z_1 - z_2] = 0$.
  2. Prove that $\operatorname{Var}(z_1 - z_2) = 2 d_k$.
  3. Evaluate $\sigma(z_1 - z_2) = \sqrt{2 d_k}$ for $d_k = 64$ and $d_k = 2048$, and explain the winner-take-all collapse without scaling.
View Complete Solution

1. Expectation: $\mathbb{E}[z_1 - z_2] = \mathbb{E}[z_1] - \mathbb{E}[z_2] = 0 - 0 = 0$.

2. Variance: $\operatorname{Var}(z_1 - z_2) = \operatorname{Var}(z_1) + \operatorname{Var}(z_2) - 2\operatorname{Cov}(z_1, z_2) = d_k + d_k - 0 = 2d_k$.

3. Collapse: For $d_k = 2048$, $\sigma = \sqrt{4096} = 64$. A $3\sigma$ difference is $\Delta z = 192$, yielding probability ratio $e^{192} \approx 2.4 \times 10^{83}$. Softmax completely saturates to a one-hot vector, vanishing the gradient.

Problem 3: Softmax Jacobian Vanishing Gradient Proof (Hard)

Derive the Jacobian matrix $J_{ij} = \frac{\partial S_i}{\partial z_j} = S_i(\delta_{ij} - S_j)$ and prove that when one logit $z_1 = M \to \infty$ relative to others, all entries decay at rate $\mathcal{O}(e^{-M})$.

View Complete Solution

Using quotient rule: $$\frac{\partial S_i}{\partial z_i} = S_i(1 - S_i), \quad \frac{\partial S_i}{\partial z_j} = -S_i S_j \quad (i \ne j)$$ When $z_1 = M \gg 1$, $S_1 \approx 1 - (T-1)e^{-M}$ and $S_k \approx e^{-M}$ for $k \ge 2$. Thus $J_{11} \approx (T-1)e^{-M} \to 0$, $J_{kk} \approx e^{-M} \to 0$, and off-diagonals decay as $e^{-M}$ or $e^{-2M}$. The entire Jacobian matrix vanishes exponentially, paralyzing backpropagation updates.
