Topic 16.4: Transformer Architecture & Causal Masking

Autoregressive decoding, causal upper-triangular masks, residual stream highways, and quadratic complexity.

1. Executive Summary & The 12-Year-Old Analogy

Having developed Scaled Dot-Product Attention, Multi-Head Attention, and Positional Encodings, we now assemble the complete Transformer Architecture.

In generative models (such as GPT-4, LLaMA 3, Claude, and Gemini), tokens cannot look into the future during autoregressive text generation. To enable simultaneous parallel training over all tokens on GPUs, we introduce Causal Masking.

$$\text{MaskedAttention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}} + M\right) V$$ $$M_{ij} = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } j \le i \\ -\infty & \text{if } j > i \end{cases}$$

The 12-Year-Old Analogy: The Blindfolded Reader & The Express Subway

  • The Time-Machine Curtain (Causal Masking): When reading a mystery book, a magic curtain covers all future pages ($-\infty$). You are only permitted to read words up to where your eyes currently rest.
  • The Express Subway (Residual Connections): The original words ride an uninterrupted express subway track ($x + f(x)$). Detectives whisper updates into the subway car, but if a detective has nothing to say, the train speeds along without losing the original message!
  • The Fact Memorizer (Feed-Forward MLP): While attention connects words across the sentence, the MLP stores factual knowledge (*"Paris is the capital of France"*).

2. Mathematical Symbol Decoder

Symbol Tensor Shape Domain Description
$T$ Scalar $\mathbb{N}$ Sequence length (context window)
$d_{\text{model}}$ Scalar $\mathbb{N}$ Token hidden dimension
$d_{\text{ff}}$ Scalar $4 \times d_{\text{model}}$ MLP intermediate dimension
$M \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times T}$ Matrix $\{0, -\infty\}$ Upper-triangular causal mask
$\text{LN}(x)$ Operator $\mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}^d$ Layer Normalization with learnable scale $\gamma$ and bias $\beta$
$A_{ij}$ Scalar $[0, 1]$ Attention weight (strictly 0 for all $j > i$)

3. Pre-LN vs Post-LN Architecture

The modern Pre-LayerNorm architecture places normalization before each sub-layer:

$$x^{(l+1)} = x^{(l)} + \text{MHA}(\text{LN}_1(x^{(l)}))$$ $$x^{(l+2)} = x^{(l+1)} + \text{FFN}(\text{LN}_2(x^{(l+1)}))$$

Because the identity path $x^{(l)} + \dots$ is unhindered by normalization, the gradient of the loss backpropagates directly to the input without attenuation:

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial x^{(0)}} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial x^{(L)}} \left( I + \sum_{l=0}^{L-1} \frac{\partial \mathcal{F}_l}{\partial x^{(0)}} \right)$$

4. Computational Complexity: The Quadratic $\mathcal{O}(T^2)$ Bottleneck

Operation FLOPs Peak Memory Hardware Bottleneck
Projections ($Q, K, V, W^O$) $\mathcal{O}(T \cdot d_{\text{model}}^2)$ $\mathcal{O}(T \cdot d_{\text{model}})$ Compute-bound (Tensor Cores)
Attention Matrix ($Q K^T$) $\mathcal{O}(T^2 \cdot d_k)$ $\mathcal{O}(T^2)$ Memory bandwidth (HBM bound)
Feed-Forward MLP $\mathcal{O}(8 T d_{\text{model}}^2)$ $\mathcal{O}(T \cdot d_{\text{ff}})$ Compute-bound

For large contexts ($T \ge 128\text{k}$), the $\mathcal{O}(T^2)$ attention matrix memory footprint requires advanced techniques like FlashAttention (SRAM tiling) and KV-Cache (autoregressive key-value reuse).
