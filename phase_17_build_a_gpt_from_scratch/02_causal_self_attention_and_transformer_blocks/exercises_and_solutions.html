Problem 1: Manual Causal Attention Softmax Calculation (Easy)

Given the unnormalized score matrix $S \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$:

$$S = \begin{bmatrix} 2.0 & 4.0 & 1.0 \\ 1.5 & 3.0 & 5.0 \\ 0.0 & 2.5 & 4.0 \end{bmatrix}$$

Apply the causal mask $M$ and calculate the exact lower-triangular attention weights matrix $A$.

View Complete Solution

Masked Logits $\tilde{S} = S + M$:

$$\tilde{S} = \begin{bmatrix} 2.0 & -\infty & -\infty \\ 1.5 & 3.0 & -\infty \\ 0.0 & 2.5 & 4.0 \end{bmatrix}$$

Applying Softmax Row-by-Row:

  • Row 1: $\text{softmax}([2.0, -\infty, -\infty]) = [1.0, 0.0, 0.0]$
  • Row 2: $\exp([1.5, 3.0, -\infty]) = [4.4817, 20.0855, 0] \implies [0.1824, 0.8176, 0.0000]$
  • Row 3: $\exp([0.0, 2.5, 4.0]) = [1.0, 12.1825, 54.5982] \implies [0.0148, 0.1797, 0.8055]$
$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 1.0000 & 0.0000 & 0.0000 \\ 0.1824 & 0.8176 & 0.0000 \\ 0.0148 & 0.1797 & 0.8055 \end{bmatrix}$$

Problem 2: Parameter Count of a Single GPT-2 Small Transformer Block (Medium)

Calculate the total parameter count of a single GPT-2 Small Transformer block ($d_{\text{model}} = 768$, $H = 12$, $d_{\text{ff}} = 3072$).

View Complete Solution

1. Attention Sub-Layer:
4 projection matrices ($W_q, W_k, W_v, W_o$) of shape $[768, 768]$ plus biases:
$4 \times (768 \times 768 + 768) = 4 \times (589{,}824 + 768) = 2{,}362{,}368$ parameters.

2. MLP Sub-Layer:
$W_1: 768 \times 3072 + 3072 = 2{,}362{,}368$
$W_2: 3072 \times 768 + 768 = 2{,}360{,}064$
Total MLP = $4{,}722{,}432$ parameters (exactly $2\times$ the attention weights!).

3. Layer Normalization:
2 layers $\times 2 \times 768 = 3{,}072$ parameters.

Total Block Parameters:
$2{,}362{,}368 + 4{,}722{,}432 + 3{,}072 = \mathbf{7{,}087{,}872}$ parameters ($\approx 7.09\text{M}$).

Problem 3: Mathematical Proof of Gradient Highway in Pre-LN (Hard)

Prove that in Pre-LN architectures, the gradient of the loss $\mathcal{L}$ with respect to layer activation $x_l$ contains an unattenuated identity component $\mathbf{I}$.

View Complete Solution

Unrolling the Pre-LN recurrence $x_{l+1} = x_l + F_l(\text{LN}(x_l))$ yields:

$$x_L = x_l + \sum_{i=l}^{L-1} F_i(\text{LN}(x_i))$$

Differentiating with respect to $x_l$ via the chain rule:

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial x_l} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial x_L} \frac{\partial x_L}{\partial x_l} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial x_L} \left( \mathbf{I} + \frac{\partial}{\partial x_l} \sum_{i=l}^{L-1} F_i(\text{LN}(x_i)) \right)$$

The term $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial x_L} \mathbf{I}$ flows backward unhindered through arbitrarily deep Transformer stacks without passing through repeated matrix multiplications or non-linear decay.
