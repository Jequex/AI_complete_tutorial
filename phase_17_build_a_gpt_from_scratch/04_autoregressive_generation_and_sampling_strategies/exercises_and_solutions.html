Problem 1: Manual Top-k and Top-p Nucleus Filtering (Easy)

Given logits $\mathbf{z} = [3.0, 1.0, 0.0, 2.0, -1.0]$ for words ['the', 'cat', 'dog', 'is', 'a']:

  1. Compute the softmax probabilities $P(w)$.
  2. Apply Top-$k$ filtering ($k=2$).
  3. Apply Top-$p$ nucleus filtering ($p=0.80$).
View Complete Solution

1. Softmax Probabilities ($\sum \exp(z) = 31.5608$):

  • 'the' ($z=3.0$): $20.0855 / 31.5608 \approx \mathbf{0.6364}$ (63.6%)
  • 'is' ($z=2.0$): $7.3891 / 31.5608 \approx \mathbf{0.2341}$ (23.4%)
  • 'cat' ($z=1.0$): $2.7183 / 31.5608 \approx \mathbf{0.0861}$ (8.6%)
  • 'dog' ($z=0.0$): $1.0000 / 31.5608 \approx \mathbf{0.0317}$ (3.2%)
  • 'a' ($z=-1.0$): $0.3679 / 31.5608 \approx \mathbf{0.0117}$ (1.2%)

2. Top-k ($k=2$): Top 2 words are 'the' and 'is'. Renormalized probabilities: $P'(\text{'the'}) = 73.1\%, \; P'(\text{'is'}) = 26.9\%$.

3. Top-p ($p=0.80$): Cumulative mass for 'the' ($63.6\%$) + 'is' ($23.4\%$) = $87.0\% \ge 80\%$. Nucleus is exactly {'the', 'is'}.

Problem 2: Memory Footprint of the KV-Cache (Medium)

Calculate the KV-Cache memory footprint for serving GPT-2 Large ($L=36, d=1280$) across $B=16$ concurrent users with context $T=2048$ in FP16.

View Complete Solution

Total elements: $2 \times B \times L \times T \times d_{\text{model}}$:

$$\text{Elements} = 2 \times 16 \times 36 \times 2048 \times 1280 = 3{,}019{,}898{,}880 \text{ floats}$$

At 2 bytes per float (FP16):

$$\text{VRAM} = 3{,}019{,}898{,}880 \times 2 = 6{,}039{,}797{,}760 \text{ bytes} \approx \mathbf{6.04\text{ GB (5.62 GiB)}}$$

Problem 3: Asymptotic Speedup Proof of KV-Caching (Hard)

Prove that generating $M$ tokens with KV-caching accelerates self-attention compute by $\frac{2}{3} M$ asymptotically over naive recomputation.

View Complete Solution

Naive attention FLOPs: $\sum_{t=1}^M 2 t^2 d \approx \frac{2}{3} d M^3$.

KV-Cached attention FLOPs: $\sum_{t=1}^M 2 t d \approx d M^2$.

$$\lim_{M \to \infty} \frac{\text{FLOPs}_{\text{Naive}}}{\text{FLOPs}_{\text{Cached}}} = \frac{\frac{2}{3} d M^3}{d M^2} = \frac{2}{3} M$$

For $M=1000$ generated tokens, speedup is $\frac{2(1000)+1}{3} = \mathbf{667\times}$ faster!
