Topic 8.4: Support Vector Machines & The Kernel Trick

Maximum margin hyperplanes, Lagrangian duality, KKT conditions, and infinite-dimensional Mercer kernels.

The 12-Year-Old Analogy: The Widest Street Between Two Rival Neighborhoods

Imagine two rival neighborhoods in a city: The Red Wolves and The Blue Bears. You are tasked with designing a straight street separating them.

You want to pave the widest possible street (Maximum Margin). The center of the street is the decision boundary ($w^T x + b = 0$), and the two sidewalks are the margins ($+1$ and $-1$).

The only houses that dictate the width of the street are the very front-row houses touching the curbs: the Support Vectors! If you demolish every other house deeper in the neighborhoods, the street doesn't move a single millimeter.

The Kernel Trick: If the Wolves live in a circle inside the town center and the Bears form an outer ring, no flat street can divide them. Imagine a magical earthquake lifts the Wolves' center zone high into the air onto a mountain. Now, you can slide a flat cardboard sheet horizontally beneath the mountain to separate them completely!

$C > 0$ Hyperparameter Penalty trade-off between margin width and violations

1. Hard-Margin Primal Formulation

To maximize the margin width $\frac{2}{\|w\|}$, we minimize the squared norm $\frac{1}{2}\|w\|_2^2$ subject to all points lying on or beyond their respective margin boundaries:

$$\min_{w, b} \frac{1}{2} \|w\|_2^2 \quad \text{s.t. } y^{(i)}(w^T x^{(i)} + b) \ge 1 \quad \forall i=1, \dots, N$$

2. Soft-Margin SVM & Hinge Loss

Introducing slack variables $\xi_i \ge 0$ allows margin violations penalized by $C$:

$$\min_{w, b, \xi} \frac{1}{2} \|w\|_2^2 + C \sum_{i=1}^N \xi_i \quad \text{s.t. } y^{(i)}(w^T x^{(i)} + b) \ge 1 - \xi_i, \quad \xi_i \ge 0$$

This is equivalent to minimizing the unconstrained Hinge Loss with $L_2$ regularization:

$$\min_{w, b} \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \max\left(0, 1 - y^{(i)}(w^T x^{(i)} + b)\right) + \frac{\lambda}{2} \|w\|_2^2, \quad \text{where } \lambda = \frac{1}{NC}$$

3. The Dual Problem & Support Vectors

Applying the KKT conditions yields the Wolfe Dual problem:

$$\max_\alpha \sum_{i=1}^N \alpha_i - \frac{1}{2} \sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{j=1}^N \alpha_i \alpha_j y^{(i)} y^{(j)} (x^{(i)} \cdot x^{(j)})$$ $$\text{s.t. } 0 \le \alpha_i \le C, \quad \sum_{i=1}^N \alpha_i y^{(i)} = 0$$

Complementary slackness ensures that $\alpha_i = 0$ for all points strictly outside the margin. Only the Support Vectors ($\alpha_i > 0$) define the decision hyperplane!

4. The Kernel Trick

Because features appear exclusively as dot products $x^{(i)} \cdot x^{(j)}$, we can substitute an arbitrary Mercer kernel function $K(x, x') = \langle \phi(x), \phi(x') \rangle$:

  • Linear: $K(x, x') = x^T x'$
  • Polynomial: $K(x, x') = (x^T x' + c)^d$
  • Radial Basis Function (RBF): $K(x, x') = \exp(-\gamma \|x - x'\|^2)$ (Infinite-dimensional Hilbert space mapping!)
