Topic 8.5: Naive Bayes & K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN)

Generative conditional independence, Laplace smoothing, instance-based lazy learning, and the curse of dimensionality.

The 12-Year-Old Analogy: Spam Detective & The Lunch Table

Naive Bayes (The Mailroom Spam Detective): You count how often words like "FREE", "WINNER", and "PRIZE" appear in spam vs friendly emails. Naive Bayes pretends every word was rolled on independent dice. Even though that's "naive", it catches spam amazingly well! Laplace smoothing ensures you never multiply by zero when a brand-new word shows up.

KNN ("Tell Me Who Your Neighbors Are"): Imagine walking into a new school cafeteria. You look at the $k=5$ closest students sitting at a table. If 4 are wearing soccer cleats and 1 has a violin, you decide it's the Soccer table! There is no studying or training phase beforehand: you simply measure physical distance to neighbors on demand.

Mathematical Symbol Decoder

Symbol Mathematical Domain Description & Meaning
$P(y = c)$ Probability $[0, 1]$ Class prior probability based on training frequency
$P(x_j \mid y = c)$ Conditional Likelihood Probability of feature $x_j$ occurring given class $c$
$\alpha$ $\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ Laplace additive smoothing parameter (default $\alpha=1$)
$k \in \mathbb{N}$ Hyperparameter Number of closest training instances to poll in voting
$d_2(u, v)$ Distance Metric Euclidean $L_2$ distance: $\sqrt{\sum (u_j - v_j)^2}$
$\mathcal{N}_k(x)$ Set of indices The $k$ nearest neighbors of query vector $x$

1. Naive Bayes Formulation & Log-Space Inference

By Bayes' Theorem, with the conditional independence assumption:

$$P(y = c \mid x) \propto P(y = c) \prod_{j=1}^d P(x_j \mid y = c)$$

To avoid numerical underflow, we perform the computation in log-space:

Log-Space Decision Rule: $$\hat{y} = \arg\max_{c} \left[ \ln P(y = c) + \sum_{j=1}^d \ln P(x_j \mid y = c) \right]$$

For discrete text/word counts, Laplace (Add-1) Smoothing prevents zero probabilities:

$$P(w_j \mid y = c) = \frac{N_{jc} + 1}{N_c + |V|}$$

2. K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) & Distance Metrics

KNN is a non-parametric instance-based lazy learner with $\mathcal{O}(1)$ training and $\mathcal{O}(N d)$ inference time:

$$\hat{y}(x_q) = \arg\max_{c} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{N}_k(x_q)} \mathbb{I}(y_i = c)$$

Distance metrics:

  • Euclidean ($L_2$): $d(u, v) = \sqrt{\sum (u_j - v_j)^2}$
  • Manhattan ($L_1$): $d(u, v) = \sum |u_j - v_j|$
  • Minkowski ($L_p$): $d(u, v) = \left(\sum |u_j - v_j|^p\right)^{1/p}$

3. The Curse of Dimensionality

As feature dimension $d \to \infty$, the volume of space expands exponentially. The ratio between the distance to the closest neighbor and the farthest neighbor approaches 1:

$$\lim_{d \to \infty} \frac{\text{dist}_{\max} - \text{dist}_{\min}}{\text{dist}_{\min}} = 0$$

In high dimensions, all points become roughly equidistant from each other, rendering proximity-based methods ineffective without dimensionality reduction!
