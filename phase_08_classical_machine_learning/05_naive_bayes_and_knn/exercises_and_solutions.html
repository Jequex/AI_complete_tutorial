Exercise 1: Multinomial Naive Bayes Spam Classification

A vocabulary has $|V| = 4$ words. Training set has 3 Spam and 2 Ham emails. Using Laplace smoothing ($\alpha = 1$), calculate likelihoods and classify "deal free project".

View Complete Solution

Priors: $P(\text{Spam}) = 0.6, \quad P(\text{Ham}) = 0.4$.

Spam score: $0.6 \times (3/10) \times (5/10) \times (1/10) = 0.0090$.

Ham score: $0.4 \times (2/9) \times (1/9) \times (3/9) \approx 0.00329$.

Result: $0.0090 > 0.00329 \implies$ Classified as SPAM ($73.2\%$ posterior probability).

Exercise 2: K-Nearest Neighbors Majority Voting

For query point $Q = (4, 3)$ and points $P_1(1, 2, A)$, $P_2(2, 3, A)$, $P_3(3, 3, A)$, $P_4(5, 5, B)$, $P_5(6, 4, B)$, $P_6(7, 6, B)$, compute $k=1$ and $k=3$ nearest neighbors under Euclidean and Manhattan metrics.

View Complete Solution

Euclidean distances: $P_3(1.00), P_2(2.00), P_4(\sqrt{5} \approx 2.24), P_5(\sqrt{5} \approx 2.24)$.

  • $k = 1$: Closest is $P_3 \implies$ Class A.
  • $k = 3$: Neighbors are $P_3(A), P_2(A), P_4(B) \implies$ Class A wins 2-to-1.

Manhattan distance yields identical 2-to-1 victory for Class A.

Exercise 3: The Curse of Dimensionality & Neighborhood Volumes

In a unit hypercube $[0, 1]^d$, compute the required edge length $e_d = r^{1/d}$ to capture $5\%$ ($r = 0.05$) of data for $d=1, 10, 100$.

View Complete Solution
  • $d = 1$: $e_1 = 0.05$ ($5\%$ of dimension).
  • $d = 10$: $e_{10} = (0.05)^{1/10} \approx 0.7411$ ($74.1\%$ of every dimension!).
  • $d = 100$: $e_{100} = (0.05)^{1/100} \approx 0.9705$ ($97.1\%$ of every dimension!).

To capture even a tiny sliver of data in high dimensions, the neighborhood must span the entire space—proximity is lost.
