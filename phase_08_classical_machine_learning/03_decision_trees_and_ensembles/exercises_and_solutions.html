Exercise 1: Gini Impurity and Shannon Entropy Calculation

A node $T$ contains 10 samples (4 positive, 6 negative). Calculate its Gini Impurity and Shannon Entropy.

View Complete Solution

Probabilities: $p_1 = 0.4, p_0 = 0.6$.

Gini Impurity: $G(T) = 1 - (0.4^2 + 0.6^2) = 1 - 0.52 = 0.48$.

Shannon Entropy: $H(T) = -[0.4 \log_2(0.4) + 0.6 \log_2(0.6)] \approx 0.9710\text{ bits}$.

Exercise 2: Information Gain of a Candidate Split

Parent node $T$ ($H(T) = 0.9710$ bits) is split into $T_L$ (3 pos, 1 neg) and $T_R$ (1 pos, 5 neg). Compute the Information Gain.

View Complete Solution

$H(T_L) \approx 0.8113\text{ bits}, \quad H(T_R) \approx 0.6500\text{ bits}$.

Weighted entropy: $H(T_L, T_R) = \frac{4}{10}(0.8113) + \frac{6}{10}(0.6500) = 0.7145\text{ bits}$.

Information Gain: $IG = 0.9710 - 0.7145 = 0.2565\text{ bits}$.

Exercise 3: Variance Reduction in Random Forests

An ensemble of $M = 100$ trees has individual variance $\sigma^2 = 1.0$. Compare ensemble variance when $\rho = 0.40$ (Bagging) vs $\rho = 0.05$ (Random Forest).

View Complete Solution

$\text{Var} = \rho \sigma^2 + \frac{1 - \rho}{M} \sigma^2$.

  • Bagging ($\rho = 0.40$): $0.40(1) + \frac{0.60}{100}(1) = 0.406$.
  • Random Forest ($\rho = 0.05$): $0.05(1) + \frac{0.95}{100}(1) = 0.0595$.

Random feature subsampling slashes variance by 85.3%!

Exercise 4: Gradient Boosting Pseudo-Residuals

Given targets $y = [10, 20, 30]^T$, baseline mean $F_0 = 20$. Compute the pseudo-residuals $r_i$ and the updated predictions $F_1(x)$ when $h_1(x) = [-8, 0, 8]$ with learning rate $\nu = 0.1$.

View Complete Solution

Pseudo-residuals: $r_1 = 10 - 20 = -10, r_2 = 20 - 20 = 0, r_3 = 30 - 20 = +10$.

Updated predictions: $F_1(x) = 20 + 0.1 \times h_1(x) = [19.2, 20.0, 20.8]^T$.
