Topic 28.4: Mixed Precision & FP8 Training

A rigorous numerical analysis of floating-point representations: IEEE FP32/FP16, Google BF16, Hopper FP8 (E4M3 vs E5M2), and Automatic Mixed Precision with Dynamic Loss Scaling.

12-Year-Old Intuition: The Rulers of the Universe

  • FP32 (The 32-Foot Engineering Tape): Reaches from millimeters to outer space. Impeccable accuracy, but heavy, slow, and expensive to haul around.
  • FP16 (The 8-Inch Pocket Ruler): Small, light, and lets you work $4\times$ faster. But anything longer than $65{,}504$ breaks the ruler (Overflow), and anything smaller than a speck of dust rounds to zero (Underflow).
  • BF16 (The Telescope Ruler): Keeps the full outer-space length of the 32-foot tape (8 exponent bits), but with slightly coarser markings. It never underflows or overflows on neural network gradients!

The Gradient Underflow Catastrophe in FP16

In deep Transformers (80+ layers), repeated chain-rule multiplications produce small gradients in the range $10^{-8}$ to $10^{-6}$.

Because FP16 normalized minimum is $6.10 \times 10^{-5}$ (and hardware Flush-to-Zero triggers below $5.96 \times 10^{-8}$), over $70\%$ of raw gradients flush to exact $0.0$. Weights stop updating, causing training loss to diverge.

Automatic Mixed Precision (AMP) & Dynamic Loss Scaling

AMP solves underflow by multiplying the loss by scale factor $S$ (e.g. $S = 65{,}536$):

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{scaled}} = S \cdot \mathcal{L} \implies \mathbf{g}_{\text{scaled}} = S \cdot \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}$$

This shifts tiny gradients $16\text{ bits}$ to the right into the safe normal range of FP16 ($10^{-8} \times 65{,}536 \approx 6.55 \times 10^{-4} \gg 6.1 \times 10^{-5}$).

Dynamic Scaling Controller:

  1. Compute backward pass with $\mathcal{L}_{\text{scaled}}$.
  2. Inspect gradients for inf or nan:
    • If Overflow: Skip optimizer step, shrink scale $S \leftarrow S \times 0.5$, reset stable counter.
    • If Clean: Unscale $\mathbf{g}_{\text{FP32}} = \frac{\mathbf{g}_{\text{scaled}}}{S}$, update master weights with FP32 Adam.
  3. If clean for $K = 2{,}000$ consecutive steps, grow scale $S \leftarrow S \times 2$.

BF16: Why It Conquered LLM Pretraining

BF16 preserves the full 8 exponent bits of FP32. Its dynamic range extends to $10^{38}$ and down to $10^{-38}$, making it completely immune to gradient underflow. It completely eliminates loss scaling, unscaling logic, and skip checks from the training pipeline.

The FP8 Frontier: E4M3 vs. E5M2

Hopper & Blackwell FP8 Recipe

  • E4M3 (1/4/3): Max 448. Packed with 3 mantissa bits for numerical precision in forward GEMMs ($Q, K, V$ projections and MLP activations).
  • E5M2 (1/5/2): Max 57,344. Packed with 5 exponent bits matching FP16 range, preventing catastrophic gradient overflow during the backward pass.
  • Combined with Delayed Scaling, FP8 cuts HBM bandwidth by half and doubles Tensor Core throughput to nearly 2,000 TFLOP/s on H100!
