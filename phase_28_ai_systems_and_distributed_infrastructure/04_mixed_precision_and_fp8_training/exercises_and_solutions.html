Topic 28.4: Exercises & Solutions

Quantitative problems on bit-level IEEE floating point decoding, mathematical bounds on loss scaling factor $S$, and FP16 vs FP8 throughput analysis.

Exercise 1: Bit-Level Floating-Point Decoding

Decode the bit pattern $\mathbf{b} = \texttt{0\_10001\_0100000000}_2$ under IEEE FP16 (bias 15). Decode $\texttt{0\_10001010\_0000000}_2$ under BF16 (bias 127). Decode $\texttt{0\_1110\_101}_2$ under FP8 E4M3 (bias 7).

Click to view Solution

Solution:

1. FP16 ($\texttt{0\_10001\_0100000000}_2$):

Sign $= 0 (+1)$. Exp $= 17_{10}$. Unbiased exp $= 17 - 15 = 2$. Mantissa $= \frac{1}{4} = 0.25$.

$$x = 2^2 \times (1 + 0.25) = 4 \times 1.25 = \mathbf{5.0}$$

2. BF16 ($\texttt{0\_10001010\_0000000}_2$):

Sign $= 0 (+1)$. Exp $= 138_{10}$. Unbiased exp $= 138 - 127 = 11$. Mantissa $= 0.0$.

$$x = 2^{11} \times 1.0 = \mathbf{2{,}048.0}$$

3. FP8 E4M3 ($\texttt{0\_1110\_101}_2$):

Sign $= 0 (+1)$. Exp $= 14_{10}$. Unbiased exp $= 14 - 7 = 7$. Mantissa $= 0.5 + 0.125 = 0.625$.

$$x = 2^7 \times (1 + 0.625) = 128 \times 1.625 = \mathbf{208.0}$$

Exercise 2: Deriving Safe Bounds on Dynamic Loss Scaling

In FP16 training, gradients span from $g_{\min} = 1.5 \times 10^{-7}$ to $g_{\max} = 1.8$. Smallest normal value is $N_{\min} = 6.10 \times 10^{-5}$; maximum finite value is $M_{\max} = 65{,}504.0$.

  1. Find minimum scale $S_{\min}$ to prevent underflow.
  2. Find maximum scale $S_{\max}$ to prevent overflow.
  3. Select an optimal power-of-2 scaling factor.
Click to view Solution

Solution:

1. Minimum Scale:

$$S_{\min} = \frac{N_{\min}}{g_{\min}} = \frac{6.10 \times 10^{-5}}{1.5 \times 10^{-7}} \approx \mathbf{406.67}$$

2. Maximum Scale:

$$S_{\max} = \frac{M_{\max}}{g_{\max}} = \frac{65{,}504.0}{1.8} \approx \mathbf{36{,}391.11}$$

3. Selected Scale Factor:

$$406.67 \le S \le 36{,}391.11 \implies \text{Choose } S = 2^{14} = \mathbf{16{,}384}$$

Exercise 3: FP16 vs. FP8 Throughput on NVIDIA H100

A 20B parameter model training step requires $1.2 \times 10^{14}\text{ FLOPs}$ per GPU on H100 ($P_{\text{FP16}} = 989\text{ TFLOP/s}$, $P_{\text{FP8}} = 1{,}978\text{ TFLOP/s}$). MFU is $60\%$.

  1. Compute weight memory in FP32, FP16, and FP8.
  2. Compute training step time in FP16 vs FP8.
  3. Calculate the speedup of FP8.
Click to view Solution

Solution:

1. Weight Storage:

  • FP32: $20\text{B} \times 4\text{ B} = \mathbf{80.0\text{ GB}}$
  • FP16: $20\text{B} \times 2\text{ B} = \mathbf{40.0\text{ GB}}$ ($50\%$ savings)
  • FP8: $20\text{B} \times 1\text{ B} = \mathbf{20.0\text{ GB}}$ ($75\%$ savings)

2. Step Times (60% MFU):

$$T_{\text{FP16}} = \frac{1.2 \times 10^{14}}{0.60 \times 989 \times 10^{12}} \approx \mathbf{202.2\text{ ms}}$$ $$T_{\text{FP8}} = \frac{1.2 \times 10^{14}}{0.60 \times 1{,}978 \times 10^{12}} \approx \mathbf{101.1\text{ ms}}$$

3. Speedup: $\frac{202.2\text{ ms}}{101.1\text{ ms}} = \mathbf{2.0\times}$ direct speedup.
