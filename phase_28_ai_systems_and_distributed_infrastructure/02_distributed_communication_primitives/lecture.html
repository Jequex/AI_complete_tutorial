Topic 28.2: Distributed Communication Primitives & Ring All-Reduce

A rigorous mathematical treatment of collective communication operations, hardware network topologies (NVLink vs InfiniBand RDMA), and the proof of Ring All-Reduce bandwidth independence.

12-Year-Old Intuition: The Overwhelmed Teacher vs. The Circle Whisper

Imagine 8 students who each have 10,000 numbers on their paper and need to find the total sum across all papers:

  • Parameter Server (The Teacher): All 8 students line up at the teacher's desk. The teacher receives all 8 papers, adds them up, copies the answers, and hands them back. If there are 1,024 students in an auditorium, the teacher collapses under 2,048 deliveries. The central hub is an unbearable bottleneck!
  • Ring All-Reduce (The Circle): The students sit in a circle. Each student cuts their paper into 8 strips and passes strip #1 to the right while receiving a strip from the left, adding their own numbers as it passes. After 7 quick passes, each student holds one finished strip. After 7 more passes sharing the answers, everyone has the complete total. No student ever handles more than two strips per turn, even with 10,000 students!

Mathematical & Algorithmic Symbol Decoder

Symbol Concept Domain Physical / Mathematical Meaning
$N$ World Size $\mathbb{Z}^+$ Total number of participating GPU workers / ranks.
$M$ Tensor Size Bytes / Elements Size of the parameter, gradient, or activation buffer being synchronized.
$B$ Link Bandwidth $\text{GB/s}$ Sustained bidirectional network transmission rate between endpoints.
$\alpha$ Network Latency $\mu\text{s}$ Fixed startup delay required to initialize and transmit a network packet (Hockney model).
$\beta$ Transfer Rate $\text{Bytes/s}$ Inverse time per byte transmitted across network wires ($= B$).
$V_{\text{total}}$ Per-GPU Volume Bytes Total volume of data transmitted by an individual GPU over an entire collective operation.

Physical Interconnects: Intra-Node vs. Inter-Node

Hierarchy of Communication Speeds

  • Intra-Node NVLink 4 (NVIDIA H100): $900\text{ GB/s}$ bidirectional per GPU across NVSwitch fabric ($3.2\text{ TB/s}$ bisection). Nearly on-chip speeds.
  • Inter-Node InfiniBand NDR RDMA: $50\text{ GB/s}$ ($400\text{ Gbps}$) per NIC. Bypasses host CPU and OS kernel via GPUDirect RDMA.
  • The Ratio: Intra-node NVLink is $18\times$ faster than inter-node network cables! Algorithms must minimize inter-node data exchange.

Collective Communication Primitives

Collective operations coordinate a group of $N$ processes:

  • Broadcast: Copies tensor of size $M$ from one root to all $N-1$ ranks.
  • Scatter: Splits root tensor into $N$ equal chunks of size $\frac{M}{N}$, sending chunk $i$ to rank $i$.
  • Gather: Collects $\frac{M}{N}$ chunks from all ranks and concatenates them onto the root.
  • All-Gather: Every rank starts with a chunk $\frac{M}{N}$ and ends with the full tensor $M$.
  • Reduce: Combines tensors from all ranks using operator $\oplus \in \{+, \times, \max\}$ onto a single root rank.
  • All-Reduce: Combines tensors from all ranks and distributes the final reduced tensor to every rank: $$\mathbf{All\text{-}Reduce} \equiv \mathbf{Reduce\text{-}Scatter} \circ \mathbf{All\text{-}Gather}$$
  • Reduce-Scatter: Performs reduction across ranks and scatters the result, so rank $i$ receives only the $i$-th reduced chunk of size $\frac{M}{N}$.

The Parameter Server Bottleneck: $\mathcal{O}(N \cdot M)$

In a parameter server architecture, the central node must receive $(N - 1)M$ bytes and broadcast $(N - 1)M$ bytes:

$$V_{\text{PS\_server}} = 2(N - 1)M \in \mathcal{O}(N \cdot M)$$

As cluster size $N$ grows, the server bandwidth saturates, throttling all worker GPUs.

The Ring All-Reduce Algorithm & Proof of Bandwidth Independence

In Ring All-Reduce, $N$ GPUs form a logical ring. The tensor of size $M$ is divided into $N$ equal chunks of size $\frac{M}{N}$.

Phase 1: Scatter-Reduce ($N - 1$ steps)

At each step $k$, rank $i$ sends chunk $(i - k) \pmod N$ to rank $(i + 1) \pmod N$ and accumulates incoming data into local memory.

$$V_{\text{scatter-reduce}} = (N - 1) \times \frac{M}{N} = \frac{N - 1}{N} M\text{ bytes}$$

Phase 2: All-Gather ($N - 1$ steps)

Each rank now holds one fully reduced chunk and broadcasts it around the ring, overwriting local copies.

$$V_{\text{all-gather}} = (N - 1) \times \frac{M}{N} = \frac{N - 1}{N} M\text{ bytes}$$

Fundamental Theorem: Total Communication Volume

$$V_{\text{total}} = 2 \cdot \frac{N - 1}{N} M\text{ bytes}$$

Asymptotic Limit ($N \to \infty$):

$$\lim_{N \to \infty} V_{\text{total}} = \lim_{N \to \infty} 2 \cdot \left(\frac{N - 1}{N}\right) M = 2 M$$

Bandwidth Independence: The network transmission time is bounded by $\frac{2M}{B}$, completely independent of whether the cluster contains 8 GPUs or 16,384 GPUs!

Latency Modeling: Ring vs. Tree All-Reduce

Under the Hockney model ($T = \alpha + \frac{S}{\beta}$):

$$T_{\text{ring}} = 2(N - 1)\alpha + 2\frac{N - 1}{N}\frac{M}{\beta}$$ $$T_{\text{tree}} = 2\log_2(N)\alpha + 2\frac{N - 1}{N}\frac{M}{\beta}$$

NVIDIA NCCL intelligently selects Tree All-Reduce for small tensors to minimize latency hops $2\log_2(N)\alpha$, and switches to Ring All-Reduce for large tensors to achieve peak bandwidth saturation $\frac{2M}{\beta}$.
