Topic 9.3: Manifold Learning — t-SNE, UMAP, & Non-Linear Embeddings

Overcoming the Curse of Dimensionality, escaping the Crowding Problem, and preserving non-linear topological manifolds.

1. Executive Summary & The 12-Year-Old Analogy

When data curves through space—like a Swiss Roll or handwritten digits—linear methods like PCA smash distant folds directly together. Manifold Learning unfolds non-linear structures into low-dimensional visualization spaces while preserving true neighborhood relationships.

The 12-Year-Old Analogy: Unfolding a Crumpled Paper Map

Imagine tightly crumpling a detailed 2D paper map of an amusement park into a dense 3D paper ball:

  • PCA with a Hammer: Smashing the paper ball flat with a hammer squashes the Roller Coaster on page 1 directly onto the Haunted House on page 10.
  • Manifold Learning (t-SNE & UMAP): Carefully smooths out the creases so nearby park paths stay together.
  • The Crowding Problem & Student-t: In 3D space, you have room for neighbors in every direction (above, below, left, right). On a flat 2D page, points crowd together. To fix this, t-SNE uses a "stretchy rubber band" (the heavy-tailed Student-t distribution) that pushes distant clusters far apart so they have room to breathe!

3. The Crowding Problem & The Student-t Heavy Tail

In high dimensions, volume grows exponentially with dimension ($V \propto R^D$), leaving ample surface area for points to be mutually distant. In 2D, surface area grows only as $2\pi R$. Original SNE used Gaussians in both spaces, causing low-dimensional points to crowd into a dense center.

t-SNE's Solution: Define low-dimensional affinities with a heavy-tailed Student-t kernel (1 degree of freedom):

$$q_{ij} = \frac{(1 + \|y_i - y_j\|_2^2)^{-1}}{\sum_{k \ne l} (1 + \|y_k - y_l\|_2^2)^{-1}}, \quad q_{ii} = 0$$

Because the Student-t density drops off as $\frac{1}{d^2}$ rather than $e^{-d^2}$, points that are moderately separated in high dimensions can be placed much further apart in 2D without incurring severe probability penalties.

4. Analytical Gradient of t-SNE

The Gradient Decomposition:

The derivative of the KL divergence with respect to coordinate vector $y_i$ is:

$$\frac{\partial D_{\text{KL}}(P \parallel Q)}{\partial y_i} = 4 \sum_{j \ne i} (p_{ij} - q_{ij}) (1 + \|y_i - y_j\|_2^2)^{-1} (y_i - y_j)$$

This acts like a system of physical springs:

  • Attractive Force ($p_{ij} > q_{ij}$): Pulls high-dimensional neighbors together if they drift apart in 2D.
  • Repulsive Force ($q_{ij} > p_{ij}$): Pushes unrelated clusters apart if they get placed too close in 2D.

5. UMAP & Fuzzy Simplicial Sets

UMAP optimizes fuzzy set cross-entropy:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{UMAP}} = \sum_{i \ne j} \left[ p_{ij} \ln \frac{p_{ij}}{q_{ij}} + (1 - p_{ij}) \ln \frac{1 - p_{ij}}{1 - q_{ij}} \right]$$

The second term $(1 - p_{ij}) \ln \frac{1 - p_{ij}}{1 - q_{ij}}$ provides a global repulsive force between non-neighbors, preserving the macro-scale arrangement of clusters much better than t-SNE.
