Topic 2.1: Practice Exercises & Solutions

Step-by-step arithmetic drills on vector norms, unit normalizations, dot products, and matrix products.

Problem 1: Norms and Unit Vector Normalization

Given the 3D vector $\mathbf{v} = [3, -4, 12]^T$:

  • Part A: Compute the $L_1$ Manhattan norm $\|\mathbf{v}\|_1$.
  • Part B: Compute the $L_2$ Euclidean norm $\|\mathbf{v}\|_2$.
  • Part C: Compute the $L_\infty$ Max norm $\|\mathbf{v}\|_\infty$.
  • Part D: Normalize $\mathbf{v}$ into a unit vector $\hat{\mathbf{v}} = \frac{\mathbf{v}}{\|\mathbf{v}\|_2}$ and verify that $\|\hat{\mathbf{v}}\|_2 = 1.0$.
💡 Click to Reveal Step-by-Step Solution

Solution:

Part A: $L_1$ Manhattan Norm

$$\|\mathbf{v}\|_1 = |3| + |-4| + |12| = 3 + 4 + 12 = \mathbf{19}$$

Part B: $L_2$ Euclidean Norm

$$\|\mathbf{v}\|_2 = \sqrt{3^2 + (-4)^2 + 12^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16 + 144} = \sqrt{169} = \mathbf{13}$$

Part C: $L_\infty$ Max Norm

$$\|\mathbf{v}\|_\infty = \max(|3|, |-4|, |12|) = \mathbf{12}$$

Part D: Unit Vector Normalization

$$\hat{\mathbf{v}} = \frac{1}{13} \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ -4 \\ 12 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 3/13 \\ -4/13 \\ 12/13 \end{bmatrix}$$

Verification:

$$\|\hat{\mathbf{v}}\|_2 = \sqrt{\left(\frac{3}{13}\right)^2 + \left(-\frac{4}{13}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{12}{13}\right)^2} = \sqrt{\frac{9+16+144}{169}} = \sqrt{\frac{169}{169}} = \mathbf{1.0} \quad \blacksquare$$

Problem 2: Dot Products, Angles, and Orthogonality

Let $\mathbf{a} = [1, 2, 3]^T$ and $\mathbf{b} = [4, -2, 0]^T$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$.

  • Part A: Calculate algebraic dot product $\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}$.
  • Part B: Determine the angle $\theta$ between $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$. Are they orthogonal?
  • Part C: Compute the cosine similarity between $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{c} = [2, 4, 6]^T$. What does this illustrate?
💡 Click to Reveal Step-by-Step Solution

Solution:

Part A: $\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = (1)(4) + (2)(-2) + (3)(0) = 4 - 4 + 0 = \mathbf{0}$.

Part B: Since $\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = \|\mathbf{a}\| \|\mathbf{b}\| \cos\theta = 0$, we have $\cos\theta = 0 \implies \mathbf{\theta = 90^\circ}$. The vectors are perpendicular (orthogonal)!

Part C: Notice $\mathbf{c} = 2\mathbf{a}$.

$$\cos\theta = \frac{\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{c}}{\|\mathbf{a}\| \|\mathbf{c}\|} = \frac{28}{\sqrt{14} \times 2\sqrt{14}} = \frac{28}{28} = \mathbf{1.0}$$

Since $\cos\theta = 1.0$, the vectors point in the identical direction. Cosine similarity isolates direction while ignoring magnitude.

Problem 3: Dimension Compatibility & Matrix Multiplication

Let $A \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times 3}$ and $B \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 2}$:

$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 2 \\ -1 & 3 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad B = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 2 & -1 \\ 0 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$$

  • Part A: Find the dimensions of $AB$ and $BA$.
  • Part B: Compute product $C = AB$ step-by-step.
💡 Click to Reveal Step-by-Step Solution

Solution:

Part A: Dimensions

  • $AB$: $(2 \times 3) \times (3 \times 2) \implies \mathbf{2 \times 2}$.
  • $BA$: $(3 \times 2) \times (2 \times 3) \implies \mathbf{3 \times 3}$.

Part B: Step-by-Step Matrix Product $C = AB$

  • $c_{11} = (1 \times 3) + (0 \times 2) + (2 \times 0) = 3 + 0 + 0 = \mathbf{3}$
  • $c_{12} = (1 \times 1) + (0 \times -1) + (2 \times 4) = 1 + 0 + 8 = \mathbf{9}$
  • $c_{21} = (-1 \times 3) + (3 \times 2) + (1 \times 0) = -3 + 6 + 0 = \mathbf{3}$
  • $c_{22} = (-1 \times 1) + (3 \times -1) + (1 \times 4) = -1 - 3 + 4 = \mathbf{0}$

$$C = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 9 \\ 3 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$
