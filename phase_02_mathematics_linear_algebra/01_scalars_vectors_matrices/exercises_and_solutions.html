Problem 1: Norms and Unit Vector Normalization

Given the 3-dimensional vector:

$$\mathbf{v} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ -4 \\ 12 \end{bmatrix}$$

Compute the $L_1$ norm, $L_2$ norm, and $L_\infty$ norm, and normalize $\mathbf{v}$ into a unit vector.

Click to view solution

Part A: $L_1$ Manhattan Norm

$$\|\mathbf{v}\|_1 = |3| + |-4| + |12| = 3 + 4 + 12 = \mathbf{19}$$

Part B: $L_2$ Euclidean Norm

$$\|\mathbf{v}\|_2 = \sqrt{3^2 + (-4)^2 + 12^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16 + 144} = \sqrt{169} = \mathbf{13}$$

Part C: $L_\infty$ Max Norm

$$\|\mathbf{v}\|_\infty = \max(|3|, |-4|, |12|) = \mathbf{12}$$

Part D: Unit Vector Normalization

$$\hat{\mathbf{v}} = \frac{\mathbf{v}}{13} = \begin{bmatrix} 3/13 \\ -4/13 \\ 12/13 \end{bmatrix}$$ $$\|\hat{\mathbf{v}}\|_2 = \sqrt{\frac{9 + 16 + 144}{169}} = \sqrt{\frac{169}{169}} = \mathbf{1.0}$$

Problem 2: Dot Products, Angles, and Orthogonality

Given $\mathbf{a} = [1, 2, 3]^T$ and $\mathbf{b} = [4, -2, 0]^T$:

  1. Compute $\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}$.
  2. Determine the angle $\theta$ between $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$.
  3. Compute cosine similarity between $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{c} = [2, 4, 6]^T$.
Click to view solution

Part A: Dot Product

$$\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = (1)(4) + (2)(-2) + (3)(0) = 4 - 4 + 0 = \mathbf{0}$$

Part B: Angle & Orthogonality

Because $\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = 0$, $\cos\theta = 0 \implies \theta = \mathbf{90^\circ}$. The vectors are orthogonal!

Part C: Cosine Similarity with $\mathbf{c} = 2\mathbf{a}$

$$\cos\theta = \frac{\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{c}}{\|\mathbf{a}\| \|\mathbf{c}\|} = \frac{28}{\sqrt{14} \times 2\sqrt{14}} = \frac{28}{28} = \mathbf{1.0}$$

Scaling a vector leaves its semantic direction completely unchanged ($\theta = 0^\circ$).

Problem 3: Dimension Compatibility & Matrix Multiplication

Let $A \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times 3}$ and $B \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 2}$:

$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 2 \\ -1 & 3 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad B = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 2 & -1 \\ 0 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$$

Calculate $C = AB$.

Click to view solution

Inner dimensions match ($3=3$), yielding a $2 \times 2$ matrix:

  • $c_{11} = (1)(3) + (0)(2) + (2)(0) = \mathbf{3}$
  • $c_{12} = (1)(1) + (0)(-1) + (2)(4) = \mathbf{9}$
  • $c_{21} = (-1)(3) + (3)(2) + (1)(0) = \mathbf{3}$
  • $c_{22} = (-1)(1) + (3)(-1) + (1)(4) = \mathbf{0}$
$$C = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 9 \\ 3 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$
