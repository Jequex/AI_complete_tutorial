Topic 2.1: Scalars, Vectors, and Matrices

The elemental building blocks of all AI mathematics: operations, geometric norms, dot products, and cosine similarity.

1. 🧒 The 12-Year-Old Analogy: The Pirate Treasure Map

🧒 The Pirate Adventure

Imagine you are hunting for buried pirate treasure with Timmy and Maya:

  1. Scalar ($x \in \mathbb{R}$): Just a single number without any direction: "Drink 3 sips of water" or "Air temperature is 78°F."
  2. Vector ($\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^n$): An arrow on your treasure map: "Walk 4 paces East and 3 paces North." It takes two numbers together $[4, 3]$ to describe this arrow. The arrow has a length ($\sqrt{4^2+3^2} = 5$ paces) and points in a direction (Northeast).
  3. Matrix ($A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$): A logbook stacking paths of multiple pirates: $$A = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 3 \\ -1 & 5 \\ 0 & -2 \end{bmatrix}$$ Pirate 1 goes $[4, 3]$, Pirate 2 goes $[-1, 5]$, Pirate 3 goes $[0, -2]$. A matrix is a table of arrows!

By universal convention in machine learning, vectors are represented as column vectors:

$$\mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ \vdots \\ x_n \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^n$$

4. Vector Norms: How Long is an Arrow?

🔬 Euclidean vs. Manhattan Distance

$$\|\mathbf{x}\|_2 = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2} = \sqrt{x_1^2 + x_2^2 + \dots + x_n^2}$$

$$\|\mathbf{x}\|_1 = \sum_{i=1}^n |x_i| = |x_1| + |x_2| + \dots + |x_n|$$

🧒 What the math is actually doing: $L_2$ calculates straight-line flight distance (crow's flight). $L_1$ calculates grid walking distance (taxi blocks). In AI, $L_1$ drives weights to 0 for sparse models.

5. The Dot Product & Cosine Similarity

$$\mathbf{x} \cdot \mathbf{y} = \|\mathbf{x}\|_2 \|\mathbf{y}\|_2 \cos\theta$$

$$\underbrace{\cos\theta}_{\substack{\text{Semantic similarity} \\ \text{score between } -1 \text{ and } +1}} = \frac{ \overbrace{\mathbf{x} \cdot \mathbf{y}}^{\text{Raw directional alignment}} }{ \underbrace{\|\mathbf{x}\|_2 \|\mathbf{y}\|_2}_{\text{Lengths of the two vectors}} }$$
🧒 Concrete Numerical Example

Let $\mathbf{x} = [4, 3]^T$ ("king") and $\mathbf{y} = [1, 2]^T$ ("queen"):

  • Dot Product: $(4 \times 1) + (3 \times 2) = 4 + 6 = \mathbf{10}$
  • Lengths: $\|\mathbf{x}\|_2 = \sqrt{16+9} = \mathbf{5.0}, \quad \|\mathbf{y}\|_2 = \sqrt{1+4} \approx \mathbf{2.236}$
  • Cosine Similarity: $\frac{10}{5.0 \times 2.236} \approx \mathbf{0.8944}$ (Very high semantic match!)
