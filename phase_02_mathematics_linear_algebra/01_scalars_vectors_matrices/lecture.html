Topic 2.1: Scalars, Vectors, and Matrices

The elemental building blocks of all AI mathematics: operations, geometric norms, dot products, and cosine similarity.

1. The 12-Year-Old Analogy: The Treasure Map

🧒 The Pirate Adventure

Imagine you are hunting for buried pirate treasure:

  1. Scalar ($x \in \mathbb{R}$): Just a single number without any direction: "Drink 3 sips of water" or "Air temperature is 78°F."
  2. Vector ($\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^n$): An arrow on your treasure map: "Walk 4 paces East and 3 paces North." It takes two numbers together $[4, 3]$ to describe this arrow. The arrow has a length ($\sqrt{4^2+3^2} = 5$ paces) and points in a direction (Northeast).
  3. Matrix ($A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$): A logbook stacking paths of multiple pirates: $$A = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 3 \\ -1 & 5 \\ 0 & -2 \end{bmatrix}$$ Pirate 1 goes $[4, 3]$, Pirate 2 goes $[-1, 5]$, Pirate 3 goes $[0, -2]$. A matrix is a table of arrows!

By universal convention in machine learning and deep learning, vectors are represented as column vectors:

$$\mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ \vdots \\ x_n \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^n$$

Symbol Pronunciation Plain English Meaning Example
$\mathbb{R}$ "Real numbers" Continuous number line ($-\infty$ to $+\infty$) $3.1415, -7, 0$
$x \in \mathbb{R}$ "$x$ in R" Single real scalar number $x = 5.2$
$\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^n$ "vector x in R-n" Column vector of $n$ numbers $[3, -1, 4]^T \in \mathbb{R}^3$
$A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ "Matrix A in R m-by-n" Grid with $m$ rows and $n$ columns $3 \times 2$ table of weights
$\mathbf{x}^T$ "x transpose" Flips vertical column into horizontal row $[1, 2]^T = [1 \quad 2]$
$\|\mathbf{x}\|$ "norm of x" The geometric length/magnitude of the arrow $\sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = 5$
$\mathbf{x} \cdot \mathbf{y}$ "x dot y" Dot product (alignment score between two arrows) $(1)(3) + (2)(4) = 11$

3. Vector Norms: How Long is an Arrow?

🧒 Manhattan Taxi ($L_1$) vs. The Crow's Flight ($L_2$)

If you want to travel from point A to point B in Manhattan, New York, you cannot fly through buildings; you must take a taxi along street blocks: $|4| + |3| = 7$ blocks. That is the $L_1$ Norm!

If you are a bird flying straight through the air, you fly the hypotenuse: $\sqrt{4^2 + 3^2} = 5$ miles. That is the $L_2$ Norm!

  • $L_2$ Euclidean Norm: $$\|\mathbf{x}\|_2 = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2} = \sqrt{x_1^2 + x_2^2 + \dots + x_n^2}$$
  • $L_1$ Manhattan Norm: $$\|\mathbf{x}\|_1 = \sum_{i=1}^n |x_i| = |x_1| + |x_2| + \dots + |x_n|$$ Used in Lasso Regularization to drive unimportant weights to exactly 0 (creating sparse networks).
  • $L_\infty$ Max Norm: $$\|\mathbf{x}\|_\infty = \max_i |x_i|$$

4. The Dot Product & Law of Cosines Derivation

The dot product multiplies corresponding components and adds them up:

$$\mathbf{x} \cdot \mathbf{y} = \mathbf{x}^T \mathbf{y} = \sum_{i=1}^n x_i y_i = x_1 y_1 + x_2 y_2 + \dots + x_n y_n$$

🔬 Complete Mathematical Derivation of $\mathbf{x} \cdot \mathbf{y} = \|\mathbf{x}\| \|\mathbf{y}\| \cos\theta$

Consider vectors $\mathbf{x}$ and $\mathbf{y}$ forming two sides of a triangle with angle $\theta$. The side connecting their tips is the difference vector $(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y})$.

  1. By the Law of Cosines: $$\|\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y}\|^2 = \|\mathbf{x}\|^2 + \|\mathbf{y}\|^2 - 2 \|\mathbf{x}\| \|\mathbf{y}\| \cos\theta$$
  2. Expanding algebraically via dot products: $$\|\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y}\|^2 = (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y}) \cdot (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y}) = \|\mathbf{x}\|^2 - 2(\mathbf{x} \cdot \mathbf{y}) + \|\mathbf{y}\|^2$$
  3. Equating both expressions: $$\|\mathbf{x}\|^2 + \|\mathbf{y}\|^2 - 2(\mathbf{x} \cdot \mathbf{y}) = \|\mathbf{x}\|^2 + \|\mathbf{y}\|^2 - 2 \|\mathbf{x}\| \|\mathbf{y}\| \cos\theta$$
  4. Subtracting $\|\mathbf{x}\|^2 + \|\mathbf{y}\|^2$ and dividing by $-2$: $$\boxed{\mathbf{x} \cdot \mathbf{y} = \|\mathbf{x}\| \|\mathbf{y}\| \cos\theta} \quad \blacksquare$$

Cosine Similarity in AI

By dividing the dot product by the vector lengths, we isolate the angle $\cos\theta$:

$$\cos\theta = \frac{\mathbf{x} \cdot \mathbf{y}}{\|\mathbf{x}\| \|\mathbf{y}\|}$$

This is how LLMs and search engines measure conceptual similarity between words and sentences:

  • $\cos\theta \approx 1.0$: Identical direction (e.g. "puppy" and "dog").
  • $\cos\theta = 0.0$: Orthogonal / completely unrelated (e.g. "banana" and "quantum physics").
  • $\cos\theta \approx -1.0$: Opposite direction.

5. Matrix Multiplication: $C = AB$

Multiplying matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times k}$ by $B \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times n}$ produces $C \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$:

⚠️ The Golden Dimension Rule

$$\mathbf{(m \times \mathbf{k}) \times (\mathbf{k} \times n) \longrightarrow (m \times n)}$$

The inner dimensions must match ($k = k$)! Each entry $c_{ij}$ is the dot product of Row $i$ of $A$ and Column $j$ of $B$.

Let A = [ [1, 2],        B = [ [5, 6],
          [3, 4] ]             [7, 8] ]

c₁₁ = (1×5) + (2×7) = 5 + 14 = 19
c₁₂ = (1×6) + (2×8) = 6 + 16 = 22
c₂₁ = (3×5) + (4×7) = 15 + 28 = 43
c₂₂ = (3×6) + (4×8) = 18 + 32 = 50

C = [ [19, 22],
      [43, 50] ]
