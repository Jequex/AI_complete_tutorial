Topic 2.4: Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors

Master the axes of stability: characteristic polynomials, diagonalization, and vanishing/exploding gradients in AI.

1. 🧒 The 12-Year-Old Analogy: The Spinning Globe

🧒 The Axis of Stability

When you spin a globe, London and Tokyo whip around in circles, constantly changing direction. But the North and South Poles never leave their line—they sit right on the axis of rotation! In linear algebra, that special arrow that refuses to rotate is an Eigenvector, and how much it stretches is its Eigenvalue ($\lambda$):

$$A\mathbf{v} = \lambda\mathbf{v}$$

2. The Characteristic Equation: $\det(A - \lambda I) = 0$

🔬 Hand Calculation Walkthrough

For $A = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 1 \\ 2 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$:

$$\det(A - \lambda I) = (4 - \lambda)(3 - \lambda) - 2 = \lambda^2 - 7\lambda + 10 = 0$$ $$(\lambda - 5)(\lambda - 2) = 0 \implies \mathbf{\lambda_1 = 5, \quad \lambda_2 = 2}$$
  • For $\lambda_1 = 5$: $(A - 5I)\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0} \implies -v_1 + v_2 = 0 \implies \mathbf{v}_1 = [1, 1]^T$
  • For $\lambda_2 = 2$: $(A - 2I)\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0} \implies 2v_1 + v_2 = 0 \implies \mathbf{v}_2 = [1, -2]^T$
