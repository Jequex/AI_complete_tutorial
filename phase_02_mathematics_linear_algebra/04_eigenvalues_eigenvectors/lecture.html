Topic 2.4: Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors

Uncovering the invariant axes of space, characteristic polynomials, diagonalization, and vanishing/exploding gradients.

1. The 12-Year-Old Analogy: The Spinning Globe

🧒 The North and South Poles

Spin a globe of the Earth. London, Tokyo, and New York whip around in circles, continuously changing their spatial direction. But look at the North Pole and South Pole.

They lie on the axis of rotation. They never leave their line!

In linear algebra, almost every vector gets rotated off its direction when multiplied by a matrix. But Eigenvectors are the special arrows that never rotate off their line—they only get stretched or shrunk by factor $\lambda$!

$$\boxed{A \mathbf{v} = \lambda \mathbf{v}}$$

2. The Characteristic Equation: $\det(A - \lambda I) = 0$

🔬 Finding Eigenvalues Analytically

Rewrite $A\mathbf{v} = \lambda\mathbf{v}$ as:

$$(A - \lambda I)\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}$$

For a non-zero eigenvector $\mathbf{v} \neq \mathbf{0}$ to exist in the null space, the matrix $(A - \lambda I)$ must have determinant zero:

$$\boxed{\det(A - \lambda I) = 0}$$

Example: A = [ [4, 1],
               [2, 3] ]

det(A - λI) = (4 - λ)(3 - λ) - (1 × 2) = 0
             λ² - 7λ + 10 = 0
             (λ - 5)(λ - 2) = 0

Eigenvalues: λ₁ = 5,  λ₂ = 2

For λ₁ = 5:  v₁ = [ 1,  1 ]^T  --> A v₁ = [ 5,  5 ]^T = 5 v₁
For λ₂ = 2:  v₂ = [ 1, -2 ]^T  --> A v₂ = [ 2, -4 ]^T = 2 v₂

3. Diagonalization: $A = Q \Lambda Q^{-1}$

If $A$ has $n$ independent eigenvectors, assemble them into matrix $Q$ and place eigenvalues into diagonal matrix $\Lambda$:

$$A = Q \Lambda Q^{-1}$$

Computing powers $A^k$ becomes effortless, because all interior $Q^{-1} Q$ terms cancel:

$$A^{100} = Q \Lambda^{100} Q^{-1} = Q \begin{bmatrix} \lambda_1^{100} & 0 \\ 0 & \lambda_2^{100} \end{bmatrix} Q^{-1}$$

4. Why Eigenvalues Matter for Deep Learning

🔬 Gradient Stability in RNNs and Deep Networks

In deep networks, activations and gradients are repeatedly multiplied across layers: $\mathbf{h}_T \approx W^T \mathbf{h}_0$.

  • If $|\lambda_{\max}| > 1$: Gradients explode to $\pm\infty$.
  • If $|\lambda_{\max}| < 1$: Gradients vanish to $0$.
  • This insight directly motivated Spectral Normalization, Orthogonal Initialization, and ResNet skip connections!
