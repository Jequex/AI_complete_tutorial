Problem 1: Full Eigendecomposition by Hand

Given symmetric matrix $B = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$:

  • Part A: Compute the characteristic polynomial $\det(B - \lambda I) = 0$.
  • Part B: Find eigenvalues $\lambda_1, \lambda_2$.
  • Part C: Find eigenvectors $\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2$.
  • Part D: Verify that $\mathbf{v}_1 \cdot \mathbf{v}_2 = 0$.
💡 Click to Reveal Step-by-Step Solution

Solution:

Part A & B: Eigenvalues

$$\det \begin{bmatrix} 2-\lambda & 1 \\ 1 & 2-\lambda \end{bmatrix} = (2-\lambda)^2 - 1 = \lambda^2 - 4\lambda + 3 = 0$$

$$(\lambda - 3)(\lambda - 1) = 0 \implies \mathbf{\lambda_1 = 3, \quad \lambda_2 = 1}$$

Part C: Eigenvectors

  • For $\lambda_1 = 3$: $(B - 3I)\mathbf{v}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} v_1 \\ v_2 \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{0} \implies v_1 = v_2 \implies \mathbf{v}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$.
  • For $\lambda_2 = 1$: $(B - 1I)\mathbf{v}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} v_1 \\ v_2 \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{0} \implies v_2 = -v_1 \implies \mathbf{v}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}$.

Part D: Orthogonality

$$\mathbf{v}_1 \cdot \mathbf{v}_2 = (1)(1) + (1)(-1) = 1 - 1 = \mathbf{0} \quad \text{(Orthogonal!)}$$

Problem 2: Fast Matrix Powers via Diagonalization

Use $B = Q \Lambda Q^{-1}$ to calculate $B^4$ without doing 4 successive matrix multiplications.

💡 Click to Reveal Step-by-Step Solution

Solution:

$$Q = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \Lambda = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad Q^{-1} = \frac{1}{2} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix}$$

$$\Lambda^4 = \begin{bmatrix} 3^4 & 0 \\ 0 & 1^4 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 81 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

$$B^4 = Q \Lambda^4 Q^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 81 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \frac{1}{2} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{\begin{bmatrix} 41 & 40 \\ 40 & 41 \end{bmatrix}}$$

Problem 3: RNN Gradient Stability

Hidden states evolve as $\mathbf{h}_t = W \mathbf{h}_{t-1}$. If $\mathbf{h}_0$ is an eigenvector with eigenvalue $\lambda$, compute the signal multiplier $\lambda^{100}$ after 100 steps for $\lambda = 1.10$ and $\lambda = 0.90$.

💡 Click to Reveal Step-by-Step Solution

Solution:

  • For $\lambda = 1.10$: $(1.10)^{100} \approx \mathbf{13,780.6}$ (Exploding gradient—overflows float limits).
  • For $\lambda = 0.90$: $(0.90)^{100} \approx \mathbf{0.0000266}$ (Vanishing gradient—past context is forgotten).
