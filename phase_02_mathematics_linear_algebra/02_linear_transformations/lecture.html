Topic 2.2: Linear Transformations & Determinants

Viewing matrices as space-warping machines: rotations, scalings, shears, and the geometric area meaning of determinants.

1. The 12-Year-Old Analogy: The Stretchy Rubber Sheet

🧒 The Two Rules of Space Warping

Imagine drawing a grid on a sheet of stretchy rubber with origin $(0, 0)$ pinned in the center. Now stretch the rubber with your hands. For it to be a Linear Transformation:

  1. Rule 1: The origin $(0, 0)$ can never move!
  2. Rule 2: All grid lines must remain straight and evenly spaced (no curves, bends, or tears).

2. Formal Definition: The Superposition Principle

A mapping $T: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^m$ is a linear transformation if and only if for all vectors $\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}$ and scalars $c_1, c_2 \in \mathbb{R}$:

$$\boxed{T(c_1 \mathbf{u} + c_2 \mathbf{v}) = c_1 T(\mathbf{u}) + c_2 T(\mathbf{v})}$$

Every linear transformation can be computed as matrix multiplication: $T(\mathbf{x}) = A\mathbf{x}$.

3. Where do Basis Vectors $\hat{i}$ and $\hat{j}$ Land?

You can understand what any matrix does by following where the two standard basis arrows land:

  • $\hat{i} = [1, 0]^T$ (1 step right on $x$-axis)
  • $\hat{j} = [0, 1]^T$ (1 step up on $y$-axis)
🔬 The Columns of $A$ are Just the Landing Sites of $\hat{i}$ and $\hat{j}$!

$$A \hat{i} = \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} a \\ c \end{bmatrix} \longleftarrow \mathbf{\text{Column 1 of } A}$$

$$A \hat{j} = \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} b \\ d \end{bmatrix} \longleftarrow \mathbf{\text{Column 2 of } A}$$

If $A = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & -2 \\ 1 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$, then $\hat{i}$ lands at $[3, 1]^T$ and $\hat{j}$ lands at $[-2, 4]^T$!

4. The Transformation Zoo

  • Scaling: $\begin{bmatrix} s_x & 0 \\ 0 & s_y \end{bmatrix}$ expands or shrinks axes independently.
  • Rotation ($R_\theta$): $$\boxed{R_\theta = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{bmatrix}}$$ Rotates all 2D space counterclockwise by angle $\theta$.
  • Shear: $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & k \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$ slides horizontal lines like a deck of playing cards.
  • Projection: $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$ squashes 2D space flat onto the 1D $x$-axis.

5. The Determinant: The Area Scaling Factor

🧒 Parallelogram Area

Take a $1 \times 1$ square formed by $\hat{i}$ and $\hat{j}$ (original area = 1). When matrix $A$ stretches space, that square turns into a slanted parallelogram. The Determinant $\det(A)$ is the area of that new parallelogram!

$$\boxed{\det(A) = ad - bc}$$

Value of $\det(A)$ Geometric Space Effect AI & Neural Network Implication
$\det(A) > 1$ Space expands Risk of exploding gradients across repeated layers
$0 < \det(A) < 1$ Space contracts Risk of vanishing gradients
$\det(A) = 1$ Area preserved (rigid rotation) Orthogonal weights (stable representation flow)
$\det(A) = 0$ Space collapses to a line or point Irreversible information loss! Non-invertible ($A^{-1}$ does not exist).
$\det(A) < 0$ Space was flipped / inverted Orientation reversal (mirror reflection)
