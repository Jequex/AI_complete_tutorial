Topic 2.2: Linear Transformations

Matrices as geometric engines of space: basis landing sites, rotations, shears, projections, and determinants.

1. 🧒 The 12-Year-Old Analogy: The Stretchy Rubber Sheet

🧒 The Rubber Sheet Rules

Imagine stretching a rubber sheet with a grid drawn on it:

  1. Rule 1: The origin $(0, 0)$ can never move!
  2. Rule 2: All grid lines must remain completely straight and evenly spaced!

A matrix is an engine that executes this stretching: $T(\mathbf{x}) = A\mathbf{x}$.

2. The Big Secret: Basis Landing Sites ($\hat{i}$ and $\hat{j}$)

The columns of any matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix}$ are literally the landing sites of basis arrows $\hat{i} = [1, 0]^T$ and $\hat{j} = [0, 1]^T$!

$$A \hat{i} = \begin{bmatrix} a \\ c \end{bmatrix}, \qquad A \hat{j} = \begin{bmatrix} b \\ d \end{bmatrix}$$

3. 📋 Mathematical & Technical Symbol Decoder Table

$\hat{i}$ "i-hat" Standard unit basis vector along $x$-axis ($[1, 0]^T$) Walking exactly 1 step East
$\hat{j}$ "j-hat" Standard unit basis vector along $y$-axis ($[0, 1]^T$) Walking exactly 1 step North
$R_\theta$ "R of theta" 2D counterclockwise rotation matrix by angle $\theta$ Spinning a steering wheel by angle $\theta$
$\det(A)$ "determinant of A" Scaling factor of area (or volume) caused by matrix $A$ How many times bigger a $1 \times 1$ rubber square becomes
$A^{-1}$ "A inverse" Matrix that completely undoes transformation $A$ Pressing "Undo" (Ctrl+Z) to unstretch the rubber sheet
$A_{\text{shear}}$ "A-shear" Transformation sliding coordinates parallel to an axis Pushing the top of a neat deck of playing cards sideways

4. The Determinant: Area Scaling Multiplier

$$\det(A) = ad - bc$$ $$\underbrace{\det(A)}_{\text{Area scaling multiplier}} = \underbrace{a \cdot d}_{\text{Main diagonal stretch}} - \underbrace{b \cdot c}_{\text{Off-diagonal shear overlap}}$$
🔬 Concrete Calculation

For $A = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{v} = [2, 1]^T$:

  • Transformed vector: $A\mathbf{v} = 2 [3, 0]^T + 1 [1, 2]^T = [7, 2]^T$
  • Determinant: $(3)(2) - (1)(0) = \mathbf{6.0}$ (Area scaled by 6x!)
  • If $\det(A) = 0$, space is squashed flat to a line or point, causing irreversible data loss.
