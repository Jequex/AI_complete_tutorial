Problem 1: Matrix Synthesis from Basis Vector Landings

A transformation lands $\hat{i}$ at $[2, 5]^T$ and $\hat{j}$ at $[-3, 1]^T$. Find matrix $A$, transform $[4, -2]^T$, and compute $\det(A)$.

Click to view solution

Part A: Matrix $A$

$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -3 \\ 5 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

Part B: Transform Vector

$$A \begin{bmatrix} 4 \\ -2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} (2)(4) + (-3)(-2) \\ (5)(4) + (1)(-2) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 14 \\ 18 \end{bmatrix}$$

Part C: Determinant

$$\det(A) = (2)(1) - (-3)(5) = 2 + 15 = \mathbf{17}$$

Problem 2: Composition of Rotations ($R_{90^\circ} \times R_{90^\circ}$)

Compute $R_{90^\circ} \times R_{90^\circ}$ and verify it matches $R_{180^\circ}$.

Click to view solution $$R_{90^\circ} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$ $$R_{90^\circ}^2 = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{bmatrix} = R_{180^\circ}$$

Problem 3: The Singular Inversion Paradox ($\det(M) = 0$)

For $M = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 6 \\ 1 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$, show that $\det(M) = 0$ and explain why $M^{-1}$ cannot exist.

Click to view solution $$\det(M) = (2)(3) - (6)(1) = 6 - 6 = \mathbf{0}$$

Both basis columns land on the line $y = \frac{1}{2}x$. The 2D plane collapses into a 1D line. Since infinitely many inputs collapse to the same output, unique inversion is mathematically impossible.
