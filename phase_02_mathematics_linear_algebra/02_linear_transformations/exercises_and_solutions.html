Topic 2.2: Practice Exercises & Solutions

Matrix synthesis from basis landing sites, composition of rotations, and singular collapse analysis.

Problem 1: Matrix Synthesis from Basis Vector Landings

A linear transformation $T: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$ maps $\hat{i}$ and $\hat{j}$:

$$T(\hat{i}) = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 5 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad T(\hat{j}) = \begin{bmatrix} -3 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

  • Part A: Write the $2 \times 2$ transformation matrix $A$.
  • Part B: Compute the transformed landing site of point $\mathbf{v} = [4, -2]^T$.
  • Part C: Compute the determinant $\det(A)$. How are areas affected?
💡 Click to Reveal Step-by-Step Solution

Solution:

Part A: Matrix Columns

$$A = \begin{bmatrix} T(\hat{i}) & T(\hat{j}) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -3 \\ 5 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

Part B: Transforming $\mathbf{v}$

$$A \mathbf{v} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -3 \\ 5 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 4 \\ -2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} (2)(4) + (-3)(-2) \\ (5)(4) + (1)(-2) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 8 + 6 \\ 20 - 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{14} \\ \mathbf{18} \end{bmatrix}$$

Part C: Determinant

$$\det(A) = ad - bc = (2)(1) - (-3)(5) = 2 - (-15) = \mathbf{17}$$

All 2D geometric regions expand in area by a factor of exactly 17x.

Problem 2: Composition of Rotations

Given the counterclockwise rotation matrix $R_\theta = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{bmatrix}$:

  • Part A: Form the matrix $R_{90^\circ}$.
  • Part B: Calculate $R_{90^\circ} \times R_{90^\circ}$.
  • Part C: Compare your product to $R_{180^\circ}$.
💡 Click to Reveal Step-by-Step Solution

Solution:

Part A: For $90^\circ$: $\cos=0, \sin=1 \implies R_{90^\circ} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$.

Part B: Matrix multiplication:

$$R_{90^\circ} R_{90^\circ} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{bmatrix} = -I$$

Part C: For $180^\circ$: $\cos=-1, \sin=0 \implies R_{180^\circ} = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{bmatrix}$. Matrix multiplication perfectly preserves function composition ($90^\circ + 90^\circ = 180^\circ$).

Problem 3: The Singular Inversion Paradox ($\det = 0$)

Let $M = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 6 \\ 1 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$.

  • Part A: Compute $\det(M)$.
  • Part B: Describe the geometric transformation of the 2D plane.
  • Part C: Why is $M$ non-invertible?
💡 Click to Reveal Step-by-Step Solution

Solution:

Part A: $\det(M) = (2)(3) - (6)(1) = 6 - 6 = \mathbf{0}$.

Part B: $\hat{i} \to [2, 1]^T$ and $\hat{j} \to [6, 3]^T = 3[2, 1]^T$. Both basis vectors land on the line $y = 0.5x$. The 2D plane collapses into a 1D line.

Part C: Infinitely many points collapse onto each point on the line. One cannot uniquely uncollapse a 1D line back into a 2D plane. Hence, $M^{-1}$ cannot exist.
