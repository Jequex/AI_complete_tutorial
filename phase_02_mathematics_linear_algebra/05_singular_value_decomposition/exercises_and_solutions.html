Topic 2.5: Practice Exercises & Solutions

Step-by-step SVD calculations, Eckart-Young approximation errors, and the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse.

Problem 1: Step-by-Step SVD of a Rank-1 Matrix

Let $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$.

  • Part A: Compute $A^T A$.
  • Part B: Find singular values $\sigma_1, \sigma_2$. Why is $\sigma_2 = 0$?
  • Part C: Find right singular vectors $\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2$.
  • Part D: Find left singular vector $\mathbf{u}_1 = \frac{1}{\sigma_1} A \mathbf{v}_1$.
  • Part E: Verify $A = \sigma_1 \mathbf{u}_1 \mathbf{v}_1^T$.
💡 Click to Reveal Step-by-Step Solution

Solution:

Part A: $A^T A = \begin{bmatrix} 5 & 10 \\ 10 & 20 \end{bmatrix}$.

Part B: $\det(A^T A - \lambda I) = \lambda^2 - 25\lambda = 0 \implies \lambda_1 = 25, \lambda_2 = 0$.
$$\mathbf{\sigma_1 = \sqrt{25} = 5, \qquad \sigma_2 = \sqrt{0} = 0}$$ $\sigma_2 = 0$ because Row 2 is $2 \times$ Row 1 (Rank = 1).

Part C: $\mathbf{v}_1 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}$, $\mathbf{v}_2 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}} \begin{bmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$.

Part D: $\mathbf{u}_1 = \frac{1}{5} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 4 \end{bmatrix} \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}$.

Part E: $5 \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}\right) \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}\right) = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 4 \end{bmatrix} = A \quad \blacksquare$

Problem 2: Eckart-Young Low-Rank Approximation Error

A matrix $X$ has singular values $\sigma_1 = 10, \sigma_2 = 6, \sigma_3 = 2, \sigma_4 = 1$.

  • Part A: What is $\text{Rank}(X)$?
  • Part B: Calculate Frobenius norm $\|X\|_F = \sqrt{\sum \sigma_i^2}$.
  • Part C: By Eckart-Young, what is error $\|X - X_2\|_F$?
  • Part D: What percentage of energy is retained by $X_2$?
💡 Click to Reveal Step-by-Step Solution

Solution:

Part A: $\text{Rank}(X) = \mathbf{4}$ (four non-zero singular values).

Part B: $\|X\|_F = \sqrt{10^2 + 6^2 + 2^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt{141} \approx \mathbf{11.874}$.

Part C: $\|X - X_2\|_F = \sqrt{\sigma_3^2 + \sigma_4^2} = \sqrt{4 + 1} = \sqrt{5} \approx \mathbf{2.236}$.

Part D: $\frac{\sigma_1^2 + \sigma_2^2}{\|X\|_F^2} = \frac{100 + 36}{141} = \frac{136}{141} \approx \mathbf{96.45\%}$ retained!

Problem 3: The Moore-Penrose Pseudoinverse

Find the pseudoinverse of $A = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 2}$ and verify $A A^+ A = A$.

💡 Click to Reveal Step-by-Step Solution

Solution:

Invert non-zero singular values ($4 \to 1/4, 2 \to 1/2$) and transpose shape ($3 \times 2 \to 2 \times 3$):

$$A^+ = \mathbf{\begin{bmatrix} 1/4 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1/2 & 0 \end{bmatrix}}$$

Verification:

$$A A^+ A = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1/4 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1/2 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = A \quad \blacksquare$$
